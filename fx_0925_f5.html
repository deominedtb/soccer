A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Hungary are relegated: third in League A Group A3 in 2024–25 on 6 points, 4–11 (W1 D3 L2, behind Germany and the Netherlands), then lost the A/B play-off to Türkiye 1–3 in Istanbul and 0–3 in Budapest. This is their first League B campaign after two editions in League A. Ukraine stay in League B: second in Group B1 in 2024–25 on 8 points, 8–8 (W2 D2 L2, behind Czechia, ahead of Georgia and Albania). They then lost their A/B play-off to Belgium, 3–1 in Murcia and 0–3 in Genk. In World Cup qualifying Hungary were third in Group F on 8 points, 11–10 (W2 D2 L2, behind Portugal and the Republic of Ireland). They went out on the last matchday, losing 2–3 at home to Ireland after leading 2–1 at half-time and conceding in the 80th minute and in stoppage time. Ukraine were second in Group D on 10 points, 10–11 (W3 D1 L2, behind France), then lost 1–3 to Sweden in the play-off semi-final. Neither side went to the World Cup. The structural headline is a relegated side with an unchanged coach and a thinned attack, at home to a side under a coach who has never managed a competitive match. The eight sides relegated from League A into League B for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 1.67 points per match over 46 League B matches. At home they took 1.91 points per match over 23 (W12 D8 L3), scoring 1.87 and conceding 0.78, with over 2.5 in 47.8%, BTTS in 52.2% and 6.35 corners won against 3.43 conceded. Four of the eight won their group, three were second and one fourth. Ukraine were one of them: second in B1 in 2022–23. On matchday one the eight went W3 D4 L1; seven of the eight played away. B · Style & early-season reality

Rossi’s Hungary have two records, and the gap between them is the shape and the opposition. In League A 2024–25 they played a back three against Germany, the Netherlands and Bosnia-Herzegovina with 37.5% of the ball. They took 12.00 shots against 15.33, committed 15.00 fouls per match against League B’s 11.48, scored 0.67 per match and failed to score in half. With a back four in 25/26 they had 45.2%, fouled 10.00 times per match, scored 1.83 per match and scored in all six, but they conceded 1.67 per match, and 10 of their 27 competitive goals against came from the 75th minute on. At Puskás Aréna they had 46.5% of the ball, took 12.57 shots against 11.00 and won 6.14 corners against 3.29. They cross heavily (17.64 per match), and Szoboszlai, Orbán and Kerkez carry the set-piece and wide delivery. Kerkez, Szoboszlai and Orbán are in the squad. Varga, the centre-forward who started 13 of 14, is not, and neither is Sallai on the right. The two newest options up front are Lukács (2 competitive starts) and Redzic (none). The evidence for Hungary’s front line tonight is four friendlies and club football.

Ukraine under Rebrov were a 4-1-4-1 side with the ball: 48.9% possession across 15 competitive matches, 51.4% in 25/26, and 21.14 crosses per match in 25/26. Away they had 45.0%, took 10.29 shots against 14.14, won 3.86 corners against 5.00 and conceded 2.29 goals per match, but scored in five of seven. Their fouls fell from 14.33 per match in the League B group to 11.57 in 25/26, and their away matches carried 5.29 cards per match, against the division’s 4.42. Maldera arrives from De Zerbi’s staff at Brighton and Marseille. His two friendlies used a 4-2-3-1 with Sarapii at centre-back and Nazaryna in midfield. He had 46.5% of the ball and 3 shots to Denmark’s 10 in the one with a box score. Whether his Ukraine keep the ball more than Rebrov’s did is a question of a CV and one box score, not of a competitive match. The defence (Trubin, Zabarnyi, Matviyenko, Mykolenko) is intact. The attack is not: the scorers of 13 of the 21 non-own goals are out, and Vanat (6 starts, 2 goals) is the most-used forward in the squad.
