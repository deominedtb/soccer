A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Iceland are relegated from League B: third in Group B4 in 2024–25 on 7 points, 10–13 (W2 D1 L3, behind Wales and Türkiye, ahead of Montenegro), then beaten 2–5 on aggregate by Kosovo in the B/C play-off (1–2 in Pristina on 20 March 2025, 1–3 in the listed home leg in Murcia, Spain, three days later). Their division history reads A/B/B/C, so this is their first League C campaign. In World Cup qualifying they were third in Group D on 7 points, 13–11 (W2 D1 L3, behind France and Ukraine). They beat Azerbaijan 5–0 at home and 2–0 in Baku, drew 2–2 at home to France, and lost 1–2 in Paris, 3–5 at home to Ukraine and 0–2 to Ukraine in Warsaw. Estonia stay in League C: third in Group C1 on 4 points, 3–9 (W1 D1 L4, behind Sweden and Slovakia, ahead of Azerbaijan), in their first League C campaign after promotion from League D; their history reads C/D/C/C. In World Cup qualifying they were fourth of five in Group I on 4 points, 8–21 (W1 D1 L6). The win was 3–2 in Chişinău, against ten men from the 5th minute, and the draw 1–1 at home to Moldova; all six defeats came against Norway, Italy and Israel. The structural headline is the opposition split inside both records: 10 of Iceland’s 14 competitive matches and 8 of Estonia’s were against sides now in Leagues A and B. B · Style & early-season reality

Iceland’s shape moved with the coach. In the 2024–25 League B group, before Arnar, they played a 4-4-2 in four of six, had 40.5% of the ball and were outshot 12.17 to 15.83. Under Arnar they have had 53.0% of the ball over eight, in five different shapes: a 4-4-2 three times, a 5-3-2 in both France matches, a 3-4-3 in the 5–0 against Azerbaijan, a 4-2-3-1 in Baku and a 4-4-1-1 in Pristina. ESPN lists tonight’s as a 4-3-3; fotbolti.net describes two forwards with Albert and Jón Dagur wide. Against Montenegro and Azerbaijan they had 55.6%, took 13.75 shots to 8.75 and put in 19.8 crosses per match, 25.5 in the two at home. Arnar’s stated plan for tonight is “clearly more of the ball … first and foremost a lot of crosses and noise in the box” (fotbolti.net, 25 September); that is a statement, not a match. Their competitive matches have been open: over 2.5 in 10 of 14 and 5 of 6 at home. They conceded late, 9 of 29 goals from the 76th minute. They foul more at home than away (14.00 against 9.86) and are rarely booked for it, one card per 8.40 fouls. The summer changes are at the back. The goalkeeper who started both 2025 clean sheets is out, and Hákon Rafn, who starts, is back from a back injury. Sverrir Ingi, who started three of the four matches against C-or-below sides, is out for the year. Tonight’s back four made 9, 6, 9 and 8 starts in the window, and one of its centre-backs, Stefán Teitur, is listed as a midfielder.

Estonia play without the ball. Over 14 competitive matches they had 37.7% of the ball and were outshot 6.86 to 22.21. They won 2.00 corners to 8.00, and did not win the corner count in any of the 14. In their own 2024–25 League C group those figures were 33.8%, 5.83 shots to 22.50 and 1.83 corners to 9.00. Against Slovakia, Azerbaijan and Moldova the gap narrowed to 43.6%, 8.83 shots to 18.67 and 2.33 corners to 6.83, and they led at half-time in three of six. Henn mostly sets up in a 4-2-3-1 (nine of 14; 4-4-1-1 four times, 5-4-1 in Oslo). They foul much more than they are fouled (13.79 against 8.79) at one card per 6.03 fouls, and all three of their red cards came away. They were level at half-time in 8 of 14, six of them 0–0, and conceded 20 of their 30 goals after the break. The summer removes most of the attack that scored their 11. The five goals of Sappinen, Sinyavskiy, Shein and Yakovlev are gone, and so is Anier. Tonight Henn starts with five at the back, a shape he used once in the 14 (1–4 in Oslo), and Mustmaa, with no competitive start in the window, alone up front. Henn expects Iceland to “want to dominate” and says “if we don’t have the ball ourselves, it will be hard” (Soccernet). The evidence for Estonia’s replacement front line is a name list, not a match.
