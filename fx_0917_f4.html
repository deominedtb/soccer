A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Celtic won the 2025–26 Scottish Premiership — 26-4-8, 73 scored, 41 conceded, 82 points — from 3rd place after 33 rounds, then lost their Champions League qualifier to LASK 4–5 on aggregate. Ferencváros finished 2nd in the 2025–26 NB I — 21-5-7, 67 scored, 31 conceded, 68 points — behind a Győri ETO side coached by Balázs Borbély, who has since become Ferencváros coach; they came through four Europa League qualifying rounds unbeaten. The structural headline is that both clubs’ domestic records are dominant and both have just been weakened or reset: Celtic by the sale of Engels and Maeda and two heavy defeats outside the league, Ferencváros by a new coach and fifteen departures in a league that publishes no xG or fouls.

B · Style & early-season reality

Celtic are a very high-possession side at home in Scotland: 65.5% of the ball, 16.5 shots for and 9.3 against, 2.13 xG for and 0.96 against and 6.63 corners won per league match last season; under O’Neill after January their matches opened up (77.8% over 2.5, 72.2% BTTS, 0.61 first-half goals conceded). This season’s six league matches are more lopsided still — 3.13 xG for, 0.89 against, 9.67 corners won — with low foul and card counts (10.0 and 1.00). Outside the league the picture reverses: 0.74 xG for and 3.19 against in the two LASK matches, and 0–3 at Rangers. Høgh, Hassan, Baur and Durán are in the domestic sample; Sørensen, Lotomba, Emenalo and Johnstone arrived on 1–3 September, so their fit is largely a CV.

Ferencváros under Keane dominated the NB I on results (away record 12-3-2) with 57.4% possession and 6.33 corners won against 3.58, conceding 0.33 goals per first half. Under Borbély, the eight qualifiers are the best-measured evidence: 48.6% possession, 1.75 xG for and 1.00 against, 5.25 corners each way and 13.0 fouls per match — a more even, more physical profile than the domestic one, against opposition up to Twente and Trabzonspor. The league sample under Borbély shows corners down to 4.00 won. Nagy, Bero and Birmančević have joined a midfield and attack that lost Abu Fani, Makreckis, Kanichowsky and five forwards; how the rebuilt side fits is shown by the qualifiers, not by last season.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Celtic’s domestic numbers overstate them against stronger sides. 3.13 league xG this season, but 0.74 xG and 1–5 at LASK and 0–3 at Rangers on Sunday.
  • Celtic are short in several positions tonight. Johnston, Jota and Osmand injured, Oxlade-Chamberlain suspended, Yang away, two signings unregistered, and the goalkeeper undecided; four signings arrived between 1 and 3 September.
  • Ferencváros’s last season was another coach’s and another squad’s. Keane left in May and 15 players followed; the qualifiers under Borbély are the relevant series, and the NB I publishes no xG or fouls to check them against.
  • The referee is new to this competition. First Europa League match, no published foul or penalty record.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, handicap and discipline. Least sensitive: totals, where both clubs’ over-2.5 rates agree across two seasons and Ferencváros’s European series.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals are more robust than the 1X2 here because both clubs’ over-2.5 rates (68%/67% last season, 67%/67% this) and Ferencváros’s 62.5% in qualifiers agree, whereas the result depends on whether Celtic’s league or LASK form describes them.
  • Halves are more robust than discipline because both first-half series are complete across two seasons, while the official has no published foul or penalty record and no Europa League match.
  • Corners are more robust for Ferencváros’s side than Celtic’s because their European series is eight matches (5.25/5.38) against Celtic’s two.
  • Team goals are more robust than the handicap because each side has at least one long scoring series, whereas neither has a margin series against comparable opposition.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: a fouls and penalty record for Kolarić. Without it, Discipline rests on a single yellow-card rate from the Croatian league; it would need the HNS, UEFA or an aggregator to publish his foul and penalty counts.
