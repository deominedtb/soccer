A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Olympiacos finished 2nd in the 2025–26 Greek Super League — 19 wins, 9 draws, 4 defeats, 51 scored, 17 conceded over 32 matches including the play-offs (17–7–2 and 45–11 in the regular season) — and reached the Champions League knockout play-off, where Leverkusen eliminated them 2–0 on aggregate. This summer NEC Nijmegen knocked them out of Champions League qualifying (0–0 home, 1–2 away). Jagiellonia finished 3rd in the 2025–26 Ekstraklasa — 15 wins, 11 draws, 8 defeats, 56 scored, 41 conceded, 56 points — and reached this league phase by beating Rangers and Iberia 1999 in Europa League qualifying. The structural headline is the home side’s upheaval: a coach appointed on 10 September, a squad turned over at almost every position, and centre-forward Ayoub El Kaabi out for the season.

B · Style & early-season reality

Olympiacos last season were the Greek league’s controlling defensive side: 60.4% possession, 0.61 xG and 0.53 goals against per match, a 59% clean-sheet rate, one first-half goal conceded in the 18 league matches with minutes counted, and only 34% of their league matches over 2.5. They generated set-piece volume — 6.88 corners per match in a league at 4.38 per team — while committing 13.3 fouls against a league mean of 14.5. This season’s four league matches show the same shape in the numbers (61.7% possession, 1.98 xG for, 0.65 against, 9.25 corners) with almost none of the finishing: three goals. What the summer changed is almost the whole spine — goalkeeper, both centre-backs, the defensive midfield and the centre-forward — and on top of that the coach changed on 10 September. The only match since Alguacil’s appointment was a 0–1 home defeat.

Jagiellonia were the opposite profile in Poland: 58.1% possession but 1.64 xG conceded per match, BTTS in 65% of league matches and over 2.5 in 65%, 12.1 fouls and 2.26 cards per match. This season the league pattern holds (57% over, 57% BTTS, one clean sheet in seven), but their European matches looked different: 42.8% possession, 6.25 corners conceded and only 2.75 won per qualifier, and three goals conceded in four. That is the one sample of Jagiellonia playing without the ball against stronger opposition, and they are without Kobayashi at centre-back. The midfield evidence is a CV: Mazurek has gone and Finndell and Klynge arrived in late July and late August.
