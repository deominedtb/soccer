A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted, and their two seasons finished ten places apart while producing almost the same goal difference. Getafe were 7th on 51 points — 38 matches, 15 wins, 6 draws, 17 defeats, 32 scored, 38 conceded, −6 — and qualified for the Conference League. Osasuna were 17th on 42 points with 11 wins, 9 draws, 18 defeats, 44 scored and 50 conceded, also −6, and stayed up. The identical goal difference is the point: Getafe got to Europe by winning fifteen matches and losing seventeen with only six draws, which is a remarkably decisive record for a side that scored 32 goals all season, while Osasuna scored twelve more goals than Getafe and finished ten places lower. The structural headline is that only one of these clubs changed anything this summer, and it changed almost everything. Osasuna replaced Alessio Lisci with Luis Miguel Ramis on 10 June 2026 on a contract to 2028, then sold Víctor Muñoz to Liverpool for €40m when the release clause was activated on 18 June. Getafe kept José Bordalás, kept the method, and spent the window adding depth for a European campaign that has already started — they have played three competitive matches in the ten days before this one.

  • Osasuna — turnover high, new manager, and the summer’s largest single sale on this card. Luis Miguel Ramis was appointed on 10 June 2026 through to 2028, replacing Alessio Lisci. Eight days later Víctor Muñoz left for Liverpool for €40m on an activated release clause — the biggest fee involving any club on this slate. The reinvestment is a fraction of it: Jonathan Dubasin for €4m fixed plus €1m in variables on a four-year deal with a €40m clause, official on 27 July; Rockson Yeboah from IFK Göteborg for €3m to 2031, official 12 August; and Diego Rico, left-back, on a free from Getafe on the same day, one year with a €10m clause. Álex Lizancos from Burgos passed a medical on 29 August with the deal still pending. Also out: Javi Galán to Celta on a free on 30 June, Juan Cruz to Real Oviedo on 24 July, Iker Benito to Querétaro on 14 August with Osasuna retaining 50 per cent of his rights, and Sheraldo Becker made permanent at Mainz for a reported €500,000. Gap: no goalkeeper and no centre-back were signed, and Yeboah’s position is not stated in any source consulted — so roughly €7m of the €40m has been reinvested and none of it verifiably in the defensive spine. That is a new manager with a rebuilt budget and an unchanged back line.
  • Getafe — turnover high in volume, low in disruption, and unusually cheap. José Bordalás continues after Conference League qualification; no change of manager or method. Seven additions, almost all free or nominal: Enes Ünal returns from Bournemouth on roughly €1.5m in variables plus a 20 per cent sell-on, three years; Johan Mojica at left-back from Mallorca for about €500,000; Saba Sazonov, centre-back, from Torino on a free to 2027 with an option; Nemanja Gudelj from Sevilla on a free for one year; Francho Serrano from Real Zaragoza on a free to 2029; Giorgi Kochorashvili on loan from Sporting CP with a €5m option; and Obed Vargas on loan from Atlético. Four winter signings — Martín Satriano, Zaid Romero, Sebastián Boselli and Mario Martín — carry over as effectively new, and Christantus Uche is back from a Premier League loan. Out: Diego Rico to Osasuna on a free, Javi Muñoz permanently to Almería, Álex Sancris to Leganés on loan with an option, Juan Berrocal authorised to negotiate with Cádiz, and Borja Mayoral declared transferable on salary grounds. A centre-back and a left-back were both addressed — Sazonov and Mojica — which is more than the home side can say. Gap: no fee is published for Kochorashvili’s loan, Vargas’s loan or the Muñoz sale, and no goalkeeper movement was recorded either way.

B · Style & early-season reality

Getafe are the most legible team on this card and the least likely to have changed. Bordalás has been in charge throughout, and 32 goals scored in 38 matches with 38 conceded is the arithmetic of his method: a low-scoring, physically contested, interruption-heavy game in which the margin is thin and deliberately kept thin. What is unusual is that this version got to Europe by being decisive rather than dour — fifteen wins and seventeen defeats with only six draws is a very high-variance record for a side that scored fewer than a goal a game. Last season they were the third most-booked team in LaLiga at 2.4 yellows a match and sixth for reds at 0.2, and the summer did nothing to soften that: the additions are Gudelj, Sazonov, Mojica, Serrano, Kochorashvili and Vargas, a list weighted to holding midfielders and defenders, with Ünal the only forward. Two league matches in and they already carry four yellows and a red. The one genuinely new variable is the fixture load. Three competitive matches in ten days including a trip to Belgrade is a schedule this squad has not carried in recent seasons, and it arrives with a manager who has never had European football to rotate around here.

Osasuna are the harder read, and the honest reason is that the evidence for what they now are is a CV rather than a body of matches. Luis Miguel Ramis has been in the job since 10 June and has taken charge of two league fixtures, one of them a goalless home draw. His squad has been reshaped by a single transaction — €40m from Liverpool for Víctor Muñoz — of which roughly €7m has gone back out on Dubasin and Yeboah, with Diego Rico added on a free and no centre-back or goalkeeper signed at all. Last season’s Osasuna scored 44 and conceded 50, which is a more open profile than Getafe’s and the reason the two clubs finished ten places apart on the same goal difference; whether that profile survives a change of manager is exactly what nobody yet knows. What can be said is that Ramis has announced changes to his eleven for this match, on grounds of both fatigue and opponent, and that his side’s three yellow cards in two matches is currently the second-lowest count in the division. El Sadar is a genuine venue advantage historically, and the market is treating it as one at 2.20 against 4.10.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Osasuna’s 44 goals scored last season belong to a different manager. Ramis arrived on 10 June, has two league matches on the board, and has already announced changes to his eleven for this fixture on grounds of fatigue and opponent. Any read that carries last season’s attacking profile forward is carrying Alessio Lisci’s work under someone else’s name.
  • The €40m is the fact of the summer and most of it is still unspent. Víctor Muñoz’s release clause was activated by Liverpool on 18 June; Dubasin at €4m plus variables and Yeboah at €3m are the reinvestment, with Diego Rico added free. No centre-back and no goalkeeper were signed by a side that conceded 50 goals in 38 matches.
  • Getafe arrive on their third competitive match in ten days, including a defeat in Belgrade. Their squad was built for a 38-match season and has just been handed a European league phase; the seven summer additions are mostly free transfers and loans, which is depth acquired cheaply rather than quality acquired. The 3–0 at Alavés on the opening day is the one result on their record that looks like a side stretched.
  • The calendar itself is unreliable evidence here. Osasuna’s opening fixture at Celta was postponed for a fungus in the pitch and played out of sequence, and two sources disagree on the scorelines of three of the four league matches these clubs have played. Both teams’ season-to-date records are therefore softer than a two-match sample would normally be.
  • Most sensitive: anything that assumes continuity in Osasuna’s attacking output, because the manager, the most valuable player and the starting eleven have all changed inside three months and the replacement spending is concentrated nowhere in particular. Least sensitive: the discipline family, where a referee averaging 4.6 cautions a match at 5.75 fouls per card meets the division’s third most-booked side of last season, already carrying a red card this one.
