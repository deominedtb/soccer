A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and neither is new to this division. Bayern won the 2025–26 Bundesliga with 89 points — 28 wins, 5 draws, 1 defeat, 122 scored, 36 conceded, and did it with a foul rate of 8.79 per match, the lowest discipline load of any side on tonight’s slate. Union finished 11th on 39 points — 10 wins, 9 draws, 15 defeats, 44 scored, 58 conceded, with a home record of 26–26 and an away record of 18–32. The structural headline is not the gap in class, which is old news and not what this board is for: it is that Union changed head coach and replaced all four senior centre-backs in one window for a combined €3m, then sold their leading forward for €17m, while Bayern kept their coach and changed only the deep midfield and the goalkeeping bench. One of these two squads can be read off last season’s numbers and the other cannot.

B · Style & early-season reality

Bayern are playing the most extreme version of themselves. Possession 71.2% across the three league matches, 21.3 shots per match against 6.7 faced, 19.33 deep completions per match against 3.00, and a PPDA of 7.09 — they win the ball back inside seven opposition passes, against a league average pressure they themselves face at 18.64. Last season the same side ran at 67.4% possession, 13.91 deep completions and a PPDA of 9.06, so the press has tightened rather than loosened under an unchanged coach. The corner column is where this shows up most plainly: 9.67 won and 2.00 conceded per match, against a divisional 5.72 per team. The one contrary data point is real and should not be smoothed away — they drew 0–0 at Schalke, one of three matches, and that single result carries their failed-to-score rate to 33.3% against 0.0% across the whole of last season.

Union have changed shape, and the change is measurable rather than asserted. Under Lustrinelli their PPDA has fallen from 15.45 to 9.44 — last season’s Union sat off and let opponents have 40.3% of the ball; this season’s presses nearly twice as aggressively and has 50.3% of it. The early cost is visible in the same rows: xG against has risen from 1.65 to 2.91 per match, deep completions conceded from 7.03 to 11.67, and shots faced from 12.9 to 17.3, with ten goals conceded in three. The new centre-back line is the unit being asked to hold that higher line, and it is four players who were not at the club in May, bought for €3m between them, two of whom one preview says are unavailable tonight. The evidence that this is a system change is a coach’s start date and three matches of pressing data, not a season — and the away fixture that produced the clearest reading of it, 0–4 at Leverkusen, is also the least representative sample a new back line could give.

C · Market-type suitability

1X2 / MoneylineOK — asymmetric evidence
One side of this fixture is exceptionally well documented and the other is barely documented at all. Bayern bring an unchanged coach, 34 matches of last season and 89 points; Union bring a head coach appointed on 1 July, four centre-backs signed this summer and three league matches. The readable part of the question is therefore the home side; the away side’s defensive level is a genuine unknown that no published figure settles. The divisional home-win rate is 51.9% over 27 matches this season against 43.8% over 306 last, which is context, not a finding.
Asian handicap / DNBDangerous
A handicap needs a readable margin and this card cannot supply one. Bayern’s three league margins are +4, 0 and +1 — the zero coming at a promoted side away — and their 25/26 home record of 68–19 belongs to a season in which they faced a different set of opponents in different states of repair. Union’s conceded column, ten in three, is drawn from a back line assembled six weeks ago and possibly missing two of its four members. The central estimate is not the problem; the spread around it is.
Totals (O/U goals)Well-suited — best evidenced on the card
This is the one family where every input is published, large and pointing the same way. The Bundesliga is running at 3.85 goals per match and 81.5% over 2.5 across 27 matches, against 3.24 and 63.7% over the full 306 of last season; Bayern went over in 94.1% of 34 matches last season and 66.7% of three this; all three of Union’s league matches have gone over. Both clubs’ goals-for and goals-against rates exist for two seasons, the divisional baseline exists for both, and the xG columns corroborate them rather than contradict them. The honest caveat is that the 81.5% divisional figure is 27 matches old and its own 25/26 value is eighteen points lower.
BTTSOK — the away attack is the weak input
The defensive half of the question is well covered: Bayern kept a clean sheet in 35.3% of last season and 33.3% of this, Union in 20.6% and 0% respectively, and both figures sit against a divisional 24.1% per team-appearance. The scoring half is exactly where Union’s evidence collapses — their €17m leading forward was sold, his replacement arrived on loan from Atlanta United in the same window, and Burke and Ilić are both out tonight. Union failed to score in 23.5% of last season and 33.3% of this; neither number describes the forward line that will actually play.

Union’s is the opposite — 1.29 per match last season, 1.33 this, from an attack that has lost its top scorer and has two forwards unavailable. Rating the two halves of this family the same way would be a mistake: one of them has two seasons of evidence and the other has three matches and a transfer ledger.

A first-half result is a different matter — Bayern have scored 0.67 first-half goals per match across three and conceded none, which on a three-match sample is not a rate at all, and Union’s 0.33 against 1.67 comes from the same three matches, one of which was 0–4 away.

Against that, his 26/27 league sample is one match at 1.00 cards. The clubs pull in opposite directions and both figures are real: Bayern commit 10.0 fouls and take 1.00 cards per match, Union 11.3 and 2.33, against a divisional 11.24 fouls per team and 3.22 cards per match. CornersWell-suited — the clearest structural signal here
The corner asymmetry is the one number on this card that says the same thing in both seasons and in both directions. The mechanism behind it is visible in the same data rather than assumed — 71.2% possession, 21.3 shots, 19.33 deep completions against 3.00 — which is why the extremes of the three-match sample are worth something instead of being noise. Treat the 9.67 as the direction being right and the level being unsettled.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Union’s 2025–26 numbers describe a team that no longer exists in the relevant places. The 1.71 goals conceded per match and 20.6% clean-sheet rate were produced by Doekhi and Leite, both of whom left, in front of a coach who left. The four replacements cost €3m combined and have three league matches together. Every defensive figure on this card that carries a 25/26 label should be read as history rather than as a current property.
  • The division itself is the widest gap on the page. The Bundesliga is running at 3.85 goals per match and 81.5% over 2.5 this season against 3.24 and 63.7% last — a 0.61-goal difference between two baselines both of which appear in the strip. Twenty-seven matches is a fifth of a season. Any read that leans on the 26/27 divisional line is leaning on a sample smaller than a single club’s season.
  • Three league matches each is the smallest sample on tonight’s slate, and both clubs’ three contain one result that distorts every rate built on them — Bayern’s 0–0 at Schalke, which alone produces their 33.3% failed-to-score, and Union’s 0–4 at Leverkusen, which alone carries much of their 2.91 xG against. Neither can be excluded and neither should be treated as typical.
  • The referee’s current-season record is one match. Brand’s 5.2 fouls per card is a two-season figure and a good one, but his 26/27 Bundesliga line reads 1.00 cards in one appearance, and his only other two matches this season returned 6.00 and 3.00 in different competitions. The discipline row rests on last season’s tolerance holding.
  • Most sensitive: Asian handicap and first-half result — both need a margin or a narrow window read off three matches, one of which is an outlier for each club, and both would move on whether Friedrich and Nsoki play. Least sensitive: corners and total goals — both carry two seasons of club rates, a divisional baseline in each season, and a visible mechanism in possession, shot and deep-completion data.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners are more robust than the handicap here because the corner gap holds in both seasons and both directions — Bayern 6.35/3.56 then 9.67/2.00, Union 5.03/4.85 then 3.33/6.00 — while the margin evidence is three results of +4, 0 and +1.
  • Total goals is more robust than BTTS here because the total draws on two published divisional baselines and four club rates that agree, whereas BTTS depends on a Union forward line that lost Ilyas Ansah for €17m and is missing Burke and Ilić tonight — the one unit on the card with no usable record.
  • First-half goals is more robust than first-half result here because the timing rates exist at 34 and 38 matches (Bayern 1.41/0.62, Union 0.44/0.79) while the result question reduces to a three-match sample in which Bayern have conceded no first-half goal at all.
  • The discipline row is readable because of the official, not the clubs: Brand’s 31 Bundesliga matches at 5.2 and 5.6 fouls per card are a longer record than either club’s 2026–27 card sample, which is three matches apiece.
  • That question is now answered, and it answered the unfavourable way. The fact I named as most decisive — whether Friedrich and Nsoki play — is settled: both are out, and Van Den Bosch is a substitute, so Union defend with Querfeld and Uduokhai, one holdover and one €1.5m signing, in front of Trimmel and Rothe as full-backs.
