A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Valencia finished 9th in 2025–26 on 49 points — 13 wins, 10 draws, 15 defeats, 46 scored, 55 conceded — with a 42.1% over-2.5 rate against a divisional 50.0%. Real Sociedad finished 10th on 46 points — 11 wins, 13 draws, 14 defeats, 59 scored, 61 conceded — and kept a clean sheet in 7.9% of matches, three in thirty-eight, the lowest rate of any club on this board, with a 78.9% BTTS rate that was the highest in the division. The structural headline is two managerial situations that are both unresolved in different ways. Valencia dismissed Carlos Corberán on Sunday 13 September 2026 — the first head coach removed in LaLiga this season — together with the football CEO Ron Gourlay and his working group, and Óscar Sánchez continues as interim coach for this match. Real Sociedad sacked Sergio Francisco on 14 December 2025 with the club 15th, and the replacement appointed then remains in post — a change nine months old rather than three months old. Neither club spent: Valencia’s largest signing cost €5m and Real Sociedad’s €1.5m.

B · Style & early-season reality

Valencia have stopped creating anything at all, and the collapse is uniform across every column. Shots have fallen from 11.82 to 8.67 per match, shots on target from 3.24 to 2.33, deep completions from 5.03 to 2.83 — the second-lowest figure on this board, behind Málaga’s 1.83 — and xG from 1.42 to 0.69, less than half. Possession is down only marginally (47.6% to 46.5%) but PPDA has loosened dramatically, from 12.55 to 16.11, the third-most passive figure here: they neither press nor build. The result is two goals in six matches, a 66.7% failed-to-score rate and one first-half goal all season. The home record is the sharpest statement of it: three matches at Mestalla, 0–0, 0–1 and 0–5, no goal scored. The defensive side is the one place the counted record flatters them — 33.3% clean sheets, higher than last season’s 23.7% — and the model disagrees at 1.72 xG conceded. Both of their goals and all four of their points have come away from home, and the one win followed the coach’s dismissal.

Real Sociedad have kept their shape and lost their floor. Possession 49.6% against 51.3%, shots 12.17 against 12.34, and a PPDA that has loosened from 9.08 to 12.80 — a 40% drop in pressing intensity, among the largest proportional loosenings on this board. The attacking model has fallen with it, 1.17 xG against 1.57, while the defensive model has worsened to 1.88 against 1.59. What has not changed is the thing that defines this club: they kept clean sheets in 7.9% of thirty-eight matches last season — three all year — and in 16.7% of six this season. They concede. The one series moving in their favour is corners: 7.67 won against 3.83 conceded, up from 5.61 and 4.50, a +3.83 differential, among the six widest on this board, built on a lot of territory and 5.00 shots on target per match. Their discipline has eased from the division’s highest foul count — 14.24 per match last season, against a divisional 12.58 — to 12.50, which is what a 40% looser press produces. They have lost three of their last four and played on Wednesday.

  • Seven Valencia players are out and an eighth is doubtful — the longest absence list on this board. Three of them are centre-backs (Diakhaby, Copete, Tárrega), one a full-back (Foulquier) and two attacking players (Sadiq, Diego López). Any reading of either end of Valencia’s game is a reading of a side missing most of one unit and part of another.
  • Real Sociedad’s team news could not be sourced and they are playing their third match in eight days. Betis away, Atlético at home, Bournemouth at home, now Mestalla — and they have lost three of the last four.
  • Both clubs’ attacking models have fallen materially and neither replaced its departures. Valencia’s xG has halved (1.42 to 0.69) on a €5m window; Real Sociedad’s has fallen a quarter (1.57 to 1.17) having sold Brais Méndez, Carlos Fernández and Karrikaburu and signed one player for €1.5m.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, the Asian handicap and both team-goals lines — an interim coach with seven absentees against a side on its third match in eight days, with a −5 and a −3 inside the twelve-match sample.
