A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Both clubs are promoted, and this is the only fixture on the slate where that is true of both sides. They came up together from the 2025–26 2. Bundesliga: Schalke as champions on 70 points from 34 matches (21-7-6, 50 scored, 31 conceded), Elversberg as runners-up on 62 (18-8-8, 64 scored, 39 conceded), with Paderborn third on the same points and also in this division. Schalke return after three seasons away; Elversberg are playing top-flight football for the first time in their history, as the 59th club ever to appear in the Bundesliga. The structural headline is that both kept the coach who did it and neither could afford to change much else. Miron Muslić remains at Schalke and Vincent Wagner at Elversberg — Wagner, 40, came from three years with Hoffenheim’s second team and a promotion to the third tier in 2025. Schalke spent almost nothing in fees and took in €200,000 from a single sale; Elversberg invested about €12.1m and sold nobody of consequence. The promoted club with the bigger name is the one that spent less. How the division usually treats sides like these is the relevant frame, and this season it is treating them inconsistently: Elversberg beat Bayer Leverkusen on the opening day and Schalke held Bayern Munich to a goalless draw, while both have conceded at rates well outside anything in their 2. Bundesliga records.

The window in one line: roughly €2.6m in fees out and €200,000 in, from one sale — one of the most financially constrained promotions in the division. Paid arrivals: Satoshi Tanaka from Fortuna Düsseldorf, €1m; Junior Dina Ebimbe from Eintracht Frankfurt, €800,000; Junior Adamu from SC Freiburg, €800,000. B · Style & early-season reality

Schalke have responded to promotion by abandoning the ball almost entirely, and the numbers are the most extreme of their kind on this board. Possession has fallen from 45.8% in the 2. Bundesliga to 34.4%, and PPDA has risen from a pressing figure to 26.88 — meaning they allow nearly twenty-seven opposition passes for every defensive action they attempt. For comparison, the most aggressive side on this slate, Liverpool, sits at 7.06, and no other club carded here is above 16. Everything downstream follows: 7.00 fouls per match, the lowest figure of any club on this board, against a divisional 11.32; 2.67 corners won against 8.67 conceded, the widest negative corner differential here; and 11.33 shots against a divisional 15.58. It is a deep block that does not engage, and against Bayern it produced a 0–0 from four shots and no corners. The cost is in the model rather than the scoreboard so far: 3.17 xG conceded per match, the worst figure on this board, against 1.33 goals actually conceded. Their attacking side is a curiosity — 5.33 shots on target from 11.33 shots, a 47% on-target rate that is the highest here — producing 1.77 xG but only 1.00 goal, and two blanks in three.

Elversberg have done the opposite of adapting and it has been more entertaining and no more solid. Possession has fallen from 53.4% to 36.5% — a bigger absolute drop than Schalke’s — but their PPDA is 16.11, still engaged where Schalke’s is not, and their fouls have risen from 10.88 to 12.00. The result is a side that concedes territory and contests it anyway: 8.33 corners conceded against 3.67 won, and 2.85 xG conceded, the third-worst on this board. What separates them from Schalke is what happens at the other end. Every one of their three league matches has finished with both teams scoring and more than 2.5 goals: 3–2 against Leverkusen, 4–3 at Mönchengladbach, 1–2 against Bayern. They have scored 2.67 per match against a 2. Bundesliga rate of 1.88, from 1.82 xG and only 10.00 shots — a conversion rate that is running well ahead of the chance volume, on three matches. Their 5.67 shots on target from 10.00 shots is a 57% on-target rate, higher even than Schalke’s, which on two clubs at once is more likely to be a three-match artefact than a shared method. Neither club has a 2025–26 xG line to test any of this against.
