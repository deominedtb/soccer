A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted this season, and the Bundesliga has not started — the league opened on 28 August 2026 with Bayern against Stuttgart, so this is matchday one and there is no 2026–27 league evidence for either side. Dortmund finished second in 2025–26 on 73 points from 34 matches, with 22 wins, 7 draws and just five defeats, 70 scored and 34 conceded for +36. Hamburg finished 13th on 38 points with 9 wins, 11 draws and 14 defeats, 40 scored and 54 conceded, −14 — survival in their first season back after seven years in the 2. Bundesliga. The structural headline sits at the back of the home side. Dortmund conceded 34 goals last season, the second-best record in the division, and the players who did it are unavailable: Niklas Süle has retired from professional football at 30 after health problems, and both Nico Schlotterbeck and Emre Can are injured. The projected back three is Waldemar Anton, Ramy Bensebaini and Joane Gadou — a summer signing from RB Salzburg who has never played a Bundesliga match.

  • Borussia Dortmund — turnover low, manager continuous, and the defence hollowed out by circumstance rather than by sales. Niko Kovač continues as head coach, so unlike most German clubs on this card the method is settled and this is not a first season. The window was modest and weighted young: Joey Veerman arrived from PSV to strengthen midfield, the most senior of the additions; Justin Lerma, an 18-year-old Colombian, came from Independiente del Valle; Kaua Prates from Cruzeiro; and Joane Gadou from RB Salzburg. No fees were published for any of these in the sources consulted. The significant departure was not a transfer at all: Niklas Süle announced his retirement from professional football at 30 after health issues and has joined the amateur side SV Tiefenbach. Felix Nmecha is reported to have starred for Germany at the 2026 World Cup and is expected to continue his midfield partnership with Jobe Bellingham. A club that finished second with almost the same squad, and lost a centre-back to retirement rather than to a rival, is the most continuous side on the German portion of this card.
  • Hamburger SV — turnover medium, funded modestly, and regrouping after losing established players. Merlin Polzin remains head coach — the man who took the club up in 2025 and kept them up in 2025–26 — though the reporting notes his contract extension was still under negotiation. The window was low-budget and broad: Patson Daka, a 27-year-old striker, free from Leicester City on 22 July 2026; Albert Grønbæk, 25, from Stade Rennes for €4.7m on 1 July; Martin Adeline, 22, from Troyes for €4m — a club that also appears on this card; Kofi Amoako from Dynamo Dresden for €1.8m; and Bilal Nadir, 22, free from Marseille on 24 July. Out: Łukasz Poręba to SV Elversberg for €1.6m, another club on this card; Giorgi Gocholeishvili, a defender, to Shakhtar Donetsk; Fábio Baldé to Strasbourg; and Damion Downs back to Southampton at the end of a loan. The reporting frames the summer as HSV having to regroup after losing important players, which is a fair description of a defence that has lost Gocholeishvili and a squad rebuilt on frees and sub-€5m fees.
  • Suspensions / absences. The most consequential list on the German portion of this card, and it is the favourite’s. Dortmund are without Nico Schlotterbeck and Emre Can, both injured, on top of Süle’s retirement — which is why Gadou, a summer arrival with no Bundesliga experience, is expected to start in a back three alongside Anton and Bensebaini. Hamburg’s concerns are also defensive: Warmed Omari is receiving conservative treatment for a shoulder injury and Miro Muheim has muscular problems. Grønbæk, who was still troubled by a knee problem in early August, is reported fully fit after scoring twice in the cup at Verl.
  • Sample. Zero league matches each. The only 2026–27 competitive football is the DFB-Pokal first round. Hamburg won 3–0 at third-division SC Verl on the Monday evening, having struggled in the first half, with Albert Grønbæk scoring on 41 and 46 minutes and Louis Lemke on 50. Dortmund were drawn away at HEBC Hamburg — the result of that tie could not be retrieved in the sources consulted. So the only observable evidence about either side in 2026–27 is a Hamburg win over a third-tier club in which they were second best for forty-five minutes.
  • Referee. Felix Zwayer, confirmed on the published matchday-one appointment list, with Robert Kempter and Nikolai Kimmeyer assisting. He is comfortably the highest-carding official working any German fixture on this card: in the 2025–26 Bundesliga he averaged 4.8 cards per match across 16 games, with 4.94 yellows and 0.13 reds per game — two reds in those 16 — against Badstübner’s 2.4, Hartmann’s 3.70, Jablonski’s 3.81 and Stieler’s 3.83. On penalties he sits mid-range: 5 in 16 Bundesliga matches, and 13 across all competitions in 2025–26 at 0.35 per match. No fouls-per-card ratio is published for him in the sources consulted, so the volume is very well established and the mechanism behind it is not.

B · Style & early-season reality

Dortmund’s 2025–26 was built on both ends: 70 goals scored and 34 conceded, five defeats in 34, a second-place finish with a +36 difference. Kovač’s continuation means the shape and the demands are known, and the summer barely touched the first eleven — Veerman is the one addition likely to affect it. What has changed is entirely involuntary and entirely defensive. The 34-goal concession rate was produced with Süle and Schlotterbeck available; Süle has retired outright and Schlotterbeck is injured alongside Emre Can, who can also cover in that area. A back three of Anton, Bensebaini and a Salzburg signing making his Bundesliga debut is a materially different unit from the one that finished second, and it is being asked to hold up on opening day in front of 81,365 people. Nmecha and Bellingham in midfield is a strong screen, and Nmecha arrives off a prominent World Cup, which cuts both ways — form and fatigue in the same player.

Hamburg are the more legible and the more limited side. Forty goals scored across 34 matches is 1.18 a game, and 54 conceded is 1.59, which is the profile of a promoted club that survived by being difficult rather than by being good. Polzin, who took them up and kept them up, stays, so the method is continuous even as the personnel churn — but the churn is real: a defender sold to Shakhtar, a midfielder to Elversberg, another player to Strasbourg, replaced by two free transfers and three signings none of which cost €5m. Grønbæk’s double at Verl is the one piece of attacking evidence and it came against a third-division side after a poor opening half. What Hamburg have going for them here is not quality but timing: they meet a Dortmund defence missing three of the players who built the second-best record in the division, on a day when nobody has a league match behind them.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Dortmund’s 34 goals conceded is the number the whole price depends on, and none of the three players most associated with it will be on the pitch. Süle retired from professional football at 30 after health problems, Schlotterbeck and Emre Can are injured, and Joane Gadou — signed from RB Salzburg this summer — is expected to start a Bundesliga match for the first time.
  • There is no 2026–27 league evidence for either side, and the cup evidence is one-sided and thin. Hamburg beat third-division SC Verl 3–0 after a poor first half; Dortmund’s first-round tie away at HEBC Hamburg produced no retrievable result in the sources consulted. Every figure in section A is a twelve-month-old record.
  • Hamburg’s 38 points and 13th place were achieved in a first season back after seven years in the second division, and the squad has since been reworked on frees and sub-€5m fees while losing a defender to Shakhtar, a midfielder to Elversberg and a further player to Strasbourg. Polzin’s contract extension was reported as still under negotiation, which is continuity with an asterisk.
  • This was a World Cup summer and it shows up here specifically: Felix Nmecha is reported to have starred for Germany at the 2026 tournament and is expected to start. A prominent tournament run compresses a player’s pre-season, and the same effect applies unevenly across both squads in ways neither club’s league record captures.
  • Most sensitive: corners and the outright — both require Dortmund’s normal territorial and defensive pattern, and the defensive half of that pattern is being improvised. Least sensitive: discipline and totals — Zwayer’s 4.8 cards a match is a property of the official, and 3.1 and 2.8 goals a fixture are large-sample properties of two clubs whose windows did nothing to suppress them.
