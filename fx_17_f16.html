B · Style & early-season reality

Charlton are a side whose league position is running well ahead of its goal difference, and the gap between the two is the story. Fifth on 11 points, but 6 scored and 7 conceded, with three of the six matches won by a single goal (2–1, 2–1, 1–0) and the last three producing two points, a 0–4 and a 0–0. That is a team winning tight matches rather than controlling them, and the summer explains part of it: every permanent arrival came free or undisclosed, three more were loans, and Lyndon Dykes was allowed to leave for Millwall — a forward who has since scored twice in a single match for his new club. Tyreece Campbell has 3 of Charlton's 6 league goals. With Godden unavailable and two of the four permanent signings out, the attack that has produced a goal a game is not being reinforced today. Their record splits cleanly into two comfortable wins and three losses, with no draws at all — a side that has either functioned or not, five times out of five.
