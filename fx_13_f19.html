A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Both have been in Ligue 1 for some time, and Brest's tenure is a club record: this is their eighth consecutive season in the division since coming up at the end of 2018–19. The structural headline on the visiting side is not a transfer. Éric Roy, Brest's head coach, died at 58 of pancreatic cancer, and on 27 June 2026 the club appointed Julien Lachuer, one of Roy's assistants since 2023, to succeed him. That is a managerial change in the technical sense — and it is a continuity appointment from inside the staff rather than an outside hire, which is the relevant fact for reading the team's shape. It came alongside a wider reorganisation: the sporting director Grégory Lorenzi left for Marseille, and the club adopted a new sporting project centred on its academy. Le Havre are the opposite: Digard remains, and is described as the only coach to have stayed in post across several seasons at this level in French football, with the club citing his attachment to the project. Two matchdays in, this is the tightest pair of records on the board — Le Havre have one point from fixtures against Monaco and Lyon, Brest have two from two draws, and neither has won.

  • Le Havre — continuity in the dugout, modest turnover, and a departure still to be absorbed. Digard continues and the technical staff around him has been strengthened rather than replaced: Razik Nedder, a former academy coach who has worked with the Algerian national setup, has taken responsibility for the midfielders, and the Portuguese coach Stefano Cunha de Sousa for the forwards — a coaching-staff expansion aimed at two specific units, which is a more precise structural signal than most summer news on this slate. In: Junior Mwanga for a reported €1.5m, the club's largest fee of the window, and Timothée Pembélé. Out: Mokrane Bentoumi and the goalkeeper Mory Diaw, with the departure of Sofiane Boufal reported as announced and further movements expected. B · Style & early-season reality

Le Havre are the more stable of the two and the better documented. Digard's longevity is unusual in a division that changes coaches quickly, and the summer's most concrete move was not a signing but the appointment of two specialist coaches to work with the midfield and the forward line — a targeted intervention into the two areas a mid-table Ligue 1 side would most want to improve. The playing squad has changed at the margins: a €1.5m midfielder in Mwanga, a defender in Pembélé, a goalkeeper out in Diaw, and Boufal's exit announced. One point from matches against Monaco and Lyon is a defensible start in context, and the context is the important part — both fixtures were against sides that finished well above them. Gap: no xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Le Havre in either season, so this card cannot say whether the point came from control or from resistance.

Brest are a club in transition for reasons that have nothing to do with football economics. Roy's death and Lachuer's promotion from within mean the technical continuity is probably greater than the phrase “new head coach” implies — an assistant of three years' standing taking over a squad he already worked with daily is a different proposition from an outside appointment, and this card treats it as such. What has changed structurally around him is real: a departed sporting director, a redrawn organisational chart, and a stated shift towards developing academy players, which is the kind of reorientation that shows up in squad composition over seasons rather than weeks. Two draws from two matches is a mildly encouraging start for a side in that position. The limitation on this card is unusually blunt: no Brest transfer business could be established at all, so nothing here describes who has arrived or left, and no positional analysis of the squad is possible. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate, card rate or goal columns for Brest, in either season.
