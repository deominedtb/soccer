A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Atlético finished 4th in 2025–26 on 69 points — 21 wins, 6 draws, 11 defeats, 62 scored, 44 conceded — a season split violently by venue: 15-1-3 at home, 6-5-8 away. Real Madrid finished 2nd on 86 points — 27 wins, 5 draws, 6 defeats, 77 scored, 35 conceded, eight behind Barcelona. The structural headline is that one club changed everything at the top and the other changed almost nothing. Real Madrid dismissed Álvaro Arbeloa on 9 June 2026, announced José Mourinho on 11 June on a contract to 2029, and then rebuilt the back line: Ibrahima Konaté arrived after being released by Liverpool, alongside Marc Cucurella and Denzel Dumfries, with Bernardo Silva joining free from Manchester City. Diego Simeóne is in his fifteenth season at Atlético, which makes this one of only three fixtures on the slate where a club’s entire 2025–26 record belongs to the man picking the team — and Atlético spent anyway, replacing Antoine Griezmann and a full-back line. Three of Real Madrid’s four significant arrivals came free or from clubs playing elsewhere on this same slate — Konaté released by Liverpool (f4), Bernardo Silva free from Manchester City (f6) — and Atlético’s left-back came from Leverkusen (f9).

B · Style & early-season reality

Real Madrid under Mourinho have produced the single largest attacking jump on this board, and it has arrived alongside a press that has gone in the opposite direction. xG has risen from 2.14 to 3.21 per match — a full goal of expected output — with non-penalty xG at 2.96, shots from 17.50 to 20.67, shots on target from 6.66 to 8.33, deep completions from 10.03 to 16.17 and corners won from 6.34 to 7.83. At the same time PPDA has risen from 10.77 to 13.38 and possession has fallen from 59.4% to 54.8%. That combination — less of the ball, less pressing, dramatically more territory and chance volume — is the clearest system signature on the slate: this is a side attacking in fewer, longer sequences and getting deeper when it does. The defensive half has improved on the model (1.18 to 0.90 xG conceded) without improving on the scoreboard (0.92 to 1.00 goals), and the clean-sheet rate has collapsed from 36.8% to 16.7%. Their fouls are the second-lowest on this board at 8.83 per match against a divisional 12.41.

Atlético have changed much less and what has changed is concentrated in one place. xG 1.88 against 1.79, xG conceded 1.34 against 1.40, PPDA 10.67 against 11.00, deep completions 10.50 against 9.24 — four series essentially static across the summer, which is what fifteen seasons of the same coach looks like. Possession is up modestly, 53.3% to 56.6%, and shots from 13.11 to 16.83. The one figure that is not static is the first half, and it is extreme: Atlético have conceded no first-half goal at all in six league matches, against 0.58 per match last season, while scoring 0.67 before the break against 0.76. Their goals have arrived late and their concessions later still. The corner column has moved the other way from Real Madrid’s — 6.50 won last season to 5.33 now, and 4.29 conceded to 3.33 — fewer at both ends. The headline number, 2.33 goals per match against 1.63 last season, is running well ahead of an xG figure that has barely moved, which is the gap to hold in mind: 14 goals from 11.3 expected.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Real Madrid’s 2025–26 record belongs to Arbeloa and to a defence that has been replaced. Mourinho was announced on 11 June; Konaté, Cucurella and Dumfries all arrived, two of them free; Militão is injured. The 35 goals conceded last season were conceded by a different back four, and this season’s clean-sheet rate is 16.7% against 36.8%.
  • Atlético’s goals are running ahead of their expected goals by a clear margin. 14 scored against 11.3 expected across six matches, on an xG line (1.88) barely moved from last season’s 1.79. The 2.33 goals per match is the number most likely to be a short-run artefact on this card.
  • Atlético’s attacking team news is unresolved at the time of writing. Julián Álvarez is a major doubt, Sørloth and Barrios face late fitness tests, and Griezmann left free in the summer. Every attacking reading on this card — team goals, BTTS, half the totals — depends on a team sheet that is not published until an hour before kickoff.
  • The first-half figures are the most extreme on the slate and rest on six matches each. Atlético’s zero first-half goals conceded is a remarkable counted fact, but zero is the hardest figure to read: it has no variance to measure, and one goal changes the rate to 0.14.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and any Atlético team-goals line — the first two because two clubs two points apart have expected-goal figures 1.33 apart, the third because three of Atlético’s forwards are doubtful. Least sensitive: Totals and first-half goals — both rest on six-match samples on each side, agreeing counted and modelled series, and a divisional baseline moving the same way.
