A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Frosinone are promoted and are back in Serie A after two seasons away — they were relegated from the top flight in 2023–24 and went up as runners-up in Serie B on 81 points from 38 matches, one behind Venezia, who are also in this division now, with Monza third. Across that campaign they were 23-12-3 with 76 scored and 34 conceded — only three defeats in 38. Como finished 4th in Serie A on 71 points — 20 wins, 11 draws, 7 defeats, 65 scored and 29 conceded, the meanest defence in the division — and are in the Champions League for the first time in their 119-year history. The structural headline is that both clubs kept the coach and then went in opposite financial directions. Massimiliano Alvini stays at Frosinone, the coach who won the promotion, and recruited breadth rather than money — fourteen or more arrivals, none of them at a headline fee. Cesc Fàbregas stays at Como, and the club spent at a scale no promoted side could match: €60m on Nico Paz permanently from Real Madrid, €30m plus bonuses on Trevoh Chalobah, and a €10m loan for Moise Kean carrying a guaranteed €25m obligation and €5m in bonuses. Both clubs’ last-season records therefore belong to the men picking the team — the rarest thing on this slate — but one of those records was made in a different division and the other by a squad that has since been rebuilt upwards.

  • Frosinone — turnover very high in bodies, low in money; no managerial change, Alvini continuing. Goalkeeper: in — Sebastiano Desplanches from Palermo. Centre-back, where the recruitment was heaviest: in — Kevin Akpoguma free, with Bundesliga experience, Giorgio Cittadini from Atalanta, Nicolò Calvani from Genoa and Fayed from Fenerbahçe. Defensive midfield: in — Florian Grillitsch from Braga and Romano Schmid from Werder Bremen, the two most experienced arrivals. Centre-forward and attack: in — Antonio Raimondo from Bologna, Alessio Zerbin from Napoli, €2m, and Fini from Genoa. Midfield: in — Luis Hasa from Napoli, €2.5m, El Azzouzi from Düsseldorf, Masini from Genoa. Out: Marchizza to Vicenza, Ndow to Spezia, Dixon to Toronto, Masciangelo, Vergani, and loan returns for Fiori (Mantova), Zilli (Cosenza) and Gori (Grosseto). Two of the arrivals, Hasa and Zerbin, came from Napoli, who also play on this slate. The window kept the promotion core and added a goalkeeper, four centre-backs and two defensive midfielders — precisely the positions the spec weights, and all of them at small or undisclosed fees.
  • Como — turnover high and expensive, aimed at the forward line and the centre of defence; no managerial change, Fàbregas continuing. Centre-forward: in — Moise Kean, a €10m loan with a guaranteed obligation to buy at €25m plus €5m in bonuses, on a contract to June 2031, joining Douvikas. Centre-back: in — Trevoh Chalobah permanently from Chelsea, €30m plus bonuses. Attacking midfield: Nico Paz made permanent from Real Madrid for €60m — the largest single fee on this card — and Mattia Liberali for €6m. Loans with options: Yan Couto, Kaiki, Samuele Ricci and Willy Kambwala, all on paid loans with purchase rights. Roughly €100m of permanent fees plus a €30m committed obligation, and a reported ten players moved out as surplus. A side that conceded 29 goals last season has bought a €30m centre-back and a €35m striker on top of it, for a Champions League season it has never played before.

B · Style & early-season reality

Frosinone have arrived in Serie A and chosen the most physically demanding way to survive in it. Their PPDA is 7.39 — the second-most aggressive press on this slate, behind Liverpool at 7.06 — on 43.1% possession, meaning they harry constantly without the ball and rarely keep it. The cost is visible in two columns and both are extreme. They commit 16.50 fouls per match against a divisional 11.21, the highest figure of any team on this board, and they concede an xG of 2.18 per match, also the highest, against a divisional 1.71. What makes the record look better than the underlying numbers is the finishing at both ends: 7 scored and 4 conceded from 1.46 xG for and 2.18 against. In Serie B they were a possession-neutral side (49.6%) who out-cornered opponents 6.16 to 4.13; in Serie A that has inverted, 5.50 won against 7.50 conceded — the worst corner concession rate on the board. Their fouls (15.79 in Serie B against a second-tier line of 14.98) were already above average in a rougher division and have risen again here. There is no 2025–26 xG to set against the current 1.46/2.18, so the only modelled evidence about this club is four matches old.

Como are doing something unusual for a side that finished fourth: they have become more dominant and less aggressive at the same time. Shots have risen from 14.45 to 22.25 per match and deep completions from 6.37 to 11.25 — both nearly doubled — while xG has gone from 1.79 to 2.32, among the six highest attacking figures on this board. Yet PPDA has risen from 7.71 to 11.20: they press considerably less, on essentially unchanged possession (61.6% to 60.8%). A side pressing less while creating more is holding the ball in the opponent’s half rather than winning it back high, and the corner column confirms it — 8.00 won against 3.00 conceded, an eight-corner-per-match attacking rate against a divisional 4.78. The discipline consequence is the cleanest set of numbers on the slate: 7.75 fouls and 0.50 cards per match, against 13.42 and 2.16 last season — a side that does not need to foul because it does not need to chase. The defensive record has loosened in exchange: xG conceded 1.04 to 1.41, goals 0.76 to 1.00, and both teams have scored in all four league matches after 42.1% last season. The 4–1 against Leipzig in midweek is the same profile at a higher level.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Frosinone’s entire 2025–26 record is a Serie B record and its modelled half does not exist. 2.00 goals per match, 60.5% over 2.5 and three defeats in thirty-eight were produced in a division scoring 2.54 per game and booking at 4.52. Understat does not cover Serie B, so there is no xG, PPDA or deep-completion baseline for this club at all — four Serie A matches are the only modelled evidence that exists.
  • Como’s record looks continuous and its squad is not. Fourth place and 29 conceded were achieved before Kean (€35m committed), Chalobah (€30m) and a permanent Nico Paz (€60m) arrived. The clean-sheet rate has already gone from 50.0% to 0.0% and xG conceded from 1.04 to 1.41 — the part of the side that produced last season’s headline number is the part that has changed most in behaviour.
  • Como have played three of four league matches away and won two of them; this is only their second home fixture’s worth of evidence in reverse. The 22.25 shots and 8.00 corners were accumulated mostly on the road, which normally suppresses both. Whether the figure is a venue artefact cannot be separated at four matches.
  • Frosinone’s most experienced signing is unavailable in the position his signing was meant to solve. Grillitsch was brought from Braga as a defensive midfielder for a promoted side pressing at 7.39 PPDA; he and Terzić are out. The 16.50 fouls per match is what that midfield does at full strength.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and anything resting on Frosinone’s attacking level — a promoted side on 7 points from 1.46 xG, with no prior modelled record, is the definition of an unsettled input. Least sensitive: Corners and first-half goals — both sit on gaps far larger than their own variance, with the shot, possession and deep-completion columns agreeing on the mechanism.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners is more robust than 1X2 here because the asymmetry is four corners per match wide and has three other series behind it. Como’s 8.00 won against Frosinone’s 7.50 conceded sits on 22.25 shots, 11.25 deep completions and 60.8% possession against 43.1%. The result, by contrast, rests on a Serie B season with no xG and a Serie A season played by a squad that has since spent €125m.
  • Totals is more robust than Team goals on the Frosinone side because the combined figure survives the missing half of the evidence. Frosinone’s 2.18 xG conceded and Como’s 2.32 xG for are both counted from this season; the total does not need Frosinone’s attacking history, which does not exist. A Frosinone team-goals line does need it.
  • BTTS is more robust than the clean-sheet lines because it is carried by the side whose change is explained. Como have gone from 50.0% clean sheets to 0.0% with PPDA 7.71→11.20 and xG conceded 1.04→1.41 to say why; a clean-sheet line asks which of two sides stops conceding, and neither has stopped in four matches.
  • The foul count is more robust than the card count, and the referee is the reason. Frosinone’s 16.50 and Como’s 7.75 are the two most extreme foul figures on this board and both have pressing data behind them. Chiffi converts at 8.7 fouls per card against a divisional 6.72 and books 2.60 per match against 3.22 this season — the same fouls produce fewer cards in front of him than in front of anyone else appointed today.
  • The single fact that would most change which family reads best is whether Como’s 22.25 shots per match is a level or a road artefact. It is nearly double last season’s 14.45, it was accumulated in three away matches and one at home, and it is the number underneath the corner reading, the totals reading and the team-goals reading simultaneously. What would settle it is two or three more home matches of shot and deep-completion data; at four matches, with three of them away, the venue and the change cannot be separated.
