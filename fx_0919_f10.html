A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Ipswich Town are promoted, as Championship runners-up on 84 points — 23 wins, 15 draws, 8 defeats, 80 scored, 47 conceded, the second-best defensive record in that division. The Championship treats its promoted sides harshly and this board has no view on whether it will again; what it can say is narrower and firmer. Everton finished 13th in the Premier League on 49 points — 13 wins, 10 draws, 15 defeats, 47 scored, 50 conceded. The structural headline is the shape of the two summers, which is the reverse of what the table would suggest. The promoted club made twelve signings for roughly £150m under a manager appointed on 23 June; the established club sold £108m of players and bought £86.3m, finishing the window a net seller. One of these squads is almost entirely new and the other lost its two leading attackers. Neither one’s 2025–26 record describes it, and for Ipswich the record is not even from this division.

  • Everton — turnover high and concentrated in attack; manager unchanged. David Moyes remains head coach, in his second spell, reappointed in January 2025; no change this window. Out, the headline: Iliman Ndiaye to Manchester City, £60m, Dwight McNeil to Crystal Palace, £22m and Beto to Fiorentina, £15m — the three players who carried the attack. Also out: Iroegbunam to Hull City, fee undisclosed but reported around £15m, which means Everton sold a player to one of the two promoted clubs playing on this card at the same kickoff time. Ten departures in all. In — seven signings, £86.3m of disclosed fees: Brennan Johnson from Crystal Palace, £22m; Merlin Röhl from Freiburg, £21m; Tyrique George from Chelsea, £18m; and Christian Nørgaard from Arsenal, permanent, on 5 August 2026 — a deal confirmed from the other side on this board’s Arsenal card. Net sellers by £22m on disclosed fees. No goalkeeper and no centre-back moved in either direction.

B · Style & early-season reality

Everton are a deep, low-foul block that concedes the ball and does not chase it. Possession 41.67%, against 43.67% last season; PPDA 15.61, against 13.46 — they press less than almost anyone, and their opponents press them at 8.15, the most aggressive figure faced by any side on this board. The consequence shows in the foul column rather than in the possession one: 8.50 fouls per match, the lowest of any club carded on this slate, down from 10.82 last season, because a side that sits off does not have to foul. What has improved is the front end — 14.75 shots per match against 11.11 last season, 5.25 on target against 3.74, and 7.25 deep completions against 5.55 — which is the part that should not be assumed, because the three players who produced last season’s 47 goals (Ndiaye, McNeil, Beto) were all sold and the £22m replacement, Brennan Johnson, has four matches. The record is one win and three draws, with 1.44 xG for and 1.52 against: a side whose chances and results both say “close”.

Ipswich arrive playing a different game from the one that got them promoted, and the numbers say so plainly. In the Championship they had 56.09% of the ball, took 15.07 shots against 10.54 faced, and won 5.70 corners against 3.93 — a side that dominated its division. In the Premier League they have 43.92%, take 11.25 shots against 16.25 faced, and win 3.50 corners against 5.50. Every one of those pairs has inverted. Their PPDA of 11.75 is more aggressive than Everton’s but it is being applied without the ball, which is the ordinary promoted-side experience and is exactly what the Championship record cannot tell you about. The clean-sheet column is the sharpest single illustration: 37.0% in the Championship, the second-best defensive record in that division at 1.02 conceded per match; 0.0% in the Premier League at 2.50 conceded. The evidence that O’Neil’s side is a different team from McKenna’s is a start date, twelve signings and four matches — not a season, and the one figure that would normally arbitrate, last season’s xG, does not exist for them at all.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Ipswich’s entire 2025–26 record is from a different division, and one column of it does not exist at all. The 84 points, 80 goals, 37.0% clean-sheet rate and 5.70 corners were produced in the Championship against a 2.59-goal, 3.85-card divisional baseline. No 2025–26 xG exists for them in any public source, so the one measure that normally survives a change of level is missing. Every Championship figure in the strip is labelled as such for that reason.
  • Everton’s goals-for rate is identical across the two seasons and the coincidence is misleading. 1.24 per match last season, 1.25 this — but Ndiaye (£60m), McNeil (£22m) and Beto (£15m) all left, and Johnson, George and Röhl arrived, with Röhl and Nørgaard unavailable today. The shot and deep-completion columns have risen sharply while the goal count has not; four matches cannot say which of those is the truer signal.
  • Twelve signings is more turnover than four matches can describe. Ipswich’s predicted eleven contains seven summer arrivals including the goalkeeper and a centre-back, under a coach appointed on 23 June. No injury list for them is published anywhere reached, so the composition of the side is both more uncertain than usual and less reported than usual.
  • The discipline row rests on a referee pattern that is deep but specific. Attwell’s 25-match record and his four Everton matches are real and counted, but four matches is four matches, and the clubs’ own foul rates — 8.50 and 11.50 — point the other way. The row is rated highly because the evidence on both sides of the collision is unusually good, not because the outcome is settled.
  • Most sensitive: the Asian handicap and total goals — the first because Everton have drawn three of four and have a goal difference of +2, the second because the two clubs’ over-2.5 rates are 25.0% and 75.0% with the divisional line sitting exactly between them. Least sensitive: the discipline row and Ipswich’s side of BTTS — the first because it rests on 25 refereed matches plus four specific ones against this home club, the second because no clean sheet in four is corroborated by xG against, shots faced and deep completions conceded all at once.

E · Short market-type guide

  • The discipline family is more robust than the match result here because Attwell has 25 matches at 4.72 cards and 4.59 fouls per card plus four Everton matches averaging 6.25 cards, while the result depends on a promoted side twelve signings deep and a home side that sold £97m of its attack.
  • Ipswich’s defensive side of BTTS is more robust than a total on this match because no clean sheet in four is backed by 1.93 xG against, 16.25 shots faced and 8.00 deep completions conceded, whereas the two clubs’ over-2.5 rates (25.0% and 75.0%) straddle the divisional 53.7% and cancel out.
  • A first-half total is more robust than a first-half result because both clubs have timing rates for two seasons and they differ sharply — Everton 0.25 for and against, Ipswich 1.00 and 1.25 — while the result question inherits Everton’s three draws in four.
  • Everton’s corner rate is more robust than their goal rate because 5.25 won and 4.50 conceded moved in step with their shot volume rising from 11.11 to 14.75, whereas their goals-for figure is unchanged at 1.24–1.25 despite the entire forward line being sold.
  • The single fact that would most change which family I choose is a published Ipswich expected-goals figure for 2025–26. Without it there is no way to tell whether their Championship defensive record — 47 conceded in 46, a 37.0% clean-sheet rate — reflected a strong defence or a strong division position, and therefore no way to read the 2.50 they are conceding now as regression or as level. What would settle it is Understat or an equivalent extending coverage to the Championship, or a club-published season xG; neither exists in any source reached today.
