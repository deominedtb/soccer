Neither club is promoted and neither is new to this division. Toulouse finished 10th in 2025–26 on 44 points — 12 wins, 8 draws, 13 defeats, 47 scored, 46 conceded. Le Havre finished 14th on 35 points — 7 wins, 14 draws, 13 defeats, 32 scored, 44 conceded, with a 41.2% failed-to-score rate and only 32.4% of matches going over 2.5 against a divisional 52.6%. Both sit in the bottom four on two points, and neither has won. The structural headline is on the home side and it is a contradiction: Toulouse changed coach in May and are now creating 1.94 expected goals per match against 1.37 last season — the largest attacking improvement of any Ligue 1 club on this board — while sitting 17th with one goal per game and no clean sheet. Le Havre have changed almost nothing and are scoring even less than the 0.94 per match that nearly relegated them.

B · Style & early-season reality

Toulouse under Askou are a different team in every column except the one that decides matches. Expected goals have risen from 1.37 to 1.94 per match and deep completions from 6.24 to 9.75 — more than half as many again — while PPDA has fallen from 12.81 to 10.43, so they press harder and get into the box far more often. The most dramatic shift is possession: 42.22% last season, 52.35% now, a ten-point jump, from a side that has just sold its most valuable player for €28m and its centre-forward to Ipswich. What has not followed is the scoreline: 1.00 goal per match against 1.42 last season, on 4.50 shots on target against 4.30. The defensive column has gone backwards in results while holding in chances — 1.75 conceded per match against 1.39, but expected goals against of 1.53 against 1.31, and no clean sheet in four. The foul rate is the one unambiguous cost of the new approach: 16.75 per match, the highest of any club on this board, against 14.36 last season and a divisional 11.74, while their card rate has halved from 2.48 to 1.25 — two figures moving hard in opposite directions.

Le Havre are the same side as last season, only quieter at both ends. Expected goals 1.10 against 1.14, non-penalty xG 0.91 against 1.00, possession 48.73% against 46.84% — essentially unmoved — and the goals have fallen from 0.94 to 0.50 per match. The one genuine change is in the press: PPDA 9.61 against 10.75, so they engage earlier, and opponents press them at 8.93 against 9.26. The cost shows in the defensive chance column: expected goals against up from 1.64 to 1.86, the higher of the two sides here, with deep completions conceded down from 7.65 to 6.50 — fewer entries, better ones. Their shot volume has risen sharply, from 10.94 to 15.00 per match, without the expected goals following it, which says the extra shots are coming from further out. The single most telling number on their side is the first-half column: no first-half goal at all in four matches, against 0.41 per match last season, on a side that failed to score in 41.2% of a full season.

They create 1.94 expected goals and score 1.00; they concede 1.53 expected and ship 1.75. A new coach appointed on 21 May, a ten-point rise in possession and a 56% rise in deep completions all say the process has changed; 17th place and no win say it has not yet mattered.
