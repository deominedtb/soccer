A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Coventry City are promoted as Championship champions, and by a distance: 95 points from 46 matches — 28 wins, 11 draws, 7 defeats, 97 scored, 45 conceded. That is 2.11 goals per match in a division whose two sides between them average 2.59, a 37.0% clean-sheet rate against a 25.0% baseline, and it is their first season in the top flight in twenty-five years. Nottingham Forest finished 16th in the Premier League on 44 points — 11 wins, 11 draws, 16 defeats, 48 scored, 51 conceded. The structural headline is what has happened since. Coventry have scored no goals in four Premier League matches and conceded ten; Forest have changed head coach and their expected goals have swung by 1.12 per match across the two ends — the largest two-way improvement of any club carded on this slate — for five points. Two clubs whose current results and current chance data disagree, in opposite directions.

B · Style & early-season reality

Forest under Glasner are a different team in the chance data and almost the same team in the table, which is the single most interesting tension on this card. Expected goals have risen from 1.29 to 1.79 per match and expected goals against have fallen from 1.70 to 1.08 — a two-way swing of 1.12 goals per match, the largest of any club carded on this slate. The mechanism is visible: PPDA down from 13.48 to 10.99, deep completions up from 5.53 to 6.75 and conceded down from 7.74 to 4.25, shots up from 12.53 to 14.50 and shots faced down from 13.32 to 10.75. They press harder, concede less territory and create more. The cost is in the discipline column and it is steep: fouls per match up from 10.71 to 15.75, the highest rate of any club on this board, and cards from 1.61 to 2.75 — a high press bought with fouls. Two things have not followed: possession is down to 43.42% from 46.75%, and the goals have not arrived at home, where they have scored none in two matches. Their corner rate has halved, from 5.24 won to 2.75, which sits oddly beside the rise in shots and deep completions and is the one column that does not fit.

Coventry are the clearest illustration on this board of what a change of division does to a dominant second-tier side. In the Championship they had 55.24% of the ball, took 16.33 shots against 11.63 faced, hit 5.57 on target and scored 2.11 per match. In the Premier League they have 39.30%, take 10.25 against 15.75 faced, hit 3.25 on target and have scored none. Every one of those pairs has moved against them and the pressing figure explains why they cannot get it back: PPDA 19.85, the most passive figure on this board by a wide margin, against opponents pressing them at 9.99. They are being pressed twice as hard as they press. The defensive collapse is the sharper half: 0.98 conceded per match and a 37.0% clean-sheet rate in the Championship, against 2.50 conceded and none in four here, on 1.82 expected goals against — and that is with £22m spent on a goalkeeper and Pinnock signed permanently. The one column that has barely moved is corners — 5.33 won and 4.24 conceded then, 4.50 and 4.25 now — which makes it the only Championship figure on this card that has survived the step up.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Coventry’s whole 2025–26 record is from a different division and its xG column does not exist. Ninety-five points, 97 goals, a 37.0% clean-sheet rate and 63.0% over 2.5 came against a Championship baseline of 2.59 goals and 3.85 cards per match. No public source publishes Championship expected goals, so nothing here converts their 97 goals into a level, and the 0.00 they are now scoring is measured against 1.07 xG rather than against anything from last season.
  • Forest’s improvement is measured over four matches and two of its architects are unavailable. Expected goals against down from 1.70 to 1.08 and deep completions conceded from 7.74 to 4.25 is a real defensive shift, but Milenković and Savona are out and Cunha is doubtful, so the back line that produced it is not the back line that will play. Glasner arrived in July with three signings and no published fees.
  • Forest’s results and chances disagree by more than any club on this board. A 1.12-goal two-way swing in expected goals has produced one win in four and no goals at home. Either the chances will convert or the swing is a four-match artefact of playing Leeds, Liverpool, Tottenham and Aston Villa — three of whom are 17th, 19th or newly promoted this morning. Two of Forest’s four opponents so far also appear on this card, both in the bottom three.
  • Coventry’s four opponents are the reason to be careful about their zero. Arsenal (top, four wins), Manchester City, Brighton (13 goals in four) and Hull. Three of those four are on this board and two are among its best-performing sides. A 100% failed-to-score rate against that set is not the same fact as a 100% rate against a normal four.
  • Most sensitive: the Asian handicap and team goals — the first because Forest have scored nothing at home and everything away across two matches each, the second because Forest are creating 1.79 xG and scoring 1.00 while Coventry are creating 1.07 and scoring nothing. Least sensitive: BTTS and the first-half total — the first because Coventry’s blank record is corroborated by possession, shots, shots on target, xG and PPDA at once, the second because the two clubs have produced one first-half goal between them in eight team-appearances against a divisional 1.27 per match.

E · Short market-type guide

  • BTTS is more robust than total goals here because Coventry’s 100% failed-to-score rate is explained by five independent columns — 10.25 shots, 3.25 on target, 39.3% possession, 1.07 xG, PPDA 19.85 — whereas both clubs sit at exactly 50.0% over 2.5 and their matches reach that number by completely different routes.
  • Forest’s expected goals are more robust than their goal count because 1.79 xG per match is supported by shots rising to 14.50 and deep completions to 6.75, while the 1.00 they have scored includes two home matches in which they scored nothing at all.
  • A first-half total is more robust than a first-half result because one first-half goal in eight team-appearances, against a divisional 1.27 per match, is a clear reading in one direction, whereas no margin inside 45 minutes exists on either side.
  • Coventry’s corner rate is more robust than any other figure they have because it is the single Championship number that survived promotion — 5.33 won and 4.24 conceded then, 4.50 and 4.25 now — while their possession fell sixteen points and their shots fell by six a game.
  • The single fact that would most change which family I choose is whether Forest’s defensive improvement belongs to Glasner or to Milenković and Savona. Expected goals against have fallen from 1.70 to 1.08 and deep completions conceded from 7.74 to 4.25, but both first-choice centre-backs are out today and a third is doubtful, so the scheme and the personnel are confounded in exactly the four matches that produced the figure. What would settle it is a published per-match defensive split for Forest with and without those two — no source reached publishes one, and until it exists the 1X2 and BTTS rows both rest on a defence that has not been tested without them.
