A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted. Inter won the 2025–26 Serie A title — their 21st, clinched with three matches to spare on 3 May 2026 — with 38 matches, 27 wins, 6 draws, 5 defeats, 89 scored, 35 conceded, a goal difference of +54 and 87 points. That is the most dominant single season on this entire card by some distance, and the 89 goals are 24 more than the next-highest total anywhere on the board. Cagliari finished 14th on 43 points with 11 wins, 10 draws, seventeen defeats, 40 scored and 53 conceded for −13 — a survival season, and one Fabio Pisacane was rewarded for. The structural headline is continuity on both sides and an unusually current head-to-head. Inter kept Cristian Chivu and made one significant signing; Cagliari kept Pisacane, who renewed until 30 June 2028 with a further option, and rebuilt around loan returns and free transfers. Meanwhile the fixture’s recent history is not ancient trivia but live evidence: Inter have won nine of the last ten Serie A meetings, scoring 26 goals at 2.6 a game, and have won all six of their recent away league fixtures at this ground, keeping clean sheets in the last three.

  • Cagliari — turnover moderate, mostly loan returns out and modest fees in. Fabio Pisacane remains, having renewed to 30 June 2028 with a club option for a further season after securing survival; conflict logged: one Serie A transfer summary listed Giovanni Stroppa as Cagliari’s new head coach, which is contradicted by the club’s own reporting and by Stroppa having renewed at Venezia through 2029 — Pisacane is the version carried. The arrivals are functional, and the source lists them by surname only: Basic from Lazio and Helgason from Lecce in midfield, Correia from Valencia, Bella-Kotchap from Verona and Gomez from Zaragoza at the back, Berardi from Pescara, and A. Rrahmani from Sparta Prague in attack. The departures are dominated by loans ending — Alberto Dossena, Marco Palestra, Michael Folorunsho, Ibrahim Sulemana and Luca Mazzitelli all returned to their parent clubs — alongside Andrea Belotti leaving on a free and Gianluca Gaetano on a permanent deal. That is five players subtracted at a stroke by the calendar rather than by choice, plus a centre-forward lost for nothing. Gap: no fees are published for any Cagliari movement in the sources consulted.
  • Inter — turnover low, targeted, and one hole opened by a release clause. Cristian Chivu remains as head coach. The window has been described across the Italian press as targeted acquisition rather than revolution, and Inter’s is the clearest example: Denzel Dumfries left for Real Madrid via a €20m release clause on 5 July — a fee confirmed on Real Madrid’s own ledger on this same card — and Inter responded by signing Djed Spence, a versatile wide defender, as the direct replacement. The club was additionally working to complete a deal for Tottenham centre-back Cristian Romero, unresolved with the window closing at 20:00 on 1 September. B · Style & early-season reality

Inter’s title season was built on both ends simultaneously: 89 scored and 35 conceded is the best attack and, jointly, among the best defences in the division, and 27 wins in 38 leaves very little room for interpretation. Chivu remains, the shape remains — a 3–5–2 with Bastoni, Barella, Calhanoglu, Dimarco and Lautaro all in place — and the summer changed one position, right wing-back, where Dumfries’s €20m release clause was triggered by Real Madrid and Spence was signed to replace him. Spence is unavailable today, which is the one genuine wrinkle: the champions field last season’s team minus the player who left and minus the player who replaced him. Against that, the specific evidence for this fixture is unusually strong. Nine wins in the last ten meetings at 2.6 goals a game, six consecutive away wins at the Unipol Domus, three consecutive clean sheets there. That is not a historical curiosity spanning decades; it is a recent, repeated pattern under broadly the same Inter.

Cagliari’s 14th place with seventeen defeats and 40 goals describes a side that survives by being difficult in the matches it can win and losing the rest. Pisacane earned his renewal by delivering exactly that, and continuity of method is real value for a club at this level. What the summer did to him is the concern: five players left simply because their loans expired — Dossena, Palestra, Folorunsho, Sulemana and Mazzitelli — and Andrea Belotti, the experienced centre-forward, left as a free agent. The replacements are Basic, Helgason, Correia, Bella-Kotchap, Berardi, Rrahmani and Gomez, none of them arriving on a published fee. A side that scored 40 goals in 38 matches has lost its most established forward and replaced him from Sparta Prague and Pescara. The 1–0 over Parma is exactly the kind of match Cagliari win; Inter away is exactly the kind they have lost six times running.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • The head-to-head here is the exception to the usual warning. Nine Inter wins in the last ten Serie A meetings at 2.6 goals a game, six consecutive away wins at the Unipol Domus with clean sheets in the last three, and 47 Inter wins to 14 across 90 meetings. Unlike most of the historical records on this card, this one belongs to broadly the current Inter under the current coach with the current spine, and both clubs kept their manager.
  • Cagliari lost five players to expiring loans in one summer. Dossena, Palestra, Folorunsho, Sulemana and Mazzitelli all returned to parent clubs, which is a squad subtraction imposed by the calendar rather than chosen, and Andrea Belotti left on a free. A side that scored 40 in 38 has replaced its most experienced centre-forward with arrivals from Sparta Prague and Pescara on undisclosed terms.
  • Inter’s one summer change is the one position unavailable tonight. Dumfries left for Real Madrid on a €20m release clause and Spence was signed to replace him; Spence is out and doubtful for the next fixture too. The champions therefore field last season’s team with a hole at right wing-back rather than a new one.
  • The referee data is a season old. Fabbri’s 4.5 cautions a game comes from 11 matches in 2024–25, with no 2025–26 figures, no fouls-per-card ratio and no penalty rate available. The divisional benchmark — 3.59 cautions, 25.34 fouls and 0.28 penalties per game across 381 appearances in 2025–26 — is the more current number in this section, and it is a league average rather than a fact about this official.
  • Most sensitive: BTTS and anything requiring a Cagliari goal, because Inter have kept three consecutive clean sheets at this ground and Cagliari have lost their centre-forward. Least sensitive: the direction of the discipline market and the corner race, both of which rest on a structural relationship — champions with the ball, survivors defending — that six consecutive visits have already demonstrated.

E · Short market-type guide

  • The 1X2 is more usable here than in almost any other fixture on this card, for the unusual reason that both clubs kept their manager and most of their squad, so the recent head-to-head — nine wins in ten, six straight away wins at this ground — describes teams that still exist.
  • The per-team card lines carry more information than the match total, because home 1.5 over at 1.58 against away 1.5 under at 1.65 is a structural statement about who defends, and that structure is confirmed by six previous visits rather than assumed. The 3.5 total depends instead on Fabbri, whose only available card rate is from 2024–25.
  • The over on totals is better evidenced than the under, because it rests on two large samples pointing the same way — Inter’s 89 goals in 38 and 2.6 a game across the recent series with this specific opponent — whereas the under rests on Cagliari’s ability to compress a game they have failed to compress six times running.
  • BTTS is the family to treat most sceptically, because its home leg has been directly falsified three times in a row: Inter’s last three away league visits here ended in clean sheets, and Cagliari have since lost Belotti for nothing.
  • Where the book has priced near-certainty, check whether it earned it. X2 at 1.07 and the corner race at 1.40 are strong claims, and here they are supported by an actual recent record rather than by a badge — which distinguishes this fixture from the other short-priced favourites on the card, where the confidence rests on last season’s table and a squad that has since been rebuilt.
