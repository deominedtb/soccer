A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted. Israel are relegated: fourth in League A Group A2 in 2024–25 on 4 points, 5 scored and 13 conceded (W1 D1 L4, behind France, Italy and Belgium). They return to League B after one edition up, having played in it in 2020–21 and 2022–23. Austria stay in League B: second in Group B3 in 2024–25 on 11 points, 14–5 (W3 D2 L1, Norway first on 13). They then lost the A/B play-off to Serbia, 1–1 in Vienna and 0–2 in Belgrade. Austria won World Cup qualifying Group H on 19 points, 22–4 (W6 D1 L1; the defeat was 0–1 in Romania). At the World Cup they were second in Group J on 4 points (3–1 v Jordan, 0–2 v Argentina, 3–3 v Algeria) and lost 0–3 to Spain in the round of 32. Israel were third in qualifying Group I on 12 points, 19–20, behind Norway (24) and Italy (18), and did not reach the play-offs. The structural headline is two different kinds of disruption. Austria keep their coach and their shape but lose most of the spine that produced their numbers. Israel keep their coach, but tonight is their first competitive match in more than ten months. Israel have not played a competitive home match in Israel since September 2024; the return fixture on 17 November is set for Debrecen (ORF). The eight sides relegated from League A into League B for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 1.72 points per match over 46 matches. Away from home they took 1.43 points per match over 23, scoring 1.52 and conceding 1.35 (over 2.5 in 52.2%, BTTS in 60.9%, failed to score in 13.0%). On matchday one the eight went W3 D4 L1; Austria were one of them, drawing 1–1 in Slovenia in September 2024, and Iceland drew 2–2 in Israel in June 2022. None of the eight had Israel’s gap since its last competitive match, so this history is a thin guide to tonight. Head to head, the four meetings since 2019 split two wins each, and all four went over 2.5 with both sides scoring: Israel won 4–2 (March 2019) and 5–2 (September 2021) at home, Austria 3–1 (October 2019) and 4–2 (November 2021) at home (FootyStats). B · Style & early-season reality

Rangnick’s Austria are a pressing, territory side in one shape. At home in Austria they had 67.4% of the ball, took 18.75 shots against 8.50, won 5.50 corners against 2.13 and put in 26.0 crosses per match. In Linz the corner split was 8.33 to 2.33. In the 2024–25 League B group they outshot opponents 18.33 to 7.00 and out-cornered them 5.67 to 2.33. The picture changes against better sides: in 25/26, with four World Cup matches in it, shots ran 10.30 to 11.60 and corners 3.40 to 3.10, and at the World Cup Austria had 45.1% of the ball. Their own fouls rose from 10.67 per match in the League B group to 13.60 in 25/26, but cards stayed low (1.33 and 1.60 per match). At home in Austria they took 0.88 cards per match and their opponents 2.13. The summer change is to the personnel that ran the press, not to the system. Laimer and Sabitzer, 36 starts between them, were the midfield engine; tonight Seiwald is paired with Xaver Schlager (8 starts). Baumgartner and Arnautović are gone from the front four, and Grüll has no competitive start in the window. Whether the pressing numbers survive is a matter of club form and CVs, not of any match this group has played together.

Ben Shimon’s Israel have two records, and they point in different directions. In League A they were a deep side: 34.1% of the ball, outshot 5.83 to 17.50, out-cornered 1.83 to 7.00, with a back five in two of the six matches. In the 25/26 qualifiers, against Moldova as well as Norway and Italy, they had 53.7% of the ball, and shots ran 13.00 to 13.00. Away from home they had 42.0% of the ball, took 8.86 shots against 16.86 and won 2.43 corners against 6.14. Their matches are open: over 2.5 in 85.7% away and in all five 25/26 qualifiers, including 4–5 against Italy and 0–5 in Norway. Their foul and card counts are low: 10.17 and 1.17 per match in League A, 11.00 and 1.20 in 25/26, and 1.86 cards per match in all their away matches together. They have not conceded a red card in 14 competitive matches. They conceded five penalty kicks in seven away matches and won two in 14 overall. Tonight they start a 4-2-3-1 with Gloukh, Solomon and Khalaili behind an uncapped centre-forward, and a centre-back pairing with little or no competitive history. The only evidence for this unit is club football and three friendlies over ten months.
