A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Fulham finished 11th in 2025–26 on 52 points — 15 wins, 7 draws, 16 defeats, 47 scored, 51 conceded — with a home-away split as wide as any on the slate: 11-2-6 at Craven Cottage, 4-5-10 on the road. Manchester United finished 3rd on 71 points — 20 wins, 11 draws, 7 defeats, 69 scored, 50 conceded — and had the highest both-teams-to-score rate in the division at 71.1%. The structural headline is that both clubs resolved a managerial question and then spent heavily on one unit each, and both are currently below where those investments were meant to put them. Fulham replaced Marco Silva with Álvaro Arbeloa on 7 July 2026 — the same Arbeloa Real Madrid had dismissed on 9 June, five weeks earlier, and whose replacement there, José Mourinho, is managing in the fixture two hours before this one. Manchester United made Michael Carrick permanent after he replaced Rúben Amorim in January 2026 and took 39 points from 17 matches. Fulham then spent £78.1m against nothing, three of the signings following Arbeloa from Real Madrid; United spent £163m against £38m, and £155m of it went on three central midfielders. Fulham are bottom of the table on one point; United are 13th on four.

B · Style & early-season reality

Fulham under Arbeloa have become a more expansive side and a worse one, and both halves are visible in the same four matches. Possession is up from 51.7% to 57.1%, deep completions from 7.76 to 11.00, shots from 12.74 to 15.00 and corners won from 4.95 to 6.25 against 3.25 conceded — a club that conceded more corners than it won last season now wins twice as many as it concedes. PPDA has fallen from 12.65 to 10.84, so they press harder too. The attacking model has improved with it: xG from 1.30 to 1.56. What has collapsed is the defence: xG conceded from 1.60 to 2.08, the second-worst figure on this board, and goals conceded from 1.34 to 1.75. The counted record is worse than either: one point from four, with 2–3 and 2–3 home defeats in which they led or were level at the break. A side creating more, pressing more and holding more, and losing. Arbeloa arrived on 7 July with three Real Madrid players behind him and the squad has visibly changed shape; four matches say it has not yet changed results.

United under a permanent Carrick are the more dominant side by every territorial measure and are converting none of it. Shots have risen from 15.68 to 21.00 per match, possession from 51.8% to 60.3%, deep completions from 7.50 to 10.25, xG from 1.90 to 2.12 and PPDA has fallen from 10.83 to 9.56. Against that: 1.75 goals per match against 1.82, 0.25 first-half goals against 0.76, no clean sheet in four, and a failed-to-score rate of 50.0% against 10.5% last season — one of the largest single deteriorations in any counted series on this board. The explanation is at least partly structural rather than statistical: they sold Højlund for £38m and bought no centre-forward, spending £155m on three central midfielders instead, one of whom (Baleba) is a late fitness call and another of whom (Ugarte) is out. Their two heaviest attacking returns came against Ipswich (5–2, 33 shots) and Sabah in Europe (4–0); in the other four competitive matches they have scored four times. The 2–3 EFL Cup exit to Brighton on Wednesday, from 2–0 up, is the most recent data point and the only one in this set that is three days old.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • United sold their centre-forward and did not replace him, and it is the most likely explanation for the one series that has collapsed. Højlund left for £38m; £155m went on Baleba, Santos and Tielemans. Shots are up a third and the failed-to-score rate has gone from 10.5% to 50.0%. Reading last season’s 69 goals into this fixture reads a forward line that no longer exists.
  • Fulham’s home record was the club’s defining asymmetry and it has already broken. 11-2-6 at Craven Cottage last season against 4-5-10 away; this season both home matches lost 2–3, both after being level or ahead at the break. The venue advantage that would normally carry a bottom-placed side is the thing that has stopped working.
  • Both clubs’ underlying numbers are better than last season and both records are worse. Fulham: xG 1.30→1.56, possession +5.4 points, one point from four. United: xG 1.90→2.12, shots 15.68→21.00, four points from four. When the model and the scoreboard disagree this consistently on both sides of a fixture, four matches cannot say which is leading.
  • The most expensive player in this fixture may not play. Baleba (£70m) is a late ankle call and Ugarte is out — two of the central midfield unit that £155m was spent on. De Ligt and Amad Diallo are also out, and Shaw is a late call.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and the halves markets — both clubs have shown they cannot hold a first-half position, and the venue signal that would normally settle the result has inverted. Least sensitive: the corner differentials and the fouls half of discipline — both rest on possession and pressing changes that are visible in four independent columns per club.
