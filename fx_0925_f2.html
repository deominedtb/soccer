B · Style & early-season reality

Armenia have two records, and the gap between them is the opposition. Against their League C group (Latvia, the Faroe Islands and North Macedonia) they had 59.2% of the ball, took 17.67 shots per match against 6.83, won 8.17 corners against 2.33 and crossed 19.0 times per match. They still conceded in all six. In 25/26, against Portugal, Hungary and Ireland, they had 39.6%, shots ran 7.33 to 18.33 and corners 1.17 to 6.00, and they conceded 3.17 per match. They foul little and are booked little: 9.86 fouls and 1.50 cards per competitive match, and their matches have averaged 3.07 cards against League C’s 4.85. The 2024–25 territory came before Melikyan. It was built on Zelarayán and Bichakhchyan through the middle, Kamo Hovhannisyan on the right, Tiknizyan on the left and Haroyan at the back, and of those only Tiknizyan is in tonight’s squad. Spertsyan scored four of the twelve goals and is the link that remains. Melikyan’s own evidence is six qualifiers against top-seeded opposition and three home friendlies without a box score. How this Armenia side plays against a League C opponent has not been seen in a competitive match.

Nicolato’s Latvia defend with five, whoever they face, and are built not to concede. They had 45.3% of the ball across 16 competitive matches and 42.3% away, took 8.62 shots against 14.19, and won 3.62 corners against 5.62. Their matches are low-scoring: under 1.5 goals in 43.8% of competitive matches, 50.0% away, and 0.25 first-half goals scored per away match. They commit more fouls than they draw (11.31 against 10.69) and take more cards (1.94 against 1.25 per match), with no red card in 16. They cross heavily (16.31 per match). Set pieces and penalties produce a large share of their goals: Černomordijs scored twice from centre-back, a header against Albania and a stoppage-time winner against Gibraltar, and Gutkovskis converted two penalties. Against Gibraltar in the play-off they had 57.6%–62.2% of the ball and won 7.5 corners per match. Tonight’s changes are in attack. Ikaunieks (13 starts) and Varšlaväns (11) were two of the three most-used attackers, and both are out. Gutkovskis and Uldriķis remain, with 20-year-old Aleksanjans uncapped. The back five is largely intact, with Jurkovskis, Balodis, Černomordijs, Cigaņiks and Savaļnieks all in the squad.
