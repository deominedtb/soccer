A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted this season. Sassuolo came up for 2025–26 and finished 11th on 49 points from 38 matches — 14 wins, 7 draws, 17 defeats, 46 scored, 50 conceded — a solid first year back. Torino finished 12th on 45 points with 12 wins, 9 draws and 17 defeats, 44 scored and, most tellingly, 63 conceded, a goal difference of −19 and the worst defensive record of any side that avoided relegation. The structural headline is that both clubs changed coach and both changes cascade from elsewhere on this card. Fabio Grosso left Sassuolo for Fiorentina, who play the day’s other 18:30 Italian fixture; Alberto Aquilani replaced him, arriving from a strong season with Catanzaro in Serie B. Torino did not confirm Roberto D’Aversa and appointed Ignazio Abate. And the two clubs did business with each other: Walukiewicz moved from Torino to Sassuolo on a permanent deal. Both lost 2–1 on matchday one — Sassuolo to Atalanta, Torino at home to Milan — and both are looking for a first point.

  • Sassuolo — turnover very high, and assembled almost entirely on temporary contracts. Alberto Aquilani is the new head coach after Grosso’s departure to Fiorentina, promoted on the strength of his work at Catanzaro in Serie B. The recruitment pattern is the most unusual on the Italian portion of this card: of the eight named arrivals, five are loans carrying options or obligations — Adzic, a forward from Juventus with an option and a counter-option; Bowie, a forward from Verona with a right or obligation to buy; Dominguez, a forward from Bologna with an option; Leysen, a defender from Union Saint-Gilloise with an option or obligation; and Obrador, a defender from Benfica with an option. Only three arrived outright: the goalkeeper Muric, bought from Ipswich Town; Walukiewicz, a defender bought from Torino; and Satalino, a goalkeeper on a free. No fees were published for any of these in the sources consulted. The departures list runs to more than a dozen names including Caligara, Ciervo, Di Bitonto, Ghion, Missori, Nuamah, Paz, Pierini and Skjellerup at the end of a loan, with Álvarez, Amendola and Antiste reported as leaving. That list also contains Cinquegrano and Odenthal, both of whom appear in the published probable eleven; the source appears to mix end-of-loan returns with genuine exits, and the contradiction is recorded rather than resolved.
  • Torino — turnover medium, aimed squarely at a defence that conceded 63. Ignazio Abate takes over after D’Aversa was not confirmed. The business is legible and points one way: Milinković-Savić was bought outright in goal, and Pietro Comuzzo arrived from Fiorentina on a loan carrying a €19m option — a centre-back, and the most expensive commitment attached to any player in this fixture. Oristanio also arrived. Against that, Walukiewicz was sold permanently to today’s opponents, so the defensive rebuild involves subtracting an existing defender as well as adding one. The published probable eleven has Mascardi in goal rather than Milinković-Savić, which is worth noting as reported. No further Torino incomings or outgoings surfaced with fees attached, and the sources on this club were noticeably thinner than for the others on this card.
  • Suspensions / absences. No injury or suspension list surfaced for either club, which is a gap. What the previews do carry is a selection note that matters: Domenico Berardi, Sassuolo’s long-standing talisman, is reported as starting on the bench, with selection battles in defence unresolved. The published probable elevens are Sassuolo in a 4–3–3 — Muric; Cinquegrano, Odenthal, Macchioni, Doig; Bakola, Matic, Adzic; Volpato, Bowie, Lauriente — and Torino in a 3–4–2–1: Mascardi; Comuzzo, Coco, Cömert; Pedersen, Gineitis, Fitz-Jim, Cacciamani; Casadei, Vlašić; Simeone.
  • Sample. One match each and identical scorelines. Sassuolo lost 2–1 to Atalanta and Torino lost 2–1 at home to Milan, so both have scored once and conceded twice, and both are on nothing. Losing narrowly to Atalanta and Milan is close to the least diagnostic pair of results available — it neither confirms nor refutes anything about two sides that finished 11th and 12th and have since changed coach.
  • Referee. Davide Di Marco of the Ciampino section, confirmed on the AIA designations, with Di Gioia and Barone assisting, Zufferli as fourth official, Pezzuto on VAR and Di Paolo assisting. He is the second inexperienced Serie A official on the Italian portion of this card: promoted to CAN in 2022, with the available record indicating only around four Serie A appearances to his name, and no aggregated cards-per-game, fouls-per-card or penalty figures published anywhere consulted. The reporting around him references scrutiny of individual decisions early in his top-flight career rather than any statistical profile. With Allegretta at Monza having no Serie A appearances at all, two of the three Italian fixtures on this card are being worked by officials with essentially no measurable top-flight record.

B · Style & early-season reality

Sassuolo’s 46 goals and 50 conceded in a first season back is a functional, unremarkable record, and the summer has done something quite specific to it: the squad has been rebuilt on borrowed players. Five of eight arrivals are loans with financial triggers, including all three new forwards — Adzic from Juventus, Bowie from Verona, Dominguez from Bologna — and both new defenders. A team assembled that way has two characteristics that matter early: the players are individually capable, because clubs like Juventus and Benfica do not loan out poor ones, and they have no shared time. Aquilani is a new coach arriving from Serie B being asked to integrate them, and Berardi — the one player around whom Sassuolo’s attacking identity has been built for a decade — is reported to be starting on the bench. The published front three of Volpato, Bowie and Lauriente contains two players who were not at the club last season.

Torino’s number is 63 and it explains the entire summer. Conceding 1.66 goals a game while scoring 1.16 is how a club finishes 12th with a −19 difference, and Abate’s recruitment has been aimed at nothing else: the goalkeeper made permanent, a centre-back brought in on a loan carrying a €19m option. The shape is a 3–4–2–1, which is a defensive structure rather than an attacking one, and Comuzzo is projected straight into the back three. But the same window sold Walukiewicz, a defender, to today’s opponents, and the published eleven has Mascardi rather than the goalkeeper the club just bought. So a defence that was the worst among survivors is being reorganised with new personnel, a new coach and an unsettled goalkeeping selection, and the one match played produced two goals conceded at home to Milan. Neither club has a stable defensive baseline to price, and both are trying to build one.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Sassuolo’s squad is built on borrowed players to an unusual degree — five of eight arrivals are loans carrying options or obligations, including all three new forwards and both new defenders. Loan-heavy squads are capable but uncoordinated early, and the coach integrating them, Aquilani, is in his first Serie A job after a season at Catanzaro in Serie B.
  • Berardi starting on the bench is a larger fact than a team-sheet note. Sassuolo’s attacking identity has been organised around him for years, and the published front three is Volpato, Bowie and Lauriente, two of whom were not at the club last season. Any attacking baseline drawn from 46 goals assumes a forward line that is not being fielded.
  • Torino’s 63 goals conceded is the defining number in this fixture and the response to it is unproven in both directions. Comuzzo arrived on loan with a €19m option and is projected into the back three, Milinković-Savić was bought outright but the published eleven names Mascardi in goal, and Walukiewicz — an existing defender — was sold to today’s opponents.
  • Two of the three Italian fixtures on this card are refereed by officials with essentially no measurable Serie A record: Allegretta with no appearances at Monza and Di Marco with around four here. That is a shared, structural reason to treat Italian discipline markets on this board as higher-variance than usual, independent of anything either club does.
  • Most sensitive: cards and corners — the first because the referee has no usable record, the second because both attacking and territorial structures are new. Least sensitive: BTTS, which rests on Torino’s 63 conceded and Sassuolo’s 46 scored and 50 conceded, large samples that this summer’s business has done nothing to reverse.
