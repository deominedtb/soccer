B · Style & early-season reality

Ipswich won promotion as a possession side — 56.1% of the ball, 5.70 corners and 5.1 shots on target per match, 37% clean sheets in the Championship. The first four Premier League matches under O’Neil show the step up in every territorial number: 44% possession, 3.50 corners, 16.5 shots faced per match, with a 4–2–3–1 used in almost every minute. The side playing that shape is new at goalkeeper, centre-back, both central midfield slots and centre-forward, which means last season’s numbers describe a different team as well as a different division, and the evidence for the new spine is four matches, two of them against Manchester United and Liverpool.

Arsenal under Arteta are the most stable profile on the slate: a 4–3–3 and 4–2–3–1 side with 56–59% possession, 0.74 and 0.68 xG against per match in the two seasons, first-half goals conceded at 0.24 and 0.25 per match, low card rates (1.34 and 1.50) and 23 set-piece goals last season. The summer replaced Martinelli, Trossard and Jesus with Tzolis and added Guimarães and Konsa. The catch is that all of that describes the first-choice side; a cup tie between a Champions League match and a league match is exactly where the published rates stop applying, and Saliba and White are out regardless.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Ipswich’s 2025–26 rates are Championship rates from a different spine. 80 goals and 56% possession came against second-tier sides with a goalkeeper, centre-forward and central midfield since replaced.
  • Arsenal’s rates describe the first-choice side. Six straight wins, one league goal conceded — and a cup tie wedged between Napoli away and Brighton away, with a preview expecting rotation.
  • Defensive absences. Saliba and White are both out, so even Arsenal’s first-choice defensive numbers are partly made by players unavailable tonight.
  • Ipswich’s four-match sample includes Manchester United away and Liverpool at home, which account for seven of the ten goals conceded.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, the handicap and BTTS — all depend on Arsenal’s selection. Least sensitive: Arsenal’s card rate, low in both seasons and across personnel changes.
