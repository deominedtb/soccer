A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

One promoted side and one club in freefall, and they are level on nothing. Venezia won the 2025–26 Serie B at the first attempt after relegation — first on 82 points from 38 matches, 24W–10D–4L, 77 scored and 31 conceded for +46, a point clear of Frosinone and six clear of play-off winners Monza, with the division's longest winning run at eight matches and the third Serie B title in the club's history; promotion was sealed with a 2–2 draw away at Spezia. Giovanni Stroppa is unchanged in his second season, and it was the fourth promotion of his career. Serie B averaged 2.56 goals a match last season, a number worth carrying because it is the division those 77 goals were scored in. Fiorentina are not promoted — this is their 23rd consecutive season in the top flight and the 100th in the club's history — and they are the more disrupted side by a distance. After what the season previews describe as a shock flirtation with the relegation battle in 2025–26, the club spent €150.30m this summer, the third-largest outlay in Serie A, appointed Fabio Grosso from Sassuolo, lost all three opening matches 0–4, 0–3 and 1–2, sacked him, and on 7 September 2026 appointed Paolo Vanoli — the man who had steered them clear of relegation in the second half of last season and left three months ago. Tonight is Vanoli's first match back, four days into the job. It is also against Venezia, the club he previously managed and took up to Serie A. Both sides therefore arrive on zero points from three, Venezia with 2 scored and 7 conceded, Fiorentina with 1 and 9.

  • Venezia — high turnover funded by one big sale, weighted to the centre-forward, and no managerial change at all. Stroppa stays, so the churn is entirely in the squad and it was financed from the top: Issa Doumbia to Sporting CP for a reported €20.53m plus €6m in add-ons, with Hans Nicolussi Caviglia to Parma for €6m and Michael Svoboda to Brighton for €5m on a four-year contract. In, with fees: Akor Adams from Sevilla for €16m plus €6m, a centre-forward and much the largest outlay; Albion Rrahmani from Sparta Prague for €7.5m plus €2m; Armel Bella-Kotchap from Verona for €7m plus €3m, a centre-back; Redouane Halhal from Mechelen for €5m; Kornel Lisman from Lech Poznań for €2.5m; and in goal Lorenzo Montipò from Verona for €250k plus Alessio Pozzi from Vis Pesaro for an undisclosed fee. Free and loan business: Toma Bašić from Lazio on a free on 27 May, Juan Jesus permanently on a free, Thierry Rendall Correia, Thorir Johann Helgason, Pasquale Mazzocchi on loan from Napoli, and — directly relevant tonight — Matías Moreno and Simon Sohm on loan from Fiorentina. The GK/CB/DM ledger is addressed at two of the three: a goalkeeper and a centre-back bought, a centre-forward as the headline fee, and no dedicated defensive midfielder signed. Gap: the terms of the two Fiorentina loans, including whether any clause governs them facing the parent club, are not published in anything reached — and Sohm is named in the probable eleven.
  • Fiorentina — very high turnover, €150.30m spent, and two managers in four days. Grosso arrived from Sassuolo in the summer and was dismissed after three defeats; Vanoli was appointed on 7 September 2026, which makes every tactical claim about the visitors four days old. The spending was heavy and spread across the spine. In, with fees: Arthur Atta from Udinese for €25m plus €5m in midfield; Mateo Pellegrino from Parma for €25m plus €5m, a centre-forward; Beto from Everton for €18m, a second centre-forward; Marin Pongračić from Lecce for €15m, a centre-back; Marco Brescianini from Atalanta for €10m; Radu Drăgușin on a €1.5m loan from Tottenham carrying an obligation to buy, a second centre-back; David de Gea on a free in goal; and Franco Mastantuono from Real Madrid. Out: Moise Kean loaned to Como with a €35m obligation — the centre-forward the two new ones replace — Albert Guðmundsson free to Lazio with an obligation, Rolando Mandragora to Torino for €5m, and the two loans to tonight's opponents. So the ledger is busy everywhere the spec asks: a free-transfer goalkeeper, €15m and a loan-with-obligation at centre-back, €25m in midfield, and €43m across two centre-forwards to replace one. What it has produced so far is one goal in three matches.
  • Suspensions / absences. Venezia's list is named and falls on the defence: Marin Šverko, Bartol Franjić and Andrea Adorante are sidelined, and Armel Bella-Kotchap — the €7m centre-back signed this summer — is a doubt. No suspension is reported on either side. For Fiorentina the notable absence is a selection rather than an injury: Drăgușin, signed on a loan carrying an obligation to buy, is reported to be left on the bench by the new manager, with Pongračić and Luca Ranieri the expected pairing. Beto is expected to make his first start for the club. Gap: no injury list at all was obtainable for Fiorentina, which on a card where the manager changed four days ago is a real limitation.
  • Sample. Three Serie A matches each and no points between them. Venezia: 2–0 down at Lecce on 23 August, 2–0 down at Milan on 28 August, and a third defeat — the opponent and scoreline of which were not obtainable, though the aggregate of 2 scored and 7 conceded across three places it at 2–3. They also beat Modena 3–2 in the Coppa Italia on 15 August and went out 2–1 at Udinese on 2 September, and beat Galatasaray 3–0 in pre-season. Fiorentina: 0–4 away at Roma on 24 August, 0–3 to Frosinone, 1–2 to Torino, one scored and nine conceded, plus a 4–1 Coppa Italia win over Benevento on 14 August. Between them these two clubs have scored three goals in six league matches and conceded sixteen. Gaps: no xG, shot, possession or corner series was obtainable for either club in either season; Fiorentina's final 2025–26 position, goals for and goals against were not obtainable in anything reached, only the characterisation that they were drawn into the relegation fight. Contradictions logged: Venezia's current position is given as 18th by one source and 19th by another; and one aggregator gives their Serie B season as 79 points and +44, which fails its own arithmetic — the 82/77/31/+46 row above is the one that reconciles, and is what this card carries.
  • Referee. Francesco Fourneau of Rome, confirmed, with Alassio and Barone on the lines, Ayroldi as fourth official, Dionisi on VAR and Maggioni as AVAR. He is 42 and experienced, and the record has an unusual shape. In 2025–26 he averaged 4.5 cards a match. In 2024–25 he averaged 2.8 — 30 yellows across 11 Serie A matches — and awarded 4 penalties in the season. His single 2026–27 match to date produced 35 fouls, 5 yellows, no reds and no penalty, a fouls-per-card ratio of 7.0. The Italian match reporting describes his style consistently: precise, and inclined to let play run rather than whistle so-called half-fouls. Those two things together are the useful part — an official who blows for comparatively few fouls but has been booking at a rising rate is punishing a larger share of what he does stop. History with either club: Fiorentina have had him five times for 2 wins, 2 draws and 1 defeat, and are still unbeaten away under him, the two most recent being a 1–1 at Frosinone two years ago and a 0–6 win at Lecce last season. No Venezia record under him was found.

B · Style & early-season reality

Venezia are the continuous side here and the one whose profile can actually be described, because nothing above the players has changed. Stroppa is in his second season, he won the division by taking 82 points from 38 matches with only four defeats, and the probable eleven is a 3–5–2 — Stankovic in goal; Schingtienne, Halhal and Haps across the back three; Hainaut and Correia as wing-backs around Kike Perez, Busio and Sohm; Yeboah and Adams in front. The summer served that shape rather than altering it: a €16m centre-forward as the headline, a centre-back, a goalkeeper, a midfielder, and the Doumbia sale funding it. What three Serie A matches have shown is the familiar promoted-side compression — two goals scored and seven conceded, against a Serie B campaign in which they scored 77 in a division averaging 2.56 goals a game. The defensive absences bite in exactly the wrong place for a back three: Šverko and Franjić out, and the €7m centre-back Bella-Kotchap a doubt. Gap: no xG, possession, foul or corner data was obtainable for Venezia in either season, so the style description above rests on the formation, the transfer weighting and the results, not on measurement.

Fiorentina cannot be described at all yet, and that is the honest position. A club that spent €150.30m — third-most in the division — lost its first three matches by a combined 9–1, dismissed the manager who bought into that squad and reinstalled the one who had kept the club up four months earlier. Vanoli has had four days. The probable shape is a 4–3–3 — de Gea; Dodo, Pongračić, Ranieri, Valdepeñas; Ndour, Fagioli, Atta; Mastantuono, Beto, Njie — which is a reasonable guess at what he prefers and nothing more, and it already contains a visible judgement: Drăgușin, arriving on a loan the club is obliged to convert, starts on the bench, while Beto makes his first start as the lone centre-forward in place of the departed Kean. There is a second-order fact worth stating plainly: the man now picking Fiorentina's team built the Venezia side that won promotion to Serie A, and two of his own club's players are on loan in tonight's opposition dressing room, with Sohm named to start. Gaps: no xG, foul or corner series for Fiorentina either; no injury list; and their 2025–26 final columns were not obtainable, so the only evidence for what this team is amounts to a four-day-old appointment, a transfer ledger and 270 minutes played under someone else.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Fiorentina's manager has been in post four days. Vanoli was appointed on 7 September 2026 after Grosso was dismissed following three defeats; the squad he picks tonight was assembled over a €150.30m summer by a regime that has already been removed, and the 0–4, 0–3 and 1–2 that got Grosso sacked are the only Serie A evidence this team has produced.
  • Venezia's numbers were made in a different division. 82 points, 77 scored and 31 conceded came from Serie B, where the average match produced 2.56 goals; three Serie A matches have produced two goals and seven conceded, which is the whole of the top-flight sample and is not enough to correct the second-tier figures against.
  • The defensive absences land on a back three. Šverko and Franjić are out and Bella-Kotchap — the €7m centre-back bought this summer — is a doubt, in a 3–5–2 that needs three of them. Every goals, handicap and BTTS reading above is partly a reading of who fills that line.
  • Two Fiorentina players are in the opposition squad and one is named to start. Matías Moreno and Simon Sohm are on loan at Venezia from tonight's visitors, Sohm is in the probable eleven, and the terms of those loans are not published in anything reached — a fact that is unusual enough to state and not specific enough to weigh.
  • Most sensitive: both-teams-to-score, priced at 1.47 on two sides that have managed three goals between them in six matches, with one making a €18m striker's first start under a manager appointed on Monday. Least sensitive: the cards family — a confirmed official with three seasons of published card rates, a measured fouls-per-card figure and a named assistant and VAR team, which is more evidence than either playing squad on this page has produced all season.
