The one thing shaping every game on this card

Six clubs have been researched for this board and three of them have changed head coach inside the last five months. That alone would be unremarkable on a September slate. What makes it the single structural fact shaping every game here is which three: Union Berlin, Fiorentina and Marseille — in each fixture, the club with the larger budget, the longer top-flight tenure or both. And in each fixture the opponent has kept the manager it had. Schalke retained Miron Muslić, who won them the 2. Bundesliga; Venezia retained Giovanni Stroppa, who won them Serie B; Rennes retained Franck Haise, who took them to sixth. The continuity on this card belongs almost entirely to the smaller side.

The disruption is not uniform in kind, which is what makes it usable rather than merely tidy. Union Berlin appointed Mauro Lustrinelli on 21 May 2026 as their third coach in five months and have abandoned the back five they have played in every Bundesliga season since promotion in 2019 — then lost two of the three centre-backs bought for the new shape to injury. Fiorentina spent €150.30m, the third-largest outlay in Serie A, lost their first three matches 9–1 on aggregate, sacked Fabio Grosso and on 7 September 2026 re-appointed Paolo Vanoli, whose first match in charge is tonight — against the club he himself took up to Serie A. Marseille have had three coaches since February, and made no signings at all this summer while selling their goalkeeper, their centre-forward and loaning out their captain at centre-back; their manager has said publicly that arrivals require further departures to be registered first.

Against that sit three sides whose figures were made somewhere else. Schalke's 70 points, 50 scored and 31 conceded came in the second tier, on a €2.65m summer and the third-lowest squad value in the Bundesliga. Venezia's 82 points and 77 goals came in a Serie B averaging 2.56 goals a match. Rennes are the exception that is still a caveat: unchanged at the top, but having sold a 20-year-old centre-back to Liverpool for £60m and replaced him from the free and undisclosed market.

Structural consequence. On every fixture on this board, last season's columns describe a team that no longer exists on one side of the tie, and a different division on the other. That pushes weight away from result markets, which need a view on which of those two fictions is less wrong, and towards the families with their own independent evidence — above all discipline, where two of the three built cards have a confirmed referee with several seasons of published card, dismissal and penalty rates behind him, and one has no appointment that could be reached at all.

The Bundesliga's treatment of promoted sides is the usual question, but Schalke arrive with an unusual version of it: the manager who won promotion, Miron Muslić, is unchanged, and the squad that won it is very largely unchanged too, because the club spent €2.65m in total this summer and carries a squad market value of €67.33m — the third-lowest in the division and €49.82m behind fifteenth-placed Hamburger SV on €117.15m. B · Style & early-season reality

Union Berlin are, for the first time in their Bundesliga history, trying to be a different kind of team — and the evidence that they can be is two matches old and already compromised. The back five was not a preference at Union, it was the institution: it carried them from the 2019 promotion through eight seasons, and Lustrinelli has replaced it with a back four and a high press in his first summer. Everything in the window served that idea — two long-serving centre-backs moved on, three new ones brought in, a forward with Championship goals added on loan, Khedira at 32 asked to hold the base of a pressing shape. The problem is arithmetic: two of those three centre-backs are injured, so the defensive line executing an unfamiliar structure is not the line that was assembled to execute it. What the two matches have produced is consistent with a side mid-transition and nothing more precise than that — a 3–3 salvaged by late goals, then a 0–4 at Leverkusen, seven conceded in 180 minutes and more than anyone else in the division. Against that, last season's home column is the one number on this page suggesting the venue matters: 26 scored and 26 conceded at the Alte Försterei across 17 matches, against 18 and 32 away. Gap: no xG, possession, foul-per-game or corner series was obtainable for Union in either season, so the press is described here from its manager's stated intent and his record at Thun, not from measurement.

Schalke are the opposite proposition in every respect, and the more legible of the two. There has been no managerial change, no stylistic reinvention and almost no spending: Muslić won the second division by eight points with a flat 4–3–3 or a 3–4–2–1 that drops wing-backs into the line, and he is running the same thing in the Bundesliga with a squad reinforced by €2.65m. The early returns split exactly the way that profile predicts. Beaten 3–0 by Augsburg, they then held the champions to 0–0 at home — a result that says the centralised defensive block transfers to this level, and says nothing at all about whether the attack does. They have not scored a Bundesliga goal, from 15 attempts and 6 on target, with a €800k centre-forward and a 32-year-old loanee left-back among the summer's answers. Karius at 33 has been busy and effective, 7.5 saves a match at 83.3%. The discipline signature is already distinctive and it is the cleanest in the league: 11 fouls in two matches, fewest of any club, which is what a side defending in a narrow block without chasing possession tends to produce. The caution is that both of these two-match samples contain an extreme — a 0–3 and a 0–0 with Bayern — and a promoted side's third fixture against a mid-table club is a different test from either. Gap: no xG series for Schalke either, and no corner data for either club.
