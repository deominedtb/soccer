B · Style & early-season reality

Genoa under De Rossi are a back-three side: last season Understat logged 1,537 minutes in a 3–5–2 against 783 in a 3–4–2–1 and 740 in a 4–2–3–1, with 48.7% possession, 12.4 fouls per match (league 12.7 per team) and 3.68 corners per match, below the Serie A team average of 4.41. This season the back three has persisted across three shapes (3–4–2–1, 3–5–2, 3–4–1–2) while possession has dropped to 45.3% and the shot count faced has risen by more than five a match. What the summer changed is the personnel of that system at its ends: four of the flank defenders are new (Ehizibue, Mitaj, Puczka, Drameh) after Norton-Cuffy and Martín were sold, and the front two is a pairing one preview calls inedito — evidence for it is a CV, not a match, and Havel, one of the summer’s forwards, is not in the squad.

Südtirol under Castori were a low-possession, high-contact Serie B side — 40.6% possession, 16.6 fouls per match against a division average of 15.0 per team, 2.37 cards per match and 4.34 corners per match. The four matches under Possanzini show different numbers on almost every one of those axes: 48.1% possession, 12.8 fouls, 2.50 corners per match, with a new goalkeeper and a new centre-back pairing behind a defence that has conceded twice.
