A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted. Both have been in League A in every edition since 2020–21, and both played in Group A2 in 2024–25. Italy were second on 13 points, 13–8 (W4 D1 L1, behind France), then lost the quarter-final to Germany 1–2 at San Siro and drew 3–3 in Dortmund. Belgium were third on 4 points, 6–9 (W1 D1 L4), and stayed in League A through the A/B play-off, losing 1–3 to Ukraine in Murcia and winning 3–0 in Genk. In World Cup qualifying Italy went W6 L2, 21–12, finishing behind Norway, who beat them 3–0 in Oslo and 4–1 at San Siro. They beat Northern Ireland 2–0 in the play-off semi-final in Bergamo, then drew 1–1 in Zenica after extra time, with Bastoni sent off in the 41st minute, and lost the shoot-out 1–4. It was a third straight World Cup missed (Euronews). Belgium qualified unbeaten, W5 D3, 29–7, then reached the World Cup quarter-finals: draws with Egypt (1–1) and Iran (0–0), 5–1 against New Zealand, 3–2 after extra time against Senegal (2–2 at 90 minutes), 4–1 against the United States, and 1–2 against Spain. The structural headline is two new coaches at matchday one, with Italy’s back line missing its most-used centre-back and left wing-back and Belgium’s missing its goalkeeper. Head to head: they drew 2–2 at this stadium in October 2024 (Italy led 2–0 after 24 minutes, then played from the 40th minute with ten after Lorenzo Pellegrini’s red card), and Italy won 1–0 in Brussels a month later. Sports Mole counts four Italy wins in the last five meetings. The RESEARCH SPEC’s transfer-fee field has no national-team equivalent. B · Style & early-season reality

Italy’s numbers depend on the opponent more than on the coach. Against 2026–27 League A sides they had 49.5% of the ball, took 10.38 shots against 11.75 and won 3.38 corners against 5.75, and their matches ran 3.75 goals and 2.13 first-half goals. In Gattuso’s eight, mostly against Estonia, Israel, Moldova and Northern Ireland, they had 59.2%, took 22.71 shots against 7.00, crossed 27.6 times and won 7.63 corners against 3.00; Opta counts their 140 qualifying shots as the lowest-quality set of any side with 100 or more (0.11 xG per shot). They foul little and draw fouls: 10.89 committed and 12.94 drawn per competitive match, and 1.56 cards per match. Their first-half goals against fell from 0.67 per match in the 2024–25 group to 0.13 in 25/26, but 25/26 holds only one match against League A opposition (Norway, 1–4, with all four conceded after half-time). The summer change is deeper than the coach. The back three that started most of the window loses Bastoni and its left wing-back Dimarco, the predicted 4-3-3 has not been played competitively by this group, and the centre-forward who started 14 of 18, Retegui, is not in the squad. The evidence for Mancini’s Italy is a CV and a predicted line-up; the nine uncapped or unstarted players in the 23 have no competitive international record at all.

Belgium hold the ball and win corners wherever they play, but in the 2024–25 League A group that did not produce goals. In that group they had 57.0% of the ball, took 14.83 shots against 13.00 and won 7.83 corners against 2.67, yet scored 1.00 per match and failed to score in half of their six. Against weaker qualifying opposition they had 68.0%, took 22.25 shots against 5.88 and won 10.38 corners against 2.63. At the World Cup the ball and corner edge narrowed to 51.1% and 4.00 against 3.83, and they went W2 D3 L1 at 90 minutes. Their card count is low (1.45 per match in the window, 1.00 in 25/26), and their matches ran 5.52 fouls per card, the League A figure exactly. Opponents booked more than Belgium did in every window. The squad keeps its spine through the middle: De Bruyne, Tielemans, De Ketelaere, Lukaku, De Cuyper and Castagne between them made 77 starts and scored 30 goals in the window. What changes is at the ends. Doku and Trossard, 34 starts and 9 goals from the wide attacking positions, are both out, and the goalkeeper is Lammens or an uncapped one in place of Courtois. How van Bommel sets the side up has no competitive evidence at all: his record is club football at Antwerp, Wolfsburg and PSV.
