A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and the two arrive with almost identical amounts of recent success and opposite amounts of continuity. Slavia retained the Czech First League in 2025–26 — their ninth title — on 71 points from 30 matches, 21 wins, eight draws and one defeat all season, with 63 scored and 23 conceded. They played in the Champions League league phase last season too, so this is their second successive campaign in the competition proper and only the fourth in the club's history. Jindřich Trpišovský remains in charge, a long tenure by any standard on this board. Lens finished second in Ligue 1 behind PSG on 70 points from 34 matches — 22 wins, four draws, eight defeats, 66 scored and 35 conceded — and won the first Coupe de France in the club's history on 22 May 2026, beating Nice 3–1. Then the manager who did all of that left: Pierre Sage departed for Crystal Palace, and Dino Toppmöller was appointed on a contract to 2028, arriving from Eintracht Frankfurt where he had been since 2023. He has already added a trophy, beating PSG 1–0 in the Trophée des Champions on 16 August 2026. The two clubs have met once before, in the 1995–96 UEFA Cup third round, which Slavia won 1–0 on aggregate — thirty years ago.

  • Slavia — continuity in the dugout, and the emptiest transfer ledger on this board. Trpišovský is unchanged, which given the results above is the most valuable single structural fact on this side of the page. Beyond that, this card has to be honest about how little it knows: no summer 2026 incoming or outgoing transfer was established for Slavia in anything reached, and no fee for any movement in either direction. The transfer material that surfaced belongs to the 2025 window — Prekop from Baník Ostrava, Cham on loan, Zafeiris on loan from PAOK, Hashioka from Luton Town — and is not this window's business, so it is not used. One departure is reported without a date: Tomáš Chorý, a 31-year-old forward under contract to June 2027, is described as free to look for a new club after spitting at an opponent in a derby. Undated in what was reached, so it is recorded and not asserted as a 2026 move. There is therefore no goalkeeper, centre-back or defensive-midfield ledger for the home side, which is the largest evidence gap on this card.
  • Lens — a new manager in the summer, one very large sale, and modest reinvestment. Toppmöller replaced Sage, so every stylistic claim about the visitors rests on a handful of matches. The window's shape is clear even if the detail is thin: out went Mamadou Sangaré to Brentford for a reported €48m, comfortably the largest transaction on either side of this fixture; in came Yacine Titraoui for a reported €8m and Michaël Cuisance for a reported €3.5m, with Franjo Ivanović arriving from Benfica on a straight loan. Florian Thauvin is captain, with Thorgan Hazard and Odsonne Édouard among the senior squad and several players on loan from other clubs. Weighted as the spec asks: roughly €11.5m spent against €48m received, and no goalkeeper or centre-back arrival established — which matters more than usual given the absence list below.

Lens are missing five players — Maik Nawrocki, Jonathan Gradit, Samson Baidoo, Nidal Celik and Jhoanner Chavez — a group weighted heavily towards the back line, in a squad that bought no defender this summer. Slavia have two fresh concerns, both picked up in the 4–0 against Zbrojovka Brno on 5 September: David Zima was forced off after a blow to the head with a mild concussion, and Lukáš Provod left early with significant swelling around the ankle and a suspected ligament injury. B · Style & early-season reality

Slavia are the most continuous side on this board and the one whose numbers most clearly describe a settled method: one defeat in thirty league matches, 23 conceded across a full season, 71 points, the same coach throughout, and an unbeaten run of eight carried into tonight including a 3–0 win over their city rivals. They also have something almost nobody else on this slate has — they played in this competition's league phase last season, so the step up is not new to the squad. What this card cannot do is say what changed over the summer, because nothing about their 2026 window was obtainable: no arrival, no departure, no fee. A settled team with an unknown amount of turnover is a specific kind of uncertainty, and it is the one this fixture carries on the home side. The two new injuries are the only fresh squad information available, and both were sustained five days ago. Gap: no possession, shot, foul or corner series for Slavia in either season.

Lens are the mirror image: the record is documented and the man who produced it has gone. Second in Ligue 1 on 70 points with 66 scored and 35 conceded, and a first Coupe de France, were Pierre Sage's; Toppmöller arrived from Frankfurt in the summer and his own evidence at the club amounts to a Trophée des Champions won 1–0 against PSG on 16 August, a 5–2 against Auxerre on the opening weekend, and three defeats in the last five including 1–0 to Lorient at the weekend. The squad underneath him lost its largest asset in Sangaré at a reported €48m and reinvested roughly €11.5m, and it now goes to Prague missing five players concentrated in defence, having signed no defender. That combination — a new coach, a big sale, a thin replacement window and a depleted back line — is the clearest structural story on this fixture, and it is the reason the row below that asks about goals rather than the one that asks about the result is the better-evidenced of the two. Gap: no underlying series for Lens either, and no current league position or points total.
