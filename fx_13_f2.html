A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Reduced-depth card: research was capped at two searches per club plus the referee lookup. The RESEARCH SPEC fields not pursued here are both clubs' 2025–26 final position, goals scored and conceded, xG, shot volume, home/away split and foul and card rates; the full ins-and-outs ledger with fees beyond the named signings below; and the suspension list. Those absences are a budget decision, not a finding.

Neither club is promoted — both were in the Bundesliga last season — but both changed head coach in May 2026, which is the structural fact this fixture turns on. Leverkusen replaced Ole Werner with Carles Martínez, brought in from Girona and described as importing an intense pressing style. Union Berlin appointed Mauro Lustrinelli on 21 May 2026, arriving from FC Thun, whom he had just led to the first Swiss Super League title in that club's history — a first Bundesliga job for a coach whose track record is in a different league entirely. One matchday in, both new appointments have already produced a defensive result worth recording: Leverkusen lost their opener 3–2 at home to newly promoted SV 07 Elversberg and sit 12th on no points; Union drew 3–3 with Eintracht Frankfurt and sit 8th on one. Six goals in each of their opening matches, and three conceded each. The venue record is one-directional: Leverkusen have won six of nine meetings with Union at the BayArena, and Union have never won here, drawing three.

  • Leverkusen — new coach, and the recruitment reached here is on the flanks rather than the spine. Martínez took over from Werner this summer, so the shape is a Girona shape being installed rather than a continuation. The signings established within the cap are Guéla Doué, the Ivorian right-back signed from Strasbourg on a five-year contract to 2031, and Afonso Moreira, reported to have impressed in pre-season — the same player who left Lyon in this window for a reported €33.6m including bonuses, a fee published on the selling side rather than by Leverkusen. Facundo Medina was expected to come into the side for his full Bundesliga debut in this match. Gap: no fee is published in anything reached for Doué or for Leverkusen's side of the Moreira deal, and no goalkeeper, centre-back or centre-forward movement was established in either direction within the two-search cap.
  • Union Berlin — low investment by design, with the club's money going into the stadium rather than the squad. The reporting is explicit that Union directed most of this summer's resources at infrastructure, including stadium work, rather than at players. The arrivals reached are Michel Aebischer from Pisa, Emmanuel Latte Lath on loan from Atlanta United, Zeno Van den Bosch on 23 June 2026 and Stanley Nsoki, made permanent on 1 July 2026. The predicted shape under Lustrinelli is a 4–2–2–2: Raab; Juranović, Querfeld, Van den Bosch, Rothe; Kemlein, Khedira; Jeong, Burcu; Latte Lath, Ljubičić — a back four containing one of this summer's signings and a front two containing another. Pre-season was poor and is reported as such: two wins in nine friendlies, the victories coming against fifth-tier Rathenow (12–2) and Aris Limassol (3–2). Gap: no fee is published for Aebischer, Van den Bosch or Nsoki, and the Latte Lath deal is described as a loan without terms.
  • Suspensions / absences. Not pursued at this depth beyond the team news carried in the match preview, which reports Medina expected to start for Leverkusen and no changes expected from Lustrinelli for Union. Gap: no injury or suspension list for either club was sought or established.

Leverkusen: 0 points, 12th, beaten 3–2 at home by a promoted club. Union: 1 point, 8th, 3–3 with Eintracht Frankfurt. Both conceded three. Union additionally carry nine pre-season friendlies with two wins, which is context rather than competitive evidence and is treated as such here.
  • Referee. Benjamin Brand, named in the match preview reached — carried here as the appointment but not corroborated by a federation designation list, so treat it as reported rather than confirmed. His record is unusually well documented for this slate. In 2025–26 he officiated 19 matches, showing 72 yellow cards and 3 red cards for 3.95 total cards per game — 3.79 yellow, 0.16 red — and awarded 4 penalties, a rate of 0.21 per match. Across a 214-match career, 101 of them in the Bundesliga, he averages 4.04 yellows and 0.10 reds a game with 66 penalties, roughly 0.31 per match. Last season therefore ran slightly below his career booking rate and slightly below his career penalty rate. D · Early-season risk notes

  • Both coaches were appointed in May 2026 and both are one competitive match into the job. Martínez arrives from Girona, Lustrinelli from a title-winning season at FC Thun and into his first Bundesliga post. Nothing on this page describes an established system; it describes two installations in progress.
  • The six-goal openers are doing almost all the work in the goals markets and they are one match each. Leverkusen's was a 3–2 home defeat to a promoted club, Union's a 3–3 draw. Neither club's last-season goal columns were pursued at this depth, so there is no base rate on this card against which to judge whether either scoreline was typical.
  • Union spent the summer on the stadium rather than the squad, by their own reporting, and went two wins from nine in pre-season. That is a club whose current XI is close to last season's, under a coach who is not — the opposite configuration to Leverkusen, who changed coach and recruited, but on the flanks rather than through the middle as far as the search reached.
  • The BayArena head-to-head — six Leverkusen wins in nine, no Union win ever — is the longest-running fact here and predates both appointments and both summers. It is priced into the 1.37 and the 1.27 corner race, and it is the least current evidence on the card.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total, where 1.35 on the over extrapolates a single matchday from each side while the season baselines that would test it were never pursued. Least sensitive: the discipline row, the only family resting on a multi-season record — Brand's 19 matches last season and 214 across his career — rather than on 90 minutes, with the caveat that the appointment is reported rather than confirmed.
