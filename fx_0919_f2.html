A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and neither is new to this division. Osasuna finished 17th in 2025–26 on 42 points — 11 wins, 9 draws, 18 defeats, 44 scored, 50 conceded — and stayed up only on the final matchday. Rayo finished 8th on 50 points — 12 wins, 14 draws, 12 defeats, 41 scored, 44 conceded, with the lowest over-2.5 rate of the two by a distance: 36.8% against a divisional 50.0%. The structural headline is that these two sides arrived at a similar place by opposite routes and then made the same two decisions in the same six weeks. Both dismissed or lost their head coach at the end of May and appointed a replacement in June; both sold their most valuable player for a fee they did not reinvest. Osasuna banked €40m for Víctor Muñoz and spent €8m; Rayo banked €42.4m for Nobel Mendy and Pep Chavarría and spent €2.6m plus four loans. Last season’s numbers on this card describe two squads that have each lost their best asset and their coach.

B · Style & early-season reality

Osasuna are the same side they were, playing worse away from home and with a hole where their best player used to be. Possession 47.1% against 46.8% last season, PPDA 11.99 against 12.07 — the press has not moved at all, which is notable given the coach has. What has moved is everything downstream of it: shots have fallen from 11.84 to 9.33 per match, deep completions from 5.05 to 3.17, and corners won from 3.76 to 3.00, the lowest corner rate of either side here and well under a divisional 4.94. The clearest single number on their card is the penalty dependence: xG 1.33 per match but non-penalty xG 0.95, so roughly 29% of what they generate comes from the spot, against a last-season split of 1.35 and 1.20 where it was 11%. A side that has lost a €40m forward-line asset and replaced him with €8m of signings is creating less from open play and converting less — 0.83 goals per match against 1.16 — and doing it while conceding 2.00. The El Sadar record is the counterweight and it is real: 0.67 conceded per home match, three home matches, against 3.33 away.

Rayo have changed shape and the change is measurable rather than asserted, but it has gone the wrong way at both ends. Possession has dropped from 53.42% to 46.32% and PPDA from 8.46 to 9.51 — they press slightly less and have the ball considerably less — while the defensive side has collapsed in a way the deep-completions column states plainly: deep completions conceded have risen from 6.26 to 11.83 per match, nearly double, and shots faced from 12.24 to 17.67. The back line doing that work is Kumbulla and Sadick on loan, behind a loaned goalkeeper, after Mumin left free and Chavarría was sold for €19m. At the other end the story is the mirror image and it is the most extreme swing on this card: a side that went over 2.5 goals in 36.8% of matches last season has done it in 83.3% of six this season, and has had both teams score in 100% of them after 44.7% last season. Under San José they are scoring more (1.67 against 1.08) and conceding far more (2.50 against 1.16). The evidence that this is a system change is a coach’s start date, six matches of pressing and deep-completion data, and a defence assembled on loan — not a season.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Both clubs’ 2025–26 numbers were produced under a different head coach and with a player who has been sold. Osasuna’s 44 goals and 42 points came under Lisci with Víctor Muñoz; Rayo’s 50 points and 44 conceded came under Íñigo Pérez with Chavarría and Mumin in the defence. Ramis arrived on 10 June and San José on 18 June. Every 25/26 figure in the strip is history on both sides of this fixture, not just one.
  • Osasuna’s season rate is an average of two different teams. Three home matches at 0.33 scored and 0.67 conceded; three away at 1.33 and 3.33. The 0.83/2.00 season line describes neither. Last season the same club ran 1.63/1.26 at home and 0.68/1.37 away — a split of the same size pointing the opposite way — so the venue effect is large, unstable, and computed here over three matches per venue.
  • Rayo’s defensive collapse is a loan-market artefact as much as a tactical one. Deep completions conceded have risen from 6.26 to 11.83 and shots faced from 12.24 to 17.67, behind Kumbulla and Sadick on loan and Audero on loan covering a goalkeeper out until January. Their most expensive permanent signing, Vertrouwd at €1.5m, is injured until October. The 2.50 conceded per match is real and six matches deep, but the unit producing it has been together for six weeks.
  • The referee has no record worth the name. Three Primera matches, no 2025–26 top-flight season, no history with either club. His 6.67 cards per match is the most extreme figure in the division and it rests on three appointments; it could be who he is, or it could be three matches. The Discipline row is rated well-suited because the collision between a high-card official and two low-card clubs is documented on both sides — not because 6.67 is a settled rate.
  • The division has moved more than either club has. LaLiga is running at 3.07 goals per match this season against 2.69 last, on 60 matches against 380. Where this card uses the 26/27 divisional line — in the totals and BTTS rows especially — it is leaning on a sixth of a season.
  • Most sensitive: the Asian handicap and 1X2 — both need a margin from two clubs whose six results are either one-goal or three-plus with nothing between, and both would move on whether Osasuna’s home or away version turns up. Least sensitive: BTTS and corners — the first because Rayo’s 6-from-6 is corroborated by xG against, shots faced, deep completions and a loan-built defence all at once; the second because both clubs’ corner rates survived a change of coach almost unchanged.

  • The single fact that would most change which family I choose is Orellana Cid’s real card rate. At 6.67 per match against a divisional 4.48 he is the most extreme input on this card and he has three matches of record. What would settle it is either a fourth and fifth appointment at this level, or a published LaLiga Hypermotion record for his seasons below — neither of which exists in any source reached today. Until one does, the discipline row is a documented collision rather than a documented rate.
