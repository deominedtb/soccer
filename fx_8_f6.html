A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted, and on a card dominated by reconstruction this fixture is the inversion of it: the lower-placed side is the settled one and the higher-placed side is the one in transition. Villarreal finished third in LaLiga last season under Marcelino, who announced in early May 2026 that he would leave at the end of the campaign; Iñigo Pérez arrived from Rayo Vallecano to replace him, and the summer has so far produced one confirmed signing. Alavés finished in the lower half after changing manager mid-season — Eduardo Coudet until 3 March 2026, Quique Sánchez Flores from that date — and have spent this summer deliberately doing as little as possible. The structural headline is that Villarreal’s league position describes a team that no longer exists in the dugout, while Alavés’s describes one that has been kept intact on purpose.

  • Alavés — turnover deliberately low, and a manager who now gets a full pre-season. Quique Sánchez Flores continues after arriving mid-season, and for the first time can shape the side from the start rather than inherit it in March. The club has been openly cautious in the market, changing as few pieces as possible to keep last season’s block together, with the coach having praised the squad since he arrived in Vitoria. Out: Jon Guridi to Sevilla, the one significant subtraction. In: Mikel Rodríguez, a young Zubieta product signed permanently as Guridi’s replacement; Miguel Rodríguez, a Galician forward from Utrecht with recent LaLiga experience and the club’s largest outlay of the window; and Nicolás Valentini, an Argentine defender from Fiorentina, to shore up the back line. Mariano Díaz, 33, signed as a free agent a year ago, has scored twice off the bench already this season and may start.
  • Villarreal — manager change, and a window still unfinished. Marcelino left at the end of 2025–26 after taking the club to third; Iñigo Pérez came in from Rayo Vallecano. The window has been close to inert. The one senior arrival is goalkeeper Péter Gulácsi, signed from RB Leipzig on 2 August 2026 for a reported €1.5m — €1m fixed plus variables — on a two-year deal, ending eleven seasons in Germany. He is 36, with 58 Hungary caps and 41 Champions League appearances, and he arrived because the position had emptied: Diego Conde left for Betis and Arnau Tenas for Mallorca, with Luiz Júnior now his competition. Beyond him the only other name is Andero Kaares, who appears to have been signed to continue developing in the lower categories. Out: Ramón Terrats, the largest sale at €2.5m, and Alfonso Pedraza. The consequential absence is in midfield — the sporting department has not replaced the departures of Dani Parejo and Thomas Partey. A Ligue 1 striker was reported as the intended final addition and remains unconfirmed, and an unnamed Villarreal centre-back had a verbal agreement to go on loan to Osasuna before an Italian club’s interest stalled it. The Spanish window is open until 1 September, four days after this match. Gap: no centre-back or defensive-midfield arrival is confirmed in either direction, and the centre-back involved in the stalled Osasuna loan is unnamed in these sources.
  • Suspensions / absences. Iñigo Pérez has acknowledged fitness concerns over Gerard Moreno — the club’s vice-captain and long-standing number 7 — and over Sergi, with both to be assessed before kickoff. No Alavés absences were reported, which is an absence of report rather than a report of full availability. No suspensions surfaced, and no referee is named in the odds file.
  • Sample. Two matches each. Alavés beat Getafe 3–0 on 15 August and drew 1–1 with Rayo Vallecano, giving them four points, third place, four goals scored and one conceded. Villarreal have drawn both of theirs 2–2 — away at Racing Santander, the side that opens this card, and then against Atlético Madrid — for two points and ninth. The longer record at this ground is one-sided: Alavés have won ten of seventeen meetings at Mendizorroza to Villarreal’s four, and Villarreal have not won here since January 2020. Separately and more durably, Villarreal have failed to keep a clean sheet in sixteen consecutive matches.

B · Style & early-season reality

Alavés are the most legible side on this card, and it is a deliberate achievement rather than an accident. A mid-season managerial appointment usually means a compromised following summer; instead the club backed Sánchez Flores with continuity, keeping the block and adding narrowly where it had lost something — Mikel Rodríguez for Guridi, Valentini into the back line, Miguel Rodríguez as the one attacking investment. The early evidence matches the intent: four scored and one conceded across a 3–0 against Getafe and a 1–1 with Rayo, which is the profile of a compact side that defends its box and takes what the game offers. Mariano Díaz scoring twice from the bench at 33 is the small detail that says the squad has depth in the one department it invested in. The caveat is honest and simple: two matches, both against opponents from the same part of the table, and a coach who has never before had this group from the first day of pre-season.

Villarreal are the harder read, and the reason is not quality. Third place is a genuinely strong platform, but it belongs to Marcelino, and Iñigo Pérez has been in the job for a matter of weeks with a squad that has lost Terrats and Pedraza and gained a goalkeeper. Two 2–2 draws — one at a newly promoted Racing side, one against Atlético Madrid — are not obviously bad results, but they share a shape: Villarreal scoring twice and conceding twice against wildly different opposition. That is consistent with the sixteen-match run without a clean sheet, and the significance of that run is precisely that it did not stop when the manager changed. A defensive record that survives a change of coach is telling you something about personnel and habit rather than about instructions. Signing Gulácsi suggests the club reached the same conclusion; whether one goalkeeper resolves it, with the striker they actually wanted still unsigned four days from deadline, is exactly what two matches cannot show.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Alavés are the one side on this slate that deliberately did not rebuild, which makes their two matches unusually informative by this card’s standards — but they are still two matches, both against opponents from the same region of the table, and Sánchez Flores has never before had this squad from the first day of a pre-season. Continuity raises the value of a small sample; it does not make it a large one.
  • Villarreal’s third place is Marcelino’s result and Marcelino has gone. What replaced him is a new manager two league matches in and a window that has barely moved: one senior arrival, a 36-year-old goalkeeper at €1.5m, brought in to fill a position vacated by two other departures rather than to improve the team. Dani Parejo and Thomas Partey have left midfield unreplaced, the Ligue 1 striker reported as the final addition has not been confirmed, and a centre-back’s loan exit is stalled rather than settled. A side that finished third has changed its manager and thinned its squad in the same summer, which is a different proposition from a side that finished third.
  • The sixteen matches without a clean sheet is the most durable number in this fixture and should be treated differently from the rest of the evidence, because it did not reset when the coach changed. Records that survive a managerial change describe personnel and habit; records that do not survive one describe instructions.
  • Contradiction, recorded not resolved. One source gives Alavés’s 2025–26 finish as 14th while citing table data showing 19th on 36 points. Nineteenth would mean relegation, which cannot be squared with their playing in LaLiga this season, so the 14th reading is the one consistent with everything else — but the discrepancy is logged rather than quietly resolved. Note also that Alavés used two managers last season, so their 2025–26 numbers are themselves two different teams averaged together.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total and cards — the first because Alavés’s defensive record rests on two soft fixtures, the second because a league-average line with no named referee and no per-team split is priced on culture rather than on this match. Least sensitive: BTTS and the handicap, both of which rest on records that span the managerial change or the venue history — sixteen matches without a clean sheet, and no Villarreal win here since January 2020.
