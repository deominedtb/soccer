B · Style & early-season reality

Cambridge are the best-functioning promoted side on this board, and the reason is visible in the structure rather than the results. Harris is the only promotion-winning manager on the slate still in post, the club addressed goalkeeper and centre-back with permanent signings (Breeze from Carlisle, Bauer from Wimbledon) rather than leaving the spine as it was, and it topped up the attacking end with four loans from higher divisions. What that has produced is a side that trades goals rather than controlling them: nine scored and eight conceded in five, and both teams scoring in every single match — 3–2, 1–1, 1–2, 2–1, 2–2. There is no 0–0 and no clean sheet anywhere in the sample. Two complications land today: Bauer, the summer centre-back, is out, and Harris is serving a touchline ban after his red card at Burton. Gap: no xG or shot data was obtainable, so this reading comes from scorelines, the ledger and the absence list.

Reading are the harder side to characterise because their four matches contain no middle. A 4–3 defeat, a 0–0, a 5–0 and a 3–1 is a set of extremes: seven goals, then none, then five, then four. The recruitment explains the upward half — Earthy, Lisbie and Brown all scored against Blackpool, and Jack Marriott already has 5 — and the 0–0 with Wimbledon shows the same side is capable of a blank. Richardson is expected to keep the eleven that won last weekend, with only Sean Patton a concern, so this is the rare fixture on this board where the visiting side's availability is both known and close to full. The unresolved piece is structural rather than tactical: their documented season is four matches against everyone else's five or six, and that gap could not be closed from the sources reached. Gap: no xG, foul or card data for either club, and no away-form split for Reading beyond the Blackpool result being a home win.
