A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Torino finished 12th in 2025–26 — 12 wins, 9 draws, 17 defeats, 44 scored, 63 conceded, 45 points; only relegated Pisa (71) conceded more. Roma finished 3rd — 23 wins, 4 draws, 11 defeats, 59 scored, 31 conceded, 73 points; only Como (29) conceded fewer. The structural headline is continuity against its absence. Gian Piero Gasperini is into his second season at Roma with contract renewals for Dybala (to 2027) and Cristante (to 2028) and a window that crossed €100m. Torino have had three head coaches since last summer: Marco Baroni was dismissed on 23 February 2026 with the division’s worst defence at that point (47 conceded in 26 matches), Roberto D’Aversa took over to June and left by mutual consent on 12 June 2026, and Ignazio Abate was appointed the same day, starting 1 July on a contract to June 2028, from Juve Stabia in Serie B.

B · Style & early-season reality

Roma under Gasperini are a volume side and the early numbers say so without ambiguity: 64% possession, 23.67 shots and 2.65 xG per league match against 0.89 conceded, two clean sheets in three. The summer bought depth into that shape rather than a new one — Castro and a permanent Malen at the top, Koulierakis and Balerdi into a back three, Molina at wing-back, de Roon beside Cristante, Mora behind the striker — and last season’s 31 conceded was produced under the same coach. The Champions League is the variable: a 1–1 in Istanbul on Thursday, a reported knee injury to Koné from that match, and four days to Monday. Gap: no Roma foul, card or corner series, and no 2025–26 xG.

Torino are in their first two months under Abate, whose Juve Stabia sides used a 3–4–1–2; previews for tonight give 3–5–2 or 3–4–1–2. The early numbers describe a low-volume side that defends a lot: 8.33 shots and 1.03 xG per match against 1.88 xG conceded, all three league matches over 2.5 with both teams scoring. The one win came with a different profile — 60% possession, 557 passes at 89%, seven shots to Fiorentina’s thirteen — while home possession so far is 41%. The rebuild replaced Maripán and most of last season’s back line with loans and cheap permanents, and the defensive record they inherit is 63 conceded; whether a new coach and new centre-backs have changed it is evidenced by three matches and by a CV from Serie B. Gap: no Torino foul, card or corner series in either season.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Torino’s 63 conceded belongs to two previous coaches. Baroni’s 47 in 26 and D’Aversa’s finish were made with Maripán and a back line that has largely gone; Abate’s three league matches (5 conceded, 1.88 xG against) are the only evidence for the new version.
  • Roma’s league numbers were made before the Champions League started. All three league matches preceded Thursday’s 1–1 in Istanbul; tonight is the first league match after a European trip, with Koné reported injured.
  • Torino’s attack changes shape tonight. Adams, scorer of the only league winner, is out and Simeone doubtful, so the four league goals were scored by a front line that is not available.
  • Individual Roma scorelines conflict between sources. Aggregates (10–1) agree; any rate that needs per-match scores beyond over 2.5 and BTTS is unsafe.
  • Most sensitive: BTTS and halves — one depends on a Torino front line nobody has confirmed, the other has no data. Least sensitive: Roma’s team goals, where shots, xG, possession and goals all agree across three matches.
