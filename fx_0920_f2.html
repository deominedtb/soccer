A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Málaga are promoted and are back in the top flight after an absence long enough that no part of the squad’s record is a LaLiga record. They finished 4th in LaLiga 2 on 73 points from 42 matches and came up through the play-off, not automatically — Racing Santander (82) and Deportivo (77) took the direct places, both of which are also in this division now and both of which Málaga have already faced. Across the full 46-match second-tier campaign including the play-off they were 23-12-11 with 79 scored and 54 conceded. Getafe finished 7th in LaLiga on 51 points — 15 wins, 6 draws, 17 defeats, 32 scored, 38 conceded, a record whose shape matters more than its position: 32 goals in 38 matches and a 26.3% over-2.5 rate against a divisional 50.0%, the extreme end of the division on that measure. The structural headline is that neither club changed anything. José Bordalás remains at Getafe and Juan Francisco “Juanfran” Funes remains at Málaga, the coach who won the promotion — and Málaga, the promoted side, spent nothing at all while Getafe spent about €19m gross on fourteen arrivals. On a slate where nearly every other fixture turns on a new coach or a rebuilt spine, this one turns on two unchanged dugouts rather than two unchanged squads — Getafe moved fourteen players each way — and on the fact that both clubs have started badly: Getafe 17th on 5 points, Málaga bottom on 3.

  • Getafe — turnover high in bodies and net-negative in money; no managerial change, Bordalás renewed for two more seasons. Centre-forward, and the correction that matters: in — Enes Ünal, returning from Bournemouth on a free transfer on 13 August 2026, permanently, to 2029. No fee was paid because Bournemouth still owed Getafe money from the 2023 deal that took Ünal to England for €16.5m; Bournemouth retain a 20% sell-on and up to €1.5m in bonuses. Also in: Martín Satriano from Lyon, about €6.5m, so the club signed two centre-forwards. Defence: in — Zaid Romero from Club Brugge, €5m, and Johan Mojica; out — Juan Berrocal free to Málaga, a defender who left for today’s opponent and may face his old club. Midfield: in — Mario Martín from Real Madrid, €3.5m, Ramón Terrats from Villarreal, €2.5m (a club on this slate) and Sebastián Boselli from River Plate, €1.5m; out — Luis Milla to Como for €6m, also a club on this slate, and Mauro Arambarri. Fourteen in and fourteen out, for a net outlay of roughly €5m — a busier and more expensive window than the two-signing summer first reported here, and one that re-signed the striker rather than selling him. Bordalás renewed for two further seasons.
  • Málaga — turnover medium in bodies, almost nil in money; no managerial change, Funes continuing. Juanfran Funes, who won the play-off, had his contract renewed along with sporting director Loren Juarros, so the promotion staff is intact. Every arrival was a free transfer or a loan. Defence, where the recruitment was concentrated: Fernando Calero free from Espanyol, José Salinas on loan from Espanyol with an option, Adam Aznou on loan from Everton with no option, and Juan Berrocal free from Getafe. Midfield: Carlos Dotor from Celta, fee undisclosed, Jens Cajuste on loan from Napoli with an option, and Pablo Martínez free from Levante. Wide: Juan Cruz on loan from Leganés, loan fee under €500,000. Out: Darko Brašanac and Josué Dorrio at contract end, Juanpe Jiménez to Gamba Osaka for an undisclosed fee, Dani Sánchez by mutual rescission. No centre-forward and no goalkeeper was signed. A side promoted on 79 goals has come up and added nobody to score them, with the forward who did it, Chupe, the subject of reported interest at around €8m that did not become a transfer.
  • Suspensions / absences. Getafe are missing five and have one suspended: Christantus Uche, Kiko, Abdelkabir Abqar, Juanmi and Ramon Terrats are injured, and Mario Martín is suspended. Málaga are missing five, three of them long-term and two of them summer arrivals: Diego Murillo (ACL), Julen Lobete (ACL) and Aaron Ochoa (meniscus) are long-term; Jens Cajuste (muscular) and Fernando Calero (broken rib) are both out — a loan signing and a free signing, two of the four defensive additions. Adam Aznou, a third defensive arrival, is doubtful with a stomach virus. Málaga therefore arrive with three of the four defenders they recruited this summer unavailable or doubtful, in front of a back line that has conceded 11 in six.

B · Style & early-season reality

Getafe are the most stable object on this slate and also the most extreme. Possession 39.5% this season against 40.1% last — a difference of half a point — and the most fouls per team of any side in the division in both seasons, 15.21 last and 14.50 now against a divisional 12.58 and 12.41. Bordalás is in his job, the squad is broadly the squad, and the method has not moved: fewest shots (9.26 last season, 10.33 now, against a divisional 12.1), fewest deep completions (2.87 and 3.17), lowest xG (0.89 and 0.85). The one thing that has changed is the defensive side, and it has changed a lot: xG conceded has risen from 1.27 to 1.69 per match while the press has loosened from 10.59 PPDA to 12.04. A side built to make matches small is still making them small at one end — 0.50 goals scored per match, 16.7% of matches over 2.5 — while conceding at a rate its structure was designed to prevent. Its over-2.5 rate has now been far below the divisional line in two consecutive seasons under the same coach, 26.3% against 50.0% and 16.7% against 56.2%, which is the single most repeatable fact on this card.

Málaga’s numbers are a promoted side’s numbers and the step up is visible in every column that exists. In LaLiga 2 they scored 1.72 per match and conceded 1.17, had both teams score in 60.9% of matches and went over 2.5 in 56.5%, all above a second-tier line of 2.63 goals per game. In LaLiga they have 0.50 scored and 1.83 conceded, and an xG line of 0.83 for and 2.13 against — the worst defensive xG figure of any club carded on this slate. The detail that makes the attacking figure worse than it looks is the penalty share: non-penalty xG of 0.58 against a total of 0.83 means roughly 30% of everything they create comes from the spot, and the open-play figure is under 0.6 per match. PPDA has gone from a pressing second-tier side to 14.09, the loosest press of any club on this card, and deep completions to 1.83 per match, the lowest. Possession is 49.9%, which is the one number that looks normal; they are having the ball without doing anything with it. The evidence that this is the division rather than a slump is that it is uniform — every attacking series is at or near the bottom of the league simultaneously — but it is six matches, three of them against Atlético, Real Madrid and Villarreal, and no 2025–26 xG exists to compare it against.

Getafe’s home record is the only asymmetry — 1-2-0 at the Coliseum against 0-0-3 away, unbeaten in six home matches counting last season — and it rests on three home matches this season producing three goals. LaLiga’s home-win rate is 43.8% against 48.9% last season. A meeting of the 17th and 20th placed sides, both scoring 0.50 per match, is a fixture where the result is the least determined thing on the card.
Asian handicap / DNBDangerous
Margins need a distribution and these two do not have one. Getafe’s six league results are −3, +1, −1, 0, 0, −1 — four matches decided by one goal or none, one rout, and a total of three goals scored all season. Málaga’s are −2, 0, −4, 0, 0, −2: three draws and three defeats of two or more, with nothing in between. Between them they have produced a single margin of exactly two in twelve matches. The handicap needs to price a gap between two sides whose entire combined output is six goals in twelve matches.
Totals (O/U goals)Well-suited — the best-evidenced family on this card by a distance
This is the rare case where a team rate has survived a change of division, a change of season and a divisional baseline moving the other way. Getafe went over 2.5 in 26.3% of 38 matches last season against a divisional 50.0%, and in 16.7% of six this season against a divisional 56.2% — under the same coach, the same squad, the same 39–40% possession and the same bottom-of-the-division shot count. Their xG is 0.85 and Málaga’s non-penalty xG is 0.58; Málaga have not scored twice in a match all season and have been blanked in three of six; Getafe have scored three goals in six league matches. The counted rate, the modelled rate, the shot volume and the deep-completion count all point the same way on both sides, across two seasons on one side. The one genuine counterweight is stated rather than hidden: LaLiga as a division is at 3.05 goals per game against 2.69, so the baseline is moving against these two clubs, and Málaga’s 1.83 conceded is a real concession rate.
BTTSOK — strong on Getafe, unreadable on Málaga’s attack
Getafe’s side is as firm as the totals line: 28.9% BTTS across 38 matches last season against a divisional 56.6%, 33.3% across six this season against 56.2%, and a failed-to-score rate of 44.7% and 50.0% — this is a club that regularly removes one half of the market by itself. The obstacle is that the same fact cuts both ways here: Getafe fail to score half the time, and Málaga have failed to score in three of six, so the market resolves on whether either side manages one. Málaga’s 0.58 non-penalty xG makes their scoring genuinely hard to read, and 30% of their expected output arriving from penalties puts it partly in the hands of an official who gives 0.55 per match. Well evidenced on the pattern, dependent on a single low-probability event on one side.
Team goalsSplit — Getafe-side readable, Málaga-side penalty-dependent

Málaga are readable in total and not in composition: 0.83 xG of which 0.25 is penalties, the loosest press on the card at 14.09 PPDA, 1.83 deep completions, no centre-forward signed after promotion, and the forward who scored the promotion goals still at the club but reportedly valued elsewhere. Discipline (cards, fouls)Well-suited — the strongest structural case on this card after totals
Every input points the same way and each is independently sourced. Getafe commit more fouls than any other side carded on this slate: 15.21 per match last season and 14.50 this, against a divisional 12.58 and 12.41, and they take 3.17 cards per match this season against a divisional 2.22 per team. Last season they took 103 yellows and 8 reds in 38 matches. The method producing that is intact: same coach, same 39.5% possession, same low-block-and-foul structure. The official reinforces rather than dilutes it — Alberola Rojas is running at 5.00 cards per match across three matches this season, above his own 4.30 and above the divisional 4.44, and in the one previous Getafe match of his in the record Getafe took four of the five cards. Málaga are the quieter side at 2.67 and 12.3, but their second-tier card rate of 2.37 was set in a division that books at 5.31 per match, so it understates what they will meet here. The one caveat worth stating: LaLiga is the one division on this slate whose card rate has not fallen this season, 4.44 against 4.52.
CornersDangerous

Getafe read 3.83 for and 4.67 against, Málaga 5.00 and 5.33, against a divisional 4.82 — all four numbers within a corner of the baseline and of each other. Málaga’s second-tier figures (4.33 for, 4.63 against against a LaLiga 2 line of 4.74) say the same thing from the division below. A side taking 10.33 shots a match and completing 3.17 passes deep is not a corner-generating structure, and nothing in either window changed the shape that produces them. With six matches per side and no series more than a corner from its baseline, there is nothing here to read.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Málaga’s entire 2025–26 record is a second-tier record and half of it does not exist. 1.72 goals per match and 60.9% BTTS were produced in a division scoring 2.63 per game and booking at 5.31 — and Understat does not cover LaLiga 2, so there is no xG, PPDA or deep-completion baseline to set against their current 0.83/2.13. Their six-match top-flight sample is the only modelled evidence that exists for this club.
  • Three of Málaga’s four defensive signings are unavailable or doubtful. Calero (broken rib) and Cajuste (muscular) are out, Aznou is doubtful; Salinas and Berrocal are the two still standing. A promoted side that recruited almost exclusively in defence is fielding a defence largely without the recruitment, having already conceded 11 in six.
  • Getafe’s low-scoring record is the most repeatable fact on this card and also the oldest. It was set by a squad that has since re-signed Enes Ünal and added Martín Satriano at about €6.5m. The rate has if anything intensified — 26.3% to 16.7% over 2.5 — but the reading rests on continuity of method rather than continuity of personnel, and the defensive half of that method has visibly weakened (1.27 to 1.69 xG conceded).
  • The divisional baseline is moving against both clubs. LaLiga is at 3.05 goals per game against 2.69 last season and 56.2% over 2.5 against 50.0%, on 64 matches. Every low reading on this card is low against a line that has itself risen, which strengthens the club-level finding and weakens any attempt to lean on the divisional number.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and the first-half result — two bottom-four sides with six goals between them in twelve matches give a result no series on this card speaks to. Least sensitive: Totals and Discipline — both rest on Getafe rates that have held across two seasons, 44 matches and an unchanged coach, and both are reinforced rather than contradicted by the second side and by the official.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals is more robust than 1X2 here because Getafe’s scoring rate has survived everything that would normally break a rate. 26.3% over 2.5 in 38 matches became 16.7% in six, across a summer, a striker sale and a divisional baseline that rose from 50.0% to 56.2% — with possession moving 40.1% to 39.5% and shots 9.26 to 10.33. The result record over the same period is one win in six.
  • Discipline is more robust than Corners because the foul count has a named, unchanged cause and the corner count has none. Getafe’s 15.21 and 14.50 fouls per match come from Bordalás’s low block at 39.5% possession, still in place; their corner figures (3.83 for, 4.67 against) sit within a corner of a 4.82 divisional line and have no structural driver in either window.
  • First-half goals is more robust than the first-half result because one has 44 matches behind it and the other has none. Getafe’s 0.37+0.39 last season and 0.17+0.33 this season are two independent sub-one-goal halves under the same coach. Who is ahead at the break is the 1X2 question asked of a smaller sample.
  • BTTS is less robust than Totals despite resting on the same facts, because it depends on a single event rather than a rate. Getafe’s 28.9% and 33.3% and Málaga’s three blanks in six are the same evidence — but 30% of Málaga’s expected output is penalties, and the referee gives 0.55 per match against a divisional 0.35, so one decision by Alberola Rojas resolves a large share of the market.
  • The single fact that would most change which family reads best is whether Málaga field a recognised centre-forward, and specifically whether Chupe starts. They signed no striker after promoting on 79 goals, their non-penalty xG is 0.58, and three of their four defensive signings are out — so the shape of their team sheet decides whether the Totals and BTTS readings rest on two low-scoring sides or on one low-scoring side and one that cannot field an attack.
