A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Albania are relegated from League B: fourth in Group B1 in 2024–25 on 7 points, 4–6 (W2 D1 L3, behind Czechia, Ukraine and Georgia). Their division history reads C/B/B/C, so this is a return to League C after two editions in B; they won their 2020–21 League C group, one point ahead of Belarus. In World Cup qualifying they were second in Group K on 14 points, 7–5 (W4 D2 L2, behind England, ahead of Serbia). They took four 1–0 or 3–0 wins and lost only to England, twice. In the play-off semi-final in Warsaw on 26 March they led through Arbër Hoxha (42′) and lost 1–2 (Lewandowski 63′, Zieliński 73′). Belarus stay in League C: third in Group C3 on 7 points, 3–4 (W1 D4 L1, behind Northern Ireland and Bulgaria, ahead of Luxembourg). Their history reads C/C/C/C. In World Cup qualifying they were fourth of four in Group C on 2 points, 4–17 (W0 D2 L4, including 0–6 against Denmark and 1–5 in Piraeus); they finished with 2–2 in Copenhagen and 0–0 against Greece. The structural headline is that both coaches are new and neither has taken a competitive match. Every rate in the strip was set under Sylvinho and Carlos Alós. Inside those records, 11 of Albania’s 15 competitive matches and 8 of Belarus’s 12 were against sides now in Leagues A and B. Against the rest, Albania went W3 D1, 6–1 (Latvia and Andorra), and Belarus W1 D3, 3–2 (Bulgaria and Luxembourg, all in autumn 2024). In the 17 League C matches of 2022–23 and 2024–25 in which a side just relegated from League B hosted a side that had stayed in League C, the home side went W6 D5 L6, with 3.06 goals per match, BTTS in 58.8% and the corner count to the host in 12. Belarus are in that line once, 1–1 in Slovakia in 2022–23 (baselines_0926.md). Format: group winners are promoted, runners-up play the fourth-placed League B sides, and no side is relegated from League C (baselines_0926.md, from f2). Belarus play their home matches in Budapest behind closed doors (Wikipedia’s League C page). Head to head: seven meetings, Albania W3 D2 L2, 10–10: 2–2 in Minsk (2006), 2–4 in Tirana (2007), 0–2 in Minsk (2010), 1–0 in Tirana (2011), 0–0 in Antalya (friendly, 2013), then 2–0 in Minsk and 3–2 in Tirana in the 2020–21 League C group (GitHub match record; ESPN has the two 2020 meetings, and Newsport gives the same count and goals). B · Style & early-season reality

Sylvinho’s Albania were a low-event side: a 4-2-3-1 in 11 of 15, over 2.5 in 4 of 15, and seven clean sheets. Their shape moved with the opponent. Against League A and B sides they had 41.7% of the ball; against Latvia and Andorra they had 69.8%, took 13.25 shots to 5.00 and put in 21.2 crosses per match. Metaratings describes them as a low-to-mid-block side that concedes the initiative and plays on vertical counters and set pieces. ESPN’s figures support that against stronger opposition, not against the weaker sides. They foul little and are rarely booked: 9.87 fouls committed to 14.87 suffered, and 1.47 cards per match (1.00 at home, one card per 9.00 fouls). The summer changes are in goal and in the dugout. The goalkeeper behind all seven clean sheets is out, and so are Laçi (14 starts, second only to Asllani) and Ajeti, the most-used centre-back (10). Maran’s evidence is two home friendlies. In the first, ESPN records a 4-3-1-2, 59.0% of the ball, 13 shots with none on target and 24 crosses in a 0–1 defeat; the second has no box score. He calls Belarus “very compact”. He says it is not important always to have the ball but what Albania do with it, and that June taught them about reacting when they lose it (Newsport). Three predicted elevens give three different shapes.

Alós’s Belarus defended deep in a back three or five. They used a 5-3-2 five times, a 3-4-2-1 four times and a 3-4-3 once. Their only back fours were the 1–5 in Piraeus and the 1–2 at Hampden. Over 12 they had 36.9% of the ball and were outshot 9.00 to 16.67, with 2.75 shots on target to 6.75. They foul more than they are fouled (15.42 to 13.33) and are booked 2.58 times per match, 3.33 away. All seven of their goals came after half-time, and they were level or behind at every half-time. Against Bulgaria and Luxembourg in 2024 they had 40.7% of the ball but won the corner count in three of four. Those four matches carried 6.75 cards per match. Goncharenko’s shape has no match evidence. ESPN records no formation for any of his four friendlies. Sports Mole’s predicted back four includes three defenders with three competitive starts between them in the window (Malkevich 1, Shevchenko 0, Begunov 2). Goncharenko singles out Albania’s attack, naming Daku and Asllani, and says Belarus must “defend well but at the same time attack”. He also says his side has “very good balance in defence and attack” (Metaratings). Those are statements, not matches, and the defenders who played the back three are largely not in the squad.
