A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, and neither is new to this division — Leeds came up in 2025–26 and this is their second consecutive Premier League season. Leeds finished 14th in 2025–26 on 47 points — 11 wins, 14 draws, 13 defeats, 49 scored, 56 conceded — with one of the widest venue splits on this board: 9-5-5 at Elland Road against 2-9-8 away. Crystal Palace finished 15th on 45 points — 11 wins, 12 draws, 15 defeats, 41 scored, 51 conceded — and won the Conference League. The structural headline is that one club kept its coach and rebuilt around him while the other lost both its manager and its most valuable player in the same summer. Leeds did not change head coach. Crystal Palace did: Oliver Glasner left for Nottingham Forest in July 2026 and a replacement was appointed in June, and the club then sold Eberechi Eze to Arsenal for £60m rising to £67m — a record fee received by Crystal Palace. Leeds spent heavily in the other direction. Two of the players involved moved between clubs that play elsewhere on this slate: Harry Wilson arrived free from Fulham and James Trafford from Manchester City for £40m.

B · Style & early-season reality

Leeds have pressed harder and made their matches smaller, which is an unusual combination and is visible in four columns. PPDA has fallen from 12.06 to 9.60 — a 20% increase in pressing intensity — while possession has dropped from 45.8% to 43.8%, shots are flat at 12.00 against 12.58, and deep completions are flat at 5.00 against 5.29. They press more, hold less, and create the same amount. The output is where the change sits: 1.75 goals per match against 1.29 from an xG of 1.54 against 1.57 — the same model, better finishing — and 0.75 conceded against 1.47 from an xG conceded of 1.32 against 1.48. Both ends are running ahead of the model, which is what an unbeaten start off four matches usually means. The counted shape is emphatically low-scoring: three of the four league matches finished 1–0, 1–1 and 1–1, and only the 4–1 against Newcastle cleared 2.5. Against that sits a 3–6 at Chelsea in the cup, which is outside every league rate above.

Palace have been taken apart in exactly the places the summer emptied. xG conceded has risen from 1.58 to 2.23, goals conceded from 1.34 to 2.75, and first-half goals conceded from 0.66 to 1.50 — they have shipped six first-half goals in four matches. The pressing figure explains part of it: PPDA has gone from 13.44 to 16.36, the second-loosest press on this board, on possession down from 45.9% to 41.9%. At the other end the model has barely moved (1.64 to 1.61 xG) but the delivery mechanism has collapsed: corners won have halved, from 4.21 to 2.25, the second-lowest figure on this board, and shots are down from 11.63 to 10.25. That is what selling a £60m creator and a £52m centre-back, changing coach, and then losing Mateta, Henderson, Riad and Disasi looks like in the data. The 4–0 against Lech Poznań on Thursday is the counter-example and it is three days old and in a different competition.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • There is no referee evidence on this card at all. Adam Herczeg is making his Premier League debut, so the discipline row rests on two team rates and nothing else. Every other fixture on this slate reads its cards row against nine to twenty-nine matches of officiating record.
  • Palace are missing four of the positions the spec weights. Mateta (centre-forward), Henderson (goalkeeper), Riad (centre-back) and Disasi (centre-back, suspended three matches). Their 2.23 xG conceded was accumulated partly without them; today’s defence is not the one that produced last season’s 51 goals against either, after Lacroix was sold.
  • Both clubs are finishing above their models at both ends on four matches. Leeds score 1.75 from 1.54 xG and concede 0.75 from 1.32; Palace score 1.50 from 1.61. Three of those four gaps favour Leeds, and none has more than four matches behind it.
  • Leeds’s low-scoring profile contradicts their own prior season. 25.0% over 2.5 this season against 55.3% last, on unchanged shots, xG and deep completions. Nothing in the style data explains it, which usually means finishing and opposition rather than method.
  • Most sensitive: Discipline, on a debut referee with no record, and 1X2, where Leeds’s position and Leeds’s model disagree. Least sensitive: BTTS — 75% on both sides, a 0% blank rate for Leeds and a 0% clean-sheet rate for Palace, both continuing rather than contradicting last season.
