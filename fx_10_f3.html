A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted; Sunderland came up in 2025 and then produced one of the more unusual seasons the division has seen. They finished 7th on 54 points — 38 matches, 14 wins, 12 draws, 12 defeats — and qualified for the Europa League league phase, their first European football in five decades, while scoring 42 goals, conceding 48 and finishing with a goal difference of −6. A European qualifier with a negative goal difference and 1.11 goals a game is not a normal object, and it tells you exactly what Régis Le Bris built: a side that wins and draws tight matches and almost never wins a loose one. Fulham finished 11th on 52 points, two behind, with 15 wins, only 7 draws and 16 defeats, 47 scored and 51 conceded for −4 — the same points band reached by the opposite method, a high-variance team that either won or lost. The structural headline is that these two clubs then went in opposite directions again: Sunderland qualified for Europe and spent a net £5.7m while losing eighteen players including their leading striker and their goalkeeper, and Fulham changed manager, spent £69.5m and banked precisely nothing, because every single departure was a free, a loan or an undisclosed fee.

  • Sunderland — turnover very high, and it is almost all outgoing. No manager change: Régis Le Bris stays. Three arrivals. Dayann Méthalie, left-back, from Toulouse at £24m; Jules Ahoka, central midfielder, from Royal Antwerp at £8.6m; Thomas Meunier, right-back, free from Lille. That is £32.6m, all of it on full-backs and a midfielder. Against that, eighteen departures. The centre-forward position was emptied: Eliezer Mayenda sold to Rennes at £18.9m, Ahmed Abdullahi to Eyupspor and Nazariy Rusyn free to Karpaty Lviv, with no centre-forward signed to replace any of them. The goalkeeping position lost Anthony Patterson to Wrexham at £8m and Matty Young on loan to Huddersfield, again with no incoming goalkeeper listed. Three centre-backs or defensive players left — Leo Hjelde to Mechelen, Jensen Seelt on loan to Swansea City, Zak Johnson to York City — alongside Dennis Cirkin free to QPR, Arthur Masuaku free to Konyaspor, Timothée Pembélé to Le Havre, and free-agent exits for Dan Neil, Bertrand Traoré, Lutsharel Geertruida and Niall Huggins. Income £26.9m against £32.6m spend, a net outlay of £5.7m. A club that has just qualified for Europe for the first time since the 1970s has responded by adding two full-backs and subtracting its striker, its goalkeeper and sixteen others.
  • Fulham — turnover high, manager new, and the income column is zero. Álvaro Arbeloa was appointed on 7 July 2026 on a three-year contract, replacing Marco Silva, who left for Benfica. Arbeloa arrives directly from Real Madrid, where he took over as interim head coach after Xabi Alonso’s departure in January 2026 and led the club for the final 28 matches of the season, finishing second in La Liga and going out of the Champions League at the quarter-final stage to Bayern Munich — which means the two managers who split last season at the Bernabéu are now both in this division and have already played each other, Alonso’s Chelsea beating Arbeloa’s Fulham 3–2 in matchweek one. He brought a Real Madrid forward with him: Gonzalo García, centre-forward, at £34.3m, alongside Shea Charles, central midfielder, from Southampton at £30m and Jonah Kusi-Asare, centre-forward, from Bayern Munich at £5.2m. Total spend £69.5m, total income £0m. The exits were all free or undisclosed: Raúl Jiménez free to Wolves, Steven Benda free to Nottingham Forest, Harry Wilson free to Leeds United, Issa Diop and Saša Lukić both to Ipswich Town for undisclosed fees, Harvey Araujo to Burton Albion, plus four loans out. Two centre-backs left with none signed; the holding midfielder Lukić left and the midfield replacement is a No. 8 rather than a No. 6.
  • Suspensions / absences. The most consequential single fact in this section: Dayann Méthalie, Sunderland’s £24m signing and their largest outlay of the window, is suspended for this fixture — a ban carried over from disciplinary action in France, not incurred in England. Simon Adingra is out with a pre-season injury; Omar Alderete is expected to return after being benched in matchweek one; Jules Ahoka is available following his move from Royal Antwerp. Fulham have Tom Cairney sidelined following knee surgery and Luc De Fougerolles ruled out with an injury picked up in the Carabao Cup tie against AFC Wimbledon, while Joachim Andersen returns from a one-match suspension — so Fulham get a centre-back back and lose another, having signed none.
  • Sample. One league match each and it is matchweek 2; both clubs are on zero points. Sunderland lost 2–1 away at Ipswich Town, beaten at Portman Road by a Jack Clarke goal in the 90th minute, so this is their first home fixture of the season. Fulham lost 3–2 at home to Chelsea in Arbeloa’s first Premier League match, staying in contention until the end, and then won 3–0 at AFC Wimbledon in the Carabao Cup. Gonzalo García scored on his debut and again in the cup tie; Fulham have five goals in their first two competitive matches, Sunderland have one in their only one.
  • Referee. Michael Salisbury, confirmed, with Blake Antrobus and Alex James on the lines, Anthony Backhouse fourth official, Matthew Donohue on VAR and Constantine Hatzidakis assisting. His profile is the most card-heavy on this section of the card: 14 Premier League matches in 2025–26 at 4.36 yellow cards and 0.07 red cards per game, with 21.9 fouls per match, which works out at roughly 5.1 fouls per card — a low ratio describing an official who books rather than warns. His last five appointments ran slightly cooler at 3.8 cards and 21.2 fouls. The number that stands furthest out is penalties: eight awarded across those matches, about 0.57 per game, which is a strict reading of contact in the box by any standard. Conflict logged: one source named Darren England as the appointed official for this fixture; two others independently give Salisbury together with the full crew above, and the full-crew listing is the one carried here. No notable history with either club surfaced.

B · Style & early-season reality

Sunderland’s seventh place is one of the most style-revealing results on this board, because it was achieved with 42 goals and a negative goal difference. Le Bris built a low-event, compact, defensively disciplined side that turned matches into narrow margins — twelve draws and only twelve defeats in 38, with the fewest goals of anyone near the top half. That is a structure, not a run of form, and structures survive summers better than squads do. What has not survived is the personnel. The striker who led the line was sold to Rennes for £18.9m and no centre-forward was signed; the goalkeeper was sold to Wrexham for £8m; eighteen players left in total against three arrivals, two of them full-backs. A team whose method depends on defensive organisation has replaced both full-back positions, lost its goalkeeper, and today loses its £24m left-back to a suspension imported from Ligue 1. The method is intact on paper and being executed by a substantially different set of people, and the one competitive match so far was a home defeat.

Fulham are the newer proposition and the more coherent one on paper. Arbeloa’s only head-coaching experience is 28 matches as Real Madrid’s interim, in which the side finished second in La Liga and reached a Champions League quarter-final — a body of work large enough to be real and short enough to be unrepresentative, and gathered with a squad that bears no resemblance to this one. What is visible is that he has recruited toward his own reference points: Gonzalo García followed him from Madrid at £34.3m, Kusi-Asare came from Bayern at £5.2m, and the £30m midfielder is Shea Charles, a passer rather than a destroyer, brought in as Lukić leaves. That is a squad being pointed at possession and forward play, and the early returns fit — five goals in two competitive matches, a goal on debut for García. What has not been addressed is the back: two centre-backs left and none arrived, Andersen returns from suspension into a defence that has just lost De Fougerolles to injury, and last season’s Fulham conceded 51 with only seven draws in 38, which is the signature of a side that does not know how to hold a tight game. Against a Sunderland built to make every game tight, that is the collision this fixture is actually about.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Sunderland’s seventh place is the single most misleading number on this card. It was achieved with 42 goals scored and a goal difference of −6 — a European qualification built on twelve draws and margins of one, not on quality of output. Any read that treats last season’s league position as a proxy for strength is reading the wrong column.
  • The squad that produced it has been dismantled. Eighteen players left against three arrivals. Eliezer Mayenda, the centre-forward, went to Rennes for £18.9m and no centre-forward replaced him. Anthony Patterson, the goalkeeper, went to Wrexham for £8m with no goalkeeper signed. The £32.6m spent went on two full-backs and a central midfielder, and one of those full-backs — Méthalie at £24m — is suspended today on a ban carried over from France.
  • Fulham’s manager has 28 matches of head-coaching experience, all of them as an interim at Real Madrid with a squad that has nothing in common with this one. He has since bought two centre-forwards and a passing midfielder and signed no defender, while two centre-backs left and De Fougerolles has been ruled out injured. Last season’s Fulham conceded 51 in 38 with only seven draws — a side that historically does not manage tight games — and the summer has added attacking weight to that profile rather than balance.
  • The head-to-head record is unusable in the direction it appears to point. Fulham are unbeaten in the last five meetings and have won four, and Le Bris has lost both his previous encounters with them — but every one of those results was against a Fulham coached by Marco Silva, who is now at Benfica, with Jiménez, Diop, Lukić and Harry Wilson in the squad. All four have left.
  • Most sensitive: BTTS and any market that needs Sunderland to score, because the club sold its striker, signed no replacement, and managed one goal in its only match. Least sensitive: discipline — Salisbury is confirmed, his 4.36 cards and 5.1 fouls-per-card profile is a stable property of the official, and it does not care that either club changed its squad.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Cards are more robust than goals here because the disciplinary input is a property of the referee: Salisbury books at 4.36 a game and 5.1 fouls per card whoever is playing, whereas both attacks in this fixture are new — one because it was sold, one because it was bought.
  • The under on totals is better evidenced than the over because it rests on a 38-match sample under a manager still in post — Sunderland’s 42 goals and 1.11 a game — reinforced by the sale of their only established centre-forward, while the over rests on Fulham’s five goals in two matches under a coach with two matches in the job.
  • The handicap is more robust than the 1X2 because −1 at 4.80 against +1 at 1.55 prices the tightness of the game rather than its winner, and tightness is the one thing Le Bris’s Sunderland has demonstrated across a full season; the winner depends on a striker Sunderland no longer employ.
  • The away cards line carries more information than the match cards line, because 1.43 on Fulham over 1.5 is the book stating which side it expects to be chasing the ball — a specific structural claim about a possession side visiting a compact block, and one that fits Salisbury’s foul-to-card ratio rather than contradicting it.
  • Where the book has priced near-indifference, it is admitting it has no read. Corners at 9.5 with 1.88 under and 1.80 over, and a race at 1.80 against 2.20, is a bookmaker declining to say who will hold territory between a low-event home side and an away manager with two matches on the job — and that admission is more informative than the number itself.
