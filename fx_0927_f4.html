A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Kosovo are the promoted side; Austria stayed in League B. Kosovo were second in League C Group C2 in 2024–25 on 12 points (W4 L2, behind Romania on 18). Their standings total of 10–7 includes the abandoned match in Bucharest, awarded 3–0 to Romania; counted as played, their record is 10–4. They went up through the B/C play-off, beating Iceland 2–1 in Pristina and 3–1 in Murcia (March 2025). No side promoted into League B in 2022–23 or 2024–25 came up through a play-off, because that route is new. This is Kosovo’s first League B edition after D (2018–19) and C (2020–21 to 2024–25). In World Cup qualifying Kosovo were second in Group B (W3 D2 L1, 6–5, behind Switzerland), winning 2–0 against Sweden at home and 1–0 in Gothenburg. They then won their play-off semi-final 4–3 in Bratislava and lost the final 0–1 to Türkiye in Pristina. Austria were second in League B Group B3 in 2024–25 on 11 points, 14–5 (W3 D2 L1, behind Norway on 13), then lost the A/B play-off to Serbia, 1–1 in Vienna and 0–2 in Belgrade. They won qualifying Group H on 19 points, 22–4 (W6 D1 L1). At the World Cup they beat Jordan 3–1, lost 0–2 to Argentina, drew 3–3 with Algeria, and lost 0–3 to Spain in the round of 32 on 2 July. Format: group winners are promoted to League A. Runners-up go into promotion play-offs against League A sides, and fourth-placed sides into relegation play-offs against League C runners-up (Wikipedia’s League B page, citing UEFA). How the division treats promoted sides: the eight sides promoted from League C for 2022–23 and 2024–25 went W6 D4 L13 away in League B, 22–40. They scored 0.22 goals per match before half-time, and won 3.61 corners to 6.00. Against hosts that had stayed in League B the split was narrower: the hosts went W9 D1 L5 in those 15 matches, and won more corners in only 7 of them. In 2024–25 alone the hosts went W4 D0 L4, when Greece won away twice and Georgia and Türkiye once each (baselines_0927.md). The structural headline: a first-time League B side whose away record is W6 D1 L1 visits a host that has not lost at home in Austria since September 2024 (W6 D3), and each is missing a large part of the spine that built those records. Head to head: the international-results record has no meeting, and Sports Mole calls this the first. The RESEARCH SPEC’s transfer-fee field has no national-team equivalent. B · Style & early-season reality

Rangnick’s Austria dominate the ball and the corner count at home, and concede little there before half-time. At home in Austria since September 2024 they had 65.6% of the ball and 18.78 shots to 8.11, and won 5.33 corners to 2.11. They won more corners in all nine of those matches, and in all six home matches against A/B-tier sides. They scored in every one, but kept only three clean sheets. Against A/B-tier sides overall they have not kept a clean sheet in 12, and BTTS landed in all six of the home ones (1–1 with Slovenia, Serbia and Bosnia, 5–1 Norway, 2–1 Romania, 3–1 Israel). They foul more than they are fouled (12.65 to 10.00) but are rarely booked: 7.91 fouls per card, and 1.11 cards per home match against their visitors’ 2.22. The shape is unchanged; the personnel is not. Laimer, Sabitzer, Baumgartner and Arnautović supplied most of the pressing midfield and 24 goals. Gregoritsch is doubtful, and the goalkeeper will be making his debut. Thursday’s 3–1 came against ten men, with two goals after the 89th minute. The evidence for the new front line is one match, and for the goalkeeper none.

Foda’s Kosovo have become a back-five side that concedes territory and wins away. Since September 2025 they have played a back three or five in eight of nine matches. In qualifying and the play-offs they averaged 40.2% possession and 9.50 shots to 11.62, conceded 4.75 corners per match, and kept four clean sheets in eight. Their matches against A/B-tier sides averaged 1.60 goals, and BTTS landed in one of ten. Away from home they won 1–0 in Gothenburg on 32.6% of the ball (0 corners to 12) and 2–0 in Ljubljana. They also won the 4–3 in Bratislava from 1–2 down at half-time, on 9 shots to 21. They are booked more than Austria per foul (5.03 fouls per card), and all three of their reds in the window came at home. The summer has changed the back line more than the method. Rrahmani, the long-time captain and centre-back, has quit. Hajdari, Thursday’s left centre-back, is not in the 23. The central-midfield pair of the window, Rexhbeçaj and Muslija, are both injured. Thursday’s 22 shots to 3 against Ireland was a home match, the opposite of the away pattern. Whether a back three without Rrahmani and Hajdari holds that pattern in Vienna is a CV question, not a match question: Sadriu has 2 caps, and Raçi 3.
