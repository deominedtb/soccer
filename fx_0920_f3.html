A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Auxerre finished 15th in 2025–26 on 34 points — 8 wins, 10 draws, 16 defeats, 34 scored, 44 conceded — and failed to score in 47.1% of their matches, among the highest blank rates of any club carded on this slate. Brest finished 12th on 39 points — 10 wins, 9 draws, 15 defeats, 43 scored, 55 conceded — with a 7-5-5 home record against 3-4-10 away. The structural headline is that both clubs changed head coach in June, a fortnight apart, and for opposite reasons. Auxerre appointed Will Still on 12 June 2026 on a contract to 2028, after Christophe Pélissier’s departure was confirmed on 4 June; Still, 33, arrived from spells at Reims and Lens with a stated plan for an intense, vertical, attacking side. Brest’s change was a bereavement: Éric Roy, the head coach, died at 58 of pancreatic cancer, and on 27 June 2026 the club promoted Julien Lachuer, one of Roy’s assistants since 2023, to succeed him. Auxerre then spent about €17.6m, a club record; Brest’s window could not be established in anything reached. Both clubs sit in the bottom half on three and five points.

B · Style & early-season reality

Auxerre under Still look almost exactly like Auxerre under Pélissier at one end and considerably worse at the other, which is the opposite of what a record window and a stated change of philosophy would suggest. Possession 43.5% against 43.9%, PPDA 13.77 against 13.30, deep completions 4.50 against 4.29 — three of the most style-sensitive series in the dataset, all unmoved. Shots are up modestly (12.25 against 10.71) and shots on target sharply (5.25 against 3.50), and the xG has risen from 1.19 to 1.52. The defence has collapsed: xG conceded from 1.47 to 2.59, goals conceded from 1.29 to 2.75 per match, and corners conceded from 4.76 to 7.75 — the third-highest concession rate on this board. The one clear improvement is at the sharp end and it is real: a side that failed to score in 47.1% of 34 matches last season has scored in all four this season. That the two deadline-day signings arrived on 1 September, after three of the four matches, is the obvious caveat on all of it.

Brest are the more interesting side of this fixture by a distance, because their underlying numbers have moved further than almost any club on the board and their points have not followed. Shots have doubled, from 11.24 to 21.75 per match; xG has nearly doubled, from 1.36 to 2.63; shots on target from 3.53 to 7.00; deep completions from 4.65 to 6.00; and PPDA has fallen from 11.25 to 8.96, a 20% increase in pressing intensity. The defensive model has improved with it, from 1.65 xG conceded to 1.26. The corner column says the same thing from the other direction: 6.25 won against 2.50 conceded, having won 3.85 and conceded 5.85 last season — a differential that has swung by nearly six corners a match. And the return is five points from four, with 1.50 goals scored against an expected 2.63. The cost is disciplinary: 15.50 fouls per match against 12.76 last season and a divisional 12.10, the second-highest figure on this board — which is what a press at 8.96 PPDA produces. A continuity appointment has produced the largest stylistic change in this fixture, and it belongs to the club whose window could not be sourced.

Every 26/27 figure on this card rests on four matches with nothing else to corroborate it, which is exactly why this fixture was triaged out on sample depth.
  • Auxerre’s squad was not complete when three of its four matches were played. Cameron Archer and Axel Tuanzebe were both announced on 1 September, after the 2–5, 1–3 and 1–3. Reading the 2.75 goals-conceded rate into today reads a defence that has since added a centre-back.
  • Brest’s transfer window could not be sourced at all. Their numbers have changed more than any other club’s in this fixture — shots doubled, xG nearly doubled, corners inverted — under a coach promoted from the existing staff, and there is no ledger reached here to say whether the squad producing that is the squad that finished 12th.
  • Brest are scoring at roughly half their expected rate and Auxerre at roughly their own. 1.50 goals from 2.63 xG against 1.25 from 1.52. When one side’s conversion is that far from its model on four matches, every goals-based reading here is one or two finishes from a different number.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and any Brest team-goals line — a side out-shooting its opponents by a distance with five points from four, and a home side whose recruitment finished three weeks ago. Least sensitive: Totals and BTTS — both rest on four separate counted series per side and a divisional baseline that has moved the same way.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals is more robust than 1X2 here because the goal evidence is corroborated four ways and the result evidence is contradicted by its own model. 75.0% over 2.5 each, 23 goals in eight matches, a combined 3.89 xG against a divisional 3.30, and Auxerre conceding 2.59 xG. The result has to reconcile Brest’s 2.63 xG with their five points.
  • BTTS is more robust than Team goals because it needs only one goal from each side and both records now supply it. Auxerre’s blank rate has gone from 47.1% across 34 matches to 0.0% across four with shots on target up by half; Brest have not kept a clean sheet all season. A Brest team-goals line, by contrast, asks the 2.63 xG to become goals, which it has not in four attempts.
  • The fouls half of Discipline is more robust than the cards half, and the referee is the whole reason. Brest’s 12.76→15.50 fouls has PPDA 11.25→8.96 behind it. Paradis showed no red card in fourteen matches last season and is running at 10.4 fouls per card this season against a divisional 6.18 — the same offences produce fewer cards in front of him.
  • Any penalty-dependent line is the least robust thing on this card. Paradis awarded one penalty in fourteen Ligue 1 matches — 0.07 per match against a divisional 0.36 — and gave none in two matches this season.
  • The single fact that would most change which family reads best is whether Brest’s 21.75 shots per match is a level or four matches of noise. It is double last season’s figure, it underpins the totals, corners and team-goals readings at once, and it has produced 1.50 goals. What would settle it is a fifth and sixth match of shot and xG data, or a published Brest squad list showing whether the personnel behind it changed — and that ledger is exactly what could not be sourced.
