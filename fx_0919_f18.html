A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Venezia are promoted as Serie B champions and they won the division outright: 82 points from 38 matches — 24 wins, 10 draws, 4 defeats, 77 scored, 31 conceded, a 42.1% clean-sheet rate against a 27.3% divisional baseline. Four defeats in a season. Lazio finished 9th in Serie A on 54 points — 14 wins, 12 draws, 12 defeats, 41 scored, 40 conceded — and were the lowest-scoring watchable side in the division: 31.6% of their matches went over 2.5 against a divisional 45.8%, they failed to score in 44.7% of them, and both teams scored in only 28.9%. The structural headline is that both clubs have changed and in the same direction. Venezia spent about €44m of a €20m-plus sale on a squad rebuilt for the top flight and kept the coach who took them up; Lazio replaced Maurizio Sarri with Gennaro Gattuso and rebuilt the defence and the forward line around him. Lazio’s numbers have already moved sharply. Venezia’s have moved too, and in the wrong direction.

B · Style & early-season reality

Venezia are the strangest set of numbers on this board: a side bottom of Serie A with no points that dominates the ball and presses harder than anyone. Possession 59.20% — the second-highest figure of any club carded today, and from a team with zero points — and a PPDA of 7.32, the most aggressive press on this entire slate, meaning they engage inside eight opposition passes. That is continuous with the side that won Serie B at 63.97% possession, so the intent has survived the step up. What has not survived is everything downstream of it. Shots have fallen from 17.21 to 13.25 per match and shots faced risen from 10.08 to 15.00; deep completions run at 4.50 against 8.50 conceded; expected goals against sit at 2.65, the second-worst figure on this board. The defensive record is the sharpest contrast anywhere on the card: 0.82 conceded per match and a 42.1% clean-sheet rate in Serie B, against 2.75 conceded and no clean sheet in four here. A high line and a heavy press, applied at a level where the opposition plays through it — and the foul rate has fallen from 15.37 to 11.00, which says they are not even getting close enough to foul.

Lazio have changed more than their coach and the chance data says so before the results do. Expected goals have risen from 1.22 to 1.82 per match and deep completions from 4.53 to 8.25 — nearly doubled — while PPDA has fallen from 16.66 to 12.68, so a side that sat off last season now presses. Under Sarri and then Tudor they were the most goal-shy team in the division: 41 scored in 38, a 44.7% blank rate, 31.6% of matches over 2.5. Under Gattuso, through four matches, they have scored in every one, at 1.50 per match, and conceded 0.75 on 1.44 expected goals against. The forward line doing it is new — Pinamonti and Guðmundsson both from Fiorentina, with Dia and Pedro gone — and so is the midfield behind it, with Frattesi in from Internazionale. The one column that has moved against them is discipline: fouls up from 10.58 to 15.25 per match, the joint-highest rate on this board, which is the ordinary cost of pressing more. The evidence that this is Gattuso’s side rather than Sarri’s is four matches, a 0.60-goal rise in expected goals, a near-doubling of deep completions and four new starters — not a season.
