A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Inter are champions: 27 wins, 6 draws, 5 defeats in 2025–26, 89 scored, 35 conceded, 87 points, the club’s 21st title, in Cristian Chivu’s first season; Lautaro Martínez led the division with 17 league goals. Udinese finished 10th: 14 wins, 8 draws, 16 defeats, 45 scored, 48 conceded, 50 points, under Kosta Runjaić, who continues. Both coaches are unchanged. The structural headline is two different kinds of renewal. Inter replaced an ageing defensive core — Sommer, de Vrij, Acerbi and Darmian all left at the end of their contracts — and lost Dumfries to Real Madrid, while spending on midfield and wing-back. Udinese cashed in on the spine: Arthur Atta, a central midfielder, went to Fiorentina for close to €40m and Thomas Kristensen, a centre-back, to Atalanta for €22m plus €3m in bonuses.

B · Style & early-season reality

Inter under Chivu are a 3–5–2 built on territory: 66% possession and 24.33 shots per league match this season, 2.44 xG for against 1.14 against, and last season’s 89 goals. The fixtures around them have been high-scoring — Inter’s 38 league matches in 2025–26 averaged 3.26 goals against a league 2.43, and this season’s three average 3.67. The summer changed the back of the side more than the front: Stones and a permanent Akanji in for de Vrij and Acerbi, Martínez in goal after Sommer, Spence (unavailable tonight) for Dumfries, Jones added to the midfield rotation. The first test at Champions League level went 2–1 against them in Madrid. Gap: no Inter card, foul or corner series and no 2025–26 xG.

Udinese under Runjaić are the lower-possession side: 43% on average and 13 shots per match this season, 1.40 xG for and 1.85 against, with probable elevens in a 3–4–2–1 around Davis, Ekkelenkamp and either Unai Gómez or Bayo. Every one of their league matches has had both teams scoring. The squad that finished 10th lost its most valuable midfielder and a first-choice centre-back, and the replacements — Unai Gómez, Chakvetadze, Traoré, Abankwah — are cheap and unproven at this level; tonight two further centre-backs are missing. What the evidence cannot say is how a back three that is partly new and partly makeshift handles a side taking 24 shots a match: that is a CV question with three matches of data behind it. Gap: no Udinese card, foul or corner series in either season.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Inter’s 35 conceded last season was made by a defence that has left. Sommer, de Vrij, Acerbi and Dumfries all played in that record; tonight’s probable back line has Stones and Martínez, and conceded twice at the Bernabéu in its first Champions League test.
  • Udinese’s back three tonight is not the one that finished 10th. Kristensen was sold, Solet and Palma are out, and the probable trio includes a summer arrival; their 1.85 xG against is from three matches with a different selection.
  • Inter’s individual league scores are unconfirmed. Aggregates (8–3) and FootyStats’ percentages are consistent with each other; per-match home scores were not reliably extracted and are not used.
  • The referee’s record is a December snapshot. Eight matches of 2025–26 cannot distinguish a strict official from a run of high-foul fixtures.
  • Most sensitive: halves and corners, with no data at any level. Least sensitive: the goals total, where a full season of Inter fixtures at 3.26 goals and three matches from each side agree.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals are more robust than BTTS here because 3.26 goals per Inter fixture last season and 67% over 2.5 for both sides this season hold regardless of who scores, while BTTS needs Udinese to score without Zaniolo.
  • Inter’s team goals are more robust than the handicap because 24.33 shots and 2.44 xG per game describe Inter alone, whereas the handicap also needs a view on whether Udinese keep scoring at the rate they have for four matches.
  • The moneyline is more robust than discipline because a 27–6–5 title season and an 18–2–3 recent head-to-head are complete records, while Pairetto’s numbers are eight matches from a season ago with two penalty figures that disagree.
  • Discipline is more robust than corners because the referee at least has a published card and foul count, while no corner count exists for either club or the league.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: Pairetto’s full 2025–26 record. If his eight-match rate of 6.1 cards and 0.6 penalties per game held across the season, discipline would carry the best official evidence on this card; a completed season table from AIA or a referee-stats service would settle it.
