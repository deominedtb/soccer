B · Style & early-season reality

Calzona’s Slovakia played a 4-3-3 in 13 of 15 competitive matches. The exceptions were a back three against Northern Ireland and Kosovo, both in the last three matches. Their possession moved with the opponent: 30.3% and 28.1% against Germany, 53.7% against C-or-below sides, and 50.7% over 15. At this level they controlled the shot count rather than the ball: 15.83 shots to 7.83 and 5.50 on target to 2.33 against Estonia, Azerbaijan and Luxembourg, with 49.3% of the ball in the three home matches among those six. They foul without being booked: 12.00 fouls per match at 7.64 fouls per card (1.53 cards per match), against 14.93 fouls and 2.40 cards from their opponents. 11 of their 19 goals came after half-time, and four from the 76th minute. The summer change is on the bench and at right-back. Weiss’s evidence is two friendlies with no box score, both recorded as 4-3-3. He says Moldova are “well organised in defensive activity” and that Slovakia “must be faster, more dynamic and technically more skilled”. Škriniar expects Moldova to sit “in a closed block, waiting for counters and our mistakes” (TASR via Sportnet). The right-back position, where Gyömbér and Pekarík made all 13 starts, has no incumbent in the matchday pool beyond a U21 player who played on Friday. That is a squad list, not a match.

Moldova defended with a back three or five in 11 of 12 competitive matches: 5-4-1 five times, 5-3-2 four times, 3-5-2 and 3-5-1-1 once each. The only back four was the 2–3 at home to Estonia. Their shape held, but their share of the game moved with the level. Against League D sides and Estonia they had 50.6% of the ball, took 13.00 shots to 9.83 and won the corner count in five of six. Against League A and B sides they had 31.3%, were outshot 8.17 to 26.00 and won it in none of six. They foul more than they are fouled at this level: 17.17 fouls committed per match against C-or-below sides, 19.33 away from home against them, and 3.33 cards per match. They concede early and late: 5 of 33 goals inside the first 15 minutes and 10 from the 76th. Popescu’s three competitive matches kept the back five and gave 29.8% possession, 7.67 shots to 23.67 and 2.67 corners to 8.33, all against Group I opposition. Weiss says Popescu “improved the organisation” and that Moldova’s defending has been better recently. Popescu frames tonight as getting past obstacles, “first of all mental ones” (Moldfootball headline). The attacking evidence is the weakest part: the scorers of 8 of the 9 goals are not here, and the two predicted forwards have no competitive start in the window.
