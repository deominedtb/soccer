A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Deportivo are promoted and are back in the top flight after a long absence — they were out of LaLiga in 2024–25 and 2023–24 and had fallen as far as the third tier in the interim. They came up as runners-up in LaLiga 2 on 77 points from 42 matches (22-11-9, 65 scored, 44 conceded), behind Racing Santander on 82 and ahead of Almería and Málaga, both of whom are also in this division now. Real Betis finished 5th in 2025–26 on 60 points — 15 wins, 15 draws, 8 defeats, 59 scored, 48 conceded — and are in the Champions League for the first time in 21 years. The structural headline is that both clubs kept their coach and then spent in opposite proportions relative to their size. Antonio Hidalgo’s contract renewed automatically on promotion and he continues at Deportivo; Manuel Pellegrini continues at Betis, which makes this one of the few fixtures on the slate where both clubs’ last-season records belong to the men picking the team — though one of those records was made in a division below. Deportivo then recruited ten senior players, including a €9m goalkeeper and a former Champions League-winning forward, while Betis’s window is not obtainable in any source reached.

B · Style & early-season reality

Deportivo have come up and become the least productive attacking side on this board, and every column says the same thing. 7.83 shots per match — the lowest figure of any club carded on this slate, against a divisional 12.1 — 3.33 on target, 5.33 deep completions, and an xG of 1.08, also the lowest here. Possession is 47.1%, down from 51.1% in LaLiga 2, and PPDA is 13.80, a passive figure. And yet they have scored 1.50 per match, comfortably above their expected 1.08 and above their own 1.55 second-tier rate at a much higher level. Nine goals from 6.5 expected across six matches is the widest positive over-performance on this card, and it has been produced with Aubameyang leading a line behind the fewest shots in the division. At the other end they have conceded in every single match — a 0% clean-sheet rate against 26.2% in LaLiga 2 — from 1.58 xG against and 5.67 corners conceded per match. Their discipline has risen sharply: 13.50 fouls per match against 11.81 in the second tier, and two red cards in six. There is no 2025–26 modelled record to test any of this against.

Betis are the mirror image: enormous chance volume, almost no goals, and a clean-sheet record built on it. 18.50 shots per match against 14.24 last season, 6.83 on target against 4.84, deep completions 6.90 to 10.17, and xG from 1.61 to 2.03 — four series all up sharply under an unchanged coach, on possession that has moved only 50.1% to 53.6% and PPDA that has not moved at all (12.77 to 12.76). The output is 1.33 goals per match, down from 1.55: eight goals from 12.2 expected, among the widest negative gaps on this board. Four 1–0 wins, three of them at home, where they have scored three goals in three matches and conceded none. The defensive record is equally lopsided: 66.7% clean sheets against 26.3% last season, but an xG conceded that has gone the wrong way, 1.32 to 1.81, and a corner concession of 6.50 per match against 5.18. Five of their six league goals conceded came in a single match, the 2–5 at Levante. A side creating far more and scoring less, conceding far more chances and shipping fewer goals — both halves of their record are running against their own model.
