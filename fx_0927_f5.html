A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Greece are promoted and are in League A for the first time. ESPN’s standings have them in League C in 2018–19, 2020–21 and 2022–23, and in League B in 2024–25. In 2024–25 they were second in Group B2 on 15 points, 11–4 (W5 L1, behind England on goal difference; the one defeat 0–3 to England in Athens). They then lost the first leg of the A/B play-off 0–1 to Scotland in Piraeus and won the second 3–0 at Hampden. In World Cup qualifying they were third in Group C, 10–12 (W2 D1 L3: 5–1 and 0–0 against Belarus, 3–2 and 1–3 against Scotland, 0–3 and 1–3 against Denmark), and did not qualify. Germany were first in Group A3 in 2024–25 on 14 points, 18–4 (W4 D2, unbeaten, ahead of the Netherlands). They beat Italy 2–1 and 3–3 in the quarter-final, then lost 1–2 to Portugal and 0–2 to France at the Finals in Munich and Stuttgart. They won qualifying Group A, 16–3 (W5 L1, the defeat 0–2 in Bratislava on the opening night). At the World Cup they won Group E (7–1 Curaçao, 2–1 Ivory Coast, 1–2 Ecuador), then went out to Paraguay 3–4 on penalties after 1–1 in the round of 32. It was Germany’s first World Cup shoot-out defeat (Wikipedia). Format: the top two in each group go to the quarter-finals. The two worst last-placed sides go straight down, and the two best last-placed and the two worst third-placed sides go into play-offs (Wikipedia’s League A page, citing UEFA). The structural headline is that the promoted side arrives almost intact and the host does not. Players outside tonight’s Greece squad made 42 of Greece’s 165 competitive starts in the window and scored 6 of their 24 goals that were not own goals. For Germany the figures are 129 of 231 starts and 34 of 52 goals, and the coach has had one match. The division prior points one way. B · Style & early-season reality

Nagelsmann’s Germany played a 4-2-3-1 in 13 of 20 competitive matches (3-4-2-1 three times, and once each 4-1-3-2, 4-4-1-1, 4-3-3 and 4-4-2). In his 19 with a usable box score it had 64.9% of the ball, took 16.89 shots to 8.79 and won 5.63 corners to 3.68. It committed 11.79 fouls to 10.84 suffered and was booked 1.55 times per match (20). At home it overwhelmed sides below this level: 19.33 shots to 7.56, 64.6% possession and more corners in 7 of 9, with 25–1 across the five home matches outside League A-tier opposition. Against League A-tier sides the control thinned: 54.5% possession, 15.00 shots to 13.00, and 4.86 cards per match at 5.22 fouls per card. At home against those sides they went W1 D1 L2. Klopp’s first match kept the 4-2-3-1 but not the pattern. Germany had 46.5% of the ball, took 10 shots to 22 and won 1 corner to 12, and committed 16 fouls to 11.
