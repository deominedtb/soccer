A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Hull City are promoted, through the play-offs rather than automatically, and are back in the top flight after a ten-year absence. They finished 6th in the Championship on 73 points — 21 wins, 10 draws, 15 defeats, 70 scored, 66 conceded — then beat Millwall over two legs and Middlesbrough in the final at Wembley, keeping three consecutive clean sheets through the run. Sixth place with a goal difference of +4 is the weakest league record of any side promoted from this division in either direction on this slate. Newcastle finished 12th in the Premier League on 49 points — 14 wins, 7 draws, 17 defeats, 53 scored, 55 conceded. The structural headline is that both clubs arrive almost unrecognisable, for opposite reasons. Newcastle sold roughly £236m of midfield and attack in one window and changed head coach in August; Hull made eighteen signings. Neither squad can be read off last season’s record, and for Hull the record is not even from this division.

  • Newcastle — turnover extreme and concentrated on selling; manager changed in early August 2026. Eddie Howe stepped down on 31 July 2026 and Matthias Jaissle was appointed on 5 August 2026 from Al-Ahli, where he had won consecutive Asian Champions League Elite titles, and before that Red Bull Salzburg — a fact carried from this board’s own card of 14 September (fx_0914_f4) and confirmed independently by the Premier League’s 2026/27 manager listing. Out, and this is the whole story: Sandro Tonali to Tottenham for an initial £92.5m rising to £100m; Bruno Guimarães to Arsenal for £75m; Anthony Gordon to Barcelona for £69.3m; Kieran Trippier left as a free agent. That is the entire central midfield and the leading wide attacker, for roughly £236m, and two of the three buying clubs’ sides play on this card — Tottenham at 13:30 and Arsenal at 16:00. In: Bazoumana Touré, reported at £42m and described as at the top of the club’s range; Ewen Jaouen from Reims, £18.5m; plus Sean Steur, Aladji Bamba and Lukas Hornicek, fees not published by any source reached. A club that banked £236m and spent something over £60m of it on five players with no Premier League record between them.
  • Hull City — promoted, eighteen signings, and the manager is the one thing that did not change. Sergej Jakirović was appointed in June 2025, took them up in his first English season, and signed a new two-year contract on 4 September 2026 running to summer 2028 with an option to 2029. He is therefore a promotion-winning coach retained and extended, not a new appointment — the opposite of Newcastle’s position. B · Style & early-season reality

Newcastle are a side whose shape survived the summer and whose quality apparently did not. Possession has fallen from 52.56% to 47.10% and PPDA is almost unchanged at 10.41 against 9.97 — they press the same amount — but the output either side of that has collapsed. Shots are down from 12.95 to 9.75 per match and shots faced up from 12.37 to 19.00; expected goals have fallen from 1.60 to 1.04 and expected goals against risen from 1.51 to 2.05, which is the largest two-way swing of any club carded today. Corners tell the same story from the other end: 6.08 won and 4.92 conceded last season, 4.50 and 5.75 now. The mechanism is not mysterious and it is in section A rather than in the tactics — Tonali, Guimarães and Gordon accounted for the central midfield and the leading wide threat, and left for £236m between them. The one column that has risen is the disciplinary one: 14.25 fouls per match against 10.16, and 2.75 cards against 1.74, which is what a side that has lost its midfield control tends to do.

Hull are playing the most extreme version of a low block anywhere on this board, and it is working so far in a way their own chance numbers only partly support. 29.62% possession — the lowest figure on this slate by eleven points — against 45.02% in the Championship; PPDA 17.91, meaning they barely press at all; 2.25 deep completions per match against 8.50 conceded; 9.50 shots against 14.25 faced. They beat Manchester United 2–0 with 28.5% of the ball and 8 shots to 21. The results are a 75% clean-sheet rate and 0.50 conceded per match; the chances say 1.45 expected goals against, so they are conceding about a goal a game less than their opponents’ chances imply. The corner column is the clearest signature of the shape: 2.50 won and 6.75 conceded per match, the most lopsided corner deficit on this card. The evidence that this is a deliberate system rather than early-season luck is four matches of possession, pressing and shot data all agreeing, against a Championship season in which the same coach had 45% of the ball — so the shape is new even though the coach is not, and the one measure that would arbitrate, last season’s xG, does not exist for them.
