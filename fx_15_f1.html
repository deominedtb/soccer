A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted; both are established top-flight sides, and this is the first competitive meeting between them in any competition. They arrive by very different routes. Roma finished third in Serie A in 2025–26 on 73 points — 23 wins, four draws, eleven defeats, 59 goals scored and 31 conceded — closing with five straight wins and sealing the place with a 2–0 at Hellas Verona on the final day. That was Gian Piero Gasperini's first season at the club, having arrived on 6 June 2025 after almost a decade at Atalanta, so the manager is not a change in this window: the shape here has a full league season and a top-three finish behind it. Fenerbahçe finished second in the Süper Lig behind Galatasaray and entered this competition through qualifying, where they needed six matches — four wins and two draws, eight scored and three conceded — to eliminate Górnik Zabrze, Sturm Graz and Lyon. İsmail Kartal is the head coach with no change recorded in the sources reached. The two starts to 2026–27 are almost mirror images: Roma lead Serie A with nine points from three, ten goals scored and one conceded; Fenerbahçe are eighth in the Süper Lig with six points from four, having lost 1–2 away to Gençlerbirliği on the opening day and 1–2 at home to Beşiktaş five days ago.

  • Fenerbahçe — high turnover, heavily weighted to the forward line, with fees published on almost all of it. Kartal is unchanged, so the churn is in the squad rather than on the bench. In, with fees: Mason Greenwood from Marseille for a reported €39m, the largest outlay of the window and a forward; Vedat Muriqi from Mallorca for a reported €15.5m, a second centre-forward; Romelu Lukaku from Napoli for a reported €6m, a third; Kojo Peprah Oppong from Nice for a reported €12m, a defender; and Nathan Aké from Manchester City for a reported £7m, a centre-back. Mattéo Guendouzi arrived from West Ham for a reported €28m in the preceding winter window rather than this summer, and is recorded here as such. Out: Sidiki Cherif to Coventry City for a reported €25m, Dominik Livaković to Barcelona for a reported €2m, Fred to Atlético Mineiro for a reported €2m, and Talisca — the club's leading scorer across all competitions last season with five — to Al Jazira by mutual agreement, no fee. Weighted as the spec asks: three centre-forwards bought, one centre-back bought, and the goalkeeper sold. Ederson is expected to start in goal, but no arrival fee or date for him was obtainable in anything reached — recorded as a gap rather than assumed.
  • Roma — high turnover in money, concentrated on the attack and the back four, and the manager did not change. Gasperini is in his second season. In, with fees: Santiago Castro from Bologna for a reported €35m plus €2m in add-ons, a centre-forward and the largest fee of the window; Rodrigo Mora from Porto for a reported €25m plus €2m; Donyell Malen's loan from Aston Villa converted to a permanent transfer for a reported €25m after 14 league goals in 2025–26; Konstantinos Koulierakis from Wolfsburg for a reported €16.5m plus €1.5m, a centre-back; and Nahuel Molina from Atlético Madrid for a reported €13m plus €4m. Daniele Ghilardi's loan from Hellas Verona was also made permanent, and Leonardo Balerdi and Marten De Roon are named among the summer arrivals in one source without fee or date — not asserted as this window's business here. Out: Artem Dovbyk on loan to Bologna at a reported €3m with a €20m option, and Tommaso Baldanzi on a free loan to Genoa with a reported €9.5m obligation. Conflict logged: the Molina fee is reported at €13m plus €4m in the Roma-side sourcing here and at €18m in the Atlético-side reporting carried on the previous slate; neither is chosen. Gap: no goalkeeper movement was established for Roma in either direction.
  • Suspensions / absences. The clearer picture is on the home side and it is a suspension rather than an injury: Guendouzi is banned for this match following a post-match brawl in the play-off win over Lyon — the €28m midfielder unavailable for the club's first league-phase game. Jayden Oosterwolde is out after surgery on a severe hamstring injury, and Marco Asensio is described as highly doubtful with a thigh strain. Roma are without club captain Lorenzo Pellegrini, still rehabilitating a persistent thigh problem, and are otherwise reported to have few concerns, with Malen and Paulo Dybala both fit. Gap: no full injury list was published for either squad, so this is what was named rather than a complete accounting.
  • Sample. Roma have played three competitive matches in 2026–27 and no European ones: 4–0 at home to Fiorentina on 24 August with a Malen hat-trick and a Pisilli fourth, 4–0 away at Lecce on 31 August with Malen twice plus Soulé and Mora, and 2–1 at home to Atalanta on 5 September, won late through Hermoso and Soulé. Ten scored, one conceded, first in Serie A, Malen on five goals across all competitions. Fenerbahçe have played ten — four league and six in Champions League qualifying — which is the largest competitive sample of any club on this board: 2W–0D–2L domestically with eight scored and six conceded, and 4W–2D–0L in Europe with eight scored and three conceded. Greenwood leads their league scoring with two. Gap: no xG, shot-volume, possession, foul or card series was obtainable for either club in either season, and Fenerbahçe's final 2025–26 points and goal columns were not published in anything reached — the figures that surfaced (52 points, 51 scored, 20 conceded, 25 matches unbeaten) are explicitly a 23 February 2026 snapshot, not a season total, and are not used as one here.
  • Referee. Jesús Gil Manzano of Spain, confirmed rather than provisional. He is the highest-carding official appointed to this slate and one of the highest on any slate this board has carried: 19 matches in 2025–26 across LaLiga, the Copa del Rey, the Europa League and World Cup qualifying, 87 yellow cards and six red, for 4.89 cards per match (4.58 yellow, 0.32 red). Over a career he averages 5.16 yellows and 0.11 reds per game with 158 penalties, roughly 0.41 per match; last season's penalty rate was lower at five in 19, 0.26 per match, with a penalty in 21.1% of his games. His foul count is published too — 460 fouls across those 19 matches, 24.21 per game — which gives 4.95 fouls per card. That figure is derived by division from two published series in the same source, not read from one, and it is stated as derived: it means he books roughly once every five fouls, which is the low-tolerance, early-booking profile the spec asks this row to identify. Gap: no history with either club was established, and the red-card rate this season (0.32) sits three times above his own career mean (0.11) on a 19-match sample, which is noted rather than explained.

B · Style & early-season reality

Roma are the more legible of the two sides here, and the reason is that the manager is the same one who produced the numbers. Gasperini took over in June 2025 and finished third with 31 conceded — a defensive figure consistent with everything his Atalanta sides were known for — and the summer then spent its two largest fees at opposite ends of the pitch: €35m on Castro at centre-forward and €16.5m on Koulierakis at centre-back, with Molina added at full-back and Malen's loan converted at €25m after the season in which he scored 14 league goals. That is additive rather than reconstructive business on a shape that already worked. Three matches have produced ten goals and one conceded, including two 4–0 wins and a 2–1 over Atalanta decided late. The honest limit on all of this is the sample: three domestic matches, no European minutes, and a second season under the same coach is exactly the situation in which last season's 59-scored, 31-conceded profile is the better guide than three weeks of results. Gap: no possession share, foul rate, shot volume or corner series is published for Roma in either season, so the style description stops at goals, fees and the manager's continuity.

Fenerbahçe are harder to characterise and the difficulty is not a shortage of matches — they have played ten — but that the matches point in two directions. In Europe they went through three rounds unbeaten, four wins and two draws, conceding three in six against Górnik Zabrze, Sturm Graz and Lyon. Domestically they have lost two of four, including at home to Beşiktaş five days ago, and sit eighth. The squad underneath that split changed most in the forward line: Greenwood at €39m, Muriqi at €15.5m and Lukaku at €6m all arrived in the same window, while Talisca — last season's leading scorer — left by mutual agreement and Cherif was sold for €25m. Three new centre-forwards is a large amount of simultaneous change in one department, and the league evidence for how it has settled is four matches in which Greenwood has scored twice and the team has eight goals. The back line has changed too, with Aké at £7m and Oppong at €12m arriving and Livaković sold, leaving Ederson expected in goal behind a line named as Semedo, Škriniar, Aké and Brown — but with Oosterwolde out after hamstring surgery. Add Guendouzi's suspension and the midfield loses its most expensive player for the occasion. Gap: as with Roma, no underlying series exists here for either season; what can be said is that ten competitive matches have produced a strong European record and a poor domestic one, and that this card does not pretend to know which of those the visit of Roma resembles.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • The two clubs' samples are not the same size or the same kind. Fenerbahçe have ten competitive matches, six of them European and unbeaten, four domestic and two of those lost; Roma have three, all domestic, all won, ten scored and one conceded. Any comparison between "form" here is a comparison between a split ten-match record and a three-match one, and the split is the more informative half.
  • Fenerbahçe bought three centre-forwards in one window — Greenwood at €39m, Muriqi at €15.5m, Lukaku at €6m — and sold the man who led their scoring last season. Four league matches and eight goals is not enough to establish what that attack now is, and the totals and BTTS rows both depend on it.
  • The home side's most expensive midfielder is suspended for this specific match after a play-off brawl, and a defender is out following hamstring surgery. Suspension-driven absences do not show up in any of the season numbers quoted above, because they are properties of this fixture rather than of the squad.
  • Last season's figures are incomplete on one side and complete on the other. Roma's 73 points, 59 scored and 31 conceded are published; Fenerbahçe's final columns are not, and the mid-season snapshot that does circulate — 52 points, 51 scored, 20 conceded to 23 February 2026 — is not a season total and is not treated as one here.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total, where a 1.55 over is being asked of a home attack with four league matches of shared history and a visiting defence with no published underlying series at all. Least sensitive: the cards total — a confirmed official with season and career figures on yellows, reds, penalties and fouls, whose derived 4.95 fouls per card and the market's 4.5 line point the same way.
