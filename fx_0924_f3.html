A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Greece are promoted and new to League A, after League C, C and B in the three previous editions. In 2024–25 they were second in League B Group B2 on 15 points, 11 scored and 4 conceded, level on points with England, who won the group. They then went up through the new A/B play-off, beating Scotland 3–1 on aggregate in March 2025 (0–1 in Piraeus, 3–0 at Hampden). Serbia were third in League A Group A4 on 6 points, 3 scored and 6 conceded (one win, three draws, two defeats), and kept their place in the same play-off against Austria (1–1 in Vienna, 2–0 in Belgrade). In 2025–26 World Cup qualifying both finished third and missed the tournament: Serbia in Group K on 13 points, 9–10 (England 24, Albania 14), and Greece in Group C on 7 points, 10–12 (Scotland 13, Denmark 11). The structural headline is how League A treats promoted sides. The eight promoted into it for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 0.79 points per match, scored 0.92 and conceded 1.73 per match, and five of the eight finished bottom of their group. On matchday one they won three, drew one and lost four. Serbia were one of the eight in 2024–25, which is where their 3–6 group record comes from. The two sides have never met in a competitive match; 021.rs lists two friendlies (Greece won 1–0 in Belgrade in 2010, Serbia 2–0 in Greece in 2014), which are not in ESPN’s record. B · Style & early-season reality

Stojković’s Serbia were a back-three side that ceded the ball to League A opposition. In the 2024–25 group they had 37.2% possession, took 9.17 shots and allowed 16.50, won 3.33 corners and conceded 6.33, and committed 12.67 fouls and took 3.33 cards per match; the division averaged 11.39 fouls and 2.06 cards per team. Against qualifying opposition the pattern reversed. In 25/26 they had 51.3% of the ball, 14.17 shots, 6.83 corners and 26.0 crosses per match, and only 1.17 cards. Paunović’s 4-2-3-1 has two competitive matches: 30.0% possession and seven shots at Wembley; 67.1%, 19 shots, 13 corners and 41 crosses at home to Latvia. The crossing volume was built around Vlahović and Mitrović, at least one of whom started in 13 of the 16 competitive matches in the record, and neither plays. Jović has five starts, and Tadić, predicted in the number-ten role, last played for Serbia at Euro 2024. What Paunović’s Serbia look like against a League A side at home is unrecorded; the nearest evidence is two friendlies against Spain and Mexico, lost 0–3 and 1–5.

Jovanović’s Greece were a 4-2-3-1 that defended well a division down. In League B they allowed 8.33 shots and 1.83 on target per match, conceded 4 in 6, three of them in one match against England, and had 48.2% of the ball. In qualifying they had more of the ball and defended worse: 57.0% possession and 15.83 shots, but 5.00 shots on target and 2.00 goals conceded per match, with Denmark scoring three twice and Scotland three and two. They were also a heavier-fouling side in 25/26: 13.17 fouls per match, 16.33 away, against League A’s 11.39. The summer change is shape rather than personnel. Greece used the back three in the last two friendlies, and the previews disagree on whether it survives tonight. Nothing in Greece’s record comes from League A, and their last five matches produced two goals.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Ten months without a competitive match. Both sides last played for points in November 2025. The only evidence since is four friendlies each, and they are the weakest record on the card: Serbia 3–12, Greece 2–4.
  • Serbia’s League A rates belong to a coach and a shape that have gone. All 14 competitive matches to October 2025 were a back three under Stojković; Paunović’s 4-2-3-1 has two competitive matches. The two centre-forwards who between them started 13 of the 16, the first-choice centre-back and a goalkeeper with eight starts are all absent.
  • Greece’s rates were earned a division down. League B and a qualifying group with Belarus supplied their numbers. League A has given its eight most recent promoted sides 1.73 goals conceded per match, and Greece’s own shape is contested between the June back three and the 4-2-3-1 that produced every competitive figure.
  • The two 25/26 windows are not like-for-like. Serbia’s includes Latvia twice and Andorra; Greece’s includes Belarus twice. Only Serbia’s six League A matches share tonight’s division, and they are two years old.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap, BTTS and Serbia team goals. All of them turn on a Serbia attack that has not played together and a Greece shape not yet named. Least sensitive: Discipline and match corners. The official’s rates hold across four competitions, and both sides’ corner totals sit near the League A line in every window.
