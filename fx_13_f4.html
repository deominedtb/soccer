A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and, unusually for this slate, neither changed manager. Christian Ilzer continues at Hoffenheim after taking them to 5th in 2025–26 and into the Europa League — the club's 18th consecutive Bundesliga season and 127th in its history. Niko Kovač continues at Borussia Dortmund, who qualified for the Champions League and whose season-long defensive record is the most substantial single number on this card: 29 goals conceded across the campaign with 13 clean sheets. That combination — two retained coaches, two European qualifiers, no promotion on either side — makes this the closest thing to a stable fixture the sheet offers, and it is the reason last season's figures are worth more here than anywhere else on the board. Both sides have played once. Hoffenheim lost 3–2 away at Köln in a match they led twice: Adam Daghim put them ahead before the interval, Said El Mala equalised six minutes into the second half, Max Moerstedt missed a penalty, Ozan Kabak restored the lead, and Thijs Dallinga scored twice in ten minutes to complete the home comeback. Dortmund beat Hamburg 2–0 through Serhou Guirassy and the new signing Giannis Konstantelias, and arrive on consecutive wins. The head-to-head is close and recent: Hoffenheim won the most recent meeting 2–1 at home on 18 April 2026, but Dortmund lead the last five 3–1 with one draw.

  • Hoffenheim — medium turnover, weighted young, and the club record went out rather than in. Ilzer continues, so the 5th-place season was produced by the coach picking this team. The headline departure is Bazoumana Touré to Newcastle United for what is reported as a club-record fee; Grischa Prömel left for Stuttgart on a free, Muhammed Damar joined Wolfsburg, and Kevin Akpoguma and Ihlas Bebou also went. In: Mats Rots, a 20-year-old left-back from Twente; Cajetan Lenz, a midfielder from Bochum; Alessandro Vogt, the Switzerland Under-21 striker who scored 16 goals in 32 competitive appearances for St. Gallen; and Adam Daghim, made permanent from Red Bull Salzburg after a loan season at Wolfsburg — Daghim scored in the opener. Patrick Wimmer is described as a standout on his competitive debut. Gap: the Touré fee is reported only as a club record, with no figure published in anything reached, and no fee at all is published for Rots, Lenz, Vogt or Daghim — so a window whose direction is clear (a record sale reinvested across four younger players) cannot be quantified here, and no figure is estimated.
  • Dortmund — low turnover in the XI, high turnover in the structure above it. Kovač continues with his brother Robert as assistant, and the changes are institutional: Ole Book replaced Sebastian Kehl as sporting director, and Roderick Van der Ham arrived from Liverpool as head of analysis. Senior players Julian Brandt, Niklas Süle and Salih Özcan were allowed to leave at contract expiry — three experienced departures on frees rather than sales. In: Joey Veerman from PSV in central midfield, Giannis Konstantelias, Ethan Nwaneri on loan, and two 18-year-old South Americans, the Brazilian left-back Kauã Prates and the Ecuadorian attacking midfielder Justin Lerma. The predicted shape is a 3–4–2–1 built around Kobel, Anton, Bellingham, Nmecha, Beier, Sabitzer and Guirassy. Gap: no fee is published for Veerman, Konstantelias, Prates or Lerma, and no goalkeeper or centre-forward purchase was established — Guirassy and Kobel are continuity, not recruitment.
  • Suspensions / absences. This is the clearest absence picture on the slate and it runs one way. Ilzer is reported to have no injured or suspended players before his first home match of the season. Dortmund lose two: Konstantelias, who scored in the opener, suffered an anterior cruciate ligament injury and is expected to miss several months, and Samuele Inácio is serving a two-match Bundesliga suspension that rules him out of this trip. A summer signing who had just scored and a suspended squad player, against a fully available home side.
  • Sample. One Bundesliga match each; the season began on 28 August 2026 and this is matchday 2. Hoffenheim: 0 points, beaten 3–2 at Köln after leading twice, with a missed penalty in it. Dortmund: 3 points from a 2–0 win over Hamburg, and reported as arriving on consecutive victories, which implies a cup or friendly result alongside it. Behind those 90 minutes sit two full seasons of continuity — 5th and a Champions League place — which on this card is a genuinely usable baseline rather than a historical curiosity, because the coaches and most of the personnel are the same.
  • Referee. Dr Matthias Jöllenbeck, confirmed for this match with Dominik Schaal and Jonas Weickenmeier as assistants and Lars Erbst as fourth official. His record comes with a conflict that is logged rather than resolved: one database gives 206 matches at 3.97 yellow cards and 0.09 red cards per game, another 839 yellows and 19 reds across 232 fixtures, which works out at 3.62 yellows per game. Both were reached; neither is corroborated, so the honest range is roughly 3.6 to 4.0 yellows a game. His 2025–26 Bundesliga subset is small and points lower on cards and sharply higher on penalties: 6 matches, 19 yellows and 1 red for 3.3 cards a match, but 4 penalties in those 6 matches. Gap: his fouls-per-card ratio is unpublished, and the penalty figure rests on six matches, which is too small to carry weight on its own.

B · Style & early-season reality

Hoffenheim are the more interesting half of this fixture precisely because their summer was a reinvestment rather than a rebuild. A club-record sale — Touré to Newcastle — was answered with a 20-year-old left-back, a Bochum midfielder, a Swiss Under-21 striker with 16 goals in 32, and the permanent signing of a forward who had spent the previous season on loan elsewhere. That is a squad getting younger at the edges while keeping its coach and its spine, and the 5th-place finish behind it was Ilzer's own work. The opening match is the one piece of 2026–27 evidence and it cuts both ways: leading twice away from home says the attack functions, conceding three in the second half and missing a penalty says the game was lost rather than simply given away. The predicted 4–4–2 — Baumann; Coufal, Kabak, Hajdari, Bernardo; Conté, Burger, Avdullahu, Daghim; Hlozek, Lemperle — is a recognisable Ilzer side with the new arrivals integrated rather than transplanted. Gap: Hoffenheim's own goals-for and goals-against columns for 2025–26 were not obtainable, nor any xG, shot volume, foul rate or card rate, so the 5th place is a result without its underlying series attached.

Dortmund's profile is the opposite and better documented in the one dimension that matters most here. Twenty-nine goals conceded across a full season with 13 clean sheets is a genuinely strong defensive record, and it was produced by the coach still in charge, behind a back line whose predicted shape — Kobel in goal, Anton, Gadou and Svensson across a three, Ryerson and the midfield screen ahead — is continuity rather than reconstruction. What changed at Dortmund this summer sat above and around the team: a new sporting director, a new head of analysis from Liverpool, and three senior players leaving on expiring contracts. The recruitment was young and midfield-weighted, with Veerman the one established name. Against that, the two absences both bite in the attacking third: Konstantelias, who scored on matchday 1, is out for months with a cruciate injury, and Inácio is suspended. Gap: Dortmund's goals-scored column, xG, shot volume, foul rate and card rate were not obtainable, so the attacking half of their profile has no series behind it — only Guirassy's continued presence and a 2–0 opener. For league context, the 2025–26 Bundesliga produced 990 goals at 3.24 per match, which is the one baseline available on this page against which a 2.5 line can be read.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • This is the one fixture on the built cards where last season's numbers still describe the teams playing. Both coaches continue, neither club is promoted, and neither spine was dismantled — which makes Dortmund's 29 conceded and 13 clean sheets the most transferable statistic on the board, and makes the 1.37 BTTS price the most questionable one.
  • Hoffenheim's opening defeat is easy to over-read in either direction. They led twice away from home and missed a penalty, then conceded twice in ten minutes. As a diagnosis of the defence it is one match; as evidence the attack functions it is also one match. Ilzer's 5th-place season is the larger sample and its goal columns were not obtainable.
  • Dortmund's two absences are both attacking and both specific: Konstantelias, who scored on matchday 1, is out for months with a cruciate injury, and Inácio is suspended for this match. Their defensive record, which is what the BTTS and totals rows turn on, is unaffected by either.
  • The referee's own statistics contradict each other — 3.97 yellows a game in one database, 3.62 in another, 3.3 across six Bundesliga matches last season — and no fouls-per-card ratio is published anywhere reached. The card line is 3.5, which sits inside that spread, so the discipline row cannot be resolved by the official's record alone.
  • Most sensitive: both teams to score at 1.37, which prices a second consecutive Dortmund concession against a defence that kept 13 clean sheets in 34 matches under the same coach. Least sensitive: the result row, where a genuinely close 2.60/3.80/2.45 is supported by two stable coaching regimes, a recent home win in the head-to-head and a documented pair of away absences.

E · Short market-type guide

  • The moneyline is more robust than the goals markets here because the result is the one question this fixture's continuity actually answers: two retained coaches, a 5th-place side at home against a Champions League qualifier, and a head-to-head that has gone both ways in the last five.
  • The handicap is more robust than both-teams-to-score because −1 at 4.45 and +1 at 1.55 are priced consistently with Dortmund's defensive record, while BTTS at 1.37 is priced against it — the same 29-goals-conceded season cannot support a short margin and a short both-score price at once.
  • Totals are less robust than either result-family row because only one side of the equation has a season-long series behind it. Dortmund's 29 conceded and 13 clean sheets are documented; Hoffenheim's goals for and against were not obtainable, leaving a 1.42 over price resting on one five-goal match and a league average of 3.24.
  • The corner race is more robust than the corner total for the usual reason and one specific to this match: a near-even race at 1.85 against 2.15 follows from a near-even moneyline, whereas the 9.5 line needs a volume baseline that does not exist for either club.
  • Where the book has priced indifference — 2.60 against 2.45, and a corner race at 1.85 against 2.15 — it is describing a genuinely balanced fixture, and it is the only such case on the built cards where that balance rests on two full seasons of stable evidence rather than on nobody knowing anything yet.
