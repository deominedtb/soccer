A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Racing Santander are promoted, and the absence behind it is long: they came up after a fourth consecutive season in the Segunda División, returning to the top flight for the first time since 2012 — fourteen years away, the longest absence of any promoted club on this slate. Rayo Vallecano are not promoted but have changed manager in a way that ties directly into another fixture on this board: Íñigo Pérez left for Villarreal, who appear at f21, and Beñat San José has taken over at Vallecas. Racing, by contrast, kept the coach who took them up — José Alberto López, described as a manager who trusts a settled group and rotates little — which is the same configuration as Elversberg and Paderborn on the German cards: promotion without a coaching change layered on top. Rayo sit 19th with no points and a goal difference of −4 after losing 5–2 away to champions Barcelona in their most recent match. Conflict logged: the same preview describes Racing as 14th on no points with −1, and in the next line as having won one, drawn one and lost one of three matches including a 3–2 home win over Elche — those cannot both be true, and neither is corroborated, so this card treats Racing's start as unestablished. The clubs last met in 2020, in the second tier, when Rayo won both meetings.

  • Rayo Vallecano — new coach, but the squad is deliberately unchanged. San José inherits a group the club has explicitly kept together: the reporting is that the bulk of the squad remains under contract and the block that produced one of the most important campaigns in the club's history stays intact. The completed business is small and mostly conversions of existing arrangements: the purchase option on Nobel Mendy was exercised, tying him to 2030, and Jozhua Vertrouwd was made permanent after a loan from Castellón carrying an obligation to buy. One arrival is specifically a cover signing — an Indonesian international owned by Como, brought in to cover the long-term absence of the goalkeeper Augusto Batalla. Reported interest, not transfers: Chelsea are described as negotiating for the full-back Pep Chavarría at around €21m and Hull City — who appear at f15 on this slate — as pursuing the centre-back Nobel Mendy at around €20m. Neither is recorded as completed and neither is treated as a departure here. Gap: no fee is published for the Mendy option or the Vertrouwd purchase.
  • Racing Santander — promoted, and the recruitment went straight into the goalkeeper and the spine. José Alberto continues. The largest fee is for a goalkeeper, which is unusual and directly answers the position the research spec asks about first: Julen Agirrezabala from Athletic Club for a reported €4.5m, the club's biggest transfer of the window. Two central figures from the promotion, Pablo Ramón and Asier Villalibre, were retained permanently. Out: Suleiman Camara Sanneh, Mario García Alvear, Javier Castro Urdín and Samuel Calera Ortiz. B · Style & early-season reality

Rayo are a continuity squad under a discontinuous manager. The club's stated approach this summer was to keep the group that delivered one of its best modern campaigns, and the transfer activity supports that: two purchase options exercised, one goalkeeping cover signing, no significant sales completed. What has changed is the person picking the team, after Íñigo Pérez left for Villarreal. Two matches into San José's tenure the side is bottom-third with no points and a −4 goal difference, the most recent result a 5–2 defeat at the champions — a scoreline that says more about the opponent than the system, though conceding five is conceding five. The one bright statistic is Camello's three goals from five shots on target, which suggests the attack is finding conversions even while the team loses. Gap: the 2025–26 figures available cover 24 of 38 matches, and no xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was obtainable, so nothing here describes Rayo's pressing height or territorial profile.

Racing are the more coherent promoted project on this slate. The pattern of recruitment is unusually legible for a newly promoted club: the biggest fee went on a goalkeeper from an established LaLiga side, two centre-backs arrived from Juventus, the two most important players from the promotion season were retained on permanent terms, and the marquee signing is a returning senior name in Canales. That is a squad reinforced in exactly the positions that decide whether a promoted team survives, under the coach who won promotion and in the same 4–2–3–1. Against that, the honest limitation on this card is severe: their start to the season could not be established, because the only source reached contradicts itself within a single preview. A promoted side's first three matches are the whole of its top-flight evidence, and this card does not have them. Gap: no Segunda goal columns, no xG, no foul or card rate, and no reliable current form for Racing.
