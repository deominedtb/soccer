A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Rochdale are the promoted side, and they came up the hard way. They finished 2nd in the 2025–26 National League on 106 points — 33W–7D–6L, 88 scored and 41 conceded, +47, with a home record of 18–2–3 and an away one of 15–5–3, then won promotion at Wembley on 10 May 2026: 2–0 down to Boreham Wood, level at 2–2 after extra time, through 3–1 on penalties. It is their first League Two season since 2022–23. The division's usual treatment of sides up from the National League is the live question, and Rochdale's version of it is stark: 5 scored and 10 conceded in five, 22nd, one win, and every one of those five goals was scored at home. Walsall are not promoted — they finished 13th in League Two in 2025–26 with 42 scored and 36 conceded after leading the division at Christmas and then taking 22 points in the entire second half of the season, a collapse that cost Mat Sadler his job in March and left Darren Byfield in charge to the end. They have started this season unbeaten — 2W–3D–0L, 9 points, 5th, 8 scored and 4 conceded, with three clean sheets in five.

B · Style & early-season reality

Walsall have started this season doing the one thing they could not do last season: defending, and in particular defending at Bescot. The 2025–26 club they are trying not to be led League Two at Christmas and then took 22 points from the entire second half, with an eight-match home winless run between Boxing Day and 21 March — a home record so poor that the club's away tally of 36 points was its best since 2015–16. Lee Grant's summer was spent on that end: a new goalkeeper in Jed Ward, two free-transfer defenders in Connolly and Dixon, and the result is four goals conceded in five matches with three clean sheets. What the same five matches also show is an attack that works in bursts rather than continuously — 3, 0, 3, 2, 0 — because the forwards who left (Matt, Gordon) were not replaced one-for-one, with Dallas and Smith arriving from Barnsley and Harrogate. Two 0–0 draws in five, one of them at home, is not the profile of a side that guarantees goals at either end. Gap: no xG, shot volume or possession data was obtainable, so the press and the build-up are described here from results and personnel, not from measurement.

Rochdale are the more legible side and the more alarming one, because the legibility comes from a single split. They arrive from a National League season of genuine quality — 106 points, 88 goals, +47, and a Wembley comeback from 2–0 down — but the manager who built that is at Stockport County, and Ian Watson has had the job since 9 June. What the five League Two matches have produced is a side that still functions at home and disappears away: all five goals scored have come at Spotland, and both away trips ended 3–0 and 1–0 without a goal. The 3–0 over Shrewsbury shows the attack is real at this level in the right conditions; the 1–4 against Gillingham, in the same building six days later, shows how little the defence is currently absorbing, and it cost them Ed Francis to a red card. Two of the six summer signings (Maguire, Hayes) are not yet fit and a third (Jenkins) is long-term absent, so the centre-back rebuild that Flint and Maguire were meant to constitute is running at half strength. Gap: no xG or foul series for either club, and Rochdale's only full-season baseline is from a division below this one.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Rochdale's only full-season numbers were made in a different division. 88 scored and 41 conceded, 106 points and a +47 difference came in the National League; the League Two sample is five matches long and reads 5 and 10. Any goals argument leaning on the 88 is leaning across a promotion.
  • The manager who won that promotion has left. Jimmy McNulty went to Stockport County — who are also on this slate — and Ian Watson took over on 9 June. The attacking record being quoted belongs to a coaching staff that is no longer at the club, which is the standard trap with a promoted side and is unusually literal here.
  • Walsall's home record is the number most likely to be misread in either direction. Last season Bescot produced an eight-match winless run between Boxing Day and 21 March; this season it has produced a 0–0 and a 3–2 under a different head coach with a new goalkeeper and two new defenders. Neither the old figure nor the two-match new one is a reliable guide.

Maguire and Hayes are back in training but not ready, Jenkins is long-term absent, Beckwith is out for weeks, Bilongo is unavailable and Francis is suspended. The side that takes the field is not the side the window was built to produce, and the defensive half of that window is the part most affected.
