A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted; both were in the Premier League last season and both changed manager this summer, which is what gives this derby its shape. Fulham finished 9th-or-thereabouts on 52 points — 15 wins, 7 draws, 16 losses, a goal difference of +4 — and replaced Marco Silva with Álvaro Arbeloa, the former Real Madrid coach. Crystal Palace finished 15th on 45 points — 11 wins, 12 draws, 15 losses, −10 — and replaced Oliver Glasner, who left at the end of his contract, with Pierre Sage on a three-year deal; despite that league position they carry Europa League football into this season, so Sage's brief is explicitly to build a squad that can handle both. Two matchweeks in, neither has a point. Fulham lost to Chelsea on the opening weekend and then at Sunderland; Palace lost at Everton and were beaten 4–1 by Manchester City, who appear on this slate at f10. The head-to-head at this ground is the most extreme record on the board: Crystal Palace have not lost at Craven Cottage since 2005, a run that includes a 3–0 win in the quarter-final of the 2025 FA Cup, a competition Palace went on to win.

B · Style & early-season reality

Fulham's summer reads as a deliberate stylistic reset rather than a reinforcement. Arbeloa replaced a manager who had been at the club for years, and the recruitment was explicitly aligned to the incoming coach: two Real Madrid academy players he had already worked with, a young permanent striker signing, and a stated preference for young, technically gifted players suited to a possession-based approach. Set against that, the departures were experience — Jiménez, Diop, Wilson, and possibly the captain — which is a squad being made younger and more ball-dominant at the same time as its instructions change. Last season's 52 points and +4 goal difference were produced by a different coach with a different idea, so they describe the club's level rather than its method. Two matches under the new system have produced no points against Chelsea and Sunderland, the latter the side with the joint-best expected-goals-per-shot defence in the division last season. Gap: no xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Fulham in either season, so nothing here quantifies whether the possession-based intent has translated into territory.

Palace arrive in the more complicated situation of the two, and it is a calendar problem as much as a football one. A 15th-place finish with 45 points and twelve draws describes a side that was hard to beat and rarely won — and it has now been given European football, a new manager on a three-year contract, and a substantially rebuilt squad. The money moved in both directions at scale: £52m out for Lacroix and £22m for Johnson, against roughly £83m of published incoming fees across five signings plus two loans. Sage's brief, as reported, is to make that squad work across two competitions rather than to consolidate. The early evidence is two defeats, one of them 4–1 to Manchester City, which is a scoreline that tells you more about the opponent than about the system. What survives all of that as genuinely relevant is the venue record: Palace have not lost at Craven Cottage in twenty-one years, and that run has outlasted several managers on both sides. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate or card rate for Palace was obtainable in either season, and no injury list at all, which for a club now playing Thursdays is a material omission this card cannot fill.
