A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted. Kazakhstan are relegated from League B: fourth in Group B3 in 2024–25 on 1 point, 0–15 (W0 D1 L5, behind Norway, Austria and Slovenia), directly. In World Cup qualifying they were fourth of five in Group J on 8 points, 9–13 (W2 D2 L4, behind Belgium, Wales and North Macedonia; both wins against Liechtenstein). Their last League C edition was 2022–23, when they won Group C3 (W4 D1 L1, 8–6, under Magomed Adiyev, with Slovakia in the group). Faroe Islands stayed in League C: third in Group C4 in 2024–25 on 6 points, 5–6 (W1 D3 L2, behind North Macedonia and Armenia), as they were third in Group C1 in 2022–23 (W2 D2 L2, 7–10) after winning Group D1 in 2020–21. In World Cup qualifying they were third of five in Group L on 12 points, 11–9 (W4 L4, behind Croatia and Czechia), including 2–1 at home to Czechia and 4–0 at home to Montenegro (ESPN standings and match record). The structural headline is the coach change on one side only, on top of a two-level gap in last season’s opposition: Kazakhstan played 12 of their 14 competitive matches in the window against sides now in League A or B, Faroe Islands 8 of 14 against sides now in League C or D. The seven sides relegated from League B into League C in 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 1.81 points per away match over 21 (W11 D5 L5), scoring 1.76 and conceding 0.95; on matchday one they went W5 D1 L1, and three of the seven won their group. At the grounds of sides that had stayed in League C, those relegated visitors went W9 D4 L4 in 17, and out-cornered the host in 13 (baselines_0926.md; Faroe Islands 2–2 Armenia, October 2024, is one of the 17). All seven had scored in League B (2 to 7 goals each, ESPN standings); Kazakhstan arrive with none. Format: in 2026–27 League C group winners are promoted, runners-up play the fourth-placed League B sides in March 2027, and nobody is relegated from League C (Wikipedia’s 2026–27 pages; Sports Mole). B · Style & early-season reality

Klakstein has used a back three or five in all ten of his competitive matches (5-4-1 five times, 3-4-3 five), where Ericson’s last four had a back four (4-1-4-1 three times, 4-3-3 once). What the shape produces depends on the opponent. Against League A and B sides Faroe Islands had 30.9% of the ball, 6.83 shots to 17.33 and 3.00 corners to 7.83; against sides now in League C or D, 48.1%, 10.75 shots to 14.25 and 4.88 corners to 5.88, W4 D2 L2. At Tórsvøllur they cross more (22.14 per match, 18.79 overall) and win more corners (5.00 per match). Their matches are close and quiet early: 12 of 14 drawn or settled by one goal, five 0–0 half-times, 0.86 first-half goals per match against League C’s 1.15. They foul little and are booked little: 10.29 fouls and 1.64 cards per match, 6.26 fouls per card, no red card in 14. Set pieces carry part of the scoring: three of their 16 goals were penalties and two more were headers, and the preview press describes a compact block built on set pieces (News Directory 3; ASNEWS). The summer change falls in central midfield, where Hendriksson Olsen, Hansson and Andreasen made 16 starts, and in attack with Frederiksberg; the back line of the window (Edmundsson, Vatnhamar, Færø, Davidsen) is not named as absent anywhere. The only evidence of play since November is two June friendlies that are not in ESPN’s record.

Kazakhstan’s shape has followed the coach. Cherchesov’s League B side began in a back five (5-4-1 twice) and moved to 4-4-2 and 4-2-3-1; Aliyev’s qualifiers were back fives (5-4-1 three times, 3-4-2-1 twice); Baysufinov’s used a back four (4-2-3-1 twice, 4-3-3 once), as did the June friendly in Hungary. Against League A and B sides they had 36.0% of the ball, 8.58 shots to 19.17 and 3.08 corners to 7.67, and scored 0.25 per match; against Liechtenstein, 69.2%, 20.50 shots to 3.00, 7.50 corners to 1.00 and 3.00 goals. They foul more and are booked more than Faroe Islands: 13.29 fouls and 2.50 cards per match at 5.31 fouls per card, two reds, and 4.00 cards per match in Baysufinov’s three qualifiers, with opponents booked 0.93. They cross little (11.57 per match). Van ’t Schip’s shape is a CV, not a match. At Ajax in 2023–24 (34 matches in ESPN’s record) he used 4-2-3-1 twelve times, 4-3-3 ten and a back three ten times, with 58.7% of the ball. At Armenia in 2025 he used 4-2-3-1 at home to Georgia (41.0%, lost 0–3) and 5-4-1 in Georgia (35.5%, lost 1–6) in the League B/C play-off; his two friendlies there (2–5 in Kosovo, 2–2 in Montenegro) have no formation in ESPN’s record.
