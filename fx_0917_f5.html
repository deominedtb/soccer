Neither club is promoted. Crystal Palace finished 15th in the 2025–26 Premier League — 11-12-15, 41 scored, 51 conceded, 45 points — but won the Conference League, beating Rayo Vallecano 1–0 in the final on 27 May 2026; this is their first Europa League league-phase match. Lech won the 2025–26 Ekstraklasa — 16-12-6, 62 scored, 45 conceded, 60 points — and reached the last 16 of last season’s Conference League. The structural headline is that Palace’s European record belongs to Oliver Glasner and a defence that has since been sold, while Lech arrive with the same coach, a largely intact attack and a European sample that includes both a 7–0 and a home 1–4.

B · Style & early-season reality

Palace under Glasner were a low-possession, three-at-the-back side that created better chances than their league position suggests: 45.9% of the ball, 1.53 xG for and 1.37 against, but only 1.08 goals per match and a 32% failed-to-score rate; they won 4.18 corners and conceded 5.24. In Europe the same side went W9 D3 L3 (26–12) in 15 Conference League matches, with 60% of them over 2.5. Under Sage the shape is still 3-4-2-1, but the four league matches show a side conceding far more: 42% possession, 17.8 shots and 2.04 xG against, 1.50 goals conceded per first half, and only 2.25 corners won. Four of the back-line options (Disasi, Ahanor, Tomiyasu, Chilwell) arrived in August or on deadline day, and Timber joined on 2 September, so how the new defence fits is mostly a CV.

Lech under Frederiksen are a high-volume, front-foot Polish side: 57.6% possession, 17.4 shots and 1.90 xG per league match last season, 6.12 corners won, and one of the lowest card rates in their league (1.68 against a division average of 4.11 per match). This season the numbers are stronger (2.05 xG for, 0.95 against, 6.86 corners) in a 4-4-2. Europe has been uneven: 4–1 at AGF followed by 1–4 at home, then 13 goals in four matches against Klaksvík and Thun. The summer changed the goalkeeper (Lis for Mrozek) and added Palma on the left; Sayyadmanesh, Kozubal, Douglas and Gholizadeh are unavailable tonight.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Palace’s European title belongs to a different coach and defence. Glasner left in May; Lacroix, Muñoz and (in January) Guéhi were sold, and the replacements arrived between August and deadline day.
  • Palace’s league season was far worse than their chance data. 15th with 41 goals from 1.53 xG per match; under Sage the xG has turned negative in four matches.
  • Palace are missing their centre-forward and goalkeeper. Mateta and Henderson are out; Sarr and Nketiah are only back in training.
  • Lech’s European evidence is uneven and the opposition modest. A home 1–4 to AGF sits beside 13 goals against Klaksvík and Thun, and four Lech players are absent tonight.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, handicap and Palace team goals. Least sensitive: discipline, where the official’s strictness and both clubs’ low card rates are long published series.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Discipline is more robust than the 1X2 here because Očenáš’s 5.53 and 5.00 yellows per match and both clubs’ low card rates are two-season series, whereas the result depends on a Palace defence assembled in the last month.
  • A Lech team-goals reading is more robust than a Palace one because Lech’s xG (1.90, then 2.05) and scoring rate hold across seasons, while Palace’s attack is without Mateta and ran 17 goals below its xG last season.
  • Totals are more robust than the handicap because Lech’s 68% and 71% over-2.5 rates and Palace’s 60% in Europe point the same way, whereas neither side’s margin is stable.
  • Lech’s side of the halves ledger is more robust than Palace’s because it has held across two seasons (1.00/0.47, then 0.86/0.29), while Palace’s first-half concessions moved from 0.66 to 1.50 on four matches.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: Palace’s back three and whether Sarr starts. The first decides whether Sage’s four-match defensive numbers or last season’s describe the side; the second whether Palace’s attack resembles the one that won the Conference League. Both settle on the team sheet.
