A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Paris FC are not promoted this season but they are only one year removed from it: they came up from Ligue 2 in 2025 alongside Lorient and Metz, and survived comfortably, finishing 11th on 44 points from 34 matches — 11 wins, 11 draws, 12 defeats, 47 scored, 50 conceded, goal difference −3. Ligue 1 has treated recent promoted sides harshly at the bottom of the table but Paris FC were never in that fight. Nice were: they finished 16th on 32 points with 7 wins, 11 draws and 16 defeats, 37 scored and 60 conceded for −23, and had to play the relegation barrage against Saint-Étienne to stay up. Both clubs then changed coach, and both are on one point after a goalless matchday-one draw — Paris FC 0–0 with Troyes, Nice 0–0 at Lorient — which leaves them 11th and 12th with no goals scored and no goals conceded between them. The structural headline is the money. Paris FC, backed by Red Bull and the Arnault family and entering the second stage of an openly stated project, spent roughly €79m in fees this summer against under €7m of income. Nice, under financial constraint, sold both centre-forwards and their holding midfielder, banked around €31m, and paid a single transfer fee of €1.7m.

  • Paris FC — turnover high and almost entirely inbound, weighted to attack. Liam Rosenior was appointed on 7 July 2026 on a two-year contract, taking over on 9 July, and succeeds Antoine Kombouaré, who terminated his contract after keeping the promoted side up; Kombouaré had himself replaced Stéphane Gilli during the 2025–26 season. Rosenior arrives having left Strasbourg for Chelsea in January and been dismissed by Chelsea after 104 days — so he is both new to this club and returning to a league he left seven months ago, and the Chelsea side that sacked him is on this same card. The window is five fees, all incoming: Patrick Zabi, midfielder, 19, from Reims at €25m on 15 June; Diego Coppola, defender, 22, from Brighton at €17.5m on 16 July; Pablo Pagis, forward, 23, from Lorient at €15m on 17 July; Luca Koleosho, forward, 21, from Burnley at €14m on 29 July; and Lassine Sinayoko, forward, 26, from Auxerre at €7.5m on 21 July. Three forwards for €36.5m and one centre-back for €17.5m. Marshall Munetsi returned from a loan at Wolverhampton, along with three other loanees. Outgoing: Willem Geubbels to Lecce at €4.6m, Alimami Gory to Abha Club at €2m, Tuomas Ollila to Guingamp at €200k, Mathys Tourraine to Pau and Lohann Doucet to Vitória Guimarães for undisclosed fees, with Rémy Riou (goalkeeper), Timothée Kolodziejczak (centre-back), Sofiane Alakouch and Lamine Gueye leaving at the end of their contracts. No goalkeeper was signed to replace Riou.
  • Nice — turnover very high and almost entirely outbound, including both strikers. Olivier Pantaloni was appointed on a two-year deal, announced 17 June 2026 with one source dating the formal appointment to 27 June, replacing Claude Puel, who was not retained after the club scraped survival through the play-off against Saint-Étienne. The sales list is long and it removes the goalscoring: Mohamed-Ali Cho to Hull City at €15.15m, Charles Vanhoutte, the holding midfielder, to Feyenoord at €5.5m, Jérémie Boga to Juventus at €4.8m, Terem Moffi to Hamburg at €2m, Tom Louchet to PAOK at €2m, Gabin Bernardeau to Lorient at €1m, Mattia Viti to Sampdoria at €300k, plus Rareș Ilie to Mantua, Aliou Baldé to St. Gallen, Kaïl Boudache free to Lyon and Kevin Carlos on loan to Cagliari. Against that, one paid transfer: Gauthier Hein from Metz at €1.7m. Nathan Ngoumou arrived from Borussia Mönchengladbach on undisclosed terms and Laurent Abergel came free from Lorient. Elye Wahi is back — footmercato records it as a loan return from Eintracht Frankfurt, while another report describes a paid loan of €1.5m with a €15m purchase option; the two descriptions are logged rather than reconciled. Juma Bah returned from a Manchester City loan among six loan returns. Conflict logged: Cho’s fee is reported at both €15.15m and €13m.
  • Suspensions / absences. Nice are without Laurent Abergel, who has a cruciate ligament tear — which is the cruellest available fact in this fixture, because Abergel was one of only three permanent additions and was brought in free from Lorient to sit in the hole Vanhoutte’s €5.5m sale created. No unavailable players are listed for Paris FC, and no suspensions surfaced on either side.
  • Sample. One league match each, matchday 2. Paris FC drew 0–0 with Troyes; Nice drew 0–0 at Lorient. Both sides therefore arrive with a clean sheet, no goal, and one point, and the entire competitive evidence for both new coaches is ninety minutes in which nothing was scored. Rosenior has taken charge of one match for this club and Pantaloni one for his; between them they have overseen 180 minutes and zero goals. That is as close to no sample as a fixture on this card gets.
  • Referee. Jérémy Stinat, reported as the designated official. His career Ligue 1 rate is 3.72 yellow cards a match against a national average of 3.53, so he is a modestly above-average booker across a long record. The number that separates him is dismissals: 7 red cards in 16 matches in 2025–26, about 0.43 per game, which is a strikingly high rate, and it is not driven by second cautions — he issues a second yellow to the same player 0.08 times a match, below the national 0.09. That combination means the reds are predominantly straight ones, which is a different and more volatile mechanism than an official who accumulates bookings into a sending-off. Gaps: a fouls-per-card ratio for Stinat could not be sourced, and neither could a penalties-per-game figure, so how early he reaches for the card relative to the number of fouls he calls, and how he reads contact in the box, are both unestablished here.

B · Style & early-season reality

Paris FC’s 2025–26 was a competent, unspectacular first season back: 47 scored and 50 conceded across 34 matches, eleven draws, a goal difference of −3, and a comfortable eleventh. That was Kombouaré’s work — pragmatic, organised, low-risk — and it is not what has been bought since. Rosenior is described as setting up in a 4–2–3–1 built around controlled possession and short passing, and the recruitment fits that far better than it fits the previous regime: a 19-year-old midfielder at €25m, a 22-year-old centre-back at €17.5m, and three forwards for €36.5m. This is a squad being converted from a survival unit into something intended to press and hold the ball, financed by owners who have made no secret of the ambition. The evidence that any of it works is one goalless draw with Troyes. It is worth stating plainly that Rosenior’s last two jobs ended in a January departure from Strasbourg and a dismissal from Chelsea after 104 days, so what is known about how his teams play in Ligue 1 comes from half a season at a different club with different players.

Nice are the more legible case and the bleaker one. Sixteenth, 60 conceded in 34 — 1.76 a game, comfortably the worst defensive record of any side on this fixture — and a relegation play-off survived rather than a season completed. Puel was not kept; Pantaloni, 59, arrives with a two-year brief that is explicitly reconstruction. What he has to reconstruct with is a squad that has had its scoring removed: Cho sold, Moffi sold, Kevin Carlos loaned out, Boga sold, and 37 goals last season already the second-lowest figure in this fixture’s recent history. The one permanent fee spent all summer was €1.7m. The screening midfielder, Vanhoutte, was sold for €5.5m and his free-transfer replacement, Abergel, has a cruciate ligament tear. Elye Wahi’s return — whatever the precise mechanism — is the closest thing to an attacking answer, and he is a player whose recent career has moved between clubs rather than settled at one. The 0–0 at Lorient is genuinely consistent with all of this: a side that can be organised into not conceding and has very little idea where a goal comes from.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Paris FC’s 47 goals and 11th place were produced by Antoine Kombouaré’s pragmatic survival side. That coach has gone, the coach before him had already gone mid-season, and the current one arrived on 7 July having been sacked by Chelsea after 104 days. Last season’s output describes a team, a method and a manager none of which are present today.
  • Nice’s 60 goals conceded in 34 matches — 1.76 a game, and a relegation play-off against Saint-Étienne to survive — is the worst defensive baseline in this fixture, and the summer subtracted from the defence rather than adding to it. Viti was sold, Vanhoutte’s screening role was sold for €5.5m, and the free-transfer replacement Abergel is out with a cruciate ligament tear. The 0–0 at Lorient is the only counter-evidence and it is one match.
  • The goalscoring on the away side has been removed almost entirely. Cho (€15.15m to Hull City), Moffi (€2m to Hamburg) and Boga (€4.8m to Juventus) all left, and Kevin Carlos was loaned to Cagliari, against one paid signing all summer at €1.7m. Any total, any BTTS leg and any team-goals read that carries Nice’s 37 goals forward is carrying a figure produced by players who have gone.
  • The head-to-head is too small to use and points nowhere in particular: eight meetings in total, Paris FC ahead 4–3, a 1–0 Paris FC win in March 2026 and a 1–1 draw in September 2025. Both of those results predate both current coaches and both squads. Rosenior and Pantaloni have met exactly once, and drew.
  • Most sensitive: BTTS and corners — both require attacking and territorial patterns from two squads assembled within the last ten weeks, one by spending €79m and the other by selling eleven players. Least sensitive: the cards family, because Stinat’s 3.72-per-match career yellow rate and 0.43 straight reds per game are properties of the referee and are unaffected by either club’s summer.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Cards are more robust than corners here because the disciplinary input is Stinat’s long-run record — 3.72 yellows a match against a 3.53 league average, and an unusually high straight-red rate — whereas corner volume needs a territorial identity from a Paris FC that has had one match under Rosenior and a Nice that has lost eleven players.
  • The under on totals is better supported than the over because Nice’s 1.09 goals a game last season has since had Cho, Moffi, Boga and Kevin Carlos removed from it, while the over depends on €36.5m of new Paris FC forwards who have played ninety goalless minutes together.
  • The three-way handicap is more usable than the 1X2 because it prices the shape of the game rather than its winner, and shape is the only thing both matchday-one results agree on: two 0–0s, two clean sheets, no goals.
  • The absence of per-team card lines in this fixture matters more than it usually would. Without them there is no way to read which side the book expects to do the fouling, and with no fouls-per-card figure available for Stinat either, the discipline family is well-supported on volume and silent on direction.
  • Where the book has priced indifference, read it as such. Home win by two or more and a one-goal draw both at exactly 3.85 on the handicap is a bookmaker saying it cannot tell an open game from a tight one — which is the correct posture toward two coaches with one match each, one squad bought for €79m and one sold for €31m.
