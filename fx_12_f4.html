A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted; they finished third and fifth in the same table. Real Madrid were 2nd on 86 points from 38 matches — 27 wins, 5 draws, 6 defeats, 77 scored, 35 conceded — eight points behind a Barcelona side that clinched the title by beating them 2–0 in May. Real Betis were 5th on 60 points with 15 wins, 15 draws, 8 defeats, 59 scored and 48 conceded; fifteen draws in thirty-eight is the number that defines their season, and it is the highest draw count of any club on this card. The structural headline is a manager change on one side only, and it is the biggest one on the slate: José Mourinho was announced on 11 June 2026 on a contract to 30 June 2029, arriving from Benfica with a reported €15m compensation, replacing Xabi Alonso, who had taken over in January and finished second. Manuel Pellegrini continues at Betis. The second thread is that two of the players Madrid released this summer went straight to tonight’s opponents: Fran García and Dani Ceballos are both Betis players now, and one of them starts. B · Style & early-season reality

Real Madrid’s 2025–26 was a second-place season of considerable quality — 77 scored, 35 conceded, six defeats — produced under a coach who took the job in January and has since been replaced. Mourinho’s arrival on 11 June is the single largest stylistic variable on this board, and what he has been given to work with is a defence rebuilt from the outside in: Dumfries at right-back for a reported €20m, Konaté free from Liverpool, Cucurella from Chelsea, all three in front of Courtois and alongside Huijsen. That was already a new unit before the injuries; with Militão, Asencio, Mendy and Tchouaméni all unavailable it is now the only unit available. Three wins from three says the arrangement is working so far, and it is worth being precise about what that evidence covers: three matches, opponents not established here, under a manager whose recognisable priorities are structure and control of transitions rather than the open, high-scoring football that produced 77 goals last season. The attacking personnel — Mbappé, Vinícius, Bellingham, Güler — carried over; Rodrygo and Endrick are injured. Gap: no xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for either club, so nothing here is argued from an underlying metric.

Betis are the opposite profile in every respect: same manager, same spine, and a season-long record that can be read at face value because Pellegrini produced it. Fifteen draws in thirty-eight matches, 59 scored and 48 conceded, fifth place — that is a side that is difficult to beat and difficult to separate from, and the market’s 5.00 on the draw tonight sits oddly against it. The summer added Fran García from Madrid at left-back, Bernal in midfield and Parrott in attack, which is reinforcement rather than reconstruction. What has been removed is midfield depth, not by transfer but by injury: Ceballos and Lo Celso are both out, and the probable eleven runs Marc Roca and Bernal in front of the back four with Fornals ahead of them, and Antony, Cucho Hernández and Riquelme in the front three. The venue is the one piece of basic fixture information that could not be settled — two sources give the Benito Villamarín and one gives La Cartuja, and both grounds have hosted this club’s home matches in recent seasons, so the disagreement is plausible on its face and is left unresolved rather than picked. The same gap applies to Betis’s underlying numbers as to Madrid’s.
