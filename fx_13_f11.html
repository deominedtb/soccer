A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, both finished in the bottom half, and both changed manager — and between them they moved more money this summer than any other pair on this slate. Nottingham Forest finished 2025–26 on 44 points from 11 wins, 11 draws and 16 defeats, a goal difference of −3, and appointed Oliver Glasner, who left Crystal Palace at the end of his contract; Palace, and the manager who replaced him there, appear on this same slate at f9. Tottenham finished below them on 41 points from 10 wins, 11 draws and 17 defeats, −9, and are in Roberto De Zerbi's first full window after he arrived at the business end of last season. Forest's summer was defined by a single sale: Elliot Anderson to Manchester City for a reported club-record £116m, described as more than double the previous record and the largest sale in the club's history, after a season in which he starred for England at the World Cup — and Manchester City appear on this slate at f10. Tottenham's was defined by the opposite: a reported outlay above £350m across ten new faces. Two matchweeks in, neither has won, and Spurs have not scored at all. The head-to-head is the most one-directional recent record on the board: Forest have won the last four meetings, conceding one goal in total across them, including 3–0 at home in December 2025 and 3–0 away in March 2026.

Glasner is installing a 3–4–2–1, which the previews describe the squad as still adapting to — a system change on top of a manager change. B · Style & early-season reality

Forest are a side whose best player has been sold and whose shape is being rebuilt at the same time. The £116m for Anderson is the largest incoming fee on this slate by some distance, and the reinvestment was spread rather than concentrated — a £34m centre-back, a £21m midfielder, a £17m midfielder immediately loaned out, two frees and a late centre-forward. Glasner's 3–4–2–1 is the same structural shape he used at Crystal Palace, and the previews are explicit that the squad is still adapting to it. What is observable is a 2–2 draw at Liverpool in which Forest led twice, which suggests the attacking pattern functions even if the game management did not, and a home record this season that the sources describe unhelpfully and inconsistently. Last season's 44 points and −3 were compiled under different management — Forest used more than one manager during 2025–26 — so they set a level rather than describing a method. Gap: no xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Forest in either season.

Tottenham are the more extreme case on this board and possibly the most interesting single situation on the slate: a club that finished 17 defeats and −9 in 2025–26, spent more than £350m on ten players including a £100m midfielder and a £85m midfielder, sold the centre-back the defence was organised around, and has begun the season with two defeats and no goals. De Zerbi is a possession coach by reputation, and the recruitment — Tonali and Fernandes in central midfield, Savinho, Marmoush and Mudryk in the forward line, Robertson and Senesi as free-transfer experience — reads as a squad assembled to dominate the ball. Two matches is not enough to say whether it does; not scoring in either of them is the only 2026–27 evidence available, and it is against Brentford, who are taking 24 shots a game, and Newcastle. Richarlison's exclusion from the squad thins the forward options further. The honest reading is that this is the least-known team on the slate: the personnel are new, the manager is new to a full window, and the sample is 180 goalless minutes. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Tottenham either.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Tottenham's £350m squad has played 180 minutes and scored nothing. Ten of the players are new, the centre-back the defence was organised around has been sold to Atlético Madrid, and a forward has been left out of the registered squad. Every market row that assumes this attack functions is assuming something not yet observed.
  • Forest have sold the player their season was built on for £116m and are installing a new manager's 3–4–2–1. Their 44 points last season came under different management, and the previews say the squad is still adapting — so neither the table nor the shape is a reliable guide yet.
  • The four-match head-to-head record — four Forest wins, one Tottenham goal conceded in total, two 3–0 scorelines — is the most striking pattern on this board. It was also compiled by squads that have since turned over by ten players on one side and six on the other, and by managers who are no longer in either dugout.
  • One preview claim could not be reconciled: that Forest have lost both home matches this season, both to Leeds United, which does not fit a two-match league programme including a draw at Liverpool. It is recorded and not used.
  • Most sensitive: both teams to score at 1.60, which requires a goal from a side that has not scored this season and has scored once in four attempts against this opponent. Least sensitive: the discipline row — a confirmed official, a 17-match sample, a leniency ranking, a penalty rate and the only derivable fouls-per-card figure on the slate, all pointing the same way as a market that leans under.
