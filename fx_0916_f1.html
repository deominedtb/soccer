A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, and neither is new to this level of European football. Milan finished 5th in 2025–26 Serie A — 20 wins, 10 draws, 8 defeats, 53 scored, 35 conceded, 70 points — under Massimiliano Allegri, and qualified straight into this league phase. Benfica finished 2nd in 2025–26 Liga Portugal — 23 wins, 11 draws, no defeats, 74 scored, 25 conceded, 80 points — under José Mourinho, and reached this league phase through three Europa League qualifying rounds. The structural headline is that both clubs changed head coach inside the same week in June 2026, and that the two samples since are of very different sizes: Milan have four Serie A matches, three of them away; Benfica have twelve competitive matches, half of them European, but all against Portuguese league opposition or qualifying-round sides.

B · Style & early-season reality

Milan under Allegri were a back-three side that did not dominate the ball: Understat logged 2,809 of last season’s league minutes in a 3–5–2, with 51.9% possession, 13.2 shots for and 11.5 against per match, 10.1 fouls per match against a Serie A mean of 12.7 per team, and 4.26 corners per match, below the league’s 4.41. Seven of their 53 goals were penalties and six came from corners. Amorim has kept a back three but changed its shape and its share of the ball: all 376 logged minutes this season are a 3–4–2–1, possession has risen to 58.3%, and the chance data has levelled (1.43 xG for and against per match). What the summer changed underneath is the front line — Ramos in, Leão, Morata and Giménez out — and the evidence that it works together is four matches in which nothing was scored before half-time. Tonight’s probable eleven also rests two of the midfield starters.

Benfica last season were a high-possession side that generated volume: 57.7% of the ball, 2.04 xG and 6.0 shots on target per match, and 7.21 corners per match in a league averaging 4.71 per team, with 11.1 fouls per match below the Liga Portugal mean of 13.8. Under Marco Silva the direction is more of the same — 65.0% possession, 2.67 xG, 8.00 corners per league match — set up as a 4–2–3–1 with Palhinha at the base. But the back line that produced last season’s 0.81 xG against has lost Otamendi, António Silva and Dedić, and its replacements’ evidence against opposition of Milan’s level is a CV: Lenglet and Circati arrived this summer, Palhinha on 26 August, and the three qualifying ties were against St. Gallen, Hearts and AGF.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Last season’s numbers belong to two coaches who are no longer here. Allegri’s 3–5–2 and Mourinho’s Benfica produced every 2025–26 rate in the strip; Amorim has four matches and Silva twelve.
  • Benfica’s current sample is large but one-sided in opposition. Twelve matches, 40 goals scored, all against Liga Portugal clubs or qualifying-round sides; the 100% over-2.5 league record is six matches long.
  • Milan’s front line and Benfica’s back line were both rebuilt in the final weeks of the window. Leão left on 29 August and Hutchinson arrived on 31 August; Palhinha arrived on 26 August, after Dedić and António Silva had gone.
  • Selection is reported rotated on both sides and unconfirmed. Modrić and Rabiot rested, Pavlidis possibly benched, the Benfica goalkeeper undecided; Milan host Lecce on Sunday.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2 and handicap — two rotated elevens, two new coaches and margins made against weaker opponents. Least sensitive: Benfica’s corners and scoring, which have held across two coaches and two seasons.

E · Short market-type guide

  • BTTS is more robust than the handicap here because both clubs have scored in every competitive match this season (Milan 4 of 4, Benfica 12 of 12), whereas Benfica’s +30 goal difference across twelve matches was built against opposition nowhere near Milan’s level.
  • A Benfica team-goals reading is more robust than a Milan one because Benfica’s xG has held above 2.0 per match across two seasons and two coaches, while Milan’s attack is a new front line with no first-half goal in four matches.
  • Corners are more robust than the 1X2 because Benfica’s 7.21 and 8.00 corners per match are stable across coaches, while the result depends on two unconfirmed, rotated elevens.
  • Discipline is more robust than halves for anyone weighting the official, because Gillett’s 5.3 fouls per card is sourced over three Premier League seasons, whereas Milan’s second-half scoring pattern is four matches old — but his record contains no UEFA league-phase match.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: the two confirmed elevens. Whether Pavlidis, Modrić and Rabiot start decides how much of either side’s scoring record applies tonight; the team sheets, published an hour before kickoff, settle it.
