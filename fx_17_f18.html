A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Written for the two markets this board is playing today. The result and double-chance families are abbreviated to a line in section C and are not analysed in depth here.

Lincoln City are the promoted side, and they came up as champions rather than through the play-offs. They won the 2025–26 League One title with 103 points — 31W–10D–5L, 89 scored and 41 conceded, +48 — sealing automatic promotion with a 2–0 win over Doncaster Rovers on 21 April, alongside runners-up Cardiff City. This is their first season at this level in more than 60 years, and the division has so far treated them the way it usually treats a promoted side's attack: 4 scored and 6 conceded in five, 19th on 5 points, from a team that scored 89 a year ago. Preston are not promoted — this is their twelfth consecutive Championship season — and they are 24th on 3 points from 6, with 6 scored and 12 conceded. The single most important structural fact on this card is theirs: Preston have not kept a clean sheet in 23 competitive matches, a run going back to 7 February.

B · Style & early-season reality

Preston are the more extreme of these two profiles and the better documented. A club in its twelfth straight Championship season spent over £11m this summer — £6m of it on one midfielder from Tottenham — sold its centre-forward to Genoa, bought neither a goalkeeper nor a centre-back for a fee, and sits bottom of the division having conceded twelve goals in six matches. The clean-sheet run is the fact that gives the whole card its shape: 23 competitive matches without one, going back to 7 February, which spans the back half of last season and all of this one, two transfer windows and a substantially changed squad. That is not a five-match sample, it is a structural condition. At the other end they are not toothless — six goals in six, scoring in five of them, Callum Lang on 3, and a 2–1 win over Blackburn — but the pattern of their matches is consistent: five of six have finished with three or more goals, and five of six have had both teams score. Gap: no xG or shot data was obtainable, so this is read from results, the ledger and the clean-sheet run rather than from underlying numbers.

Lincoln are the quieter side and the harder one to price, because everything that made them champions has either been left behind or has not yet transferred. The 89 goals and 103 points of last season came under Michael Skubala, now at Bristol City, and the replacement is a joint appointment — Chris Cohen and Tom Shaw — working with a squad that was topped up on frees and undisclosed deals rather than rebuilt. The Championship return so far is 0.8 goals a game, which is the familiar promoted-side compression, but the defensive side has held up better than the attacking one: six conceded in five, and only two in two away matches, on a three-match unbeaten run. The venue split is interesting in the wrong direction for the usual reading — they have won away and drawn twice at home, with their better goal record on the road. The 0–4 at Bournemouth is explicitly not a form line: mass changes were made and the first-choice team was rested for this. Gap: no individual league scorelines, no xG and no foul or card data for Lincoln, so the away-form claim rests on aggregate splits only.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Lincoln's 89 goals were scored in League One, under a manager who has left. Skubala went to Bristol City and the club appointed joint head coaches; the squad was topped up on frees with no published fee in either direction. The attacking figure that makes Lincoln look dangerous belongs to a different division and a different staff.
  • Preston's clean-sheet run is the strongest single fact on this card and also the oldest. Twenty-three competitive matches back to 7 February spans two windows and a squad that has since added £11m of players and lost its centre-forward. It is structural evidence, but it is not evidence about this exact XI.
  • Lincoln's most recent result should be discounted deliberately. The 0–4 at Bournemouth came with mass changes and a rested first-choice XI. Anyone reading the last six days of Lincoln form without that context is reading a reserve team.
  • Lincoln's individual league scorelines could not be established. Only the aggregate splits were obtainable — 2 for and 4 against at home, 2 for and 2 against away — so the visiting side's match-by-match goals distribution, which is exactly what a 2.5 line turns on, is not visible on this page.
