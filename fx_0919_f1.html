D · Early-season risk notes

  • Neither club’s 2025–26 record describes the team that will play. Villa finished 4th on 65 points and won the Europa League with Martínez in goal, Konsa at centre-back, Watkins at centre-forward and Rogers behind him — all four were sold. Tottenham finished 17th with Romero and Vušković at centre-back — both sold — under a manager appointed in March whose first window this is. Every 25/26 figure in the strip should be read as history rather than as a current property, on both sides of the fixture rather than just one.
  • Four league matches each is the whole current sample, and it contains two 0–0s and a 0–4. Tottenham’s 100% failed-to-score rate and Villa’s 75% are among the most extreme figures that will appear on this board, and they are computed over four matches. The xG columns support the direction — 1.02 and 0.82 per match against a divisional 1.64 — but a rate of zero cannot be extrapolated and is not evidence that scoring is impossible, only that it has not happened yet.
  • Villa’s absence list removes part of the rebuild itself. Pau Torres and Cissé are centre-backs and Goretzka and Onana midfielders, on a side that has just replaced its centre-back and midfield units. The same is half-true at Tottenham, where Tonali — the most expensive signing of the window on either side — has only just recovered and may not start. The teams that take the field may be a further step away from the squads the transfer ledger describes.
  • The division is not where it was either. The Premier League is running at 34.1% home wins and 34.1% draws this season against 42.6% home wins in 2025–26, and at 51.2% BTTS against 56.1%. That is 41 matches against 380. Any read that leans on the 26/27 divisional line is leaning on a tenth of a season, and this card leans on it in the BTTS row in particular.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2 and the Asian handicap — both need a margin from two sides that have produced seven defeats or goalless draws in eight, off squads rebuilt six weeks ago, and both would move on whether Tonali and Porro start. Least sensitive: BTTS and the discipline row — the first because the failure to score is corroborated by shots, shots on target, xG and the transfer ledger on both sides at once, the second because Barrott’s 24-match record is the largest verified sample anywhere on this card.
