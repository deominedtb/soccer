A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

England are promoted: League B Group B2 winners in 2024–25, W5 L1, 16–3, all six matches under interim coach Lee Carsley. The one defeat was 1–2 to Greece at Wembley. Their division history, 2018–19 to 2026–27, reads A/A/A/B/A. Their previous League A edition, 2022–23, ended fourth in Group A3 with no win: W0 D3 L3, 4–10. At home that season they drew 0–0 with Italy and lost 0–4 to Hungary at Molineux, then drew 3–3 with Germany at Wembley. Under Tuchel they won qualifying Group K with eight wins, 22–0. At the World Cup they won Group L (4–2 Croatia, 0–0 Ghana, 2–0 Panama). They then beat Congo DR 2–1, Mexico 3–2 in Mexico City and Norway 2–1 after extra time. B · Style & early-season reality

Tuchel’s England played a 4-2-3-1 in 12 of his 16 competitive matches (4-3-3 twice, 4-1-4-1 twice). Their possession fell with the opposition’s level. They had 74.4% in qualifying, where they took 18.88 shots to 4.25 and 7.50 corners to 2.12 and conceded 2.1 xG in eight. That rose to 54.3% at the World Cup and fell to 45.7% in the knockout rounds. They had 35.8% against Argentina (5 shots to 15), and 33.1% against Mexico after Quansah’s 54th-minute red card. After Gordon scored against Argentina, England made 26 passes and had 11.9% of the ball until the winner (Opta via Flashscore). After the World Cup Tuchel said English players cannot control games with possession the way Spain’s can, and spoke of having to “embrace the chaos” with more direct play (Sky Sports). Under Tuchel England commit fewer fouls than they suffer (9.93 against 12.60) and are rarely booked (0.94 cards per match). Across the window they crossed far more than their opponents (21.2 per match to 11.2), and 61% of their goals (35 of 57) came after half-time. This summer the change is in personnel, not in the coach: Rice, the most-used starter after Pickford and Kane, is out. So are three of the semi-final back four (Stones, James, Spence), and the right-back choice is between a centre-back and a recalled Alexander-Arnold. Any claim about the shape tonight rests on a press conference and previews, not a match.

Spain’s shape is settled and their personnel continuous. They used a 4-3-3 in 11 of 24 and a 4-2-3-1 in 13, including every World Cup match from the last group game onwards. They hold the ball against everyone: 62.9% across the window, 59.6% against League A-tier sides and 63.8% at the World Cup. At the World Cup they allowed 6.29 shots and 1.43 on target per match, conceded one goal and 2.4 xG in eight (FotMob’s lowest), and Unai Simón set a record of six consecutive World Cup clean sheets (Wikipedia, citing Marca). Their attacking volume dropped there, from 22.83 shots and 3.50 goals per match in qualifying to 17.14 and 1.62. Four of the eight finished under 1.5 goals. Against League A-tier sides their margins were narrow: 10 of 13 finished within one goal. Opponents are booked more than Spain (2.25 per match against 1.29), and against League A-tier sides 2.85 against 1.62. Spain commit 11.10 fouls per match, more than England. Corners run 6.29 to 3.14 in their favour overall, and 5.18 to 4.09 against League A-tier sides. In the three competitive meetings Spain had 54.8%, 73.1% and 65.1% of the ball and won 23 corners to 6. The only structural change is at right-back, where the man six previews name has no competitive start in the window.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • The promotion label misreads England. They came up from a League B season under an interim coach, after one relegation from a winless League A edition, and have since reached a World Cup semi-final. The promoted-host prior (W5 D8 L11) was built on Austria, Czechia, Hungary, Wales, Scotland, Israel, Bosnia-Herzegovina and Serbia.
  • Both records were built largely below this level. Fifteen of England’s 22 matches and 11 of Spain’s 24 came against sides outside League A. England’s seven home matches include two against League A-tier sides, and Spain’s World Cup mixes Cape Verde and Saudi Arabia with France and Argentina.
  • This is both sides’ first match in 69–70 days, with no friendly in between. It is England’s first since a semi-final whose late stages brought heavy criticism of the coach (Sports Mole, SI, Sky Sports), and Spain’s first as world champions.
  • The personnel changes sit on one side. Tonight’s England squad made 144 of 242 starts; four of the semi-final eleven are out, and the right-back and Rice’s place in midfield are open. Spain’s 23 made 214 of 264, and ten of the final eleven are in it.
  • Most sensitive: totals, BTTS and England team goals. England’s scoring and conceding records meet the World Cup’s best defence and a Spain attack that slowed there, with a changed England back line and midfield. Least sensitive: the margin reading and the corner total, where every window reached lands in the same band.
