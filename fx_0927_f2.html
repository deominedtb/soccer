A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted; both were in League A in 2024–25 and both stayed. The Netherlands were second in Group A3 on 9 points, 13–7 (W2 D3 L1, behind Germany on 14). They then lost the quarter-final to Spain on penalties, after 2–2 in Rotterdam and 2–2 at 90 minutes (3–3 after extra time) at Mestalla. Serbia were third in Group A4 on 6 points, 3–6 (W1 D3 L2, behind Spain and Denmark). They kept their place through the March 2025 A/B play-off, 1–1 in Vienna and 2–0 in Belgrade against Austria. In World Cup qualifying the Netherlands won Group G on 20 points, 27–4 (W6 D2, ahead of Poland). At the World Cup they won Group F (2–2 Japan, 5–1 Sweden, 3–1 Tunisia), then went out to Morocco on penalties after 1–1 in the round of 32. Serbia finished third in Group K on 13 points, 9–10 (W4 D1 L3, behind England and Albania), and did not qualify. Format: in this edition the top two in each group go to the quarter-finals. The two worst last-placed sides go straight down, and the two best last-placed and the two worst third-placed sides go into play-offs (Wikipedia’s League A page, citing UEFA). The structural headline is that both coaches are new to competitive football with these squads, and both attacks are without the players who scored most of the window’s goals. Paunović has had three competitive matches, and has used a back four in all seven of his matches. Stojković used a back three in all 13 of his competitive matches in the window. Xavi has had one match. Players outside tonight’s Serbia squad made 68 of Serbia’s 187 competitive starts since September 2024 and scored 9 of their 14 goals that were not own goals. For the Netherlands the figures are 70 of 231 starts and 23 of 55 goals. The division prior is thin on the home side. In the 48 League A matches of 2022–23 and 2024–25 between two sides that had both been in League A the edition before, the home side went W18 D14 L16 and 33 of 48 finished within one goal (baselines_0927.md). Head to head: the international-results record has no meeting between the Netherlands and an independent Serbia. It lists FR Yugoslavia and Serbia and Montenegro as Serbia, and against them the Netherlands won all three meetings: 2–1 in the 1998 World Cup round of 16 (Toulouse), 6–1 in the Euro 2000 quarter-final (Rotterdam) and 1–0 at the 2006 World Cup (Leipzig). Against SFR Yugoslavia, 1967–92, the Netherlands went W3 D1 L2. None of the nine was played in Belgrade.
