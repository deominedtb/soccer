The structural headline is one club at the end of a managerial chain that runs through half this slate, and one club that spent less than anybody in the division. B · Style & early-season reality

Milan under Amorim have more of the ball, press slightly harder, and have moved all of their scoring into the second half. Possession has risen from 51.9% to 58.4% and PPDA has fallen from 14.62 to 12.51, while shots (13.50 against 13.24) and xG (1.80 against 1.71) are close to unchanged. That is a side controlling matches more without creating meaningfully more from it — and the corner column agrees, falling from 4.26 won to 3.00, among the five lowest corners-won figures on this slate, on 58.4% of the ball. The striking counted fact is the timing: Milan have scored 0.00 first-half goals in four league matches, against 0.53 last season. All seven of their goals have arrived after the interval, and they trailed 0–2 at half-time at Lazio before drawing 2–2. They concede 0.50 before the break. Defensively the model has loosened (1.21 to 1.43 xG conceded) while the counted rate has too (0.92 to 1.00), and the clean-sheet rate has fallen from 39.5% to 25.0%. Their most recent match is the 0–2 home defeat to Benfica in the Europa League four days ago, which sits outside every league rate quoted here.

Lecce are scoring more than last season and defending far worse, and the second half of that is the more extreme. xG conceded has risen from 1.71 to 3.08 per match — nearly doubling, and the second-worst defensive figure on this entire board — while PPDA has loosened from 11.93 to 14.09 and possession has stayed at 40.4% against 41.6%. They are pressing less and being opened up far more often, on unchanged territory. At the other end, xG has risen from 0.98 to 1.51 and goals from 0.74 to 1.25 per match, which is real improvement from a very low base — but the shot count has not moved (9.00 against 9.82, the second-lowest here) and deep completions are 3.25 against 3.42, the fourth-lowest on this board. The improvement is in chance quality rather than volume, from a club that spent €7.40m and borrowed three players from Torino. The pattern is bimodal: two comfortable results (2–0 at Venezia, 3–2 v Monza) and two in which they were taken apart (0–4 v Roma, 0–1 at Cagliari with 20 shots against). Their 50% failed-to-score rate has survived the improvement intact — identical to last season’s across a full campaign.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Milan’s 2025–26 record belongs to Allegri, who now manages Napoli — the club whose fixture opened this slate. Amorim arrived on 16 June, Leão was sold for €38m, Bennacer’s contract was terminated, Füllkrug’s loan ended, and Gonçalo Ramos arrived from PSG for roughly €70m. The 53 goals and 35 conceded describe a different attack and a different centre-back pairing.
  • Milan’s zero first-half goals is the most specific fact on this card and the least statistically robust. Four matches, seven goals, all after the break. A zero has no variance to estimate and reverts on a single goal; it is reported because it is counted, not because four matches establish a tendency.
  • Three of Milan’s four league matches have been away. They took five of their eight points on the road, and their only home league match was a 2–0 against Venezia. Last season their away record (11-5-3) was better than their home one (9-5-5), so venue does not resolve in the usual direction here either.
  • Lecce’s defensive model has nearly doubled in severity and their results have not followed yet. 3.08 xG conceded against 1.71 last season, on 1.75 goals conceded. Two of their four matches were comfortable and two were routs. A €7.40m window and three loans from a single club is thin cover for a gap that size.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and Corners — the first two because the counted and modelled gaps disagree by a wide margin, the third because four sub-baseline readings have no mechanism. Least sensitive: the first-half family and the Lecce half of BTTS — a 50% failed-to-score rate that has now held across 42 matches, and a first-half scoring record that is specific, counted and repeated across four matches.
