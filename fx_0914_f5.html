A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Villarreal finished 3rd in 2025–26 — 22 wins, 6 draws, 10 defeats, 72 scored, 46 conceded, 72 points — under Marcelino, who left when the two sides could not agree a renewal. Real Betis finished 5th — 15 wins, 15 draws, 8 defeats, 59 scored, 48 conceded, 60 points — and are in the Champions League for the first time in 21 years, under Manuel Pellegrini, who continues and who coached Villarreal from 2004 to 2009. The structural headline is Villarreal’s change of coach: Íñigo Pérez was announced on 1 June 2026 on a contract to June 2029, arriving from two and a half years at Rayo Vallecano that included a Conference League final. His Villarreal have not won in five competitive matches.

B · Style & early-season reality

Villarreal under Pérez are a possession side so far — 62% on average, 15.25 shots and 1.92 xG per league match against 1.38 conceded — and the results have gone the other way: 1.50 scored and 2.00 conceded per game, no clean sheet in four. That gap between process and outcome is the defining fact of their start, and it arrives with a new coach, a new goalkeeper at 36 (Gulácsi) in the probable XI and a rebuilt base of midfield after Partey and Terrats left and Saliba arrived. Last season’s 72 goals under Marcelino were made by a different coach’s method; Pérez’s Rayo record is a CV. Tonight two defenders, Foyth and Carlos Romero, are unavailable. Gap: no foul or corner series.

Betis under Pellegrini are the mirror image: 52% possession and 15.75 shots per league match, 1.72 xG for and 1.81 against, and three wins with three clean sheets, all 1–0-shaped, alongside a 5–2 defeat at Levante. The team that drew 15 of 38 last season has not drawn yet. The summer added a centre-forward (Parrott), a defensive midfielder (Bernal) and two Real Madrid players (Ceballos, Fran García) to a squad whose coach has not changed, and the Champions League debut in Lille came from behind. What the evidence cannot yet say is whether three clean sheets against 1.81 xG conceded per match is a defence or a run. Gap: no foul or corner series.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Villarreal’s 72 goals and third place belong to Marcelino. Pérez’s team has four league matches and a Champions League defeat; the xG says the attack is creating, the table says it is not converting, and nothing separates those yet.
  • Betis’ defensive record is three clean sheets against 1.81 xG conceded per match. The one match where the chances converted was 5–2 at Levante. The 75% clean-sheet rate is the least stable number on this card.
  • Betis’ 15 draws last season have not reappeared. Four league matches without a draw is a small sample against a 38-match tendency under the same coach.
  • Both sides played five-goal Champions League matches on 8 September. Rotation for tonight is unpublished beyond probable elevens, and Ceballos is reported short of rhythm.
  • Most sensitive: the moneyline, where results and chance quality contradict each other on both sides. Least sensitive: the discipline family’s club component — a full season of below-average booking for both clubs beside a computed league baseline — though the referee component is unsettled.
