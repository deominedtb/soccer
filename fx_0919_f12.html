A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and neither is new to this division. Athletic Club finished 12th in 2025–26 on 45 points — 13 wins, 6 draws, 19 defeats, 43 scored, 58 conceded, a season in which they conceded eleven goals more than their expected-goals-against of 1.24 per match implied and kept a clean sheet in 15.8% of matches. Alavés finished 14th on 43 points — 11 wins, 10 draws, 17 defeats, 44 scored, 56 conceded, with a 13.2% clean-sheet rate. Those are the two weakest clean-sheet records in LaLiga last season and they belong to the two clubs in this fixture. The structural headline is that the club with the new head coach is the one that signed nobody. Athletic replaced Ernesto Valverde with Edin Terzić and then made no permanent signings at all, selling €6m of squad and finishing the window €10.5m up. Alavés kept Quique Sánchez Flores and spent a net €7m. One of these sides has a new idea and the old players; the other has the old idea and slightly different players.

B · Style & early-season reality

Athletic under Terzić are creating far more and pressing far less, which is an unusual combination and a measurable one. Expected goals have risen from 1.46 to 1.96 per match and non-penalty xG from 1.35 to 1.96 — the largest attacking improvement of any club carded on this slate — while PPDA has moved from 10.10 to 13.54, meaning they allow opponents a third more passes before engaging. Possession is almost unchanged at 49.24% against 48.56%. The defensive side has gone the other way and the deep-completions column says how: 4.00 conceded per match last season, 7.60 now, with xG against up from 1.24 to 1.53, on a back line missing Vivian until October and Egiluz until January. Corners follow the same shape: 6.40 won and 5.60 conceded, against 5.76 and 3.87 last season — more won, and considerably more given up. The foul column is the one clear improvement: 10.60 per match against 13.68, well below a divisional 12.36. The evidence that this is Terzić’s side rather than Valverde’s is five matches and a change of pressing scheme, applied to a squad with not one new permanent player in it — which makes it a cleaner natural experiment than most, and a very small one.

Alavés have changed nothing and improved at both ends, which is the harder thing to explain and the easier thing to measure. Expected goals up from 1.41 to 1.77, expected goals against down from 1.39 to 1.02 — and non-penalty xG against down to 0.90, the lowest figure of any club carded today. The mechanism is in the pressing and shot rows: PPDA 10.51 against 10.38 — unchanged — but shots faced down from 12.47 to 10.17 and deep completions conceded from 6.05 to 6.33, essentially flat, while their own shots have risen from 12.18 to 14.17 and shots on target from 4.03 to 5.17. So the improvement is in the quality of what they concede rather than the volume, on a side that has lost two centre-backs to season-ending knee injuries and whose one midfield signing is among them. Possession has fallen from 48.03% to 43.95% and the foul rate from 14.92 to 13.67, both consistent with a side sitting a little deeper. Six matches, one coach, almost the same squad: of the two sides here, this is the one whose current numbers have the fewest confounders — and its clean-sheet rate last season was still the worst in the division.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Athletic’s 2025–26 numbers were produced by a different head coach and are the one set on this card where the squad is genuinely the same. Terzić was announced on 5 May 2026 and made no permanent signing; the five additions are loan returns. That makes the five-match sample unusually interpretable — every change is coaching rather than personnel — and unusually small. A 0.50-goal rise in expected goals per match over five matches is one good afternoon away from looking different.
  • Both defences are missing a centre-back and both were the worst in the division last season. Athletic are without Vivian until October and Egiluz until January; Alavés have lost Garcés and Mikel Rodríguez to anterior cruciate ligament injuries until May, one of them a summer signing brought in to replace Guridi. The clean-sheet rates in the strip — 15.8% and 13.2% last season, 40.0% and 33.3% now — describe units that are neither what they were nor at full strength.
  • Alavés’ expected goals against have fallen 0.37 per match with no change of coach and almost no change of squad, which is the sort of improvement that most often reverts. It is corroborated by shots faced falling from 12.47 to 10.17, but their deep completions conceded are essentially flat at 6.33 against 6.05 — so the volume of opposition entries into their box has not changed, only what has come of it.
  • Athletic’s outstanding fixture is an unexplained gap in the record. The division has reached jornada 7 and they have played five matches. Every Athletic rate on this card is therefore over five matches where Alavés’ is over six, and no source reached says why the fixture is outstanding or when it will be played.
  • The division has moved further than either club. LaLiga is running at 3.07 goals per match and 56.7% over 2.5 this season against 2.69 and 50.0% last, on 60 matches against 380. Both clubs sit below the new divisional line on totals and BTTS while their own expected goals sit above it — the disagreement in the totals row is really a disagreement between two baselines.
  • Most sensitive: the Asian handicap and BTTS — the first because both clubs produce three-goal results and one-goal results with nothing between, the second because a 38-match record saying both sides always concede is contradicted by a five- and six-match record saying they no longer do. Least sensitive: the discipline row and team goals — the first because a 19-match referee record and two two-season club card rates all point low together, the second because both attacks have improved on shots, shots on target and expected goals simultaneously and neither is outscoring its chances.
