A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Norway are promoted and new to League A, after League B in each of the three previous editions. In 2024–25 they won League B Group B3 on 13 points, 15 scored and 7 conceded (W4 D1 L1, the defeat 1–5 in Austria), which carried direct promotion. Since then they have won all eight World Cup qualifiers in Group I, 37–5 on 24 points, with Italy second on 18. At the World Cup they finished second in Group I on 6 points (4–1 v Iraq, 3–2 v Senegal, 1–4 v France). They then beat Ivory Coast 2–1 and Brazil 2–1 before losing the quarter-final to England 1–2 after extra time. Denmark stay in League A, where they have played every edition since 2020–21. In 2024–25 they were second in Group A4 on 8 points, 7 scored and 5 conceded (Spain 16), then lost the quarter-final to Portugal 3–5 on aggregate after extra time. In qualifying they were second in Group C on 11 points, 16–7 (Scotland 13). They beat North Macedonia 4–0 in the play-off semi-final and lost the final to Czechia on penalties after 2–2, so they missed the World Cup. The structural headline is that the promoted side’s record is the more current one and its side the more settled. The eight sides promoted into League A for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 0.79 points per match, scored 0.92 and conceded 1.73 per match; the four of them at home on matchday one won two, drew one and lost one. None of the eight arrived from a World Cup quarter-final, so the history is a thin guide to this side. ESPN lists the last five meetings, back to 2003: Denmark four wins, one draw. They include 1–1 in Oslo and 0–2 in Copenhagen in 2011, and the most recent was a friendly in Copenhagen on 8 June 2024 (1–3). The RESEARCH SPEC’s transfer-fee field has no national-team equivalent. B · Style & early-season reality

Solbakken’s Norway are a back-four side built around two centre-forwards and a low foul count. In League B they had 53.4% of the ball, 16.00 shots against 9.33, won 6.00 corners and conceded 2.00, and committed 7.83 fouls and 1.00 card per match, against League A’s 11.39 fouls and 2.06 cards per team. In qualifying they were more dominant still. At the World Cup against stronger opposition they held 52.2% of the ball but were outshot 10.60 to 14.60 (five matches) and conceded more corners than they won, 4.50 to 5.50, while still scoring in all six. Their card rate sat below Denmark’s and the division’s in every window on the strip: 0.40 per match in 25/26 and 0.33 at a World Cup that averaged 2.71 cards per match (1.36 per team) in ESPN’s record. The attacking structure is the part that changes tonight. Haaland and Sørloth started together in 19 of 20 competitive matches, in a 4-4-2 or with Sørloth as the right forward of a 4-3-3, Ødegaard listed on the right of midfield in both. The previews’ 4-3-3 has Nusa and Schjelderup or Bobb in Sørloth’s place. Schjelderup and Bobb have four and three competitive starts; Strand Larsen, the like-for-like option, has one. The evidence that Norway play the same way without Sørloth is a CV rather than a match. The one competitive match without either striker was the rotated 1–4 against France.

Riemer’s Denmark switched to a back four from his first match and play two ways depending on opposition. Against qualifying opposition in 25/26 they had 66.9% of the ball, took 20.00 shots and allowed 7.86, and won 8.25 corners and 29.0 crosses per match. Against League A opposition under Riemer (Spain, Serbia, Portugal twice) they had 45.5% of the ball and 13.67 shots each way (shots on three), won 5.25 corners and conceded 7.25, and scored 1.00 and conceded 1.25 per match. In every window their opponents fouled them more than League A’s 11.39: 12.83 per match in the 2024–25 group, 13.50 in 25/26 and 13.75 against League A sides under Riemer. Denmark’s own fouls fell from 11.33 per match in the 2024–25 group (13.00 in the four back-three matches before Riemer) to 10.25 in 25/26, and their cards from 2.67 to 1.13. The summer change is personnel at the back. There is a new goalkeeper with two competitive starts. A likely Andersen–Nelsson pairing has shared two competitive starts in the record, one of them in a back three. The right-back of both March play-offs (Bah) and three regular starters since 2024 (Vestergaard, Nørgaard, Eriksen) are gone. The Hjulmand–Højbjerg midfield and Højlund up front are what carries over. None of this back line has played a competitive match together; the evidence for it is club form.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Norway’s biggest rates were earned a level down. The 2024–25 group was League B, and the qualifying group included Moldova (16 goals in two matches) and Estonia. The World Cup is the only window against League A-level sides, and it was played at neutral venues in North America, not at Ullevaal.

  • The two sides are at different points in the calendar. Norway’s last competitive match was 75 days ago with most of tonight’s squad; Denmark’s was 177 days ago, followed by two friendlies.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and Denmark team goals, all of which turn on a Denmark back line and goalkeeper that have not played together and on Norway’s shape without Sørloth. Least sensitive: Norway team goals and the team half of Discipline. Norway’s scoring and low card rates hold across League B, qualifying and the World Cup.
