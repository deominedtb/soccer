A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, both are reigning domestic champions, and neither has changed manager: Peter Bosz remains at PSV and Arda Turan is in his second season at Shakhtar, having been appointed on a two-year contract announced on 27 May 2025. PSV won the 2025–26 Eredivisie — their 27th title, sealed on 5 April 2026 — and enter this as three-time defending champions. Shakhtar won the Ukrainian Premier League for the sixteenth time, clinching it on 28 April 2026 with five rounds to spare, on a record of 22 wins, six draws and two defeats for 72 points from 30 matches. The structural fact that separates this fixture from every other one on the board sits on the visiting side and has nothing to do with the squad: because of the security situation, Shakhtar play their Champions League home matches at Stamford Bridge, so a club that is nominally the away team tonight is a club with no home ground in this competition at all. That travel profile is not a hardship on their record — they were unbeaten in all fifteen away league matches last season and have won all three away league games this one. Both sides are second domestically as they arrive: PSV on 13 points from five, two behind AZ Alkmaar; Shakhtar on 12 from five, level with Polissya Zhytomyr and behind on head-to-head.

  • PSV — medium turnover, concentrated in midfield, and a window the manager himself called unsettling. Bosz is unchanged. In, with fees: Kodai Sano from NEC Nijmegen for a reported €14.5m, described in the Dutch reporting as the largest Eredivisie transfer of the summer, and Sven Mijnans from AZ for a reported €13m — both midfielders. Filip Kostić arrived as a free agent after his Juventus contract expired. Lutsharel Geertruida, Sami Ouaissa, Ayoni Santos and Sam Lammers are also named among the players formally presented by deadline day, without fees. Out: Joey Veerman, who was reported available at around €20m through the window; Ismael Saibari; and reserve goalkeeper Joël Drommel to Twente after five years. Conflict logged: one strand of the reporting describes a frustrating window with few additions and several deals collapsing late — Bosz called the market "a source of unrest" — while another lists seven presented arrivals; both are recorded, neither is resolved. Weighted as the spec asks, this is a midfield window: the two fees went to central midfielders, and no centre-back or first-choice goalkeeper movement was established in either direction beyond the reserve keeper leaving.
  • Shakhtar — the defining piece of business was an outgoing one, and it completed this summer. Turan is unchanged. Heorhiy Sudakov's move to Benfica is the club's headline transaction and its structure is published by the buying club: a loan for €6.75m during 2025–26 carrying a mandatory permanent obligation of €20.25m from 2026–27, plus up to €5m in variables — so the midfielder is gone for good as of this season. In: Gleiker Mendoza from Kryvbas, a midfielder; Alan Matturro from Genoa, a defender; and Ryan Roberto from Flamengo, a forward. Gap: no fee was published for any of the three in anything reached, and no goalkeeper movement was established. Not asserted: two eye-catching arrivals — Oleksandr Zinchenko at a reported £23m and a Willian return at a reported £6.2m — surfaced only on a speculative fan wiki and appear in no reliable source reached, so they are recorded here as unverified and are not used anywhere on this card.
  • Suspensions / absences. This is the most heavily depleted fixture on the slate and the weight falls on the visitors. Shakhtar are named without Tymur Puzankov, Denys Tvardovskyi, Prosper Obah, Marlon, Pedrinho, Kiril Fesyun, Alan Matturro and Eguinaldo — eight players, one of them a summer signing who has not yet been available. PSV lose Mauro Júnior to suspension, carried over from a red card in this competition in January, and are without Alassane Pléa, Sami Ouaissa and Jerdy Schouten through injury — Ouaissa being another of the summer arrivals. Gap: no positions or return dates were published for the Shakhtar list, so the depth of the loss cannot be located department by department.

PSV: 4W–1D–0L, 18 goals scored and six conceded, unbeaten in five, with a 5–1 home win over Groningen on 23 August and a 6–1 win away at Utrecht on 30 August — both quoted in the league's own season summary as among the biggest results of the campaign so far. It is not a source of the grade this spec prefers, so it is flagged rather than relied on, but if it is right the appointment leans against the home side, which is the opposite of the way the market has set the two team card lines. B · Style & early-season reality

PSV are the more measurable of the two, and what is measurable is the scoring. Five matches have produced 18 goals and six conceded, including a 5–1 and a 6–1, from a side that has won the last three Dutch titles under the same manager. The summer did not change the shape so much as recycle its middle: €14.5m for Sano and €13m for Mijnans came in, Veerman and Saibari went out, and the defence and goalkeeping department were left essentially untouched in anything that was published. That combination — heavy central-midfield turnover, an unchanged back line, an unchanged coach — is the opposite of the pattern that usually makes early-season numbers unreliable, and it is why PSV's 18 goals in five are treated here as a reasonable if short guide rather than as noise. The complication is the one Bosz named himself: he described the window as a source of unrest, several deals fell through late, and two of the players presented in it — Ouaissa injured, and Mauro Júnior suspended from a red card served over from January — are unavailable for the opening night. Gap: no possession, foul, corner or shot series is published for PSV in either season, so nothing here describes how those 18 goals were produced.

Shakhtar are the more unusual case on this board and the reason is structural rather than tactical. A club that plays its European home matches at Stamford Bridge does not have a home-and-away split in the ordinary sense, and its record reflects a side long adapted to that: unbeaten in all fifteen away league matches last season, all three away league matches won this season, five consecutive away wins across all competitions coming into tonight, and the only defeat of their current campaign suffered at home to Polissya. Underneath that, the squad lost its most valuable midfielder for good this summer — Sudakov's obligation to Benfica triggering at €20.25m — and replaced him with three signings whose fees nobody has published. Then eight players are named as unavailable, including the defender signed from Genoa. Turan's second season therefore starts with the away form of a very settled team and the availability of a very unsettled one, and this card does not claim to know which of those the Philips Stadion sees. Gap: no xG, shot, possession or discipline series exists for Shakhtar either, and their published 2025–26 goal figures carry the league/all-competitions ambiguity noted above.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Twelve named absentees across the two squads is the largest availability disruption on this slate, and it is unevenly documented: PSV's four are named with reasons — one suspension carried over from a January red card, three injuries — while Shakhtar's eight are a list of names with no positions and no return dates. A defensive line missing three players and a bench missing three are the same list from here.
  • Shakhtar have no home ground in this competition. Their European home matches are played at Stamford Bridge for security reasons, which means the usual home/away framing does not apply to them and their remarkable away record — unbeaten in fifteen last season, three from three this — is a property of a club that effectively always travels, not evidence of a road specialism that will hold in one match.
  • PSV spent both of their published fees on central midfielders and left the goalkeeping and centre-back departments untouched, while conceding six in five. The scoring column is the well-evidenced half of their season; the defensive one is six goals in five matches with no underlying numbers behind it, and both the totals and BTTS rows lean on that weaker half.
  • Two of Shakhtar's reported summer arrivals — a £23m Zinchenko signing and a Willian return — appear only on a speculative fan wiki and in no reliable source reached. They are recorded as unverified and used nowhere. Any account of this squad that includes them is describing a team this card cannot confirm exists.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total at 1.42, which is being carried by one side's five-match scoring rate against a defence whose available personnel cannot be established. Least sensitive: the cards total — a confirmed official with 22 matches of published cards, fouls and penalties behind him, and a 3.5 line consistent with that record.
