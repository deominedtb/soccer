A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted this season, though 2025–26 was Sunderland’s first Premier League season back. Sunderland finished 7th in the 2025–26 Premier League — 54 points, 42 scored, 48 conceded — after a record £161m summer for a promoted club, and went out of the FA Cup in the fifth round. AZ finished 7th in the 2025–26 Eredivisie — 52 points, 58 scored, 51 conceded — but won the KNVB Cup, beating NEC 5–1 in the final on 19 April 2026, and reached the Conference League quarter-finals, where Shakhtar eliminated them. The structural headline is that Sunderland’s results last season outran their chance data (42 goals from 38.9 xG, 48 conceded from 54.4 xG against), while AZ’s chance data this season runs well ahead of an already strong set of results, against Eredivisie opposition.

B · Style & early-season reality

Sunderland last season were a low-possession, low-volume Premier League side: 44.9% of the ball, 10.5 shots for and 14.3 against, 1.02 xG for and 1.43 against, 3.63 corners and 10.7 fouls per match, with 34% of league matches ending without a Sunderland goal. They scored 0.42 and conceded 0.61 per match before half-time. The four league matches this season are more balanced — 49.3% possession, 1.55 xG for and 1.45 against, corners 3.75 for and 3.25 against — but have produced three goals. What the summer changed is the left side and the centre of defence: Méthalie, Fofana and Danso all arrived in the last fortnight of the window, so the evidence for how they fit is a CV, not a match.

AZ under Echteld have become a dominant Eredivisie side on the numbers: 58.6% possession, 20.0 shots for and 9.0 against, 2.65 xG for and 1.08 against, and 9.17 corners won against 3.50 conceded per league match — up from 1.88 xG and 5.76 corners last season. They are a low-card side (1.82 per match last season, five yellows in six league matches this season). The caveat is the level: every number this season comes from the Eredivisie plus one match at PSV, and last season AZ lost 3–1 at Crystal Palace in the Conference League league phase. Parrott’s sale on 20 August has not reduced the scoring — 11 goals in the four matches since — but that is four matches.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Sunderland’s last-season position overstates their underlying numbers. 42 goals from 38.9 xG and 48 conceded from 54.4 xG against; this season the gap has reversed.
  • Three Sunderland signings arrived in the final fortnight. Méthalie (21 August), Fofana (2 September) and Danso (3 September) appear in none of the league samples.
  • AZ’s numbers are Eredivisie numbers. 17 goals and 2.65 xG per match against domestic opposition; their European record against English clubs is three wins in 22.
  • AZ sold their centre-forward on 20 August. The scoring has held, but on four matches.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2 and handicap — results and chance data disagree for both clubs and the leagues differ in level. Least sensitive: halves, where both clubs’ first-half series are complete across two seasons and point the same way.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Halves are more robust than the 1X2 here because both first-half series are complete in both seasons (0.42/0.61 and 0.25/0.25; 0.97/0.71 and 0.83/0.50), while the result depends on two clubs whose results and xG disagree.
  • BTTS is more robust than the handicap because AZ’s scoring record and Sunderland’s 34% failed-to-score rate are both long series, whereas the margin question has no common scale between the leagues.

  • Discipline is more robust than corners because Pignard’s card and foul rates are published by season and competition, whereas AZ’s 9.17 corners per match are six Eredivisie matches old.
