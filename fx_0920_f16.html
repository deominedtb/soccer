A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Villarreal finished 3rd in 2025–26 on 72 points — 22 wins, 6 draws, 10 defeats, 72 scored, 46 conceded — with one of the most venue-dependent records on this board: 15-1-3 at the Cerámica, 7-5-7 away. Levante finished 16th on 42 points — 11 wins, 9 draws, 18 defeats, 47 scored, 61 conceded — the joint-worst goals-against figure on this slate, level with Real Sociedad. The structural headline is one coaching change of very recent date and one of nine months’ standing, and a transfer relationship running directly between the two clubs. Íñigo Pérez was announced at Villarreal on 1 June 2026 on a contract to June 2029, arriving from two and a half years at Rayo Vallecano that included a Conference League final; Luís Castro has been at Levante since December 2025, appointed after Julián Calero was dismissed with the club bottom on nine points. Two players move between these clubs in this fixture’s own team sheets: Etta Eyong joined Levante from Villarreal this summer, and Daniel Requena went the other way on loan from Villarreal to Levante on 17 July. Both clubs are in the bottom six after six and five matches respectively, on five points each.

B · Style & early-season reality

Villarreal have undergone the largest change in possession share of any club on this board, and it is measurable in five columns at once. Possession has risen from 44.5% to 61.6% — seventeen points, by far the biggest move on this slate — PPDA has fallen from 14.77 to 8.78, deep completions have more than doubled from 7.61 to 16.33, corners won have gone from 4.18 to 7.33 against 4.17 conceded, and xG from 1.81 to 2.55. A side that sat off and counter-attacked under the previous coach now presses high and holds the ball, and Íñigo Pérez’s Rayo Vallecano played exactly that way. The results have gone the opposite direction: one win in six, fifteenth, both home matches lost, and — the single most striking counted fact on this card — no clean sheet in six league matches against 21.1% last season, with xG conceded up from 1.41 to 1.75. They are scoring 1.67 per match from 2.55 expected, so the attack is under-converting as well. Ten goals scored and eleven conceded in six, from a club that scored 72 and conceded 46.

Levante have changed less and split in two. Possession is 40.5% against 42.8%, PPDA 15.38 against 12.78 — slightly less ball and a distinctly looser press — with deep completions down from 5.47 to 4.40 and shots from 12.53 to 11.60. The xG line has barely moved, 1.57 to 1.67 for and 1.75 to 1.80 against, which makes them one of the most continuous sides on this board under a coach now nine months into the job. What is not continuous is the venue split, and it is absolute: seven goals in two home matches (5–2 against Betis, 2–4 against Barcelona) and zero in three away (0–3, 0–0, 0–0). Their 60.0% failed-to-score rate is the fifth-highest on this board, and every one of those blanks came on the road; last season they were at 31.6% overall with a 4-4-11 away record. The discipline column has fallen sharply and without an obvious cause: 1.00 cards and 10.00 fouls per match against 2.34 and 12.26 last season, which on five matches is the kind of drop that usually reverts. Their first-half concession is 1.20 per match against 0.68 last season, among the largest first-half deteriorations on this board.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Villarreal’s 2025–26 record was produced by a fundamentally different team. Seventeen points of possession, six points of PPDA and a doubling of deep completions separate this Villarreal from the one that finished third. Parejo and Partey left and were not replaced; the only named senior arrival is a developmental one. Reading 72 goals and 46 conceded into this fixture reads a different method.
  • The Cerámica advantage that defined Villarreal’s season has inverted. 15-1-3 at home last year; both home league matches lost this year, 2–3 and 1–2. Their remaining four league matches were away, where they took four of their five points.
  • Levante’s record is two records. Seven goals in two home matches, none in three away; a 60.0% failed-to-score rate concentrated entirely on the road. Every rate quoted for them averages a side that scores five against Betis and a side that has not scored away all season.
  • Levante’s transfer ledger has no prices at all and their squad turned over almost completely. Around fifteen in and sixteen out under a salary limit the sporting director described as binding; no fee was obtainable for any of it. Their continuity in the xG columns may therefore be more apparent than real.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and any Levante team-goals line — a side that has not scored in three away matches, missing the forward it signed from today’s opponent. Least sensitive: Corners — a 3.7-corner gap with a coaching change and four supporting series on one side, and 38 matches of continuity on the other.
