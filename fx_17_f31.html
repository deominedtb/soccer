B · Style & early-season reality

MK Dons have produced one of the more unusual starts on this board: four draws in five, including a 0–0 with Leicester City and a 3–3 with Wycombe Wanderers — the same team producing both a goalless afternoon and a six-goal one inside five matches. Structurally they are the better-placed of this slate's promoted sides in one respect and the worse in another. Better, because Warne stayed and because the club actually spent, putting £600,000 into a forward from Rotherham and adding two defenders. Worse, because the goalkeeper who was there left on a free to Sheffield Wednesday and no fee was spent replacing him, and nine goals conceded in five is the early consequence. Their scoring is the healthier half — seven in five, Aaron Collins on four in all competitions — which for a promoted side is a real finding rather than a neutral one. Gap: no xG or shot data, and two of their five scorelines could not be established individually, so the distribution behind the four draws is only partly visible.

Peterborough are the more legible side and the more limited one. Three goals in five league matches, three of those five ending with them on nought, a three-match winless run, and a summer in which three attacking players left — Hayes to Rochdale, Odoh to Salford, Lisbie to Millwall — and three arrived without a published fee between them. The top scorer's four goals are mostly not league goals; the leading league scorer, Ben Woods, has two, out of a team total of three. At the other end they have conceded nine, including a 4–0 at Blackpool, but they also produced a 0–0 with Stevenage, so the defence is not uniformly porous — it is the attack that has been constant. Luke Williams is unchanged in the job. Gap: no injury list, no xG, no card data — the availability of the three summer arrivals is unknown.
