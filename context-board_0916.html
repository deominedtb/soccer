B · Style & early-season reality

Milan under Allegri were a back-three side that did not dominate the ball: Understat logged 2,809 of last season’s league minutes in a 3–5–2, with 51.9% possession, 13.2 shots for and 11.5 against per match, 10.1 fouls per match against a Serie A mean of 12.7 per team, and 4.26 corners per match, below the league’s 4.41. Seven of their 53 goals were penalties and six came from corners. Amorim has kept a back three but changed its shape and its share of the ball: all 376 logged minutes this season are a 3–4–2–1, possession has risen to 58.3%, and the chance data has levelled (1.43 xG for and against per match). What the summer changed underneath is the front line — Ramos in, Leão, Morata and Giménez out — and the evidence that it works together is four matches in which nothing was scored before half-time. Tonight’s probable eleven also rests two of the midfield starters.

Benfica last season were a high-possession side that generated volume: 57.7% of the ball, 2.04 xG and 6.0 shots on target per match, and 7.21 corners per match in a league averaging 4.71 per team, with 11.1 fouls per match below the Liga Portugal mean of 13.8. Under Marco Silva the direction is more of the same — 65.0% possession, 2.67 xG, 8.00 corners per league match — set up as a 4–2–3–1 with Palhinha at the base. But the back line that produced last season’s 0.81 xG against has lost Otamendi, António Silva and Dedić, and its replacements’ evidence against opposition of Milan’s level is a CV: Lenglet and Circati arrived this summer, Palhinha on 26 August, and the three qualifying ties were against St. Gallen, Hearts and AGF.
