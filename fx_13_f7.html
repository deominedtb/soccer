A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Reduced-depth card: research was capped at two searches per club plus the referee lookup. The RESEARCH SPEC fields not pursued here are both clubs' full 2025–26 goal columns, home/away splits and foul rates, and the complete ins-and-outs ledger beyond the named deals below. Those absences are a budget decision, not a finding.

Neither club is promoted this season, and the distinction matters because one of them was promoted last season: Sunderland came up in 2025–26 and finished 7th on 54 points in their first campaign back, qualifying for the Europa League — so they arrive here in a second top-flight season with European football added to the calendar, not in the fragile first one. Brentford are in their sixth consecutive Premier League season. Neither changed manager: Keith Andrews continues at Brentford, and Régis Le Bris signed a contract extension at Sunderland running to June 2028. Both spent heavily and both broke their club record doing it. Two matchweeks in, Brentford have four points from six — a 3–0 win over Tottenham and a 1–1 draw at Leeds — and sit in the division's top five; Sunderland lost on the opening day at Ipswich Town and then beat Fulham 1–0, Wilson Isidor scoring shortly after coming on. Three of those four opponents appear elsewhere on this slate.

  • Brentford — a club-record window, and the money went into the spine. Andrews continues, so the shape is not being rebuilt from a CV. The headline is Mamadou Sangaré from Lens for a reported £41m, the most expensive signing in the club's history — and Lens appear on this same slate at f13, which makes this the one transfer linking two fixtures on the sheet. Alongside it, El Hadji Malick Diouf arrived from West Ham for a reported £35m, with Jaidon Anthony and Jannik Schuster for undisclosed fees and Callum Wilson on a free. Out: Jordan Henderson to Chelsea after a short stay, and Ethan Pinnock to Coventry City for a reported £5m — Coventry being the promoted club that appears on this slate at f10 — among seven senior exits. Gap: no fee is published for Anthony or Schuster, and no goalkeeper movement was established in either direction.
  • Sunderland — second season up, and the record broken again. Le Bris continues on a deal to 2028. Dayann Méthalie arrived from Toulouse for a reported £24m, described as the biggest signing in the club's history; Jules Ahoka came from Antwerp for a reported £8.6m and Thomas Meunier from Lille on a free. The structural fact behind the recruitment is the calendar: a 7th-place finish brought Europa League football, and the club's own summer was framed as coping with a significantly busier schedule. Gap: the wider outgoing ledger was not pursued at this depth, and no centre-forward or goalkeeper arrival was established.
  • Suspensions / absences. One-sided and all on the home side. Brentford are without Mathias Jensen, described as a minor issue, with Josh Dasilva, Sepp van den Berg and Antoni Milambo also sidelined — four absentees including a centre-back. No Sunderland absence was reported in the preview reached, and no suspension is reported for either side.
  • Sample. Two Premier League matches each and this is matchweek 3, but unusually for this slate the underlying numbers exist. Brentford are averaging 24 shots per 90 minutes, the second-highest volume in the division behind Manchester United's 27, off a 3–0 win over Tottenham and a 1–1 draw at Leeds. Sunderland's number is the mirror image and comes from a full season: they conceded 0.11 expected goals per shot in 2025–26, the joint-lowest figure in the Premier League alongside champions Arsenal, and across the opening two matchweeks of this season only Arsenal have conceded fewer shots on target. This is the only fixture on the built cards where both sides have a published underlying series rather than a scoreline.
  • Referee. Thomas Bramall, named in the club's own match preview — the Sheffield-born official who took the opening fixture of this Premier League season, Arsenal's 3–0 win over newly promoted Coventry City. His record comes with a spread wide enough to be worth stating as a range rather than a figure. Conflict logged: one database gives 473 yellow cards, 9 straight reds and 6 second yellows across 146 career matches — 3.24 yellows a game, with 46 penalties for roughly 0.32 per match — while another reports 2.73 yellows a game across 308 fixtures, and a Premier League-only figure of 4.11 cards a game circulates from an earlier period. All three were reached; none is corroborated. The honest reading is that his career booking rate is somewhere between 2.7 and 3.3 yellows a game with a low red-card rate of 0.06. Gap: no fouls-per-card ratio is published, and no Bramall record specific to either club was found.

What it has to absorb is that Sunderland finished 7th last season on 54 points, qualified for Europe, broke their transfer record and have the division's joint-best expected-goals-per-shot defence from 2025–26. That is a well-resourced second-season side, not a promoted one finding its feet, and 4.75 is a long price against it. Four Brentford absentees on the day is the counterweight.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Sunderland are in their second Premier League season, not their first. The promoted-side discount that would apply to a newly-up club does not apply here: they finished 7th on 54 points, qualified for the Europa League, and broke their club record again this summer on a £24m signing from Toulouse.
  • Brentford's 24 shots per 90 is the most useful number on this card and it is drawn from two matches. It is also the number the corner and totals rows both lean on, so if it regresses, three market rows move together rather than independently.
  • The European calendar is a real structural factor on the visiting side and this card cannot measure it. Sunderland's own summer was framed around coping with a busier schedule; whether that shows up as rotation in a Saturday league fixture was not established.
  • The referee's own statistics are the widest spread on any built card — 2.73, 3.24 and 4.11 cards a game from three sources for the same official — and the appointment comes from a club preview rather than a governing-body list. That combination makes the discipline row's arithmetic softer than it looks.
  • Most sensitive: both teams to score, which needs a Sunderland attack whose only league goal so far came from a substitute, against a side that has conceded once in two matches. Least sensitive: the goals total, the only row on this board where both directions are backed by published underlying series — 24 shots per 90 on one side, 0.11 expected goals conceded per shot on the other.
