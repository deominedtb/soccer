A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted, and last season’s finishing positions — Palace 15th, City runners-up behind Arsenal — are unusually poor guides here, because both clubs replaced their manager fourteen days apart in June. Crystal Palace appointed Pierre Sage on 15 June 2026 on a three-year contract, after Oliver Glasner confirmed he would not renew, a decision Glasner said had been taken months earlier following a meeting with chairman Steve Parish during the October international break. Manchester City appointed Enzo Maresca on 29 June 2026, also on three years, with roughly £17m in compensation paid to Chelsea, to succeed Pep Guardiola. The structural headline is that this is the most heavily rebuilt fixture on the card by a distance: two new head coaches, and a Manchester City squad that has sold the entire spine of the Guardiola era and spent a club record replacing it.

  • Manchester City — turnover very high, concentrated in midfield and central defence. Out: Rodri, Bernardo Silva, John Stones, Nathan Aké, Tijjani Reijnders and Nico González — a clear-out of experience rather than of squad filler. In: Elliot Anderson from Nottingham Forest for a club-record £116m and Ayyoub Bouaddi from Lille for an initial £81.4m plus £4.2m in add-ons — the same Bouaddi who appears on this card as a Lille departure — along with goalkeeper Gerónimo Rulli, Mathys Detourbet from Troyes for £21.5m, Jeremy Monga from Leicester City for £12m and Pierce Charles. Rayan Cherki, Abdukodir Khusanov, Antoine Semenyo and Marc Guéhi were recruited in the closing eighteen months of Guardiola’s tenure. Maresca has said publicly that he wants “one or two more captains” before the window closes on 1 September — the manager himself describing the leadership spine as unfinished.
  • Crystal Palace — turnover very high and concentrated in central defence, where the money went out and free transfers came in. Pierre Sage replaces Glasner and inherits a back line sold from under the position. Marc Guéhi left in January 2026 to tonight’s opponent; Maxence Lacroix followed him to Chelsea this summer for £52m, the only outgoing fee these sources report; and Chadi Riad is injured — so all three senior centre-backs from last season are unavailable to the new manager. The replacements cost nothing in fees: Takehiro Tomiyasu and Oscar Mingueza both arrived free, Mingueza from Celta Vigo and Tomiyasu from a club these sources do not name, joined by Axel Disasi on a season-long loan from Chelsea. The Disasi terms are the telling detail — Palace pay a loan fee and his full salary, with no option and no obligation to buy, for a 28-year-old who has not appeared for his parent club since January 2025 and has spent two seasons on short-term loans at Aston Villa and West Ham. He was identified specifically as the priority replacement for Lacroix. Further forward: Anan Khalaili for £21m from Royale Union Saint-Gilloise, Zavier Gozo for £11.12m from Real Salt Lake, Evann Guessand on loan from Aston Villa, and Dwight McNeil from Everton in a straight swap for Brennan Johnson, both on four-year deals with no cash fee disclosed. Sage has said he expects one or two more arrivals before the window shuts. Gap: no goalkeeper or defensive-midfield business is reported in either direction, and the Mateta and Sarr exit rumours are unresolved with the window open until 1 September — the day after this match.
  • Suspensions / absences. Palace are without Ismaila Sarr, still in individual training with a groin injury, and centre-back Chadi Riad with a knee injury; goalkeeper Dean Henderson is carrying an ankle and foot problem, leaving that position unsettled too. City are without Jeremy Doku, working back from a calf issue. Expected shapes have Foden, Nico O’Reilly and Semenyo behind Erling Haaland for City, and McNeil and Eddie Nketiah behind Jean-Philippe Mateta for Palace. No suspensions surfaced; no referee is named in the odds file.
  • Sample. One league match each. Palace lost 2–0 at home to Everton and sit 16th on nothing; City beat Bournemouth 2–1 and sit seventh on three points, though Maresca’s start has been characterised as mixed rather than clean. City are unbeaten on their last ten trips to Selhurst Park — a record built by Guardiola’s City against Hodgson’s and Glasner’s Palace, none of whom is involved tonight.

B · Style & early-season reality

Manchester City are the most changed team on this card and the change runs deeper than the dugout. Selling Rodri, Bernardo Silva, Stones, Aké and Reijnders in one summer removes the players who made Guardiola’s control model work — the single pivot, the tempo-setter, the ball-playing centre-backs — and the replacements are a £116m 23-year-old and an £81.4m teenager who has never played in this league. Maresca will bring a recognisable possession structure; he learned it here as Guardiola’s assistant, and the family resemblance is precisely why the possession-dependent parts of this fixture remain readable. What is not readable is the execution. A rebuilt midfield takes months to develop the automatisms that make a high line safe, and Maresca saying he still wants “one or two more captains” three days before deadline is a manager telling you the squad is not the one he intends to finish the season with. Haaland is the continuity, and one 2–1 against Bournemouth is the whole of the league evidence.

Palace are rebuilt differently — less money, more damage. Glasner’s three-at-the-back system took them to 15th and gave them an identity built on a settled back line, and that back line has been dismantled: Guéhi sold in January to the opponent standing in front of them tonight, Riad injured, and Henderson — the goalkeeper — carrying an ankle and foot problem. Pierre Sage arrives from a very different footballing culture with a three-year mandate and one competitive league match, a 2–0 home defeat to Everton in which Palace did not score. The attacking group is being assembled in public: McNeil expected to start alongside Nketiah behind Mateta, with Sarr unavailable and a persistent story about Mateta’s own future that the preview lineups contradict. Whether Sage keeps Glasner’s back three or moves to a four is exactly the sort of question that decides corner and card volume, and there is no honest way to answer it from ninety minutes.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Both clubs changed head coach within a fortnight in June — Sage to Palace on 15 June, Maresca to City on 29 June, both on three-year deals. Palace’s 15th place and City’s runners-up finish were produced by Oliver Glasner and Pep Guardiola respectively. Every last-season number on this fixture describes a team that no longer exists in either dugout or in personnel.
  • City’s rebuild is the largest single structural change anywhere on this card. Rodri, Bernardo Silva, Stones, Aké, Reijnders and González out; Anderson at a club-record £116m and Bouaddi at an initial £81.4m in, alongside Rulli, Detourbet, Monga and Charles. Maresca stating he wants “one or two more captains” before 1 September is the manager confirming the squad is still incomplete on the day of this match.
  • Palace’s defensive and goalkeeping situation is unsettled in a way their price does not obviously reflect: Guéhi sold in January to tonight’s opponent, Lacroix sold to Chelsea for £52m this summer, Riad out with a knee injury, Henderson carrying an ankle and foot problem, and Sarr still in individual training. The three centre-backs who arrived to cover it cost no fee between them — two frees and a loan with no purchase option — which is a different kind of rebuild from City’s and a thinner one.
  • Correction to an earlier reading of this fixture. The first version of this card named Dwight McNeil as an incoming addition on the strength of a projected starting eleven rather than a transfer report, and carried Fikayo Tomori and Christos Tzolis as reported targets. Checked against the completed window: McNeil did sign, but as the makeweight in a swap with Everton for Brennan Johnson rather than as the purchase the lineup implied — while Tomori and Tzolis did not sign at all, and neither appears anywhere in Palace’s confirmed business. A projected XI is evidence about a team sheet, not about a transfer, and it should not have been used as the latter.
  • Flags, recorded not resolved. Palace are described as being tasked with competing in the Europa League this season despite a 15th-place finish, and the qualification route is not established in these sources. A “Mateta exit bombshell” headline sits alongside a preview that expects him to start. And one City source places Maresca’s spell as Guardiola’s assistant in the 2018–19 treble season while the same passage says 2022–23; the two are inconsistent and neither is relied on above.
  • Most sensitive: the outright and the goals total — both require knowing how far along City’s reconstructed midfield is, and two competitive matches under a new manager cannot say. Least sensitive: discipline and the corner race — the first because per-team lines make the asymmetry checkable against named absences, the second because possession share is the one City property the rebuild did not touch.
