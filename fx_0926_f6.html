A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Czechia are promoted: League B Group B1 winners in 2024–25 on 11 points, 9–8 (W3 D2 L1, ahead of Ukraine, Georgia and Albania). Their division history reads B/A/B/A. Their only previous League A edition, 2022–23, ended fourth in Group A2 on 4 points, 5–13 (W1 D1 L4), and they went down; at home in Prague they beat Switzerland 2–1, drew 2–2 with Spain and lost 0–4 to Portugal. Sports Mole puts their overall Nations League record at W10 D3 L9 in 22. In World Cup qualifying they were second in Group L on 16 points, 18–8 (W5 D1 L2), behind Croatia. They reached the World Cup through two home play-offs, both drawn after extra time and won on penalties: 2–2 with the Republic of Ireland and 2–2 with Denmark (1–1 at 90 minutes). At the World Cup they finished bottom of Group A on 1 point: 1–2 to South Korea in Guadalajara, 1–1 with South Africa in Atlanta, 0–3 to Mexico in Mexico City. Croatia have played in League A in every edition. In 2024–25 they were second in Group A1 on 8 points, 8–8 (W2 D2 L2, behind Portugal), then lost the quarter-final to France on penalties after 2–0 in Split and 0–2 in Saint-Denis. They won qualifying Group L on 22 points, 26–4 (W7 D1), including the 5–1 against Czechia in Osijek and the 0–0 in Prague. At the World Cup they were second in Group L (2–4 to England, 1–0 against Panama, 2–1 against Ghana) and lost 1–2 to Portugal in the round of 32. B · Style & early-season reality

Czechia’s shape changed with each coach, and none of the three is in charge tonight. Hašek mostly played a 4-2-3-1 (eight of his 13 matches in ESPN’s record). In qualifying (seven matches under Hašek, one under Köstl) Czechia had more of the ball than the opposition: 59.0%, 17.75 shots to 8.50, 7.75 corners to 2.62. His side also won more tackles (160), made more interceptions (88) and won more aerial duels (238) than any other UEFA qualifier, and scored eight set-piece goals, the most of any European side (Squawka, via search summary). Koubek used a back three in all five of his matches (3-4-3, 3-4-2-1 three times, 3-5-2) and had 23.0% to 57.8% of the ball. Against League A-tier sides Czechia had 40.5% (two box scores). Czechia foul a lot and are rarely booked for it: 15.00 fouls per match at home, one card for every 8.08 fouls. Their cards fell from 3.33 per match in the 2024–25 Nations League to 0.88 in qualifying. They conceded 5 of their 25 competitive goals before half-time and 20 after it. Sports Mole’s possible eleven keeps a back three. Denia’s record is with Spain’s Olympic side (gold in 2024), so the evidence for his shape is a CV, not a match.

Croatia played a 4-2-3-1 in 13 of 20 competitive matches under Dalić, with a back three in four of the six 2024–25 Nations League group matches (and a 5-4-1 in another), at home to the Faroe Islands and against England at the World Cup. They dominated weaker sides: 70.0% possession, 22.62 shots and 9.25 corners per match in qualifying. Against League A-tier opposition they had 48.3% of the ball, took 12.43 shots to 15.00 and won 4.00 corners to 7.14. The two meetings split the same way: Croatia had 61.4% and 57.7% of the ball, but won 0 corners to 6 in Osijek. Away from home their goals came early, six of eight before half-time, and they failed to score in 3 of 7. Their away discipline was loose: 2.57 cards per match on 9.00 fouls, with both of the window’s red cards. The personnel is continuous: Livaković, Šutalo, Modrić, Perišić and Gvardiol made 14 to 18 starts each, and all are in the squad. What changes is the bench. Bilić has no match in this spell, and his previous spell ended in 2012; any claim about his shape tonight is a CV, not a match.
