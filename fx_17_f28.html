A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Written for the two markets this board is playing today. The result and double-chance families are abbreviated to a line in section C and are not analysed in depth here.

Bromley are the promoted side, they came up as champions, and this is the first League One season in the club's history. They won the 2025–26 League Two title with 87 points from 46 matches — 24W–15D–7L, 71 scored and 46 conceded — finishing one point ahead of Milton Keynes Dons, who also appear on this slate, and including the division's longest unbeaten run at 21 matches. It was the club's first fourth-tier title in its 135-season history, delivered by Andy Woodman under a goal-setting programme he renamed from "Project 73" to "Project Champions". The step up has been brutal in precisely the way promoted sides are most often punished: 21st, 4 points from 5, 4 scored and 15 conceded — three goals against per game — with three straight defeats. Blackpool are not promoted; this is their fourth consecutive League One season, and they sit 12th on 7 points from 5, 9 scored and 7 conceded.

B · Style & early-season reality

Bromley are the purest example on this slate of promotion without reinforcement. They won League Two with 87 points and a 21-match unbeaten run — a genuinely excellent fourth-tier season, and a defensive one as much as anything, with 46 conceded in 46 matches — and then entered the third tier having paid no published fee for any player, topping the squad up with five frees and three loans. The manager who built it stayed, which is the one continuity advantage they hold over the other promoted clubs on this board, but continuity of personnel is exactly what produces 15 goals conceded in five matches at a higher level: 7 at Hillsborough, 5 at home to Leyton Orient, 2 to Wimbledon. What makes today different in kind rather than degree is that the defence has now been physically broken as well as outclassed — three defenders and the first-choice goalkeeper lost to injury on Monday, with Owen Mason stepping in. The attacking side of the ledger is thin but not absent, and it has a shape worth noting: three of their four goals have come away from home, and Victor Adeboyejo, a free transfer from Mansfield, has scored three of them. Gap: no xG or shot data was obtainable, so this is read from scorelines, the ledger and the injury news.

Blackpool are a mid-table third-tier side whose season has been volatile rather than poor. Nine scored and seven conceded in five, with a 4–0 and two away defeats inside a fortnight, is the record of a team that has not settled — and the squad construction explains some of it, since the forward line is three loans (Charles, Yates and, defensively, Williams) rather than a bought attack, while the permanent money went to a goalkeeper and a centre-back. Four of their five league matches have produced three or more goals, and four of five have featured both teams scoring. The two absences bite on the attacking side: CJ Hamilton is gone for 10–12 weeks and Honeyman until after the international break, with Niskanen and Bloxham possible returns. Against a defence conceding three a game they will not need much, but their own record says they concede too — seven in five, including three at Reading six days ago. Gap: no xG, possession or foul data for either club, and one of Blackpool's own five results had to be derived rather than read.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Bromley's 87-point title season was built on a defence that is no longer functioning. They conceded 46 in 46 League Two matches; they have conceded 15 in five League One ones. Nothing about the 21-match unbeaten run transfers to this division on the evidence so far, and no fee was spent to help it transfer.
  • The visiting defence changed shape three days ago. Three defenders and goalkeeper Shamal George — himself a summer arrival — were lost on Monday, with Owen Mason expected to debut in goal behind Grant and Miller. Every concession number quoted above was produced by a less disrupted back line than the one starting today.
  • Bromley's home and away split runs against intuition and is only two matches deep on the away side. Three of their four goals came on the road, but one of those away games was a 7–2 defeat in which the goals arrived inside a rout. A scoring leg built on that is thinner than the raw split suggests.
  • One of Blackpool's five results is derived rather than read. Their opening fixture resolves to 1–1 from the published totals but was not found stated anywhere — and it is one of the two matches on which the "four of five over 2.5" claim depends being counted correctly.

Least sensitive: the goals total, where the mechanism is a promoted defence conceding at three a game that has just lost its goalkeeper and three defenders — a cause that is documented, current, and pointed in one direction.
