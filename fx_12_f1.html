A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted; both were in this division last season and finished eleven places apart. Lyon were 4th on 60 points from 34 matches — 18 wins, 6 draws, 10 defeats, 53 scored, 40 conceded, +13 — which took them into the Champions League third qualifying round. Auxerre were 15th on 34 points with 8 wins, 10 draws, 16 defeats, 34 scored, 44 conceded, −10, surviving by two points over Nice, who then had to play off to stay up. The structural headline is that only one of these clubs changed manager, and it is the one that also rebuilt its squad from the goalkeeper forward: Will Still joined Auxerre in early June 2026, and his first window brought in a new first-choice goalkeeper, two centre-backs and a centre-forward, while the club's leading scorer was sold. Lyon kept Paulo Fonseca and instead conducted a selling window — 14 arrivals for a reported €26.10m against 9 departures for €36m, a net positive of €9.90m — losing Afonso Moreira for a reported €33.6m including bonuses and Malick Fofana in the closing hours of the window, and replacing the goals with two loans. The long-run head-to-head is one-directional: Lyon are unbeaten in their last seven home matches against Auxerre, who have never won at this stadium, and the last meeting finished 3–2 to Lyon.

  • Lyon — turnover high in volume, and the two players sold were the two who created most. Fonseca continues; no manager or system change this summer, which makes Lyon the only side in this fixture whose last-season numbers were produced by the coach who will pick the team. The window was explicitly a degreasing exercise and Fonseca said so publicly, calling himself satisfied and noting the club "knew we had to sell players and that Malick would probably have to leave." Out: Afonso Moreira, at a reported €33.6m including bonuses — the largest fee involving either club on this card; Malick Fofana, on deadline day, fee unreported in the sources consulted; Ainsley Maitland-Niles to Everton, fee unreported. Nicolas Tagliafico was reported close to Atlético Madrid and the move did not happen, so he stays. In, weighted as the spec asks: centre-back Felix Bacher; midfield Mads Bidstrup, plus Youssouf Fofana on loan; centre-forward Loïs Openda on loan from Juventus, described as undisputed in the position; also Mohamed Ouédraogo, Julien Duranville and Noham Kamara. Gap: individual fees are published for Moreira alone; the €26.10m and €36m figures are aggregate window totals, not per-player prices, and no fee at all is published for Malick Fofana, Bacher, Bidstrup or either loan. B · Style & early-season reality

Lyon's 2025–26 was a fourth-place season built on a solid rather than spectacular goal record: 53 scored and 40 conceded across 34 matches, roughly 1.6 and 1.2 a game, with ten defeats — more than a side finishing fourth would usually carry, and a sign of a team that took its points in clusters. The continuity here is real and worth stating plainly, because it is rare on this card: Fonseca is the same manager, so the shape and the pressing height are not being rebuilt from a CV. What changed is who occupies the front line. Moreira and Malick Fofana, the two most valuable attacking assets, were both sold, and the replacement at centre-forward is Loïs Openda on loan from Juventus, with Youssouf Fofana loaned in to sit in midfield. The probable XI — Greif; Athekame, Bacher, Niakhaté, Tagliafico; Bidstrup, Morton; Nuamah, Tolisso, Boudache; Openda — contains a summer signing at centre-back, both central midfielders new or newly promoted into the role, and a loanee at centre-forward. Two league matches is not enough to say what that combination does to Lyon's territorial or shooting profile, and the specific numbers that would settle it are not available: gap — no xG, shot-volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate for Lyon's 2025–26 season was obtainable from any source reached within budget, which is the reason no such figure appears anywhere on this card.

Auxerre are the side where every stylistic input has moved at once. A new manager arrived in June; the goalkeeper is new; the centre-forward who led the line was sold to Paris FC for €8m and replaced by a loanee and two late arrivals; and the two players who would ordinarily anchor the defensive structure, Okoh and Diousse, are both out until at least the New Year with cruciate injuries, with Sierralta and Senaya also unavailable. Last season's 34 goals scored and 44 conceded describe a side that was hard to beat and rarely won — ten draws in 34 — under a coach who has gone. What has replaced it in two matches is a defence that shipped three at home to Angers and five at Lens, eight in total, which is the single most concrete piece of 2026–27 evidence available about either team. The honest caveat is that two matches is two matches, and one of them was away at a side that has started strongly; the honest counterweight is that the absences behind those eight goals are documented and long-term, not a matter of form. Where a stylistic claim would need shot volume, crossing counts or set-piece frequency to be made properly, the same gap applies as for Lyon: none of those series was obtainable for Auxerre, so this card describes personnel and results, not playing patterns it cannot evidence.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Auxerre's eight goals conceded come from two matches played without four defensive players — Okoh and Diousse with cruciate injuries and reported out beyond the New Year, Sierralta undisclosed, Senaya hamstring — and behind a goalkeeper signed this summer to replace one who left on a free. The number is real; treating it as a settled property of this defence rather than a snapshot of an unavailable one is where it misleads, particularly with Tuanzebe possibly debuting here.
  • Lyon's 53 goals last season were scored by an attack that no longer exists. Afonso Moreira left for a reported €33.6m including bonuses and Malick Fofana went in the closing hours of the window; the centre-forward is Openda on loan from Juventus and the midfield anchor is Youssouf Fofana, also on loan. Two league matches, three goals scored. Any total or team-goals reasoning that reaches back to 2025–26 for Lyon is reading a different squad.
  • The head-to-head record is long and one-directional — seven home matches unbeaten, no Auxerre win at this stadium ever — but it was compiled by squads and coaching staffs that have both turned over, most drastically on the visiting side, where the manager arrived in June and the goalkeeper, centre-forward and at least two defenders are new.
  • Neither club's underlying numbers were obtainable. No xG, shot volume, home/away split, foul rate or card rate was published in any source reached for either side, for either season. Every market row above is reasoned from personnel, results and the referee's own record, and where a row would need those series — the corner line above all — the card says so instead of estimating them.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total, where a genuinely striking two-match concession rate is attached to a defence missing four players, and where both attacks were rebuilt in the window that closed three days ago. Least sensitive: the discipline row, which is the only family here resting on a confirmed appointment and a completed 17-match referee sample rather than on two matchdays — with the caveat that the fouls-per-card ratio behind that sample is not published.
