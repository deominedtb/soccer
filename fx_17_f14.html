A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Written for the two markets this board is playing today. The result and double-chance families are abbreviated to a line in section C and are not analysed in depth here.

York City are promoted, and they arrived with the biggest attacking record on this slate. They won promotion out of the 2025–26 National League with a club-record 108 points and 114 goals scored — two points clear of Rochdale, who finished second on 106 and came up through the play-offs, and who appear on this same card. That is a division-winning attack rather than a play-off survivor, and the early League Two evidence says the attack has travelled while the defence has not: 9 scored and 8 conceded in five, 9th on 8 points, and both teams have scored in every one of York's five matches. Swindon Town are the opposite kind of side in every structural respect — not promoted, not relegated, a sixth consecutive League Two season — and they sit 2nd on 10 points, 3W–1D–1L, 8 scored and 6 conceded. The fixture is a promoted attack that concedes against an established side that has just won 3–1 twice.

B · Style & early-season reality

York City are the clearest example on this slate of a promoted side whose attack has survived the step up and whose defence has not. The 114 goals that won the National League were scored by a squad that has since lost its centre-back for £750,000 and its attacking midfielder on a free, and gained one defender, one forward and one free midfielder — and the early return is a side that scores in every match and concedes in every match. Nine goals and eight conceded in five is a symmetrical record, and the symmetry is the point: five matches, five occasions on which both teams scored, including a 3–2 win, a 3–2 defeat and two 1–1 draws. That is not a side controlling matches; it is a side trading in them, which is exactly the profile a division-winning non-league attack tends to bring into the EFL before the defensive adjustment happens. The head-coach change cuts the same way — Scott Lindsey inherited the promotion, he did not build it. Gap: no xG or shot data was obtainable, so the trading pattern is described from scorelines rather than from underlying numbers.

Swindon are the settled side and, unusually for a sixth-season League Two club, the one with momentum attached to a named individual. Holloway is unchanged, the back line was replaced rather than depleted — Negru in from Oxford United as Delaney left for Oldham, Clark added at right-back — and the centre-forward signed from AFC Wimbledon has 5 in 6 with a hat-trick last weekend. Their results have a shape to them that York's do not: a 0–0 on the opening day at Northampton, then two 3–1 home wins, which is a side capable of shutting a match down and a side capable of winning one comfortably. The caution is that two of their five league matches could not be established at all, so the 8 scored and 6 conceded is a total whose distribution is only partly visible — and the portion that is visible (4 goals in two home wins against 2 conceded) is flattering relative to the whole. Gap: no xG, foul rate or card data for either club, and no away-form split for Swindon beyond the opening 0–0.
