The one thing shaping every game on this card

What separates this slate from the one before it is how far apart those samples now are. Bayern have played two Bundesliga matches. Roma have played three. PSV and Shakhtar have played five each. The cards say so rather than splitting the difference.

The second thread is how unevenly the summer has been documented. On two of the three fixtures built so far the transfer ledger is fully priced — Fenerbahçe bought three centre-forwards for a published €39m, €15.5m and €6m, Roma spent €35m, €25m, €25m and €16.5m, PSV paid €14.5m and €13m for two central midfielders. Where the money is visible it points at the same places the spec asks about — Fenerbahçe sold their goalkeeper, Bayern sold their back-up goalkeeper and spent €55m on a defender, PSV spent both of their fees in central midfield and left goalkeeper and centre-back untouched while conceding six in five, and Shakhtar’s defining transaction was outgoing, Sudakov’s €20.25m obligation to Benfica triggering this summer.

The third thread is the referees, and it is the reason three discipline rows on this board carry evidence rather than a note about a pending designation. The card lines follow the same order: 4.5 on Gil Manzano’s fixture, 3.5 on Meler’s, and 2.5 — the lowest total on the slate — on the one where the official’s record is least established.

A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted; both are established top-flight sides, and this is the first competitive meeting between them in any competition. They arrive by very different routes. Roma finished third in Serie A in 2025–26 on 73 points — 23 wins, four draws, eleven defeats, 59 goals scored and 31 conceded — closing with five straight wins and sealing the place with a 2–0 at Hellas Verona on the final day. That was Gian Piero Gasperini's first season at the club, having arrived on 6 June 2025 after almost a decade at Atalanta, so the manager is not a change in this window: the shape here has a full league season and a top-three finish behind it. Fenerbahçe finished second in the Süper Lig behind Galatasaray and entered this competition through qualifying, where they needed six matches — four wins and two draws, eight scored and three conceded — to eliminate Górnik Zabrze, Sturm Graz and Lyon. İsmail Kartal is the head coach with no change recorded in the sources reached. The two starts to 2026–27 are almost mirror images: Roma lead Serie A with nine points from three, ten goals scored and one conceded; Fenerbahçe are eighth in the Süper Lig with six points from four, having lost 1–2 away to Gençlerbirliği on the opening day and 1–2 at home to Beşiktaş five days ago.

B · Style & early-season reality

Roma are the more legible of the two sides here, and the reason is that the manager is the same one who produced the numbers. Gasperini took over in June 2025 and finished third with 31 conceded — a defensive figure consistent with everything his Atalanta sides were known for — and the summer then spent its two largest fees at opposite ends of the pitch: €35m on Castro at centre-forward and €16.5m on Koulierakis at centre-back, with Molina added at full-back and Malen's loan converted at €25m after the season in which he scored 14 league goals. That is additive rather than reconstructive business on a shape that already worked. Three matches have produced ten goals and one conceded, including two 4–0 wins and a 2–1 over Atalanta decided late. The honest limit on all of this is the sample: three domestic matches, no European minutes, and a second season under the same coach is exactly the situation in which last season's 59-scored, 31-conceded profile is the better guide than three weeks of results. Gap: no possession share, foul rate, shot volume or corner series is published for Roma in either season, so the style description stops at goals, fees and the manager's continuity.

Fenerbahçe are harder to characterise and the difficulty is not a shortage of matches — they have played ten — but that the matches point in two directions. In Europe they went through three rounds unbeaten, four wins and two draws, conceding three in six against Górnik Zabrze, Sturm Graz and Lyon. Domestically they have lost two of four, including at home to Beşiktaş five days ago, and sit eighth. The squad underneath that split changed most in the forward line: Greenwood at €39m, Muriqi at €15.5m and Lukaku at €6m all arrived in the same window, while Talisca — last season's leading scorer — left by mutual agreement and Cherif was sold for €25m. Three new centre-forwards is a large amount of simultaneous change in one department, and the league evidence for how it has settled is four matches in which Greenwood has scored twice and the team has eight goals. The back line has changed too, with Aké at £7m and Oppong at €12m arriving and Livaković sold, leaving Ederson expected in goal behind a line named as Semedo, Škriniar, Aké and Brown — but with Oosterwolde out after hamstring surgery. Add Guendouzi's suspension and the midfield loses its most expensive player for the occasion. Gap: as with Roma, no underlying series exists here for either season; what can be said is that ten competitive matches have produced a strong European record and a poor domestic one, and that this card does not pretend to know which of those the visit of Roma resembles.

Across the card

  • Best structurally supported family across the three cards built: discipline. All three appointments are confirmed and two of them are documented at a depth this board rarely gets — Gil Manzano at 4.89 cards and 24.21 fouls a match, Meler at 4.59 and 25.41, both with career figures and penalty rates behind them. On the Fenerbahçe card the official’s record and the 4.5 line point the same way; on the PSV card the only card-by-side figure available for the official points the opposite way to the team lines the book has set. Both are usable, because in each case there is a published record to compare the price against.
  • Least supported family: corners. Not one corner count was obtainable for any of the six clubs researched, in either season. Every corner row on this board is therefore reasoning from goals, territory and squad news to a market with no direct evidence attached, and all three lines — 9.5, 9.5 and 10.5 — are priced close to even, which is the market conceding the same thing.
  • The one ordinary fixture: Fenerbahçe – Roma. It is the only card so far whose result market has three genuinely live prices — 3.50 / 3.60 / 2.05, nothing shorter than two — against a documented structural gap: an unchanged manager and a 31-goals-conceded season on one side, a squad that replaced its entire forward line and lost its most expensive midfielder to suspension on the other. Everything on that page is still an open question, which is not true anywhere else on this board yet.
  • Where the book has priced indifference. Three times so far, and each means something different. Bodø/Glimt’s card line at 1.75 / 1.78 and the Bayern corner total at 1.80 / 1.85 are honest blanks — no club card rate and no corner series exists for either side. Fenerbahçe’s and Roma’s team card lines are priced identically at 2.5, 1.65 / 1.90, which is the book declining to distinguish two clubs and letting one high-carding official carry the whole family.
  • Where the book has priced certainty. Bayern at 1.07, the 12 double chance at 1.02, over 2.5 at 1.10 and the corner race at 1.06 are four settled rows on one fixture. That is a statement about the price rather than the game: each of them has already absorbed the 122-goal season, the venue and the four absentees, and none of them can be reasoned about in either direction any more.

Scope. This page rates market families only. It contains no selections, no sides, no recommended lines and no confidence labels — by design. Where evidence is thin it says so rather than filling the gap.
