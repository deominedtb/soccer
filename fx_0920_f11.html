A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, but Nice came closer to going down than any club carded on this slate: they finished 16th in 2025–26 on 32 points — 7 wins, 11 draws, 16 defeats, 37 scored, 60 conceded — and survived only through a relegation play-off against Saint-Étienne, having also lost a Coupe de France final. The 60 goals conceded and the 17.6% clean-sheet rate are among the worst figures on this board, second in both cases to Real Sociedad. Lille finished 3rd on 61 points — 18 wins, 7 draws, 9 defeats, 52 scored, 37 conceded — and qualified for the Champions League, where they lost 3–2 at home to Real Betis on 8 September, a club that also plays on this slate. The structural headline is two coaching changes made three weeks apart in June, at opposite ends of the table and for opposite reasons. Nice appointed Olivier Pantaloni on 17 June 2026, after Claude Puel had been called in as an emergency measure following Franck Haise’s departure and was not retained — that is three men in one season. Lille appointed Davide Ancelotti on 1 June 2026 to succeed Bruno Génésio, who left for Marseille and appears later on this same slate at f18.

B · Style & early-season reality

Nice have changed shape more than any club on this board and have scored one goal doing it. Possession has risen from 46.3% to 60.7% — fourteen points, the second-largest move on this slate — PPDA has fallen from 13.29 to 10.35, and deep completions have risen from 5.53 to 7.50. They hold the ball, press harder and get into the final third more often. The output is 0.88 xG per match, the third-lowest on this board, and 0.25 goals — one in four matches — from 12.25 shots and just 2.75 on target, the second-lowest on-target count here. They are creating volume without quality and converting none of it. The corner column confirms the territorial change and the lack of end product at once: 6.50 won against 2.50 conceded, having won 5.09 and conceded 5.74 last season — a differential that has swung by more than four corners a match and produced a single goal. Their fouls have fallen from 12.35 to 8.25, the third-lowest on this board, which is what possession does. The defensive figure remains poor on the model, 1.97 xG conceded, though the counted rate has improved from 1.76 to 1.25.

Lille under Ancelotti have done something unusual: they have become considerably worse on the model and considerably better on the scoreboard. PPDA has risen from 10.17 to 16.01 — the loosest press of any club carded on this slate — possession has fallen from 55.8% to 45.1%, shots from 13.59 to 10.75, deep completions from 9.44 to 7.00, and xG conceded has risen from 1.14 to 1.74. Five series, all moving the wrong way. And they have conceded two goals in four matches, kept three clean sheets, and not conceded before half-time at all. Their attacking conversion is carrying it: 6.00 shots on target per match from 10.75 shots, a 56% on-target rate, producing 1.75 goals from 1.37 xG. The corner figure is the most extreme on the card in the other direction: 1.75 won per match, the lowest of any club anywhere on this board, against 5.71 last season. A side that had the lowest over-2.5 rate in Ligue 1 last season at 38.2% has made its matches smaller still, from a structure that the underlying numbers say should be leaking.
