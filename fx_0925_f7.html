A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Both sides are promoted from League C. Sweden won Group C1 in 2024–25 on 16 points, 19–4 (W5 D1 L0, ahead of Slovakia, Estonia and Azerbaijan). They were in League B in 2022–23 and League A in 2020–21, so this is a return after one edition out. Romania won Group C2, W5 D1 L0 and 15–3 as played (ahead of Kosovo, Cyprus and Lithuania). The 0–0 at home to Kosovo was abandoned after the 90th minute and awarded 3–0, which makes the official record 18 points and 18–3. Romania were in League B in 2020–21 and 2022–23. In World Cup qualifying Sweden took two points from four matches under Jon Dahl Tomasson. Under Graham Potter they won play-off ties in Ukraine (3–1) and at home to Poland (3–2). At the World Cup they beat Tunisia (5–1), lost to the Netherlands (1–5), drew with Japan (1–1) and lost to France (0–3) in the round of 32. Romania were third in Group H behind Austria and Bosnia-Herzegovina and lost the play-off semi-final 0–1 in Türkiye. The eight sides promoted from League C into League B for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 1.30 points per home match (W8 D6 L9) and 0.96 per away match (W6 D4 L13). Their home matches had over 2.5 in 39.1% and BTTS in 39.1%, with 4.61 corners won against 4.87 conceded. No two of those eight shared a group, so no promoted side has hosted another in the last two League B editions. On matchday one the eight went W4 D3 L1. Group B4 also holds the two sides relegated from League A, Poland and Bosnia-Herzegovina, who meet tonight in Warsaw. Head to head: the Swedish FA’s match guide counts 12 meetings, Sweden W6 D3 L3, 24–12. ESPN’s record since 2003 lists none, and no date for the last meeting was found. The sides meet again in Romania on 5 October. The RESEARCH SPEC’s transfer-fee field has no national-team equivalent. B · Style & early-season reality

Sweden have two records, and the break between them is the coach. Under Tomasson, in the League C group and the first four qualifiers, they had 62.4% of the ball, took 19.20 shots against 8.80, crossed 20.30 times and won 8.00 corners against 2.40. Against Azerbaijan, Estonia and Slovakia that meant 3.17 goals per match. Against Slovenia, Kosovo and Switzerland it meant 0.50. Under Potter they have had 43.9% of the ball, taken 10.50 shots against 12.50 and won 4.00 corners against 5.50, and scored in seven of eight. They also kept no clean sheet in those eight, and have none in 12 competitive matches since September 2025. The front two is the constant: Gyökeres and Isak scored 19 of Sweden’s 36 competitive goals in the window, and both are in the 23. Ayari, Gudmundsson, Lindelöf and Karlström are too. The change is at the back: Hien and Lagerbielke, with 23 starts at centre-back, are out, and so are the two goalkeepers who started Potter’s last seven competitive matches. Sports Mole predicts a 3-5-2 with Stroud, Starfelt and Lindelöf; a Swedish preview headline predicts 4-4-2. Potter’s own words in the match guide are that Romania can press both high and low.

Romania’s record belongs to a coach who is not there. Under Lucescu, in 4-3-3, they had 52.9% of the ball, took 13.67 shots against 9.87 and conceded 0.93 goals per match over 15 competitive matches. Their opponents took 3.27 cards per match to Romania’s 2.00. They scored late, 12 of their 34 goals from the 75th minute against 3 of 14 conceded, and took seven penalty kicks against one. Away from home without San Marino they had 43.8% of the ball, took 11.43 shots against 14.00 and won 4.00 corners against 4.57. In the four 25/26 matches without San Marino (Cyprus away, Austria, Bosnia away, Türkiye) they won 1.75 corners against 6.00. The spine of that side is in the 23: Rațiu, Burcă, Drăgușin, Bancu, Răzvan Marin, Stanciu, Man and Mihăilă. The striker is not: Drăguș and Bîrligea are out, which leaves Munteanu (no competitive start) or a front line built from Man, Mihăilă, Ianis Hagi and Tănase. Nobody has seen Hagi’s Romania in a competitive match. The evidence for its shape is two friendlies, one of them with an empty box score. The Wales win had 42.8% of the ball, 22 shots against 20 and 10 corners against 12.
