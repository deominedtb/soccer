A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Rayo finished 8th in 2025–26 — 12 wins, 14 draws, 12 defeats, 41 scored, 44 conceded, 50 points — and reached the Conference League final on 27 May 2026, finishing runners-up. Espanyol finished 11th — 12 wins, 10 draws, 16 defeats, 43 scored, 55 conceded, 46 points. The structural headline is Rayo’s: they have lost their stadium. The Comunidad de Madrid, which owns Vallecas, ended the club’s concession and suspended use of the ground after an audit found serious security, maintenance and hygiene deficiencies, with works of several months and no completion date; Rayo have played home matches at Leganés’ Estadio Ontime Butarque (capacity 14,436) since 20 August, and their 1–1 with Alavés there drew the lowest LaLiga attendance of the season, 4,280. They did it with a new head coach and without two first-choice defenders sold in August.

B · Style & early-season reality

Rayo under Iñigo Pérez were a front-foot 4–2–3–1 side — 2,249 Understat minutes in that shape, 53.4% possession, 13.9 shots and 5.68 corners per match (league 4.83 per team), and a heavy contact profile: 13.5 fouls, 97 yellows and 9 reds across the league season, on top of a 15-match Conference League run to the final. The shape has survived the change of coach — 4–2–3–1 is still the most-used under San José, with 4–1–4–1 and 4–3–3 alongside — and so has the corner count (6.00 per match). Possession has not (44.7%), and shots faced have risen by five a match, although two of those five matches were away at the top two. The left side of the defence is new in both positions after Chavarría and Mendy were sold, and the goalkeeper is a loan signing covering an injury; evidence for how San José’s back line functions is five matches, two of them against Barcelona and Real Madrid.

Espanyol under Manolo González are a lower-possession side that alternates between a 4–2–3–1 and a 4–4–2 — 42.3% possession and 4.68 corners per match last season, 13.8 fouls and 2.37 cards. This season two of those numbers have moved sharply on five matches: fouls up to 18.2 per match and 17 yellow cards, the most in the division, while corners have fallen to seven in five matches. The attack has shifted its timing too — 0.34 first-half goals per match last season, six of eight before the break this season. The summer replaced two loaned-in wide players, Carlos Romero and Ngonge, with two more loans, Hartman and Zaragoza, so the flank personnel is again temporary; the centre-back group lost four and added two loans.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Rayo’s home numbers belong to a stadium they cannot use. Last season’s home split was built at Vallecas; Butarque is a borrowed ground in another municipality with two Rayo matches and a 4,280 attendance on record.
  • Rayo’s five-match sample is schedule-skewed. Away at Barcelona and Real Madrid account for 9 of 14 goals conceded and much of the rise in shots faced.
  • Espanyol’s discipline and corner shifts are large for five matches. Fouls up from 13.8 to 18.2 and corners down from 4.68 to 1.40 per match, with the same coach and a loan-rebuilt flank.
  • Fixture congestion and absences. Espanyol play again on Friday, Rayo on Saturday; Rayo are without their top scorer of last season (De Frutos, suspended) and four injured players, Espanyol without three forwards.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2 and the handicap — the home advantage has moved to Leganés and the margin data is two fixtures deep. Least sensitive: Rayo’s corners-for rate, stable at 5.68 and 6.00 through a change of coach and ground.
