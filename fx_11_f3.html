A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted, and they finished one place apart. Atalanta were 7th on 59 points — 38 matches, 15 wins, 14 draws, 9 defeats, 51 scored, 36 conceded, +15 — and Bologna 8th on 56 with 16 wins, 8 draws, 14 defeats, 49 scored, 46 conceded, +3. The fourteen draws are the number that stands out: Atalanta lost only nine matches all season and still finished seventh, because they drew more than a third of them. The structural headline is that both clubs replaced their manager, and only one of them also dismantled its spine. Maurizio Sarri left Lazio for Atalanta, announced on 15 June 2026 on a three-year contract to June 2029 — the same move that put Gennaro Gattuso in the Lazio dugout. Domenico Tedesco, previously at Fenerbahçe and with the Belgium national team, replaced Vincenzo Italiano at Bologna on a contract to 30 June 2028 with an option for a further season. Bologna then sold Santiago Castro to Roma, in the deal Roma report at €35m including bonuses, and Jhon Lucumí to Juventus — centre-forward and centre-back gone in the same window a new manager arrived. The head-to-head is emphatically against the hosts: Bologna have won four of the last seven Serie A meetings, Atalanta have lost three of their last four home fixtures against them, and Bologna have won four of their last five matches played at Bergamo across all competitions.

  • Atalanta — new manager, heavy defensive outflow, and no published fee anywhere. Sarri arrives from Lazio, announced 15 June 2026, three years to June 2029, and the shape reported for this match is a 4–3–3: Carnesecchi; Zappacosta, Kossounou, Scalvini, Bernasconi; Pasalic, Gaetano, Ederson; De Ketelaere, Krstović, Raspadori. The window is dominated by departures and almost all of them are defenders. Out: Berat Djimsiti to Al-Diriyah, Marco Palestra to Chelsea, Ben Godfrey to Rangers, Alessandro Cittadini to Frosinone and Bonfanti to Las Palmas, plus El Bilal Touré to Parma, Daniel Maldini to Cagliari, Marco Brescianini permanently to Fiorentina, Vavassori to Palermo and Yunus Musah back to Milan at the end of his loan. In: Gianluca Gaetano from Cagliari and Thomas Kristensen from Udinese, with Ilaix Sulemana and Vlahovic returning from loans. Five defenders out against one defender in — and Kristensen is unavailable for this match. Gap: not one fee is published for any Atalanta movement in any source consulted, incoming or outgoing, including the Palestra sale to Chelsea. Conflict logged: one transfer board additionally lists Elmas from Leipzig and Franck Kessié from Al-Ahli on a free as arrivals and Marten de Roon to Roma as a departure; a second board covering the same window lists none of the three, and Roma’s own ledger does not record De Roon. None of the three is carried as established.
  • Bologna — new manager, and the two most important players in the side sold in the same summer. Domenico Tedesco replaced Vincenzo Italiano on a deal to 30 June 2028 with an option. The two defining exits are Santiago Castro to Roma — reported from the Roma side at €35m including bonuses, which makes it the largest fee involving any Serie A club on this card — and Jhon Lucumí to Juventus. Also gone: Remo Freuler to Olympiacos, Charalampos Lykogiannis and Bonifazi released, Federico Ravaglia to Watford, Thijs Dallinga to Cologne, Dominguez to Sassuolo, Fabbian to Fiorentina, Aebischer to Pisa, Sosa to Colo-Colo, Raimondo to Frosinone and Ilić to Cesena. The replacements for Castro are two forwards, neither bought outright with certainty: Artem Dovbyk from Roma on a loan with a right to buy, on a contract to 30 June 2027, and Roberto Piccoli from Fiorentina. Also in: Arthur Theate from Eintracht Frankfurt, Alhassane from Real Oviedo, Amondarain from Estudiantes, Tommaso Pobega from Milan and Caccavo from Lumezzane. Gap: no fee is published for any Bologna movement other than Castro’s, which is sourced from the buying club. Conflict logged: Piccoli is recorded as a permanent signing by one board and a loan by another; Freuler is recorded as released by one and as a permanent transfer to Olympiacos by the other; and Theate, Pobega and Caccavo appear on only one of the two lists.
  • Suspensions / absences. No suspensions for either side. Atalanta are without Ahanor, Hien, Sulemana and Kristensen — two of them centre-backs, in a squad that has just sold or loaned out five defenders and signed one, who is himself among the four unavailable. Bologna are without Casale. That is a home side missing three-quarters of the defensive reinforcement it might have had, against a visiting side missing one player.
  • Sample. One league match each; matchday 2. Atalanta beat Sassuolo 2–1, with Raspadori and Krstović scoring — Sarri’s first competitive result. Bologna lost 1–0 at home to Lazio, the same match recorded from the other side as Gattuso’s debut win. Both scorelines are corroborated across sources. Two matches of evidence for two new managers is as thin as a sample gets, and in Bologna’s case the one data point is a home defeat by a single goal to a side that has itself just changed manager.
  • Referee. Daniele Doveri, confirmed in the matchday-2 designations. He is described as a highly experienced official with unusually even card management — 2.0 yellows a game to the home side and 2.1 to the away side, a symmetry worth noting in a fixture where the market has set the two per-team lines at different prices. Conflict logged: two readings of his 2025–26 season are irreconcilable. The other reports 6 matches, 26 yellows, no reds and no penalties, at 4.3 cards a game and 5.6 fouls per card — a notably quicker trigger. The gap between 6.7 and 5.6 fouls per card is the whole question for a 2.5 card line, and it is not resolved here. Gap: the assistants, fourth official and VAR appointments were not published in the source reached, and no Doveri record specific to either club was found.

B · Style & early-season reality

Atalanta’s 2025–26 was a season of control without conversion: 51 scored, 36 conceded, only nine defeats, and fourteen draws. That is the profile of a side that rarely got beaten and rarely put a game away, and it belongs to a coaching regime that has now gone. Sarri’s arrival is the most consequential stylistic variable on this card, because the two methods are not adjacent. Whatever Atalanta become under him, the evidence for it is one match — a 2–1 home win over Sassuolo — and a CV. Meanwhile the squad he inherited has been thinned precisely where a new defensive structure has to be built: Djimsiti, Palestra, Godfrey, Cittadini and Bonfanti all left, Kristensen was the one defensive arrival and he is unavailable, and Hien and Ahanor are also out. The reported back four of Zappacosta, Kossounou, Scalvini and Bernasconi is what is left rather than what was planned. Krstović, who scored on matchday one, is the centre-forward Atalanta bought from Lecce for a reported €25m plus a sell-on in an earlier window — and on the evidence of one game, the attack is the part of this side that has carried over intact.

Bologna are the harder case and the more radically changed. Tedesco replaces Italiano, and the squad handed to him is missing the centre-forward who was sold to Roma for €35m and the centre-back who was sold to Juventus, along with Freuler in midfield and Lykogiannis, Ravaglia, Dallinga, Fabbian and Aebischer besides. Forty-nine goals scored and forty-six conceded last season is a balanced, mid-table profile; the summer removed the principal source of the first number and one of the pillars of the second. The replacements are a loan with an option in Dovbyk, Piccoli from Fiorentina on terms the sources disagree about, and Theate at the back. That is a squad rebuilt on borrowed and unpriced players, under a manager who has been in the job weeks and lost his opening match at home. Against all of that sits the one genuinely striking historical fact on this page: Bologna have won four of their last five matches at Bergamo and Atalanta have lost three of their last four home meetings with them — a record built by different squads and different managers on both sides, but a long and one-directional one.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Both managers are new and both have one match of record. Sarri was announced on 15 June and Tedesco replaced Italiano in the same window; between them they have overseen a 2–1 home win and a 1–0 home defeat. Every season-long figure quoted for either club — Atalanta’s fourteen draws, Bologna’s 49 goals — was produced by someone else.
  • Bologna sold their centre-forward and their centre-back in the same summer they changed coach. Castro went to Roma in a deal the buying club reports at €35m including bonuses and Lucumí went to Juventus; the replacements at the sharp end are Dovbyk on a loan with a right to buy and Piccoli on terms two sources describe differently. This is not a squad that can be read off last season’s table position.
  • Atalanta’s defence has been emptied and not refilled. Djimsiti, Palestra, Godfrey, Cittadini and Bonfanti all left; Kristensen was the only defensive signing and he is unavailable, as are Hien and Ahanor. A new manager building a new defensive structure is doing it with the players who happen to be fit.
  • The venue record is the one long-run fact on this page and it cuts against the favourite. Bologna have won four of their last five matches at Bergamo and four of the last seven Serie A meetings overall, and Atalanta have lost three of their last four home fixtures against them — while the market prices Atalanta at 1.95 and Bologna at 3.85. Both cannot be right, and the record is the larger sample even though the squads that produced it have largely gone.
  • Most sensitive: the discipline family, where the appointed official’s card rate and fouls-per-card ratio are reported two incompatible ways and the market itself prices an arithmetical contradiction between the 2.5 match line and the two 1.5 team lines. Least sensitive: nothing on this fixture is genuinely robust — the closest is the asymmetry in the both-teams-to-score row, where the depleted state of the Atalanta back line is documented rather than inferred.

E · Short market-type guide

  • The handicap is more robust than the outright here because it prices margin rather than winner, and the one thing both season-long records agree on is that these are narrow teams — fourteen draws for Atalanta, a +3 goal difference for Bologna — even though the managers who produced those records have both left.
  • Both-teams-to-score is more robust than totals because it turns on a documented squad fact rather than a projection: five Atalanta defenders departed, one arrived, and three defenders including that arrival are unavailable. Totals depends on two attacks that have each been rebuilt around players who arrived on loan or under a new coach.
  • The corner race is more robust than the corner line because the race only requires the territorial favourite to be the territorial favourite, which the 1.95 outright already asserts, whereas the 9.5 line requires a volume estimate for two teams whose playing style changed managers eleven weeks ago.
  • Every other market on this fixture is more robust than the cards market, for a specific and checkable reason: Doveri’s 2025–26 record appears as both 3.6 cards a game at 6.7 fouls per card and 4.3 a game at 5.6, and a 2.5-card line is precisely the level at which that difference decides the outcome.
  • Where the book has priced indifference, it is saying something true. Over and under 2.5 goals at 1.87 and 1.85 is the flattest total on the card, and in this case that is not a missing edge but an accurate description of the evidence: two new managers, one match each, three goals between them, and two squads that lost their most valuable players this summer.
