A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted and both were in this division last season. Espanyol finished 11th on 46 points — 12 wins, 10 draws, 16 defeats, 43 scored, 55 conceded, the lowest goals-for figure of any club on tonight's board. Elche finished 15th on 43 points — 10 wins, 13 draws, 15 defeats, 49 scored, 57 conceded — with the most lopsided home-away split on the slate: 9-8-2 and 30–19 at home, 1-5-13 and 19–38 away. The structural headline is a correction to what this card first carried. Elche have changed head coach: Eder Sarabia announced on 27 May 2026 that he was stepping down — his own decision, to rest, not a dismissal — and Martín Anselmi was appointed on 13 June 2026 on a one-year deal with an option for another. Anselmi is Argentine and arrives from Cruz Azul, Porto and Botafogo, with no previous season in Spain. Only Manolo González is the man who finished last season here, and the squads underneath both turned over heavily, almost entirely through loans and purchase options rather than fees. An earlier version of this card, built from a Spanish preview, named Sarabia as Elche's coach and reported him suspended from the bench. The confirmed team sheet names Anselmi and the club's own announcement dates the change to June: the preview was wrong and so was the card. A second headline belongs with the officials rather than the clubs: Jon Ander González Esteban was promoted to Primera this season and this is his fourth LaLiga match.

B · Style & early-season reality

Espanyol are the same side they were, playing more aggressively and getting nothing from set plays. Their PPDA is 12.20 against 12.16 last season and their possession 46.7% against 42.3% — almost unchanged — but their foul rate has risen from 13.79 to 17.67 per match, the highest of any club on tonight's board, and their card rate from 2.37 to 3.17. They have already had a man sent off, against Rayo three days ago. The striking column is the corner one: 2.17 won per match against a divisional 4.97, down from 4.68 last season, with 4.33 conceded — the lowest corner count on the board by some distance, from a side taking 12.0 shots and generating 1.39 xG. Deep completions are up from 4.50 to 6.17, so they are getting into the final third more; they are simply not forcing corners when they get there.

Elche have inverted themselves and the numbers say so plainly. Last season's Elche were a possession side that pressed hard — 57.7% of the ball, a PPDA of 8.29, the most aggressive on this slate — and were nearly unbeatable at home (9-8-2) and nearly winless away (1-5-13). This season the press has gone: PPDA 12.08, possession 52.4%, and the home-away split has reversed — 0-0-3 and 4–11 at home, 0-2-1 and 4–5 away. Their xG against has risen from 1.81 to 2.44 per match and deep completions conceded from 6.21 to 8.83, with the centre-back who left for €12m replaced by a loan option from Cádiz. The first-half column is the most extreme figure in this whole strip: 0.17 first-half goals scored per match against 1.50 conceded, where last season read 0.50 and 0.66. The honest qualification is that three of their six matches were against Barcelona, Real Madrid and Real Sociedad, two of them at home, and a 0–5 against Barcelona is doing a large share of the work in every conceded rate above.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Elche's conceded columns are a fixture list as much as a defence. Barcelona (0–5) and Real Madrid (2–3) at home and Real Sociedad and Athletic besides, inside six matches. Removing the Barcelona result alone moves their goals-against from 2.67 to 2.20 per match. Their 2.44 xG against and 8.83 deep completions conceded carry the same distortion, and no published figure separates schedule from decline.
  • Elche's home-away split has inverted completely and nothing explains it. 9-8-2 and 30–19 at home last season against 1-5-13 and 19–38 away; 0-0-3 and 4–11 at home this season against 0-2-1 and 4–5 away. The coach is the same man. Three home matches is a tiny sample, but it is a tiny sample pointing the opposite way to thirty-eight.
  • Espanyol's foul rate has risen by nearly four per match with the style unchanged. 13.79 to 17.67 fouls, 2.37 to 3.17 cards, PPDA 12.16 to 12.20 and possession up. A side fouling more without pressing more is usually a side defending deeper in worse positions, and with two centre-backs gone for nothing and two loanees in, that reading is plausible but not published.
  • The referee has never worked a full season at this level. Four LaLiga matches including tonight, against 68 in Segunda. His card tolerance is remarkably steady across those 68 — 4.45, 4.48 and 4.70 per match — and his three Primera matches are right on it at 4.67, which is reassuring as far as it goes. His penalty rate is published nowhere, and the spec's own point stands: an unknown official is itself a reason discipline markets carry more variance, even when his cards look predictable.
  • Most sensitive: corners and the handicap — corners because Espanyol's count has halved with no mechanism in the shot or possession data, the handicap because five of Elche's six matches were within a goal and the sixth was 0–5. Least sensitive: BTTS and the club side of discipline — both rest on two full seasons of rates in the same division and six matches of this one, which nothing else on tonight's board can say. Elche's coaching change qualifies that continuity without removing it: the rates are the club's, not Sarabia's.
