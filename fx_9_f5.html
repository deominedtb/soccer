A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Elversberg are promoted and this is the first Bundesliga match in the club’s history — they are the 59th club to play in the division, promoted as runners-up in the 2025–26 2. Bundesliga on 62 points from 34 matches (18 wins, 8 draws, 8 defeats, 64 scored, 39 conceded), sealed on the final day, 17 May 2026, with a home win over bottom-placed Preußen Münster. The club comes from a municipality of roughly 13,000 people and plays in the Ursapharm-Arena an der Kaiserlinde, which holds 10,000 and is the smallest ground in the division. Leverkusen finished 6th on 59 points with 17 wins, 8 draws and 9 defeats, 68 scored and 47 conceded for +21, qualified for the Europa League and reached the DFB-Pokal semi-final. The structural headline is not the obvious one. The visiting side is the more disrupted of the two: Leverkusen have sold roughly €72m of named talent this summer, reinvested about €7m in fees, and replaced the manager who took them to a cup semi-final. Elversberg, by contrast, kept the coach who took them up and paid the largest fee of their existence. The gap between these clubs is enormous and it is not the gap that has moved this summer.

  • SV 07 Elversberg — promoted, turnover low-to-medium, and the coach who did it stays. Vincent Wagner, 40, took charge before the 2025–26 season, replacing the long-serving Horst Steffen, and won promotion in his first year; he continues. That is the most important continuity fact on the German portion of this card, because it means the system that scored 64 goals is intact at the top even if the division has changed. The window’s headline is Cole Campbell, a 20-year-old United States youth international, signed permanently from Borussia Dortmund for €6m on a contract to 2030 after a loan spell at Hoffenheim — a substantial outlay by this club’s standards. Against €72m of published outgoing fees, the incoming ledger is Aleksa Damjanović from Crvena Zvezda for €5m and Luca Erlein from Hoffenheim for €2m, plus two players returning from loan — Abdoulaye Faye from Lorient and Farid Alfa-Ruprecht from Bochum — and Onyeka loaned out to today’s opponent. That is a club that has sold its first-choice centre-back and its left-back, banked a large profit, and handed a new coach a squad thinner in defence than the one that conceded 47 last season. B · Style & early-season reality

Elversberg went up by scoring: 64 goals in 34 matches, 1.88 a game, the highest rate of the three promoted clubs and more than the champions Schalke managed, with 39 conceded behind it. That is an attacking promotion rather than a defensive one, and the people who produced it are still here — Wagner in the dugout, Kristof in goal, Petkov and Zimmerschied in the side — with a €6m attacking signing added from Borussia Dortmund. The difficulty is the one every promoted side faces and this club faces in an extreme form: second-division goal output is the least transferable statistic in football, and Elversberg’s resources sit far below every other club in the division. The ground is a genuine variable rather than a colour detail. Ten thousand seats is the smallest pitchside environment in the Bundesliga and a tight, full stadium changes the texture of a match — more second balls, more contested restarts, less space for a possession side to settle. What no evidence exists for is whether Wagner asks his team to keep attacking at this level or to compress, and matchday one supplies none.

Leverkusen’s numbers — 68 scored and 47 conceded, sixth place, a cup semi-final — belong to Kasper Hjulmand and to a back line containing Piero Hincapié and Alejandro Grimaldo. Both are gone, and with Alajbegović sold to Juventus the club has taken €72m out of the squad and put roughly €7m of fees back in. Martínez Novell arrives from Toulouse, a Ligue 1 club operating on a fraction of Leverkusen’s budget, which is a real appointment with a real method but not one with any evidence attached at this level. So the away side’s attacking record is broadly intact and its defensive record is not: the centre-back who anchored it and the left-back who supplied much of the width and set-piece delivery have both left, and the replacements are a €5m signing from Belgrade, a €2m signing from Hoffenheim and two returning loanees. Reading last season’s 47 conceded into a trip to a hostile 10,000-seat ground on the opening day, under a new coach, with a rebuilt defence, is the specific error this fixture invites.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Elversberg’s 64 goals and 39 conceded are 2. Bundesliga figures with no top-flight baseline of any kind behind them — the club has never played a Bundesliga match, so unlike most promoted sides there is not even a historical top-flight record from a previous era to compare against.
  • The venue is a structural variable, not decoration. Ten thousand seats makes this the smallest ground in the division, and the club is drawn from a municipality of about 13,000 people. A tight, full stadium on a debut day affects contact, restarts and the space available to a technical side in ways that neither team’s prior data captures.
  • Leverkusen’s 47 goals conceded were achieved with Piero Hincapié at centre-back and Alejandro Grimaldo at left-back. Hincapié went to Arsenal for €40m and Grimaldo left; the replacements are Damjanović at €5m and Erlein at €2m plus two returning loanees. Carrying last season’s defensive rate forward assumes a back line that no longer exists.
  • Both dugouts changed within the last fourteen months and only one of them changed this summer. Wagner has a full season and a promotion behind him at Elversberg; Martínez Novell arrives from Toulouse with no Bundesliga match to his name and a squad €72m lighter than the one that finished sixth. The evidence for how Leverkusen will play is a CV, not a fixture.
  • Most sensitive: corners and the outright. Corners need territorial patterns from a debutant and from a side that has lost its principal wide creator; the outright needs both a promoted club’s true level and a rebuilt Leverkusen’s, neither of which exists yet. Least sensitive: discipline — a confirmed 3.70-card official, a debut occasion, a tight ground and a resource mismatch are four independent pushes that all survive the sample problem.
