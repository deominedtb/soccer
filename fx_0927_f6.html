A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Norway are the promoted side, in League A for the first time; Portugal stayed. ESPN’s standings and schedules have Norway in League C in 2018–19 and League B in 2020–21, 2022–23 and 2024–25. In 2024–25 they won Group B3 on 13 points, 15–7 (W4 D1 L1: 0–0 in Almaty, 2–1 and 1–5 against Austria, 3–0 and 4–1 against Slovenia, 5–0 against Kazakhstan). They then won all eight qualifiers in Group I, 37–5, including 3–0 against Italy in Oslo and 4–1 in Milan, and 11–1 against Moldova. At the World Cup they beat Iraq 4–1 and Senegal 3–2 and lost 1–4 to France, then beat Ivory Coast 2–1 and Brazil 2–1 before losing to England 1–2 after 1–1 at 90 minutes, their first World Cup quarter-final (Wikipedia). Portugal won Group A1 in 2024–25 on 14 points, 13–5 (W4 D2, unbeaten). They lost 0–1 at Parken in the quarter-final and won 5–2 after extra time in Lisbon, beat Germany 2–1 in Munich and won the final against Spain on penalties after 2–2. They won qualifying Group F, 20–7 (W4 D1 L1; the defeat 0–2 in Dublin). At the World Cup they were second in Group K (1–1 DR Congo, 5–0 Uzbekistan, 0–0 Colombia), beat Croatia 2–1 and lost 0–1 to Spain in the round of 16 (Wikipedia). Format: the top two in each group go to the quarter-finals; the two worst last-placed sides go straight down, and the two best last-placed and two worst third-placed sides go into play-offs (Wikipedia’s League A page, citing UEFA). How the division treats promoted sides at home: in the 24 League A matches of 2022–23 and 2024–25 in which a promoted side hosted one that had stayed, the host went W5 D8 L11, 21–34, was level at half-time in 13 and won more corners in 9 (baselines_0927.md). Portugal away in League A across all four editions: W5 D5 L1, 17–8. The structural headline is that the two priors point opposite ways. The division’s promoted hosts have lost almost half their home matches against stayed-in-A visitors, and Portugal have lost once in 11 League A away matches. Norway have won all eight competitive matches at Ullevaal since September 2024 (36–5), including Italy 3–0 and Denmark on Thursday, and 17 of their 21 competitive matches overall. Head to head: the international-results record has 11 meetings, Portugal W8 D2, Norway W1, 18–5 (Sports Mole gives the same count). In Oslo Norway are W1 D1 L3, 2–4; their one win was 1–0 in a Euro 2012 qualifier on 7 September 2010. The last meeting was a 3–0 friendly in Porto on 29 May 2016. B · Style & early-season reality

Solbakken’s Norway are built around Haaland and settled personnel. In their 20 competitive matches with a usable box score they had 54.7% of the ball, took 15.85 shots to 10.10 and won 5.10 corners to 3.50. They committed 9.00 fouls to 10.25 suffered and were booked 0.81 times per match. At Ullevaal they overran everyone below this level: 18.00 shots to 7.38, 5.88 corners to 2.50, 2.25 first-half goals per match, and every one of the eight over 2.5. Against League A-tier sides the profile changes: 43.0% of the ball, 11.00 shots to 13.00, and 4.00 corners to 5.25 (more in 1 of 4). They still scored in all five and won three, but conceded 1.60 per match, and Thursday’s 3–2 had Denmark level on shots and ahead on corners. Haaland scored 32 of their 64 non-own goals; the next-best scorer, Sørloth, is out. The shape has held: a 4-3-3 in every match since the World Cup began, with Ødegaard, Berge and Berg in midfield. The summer changed little beyond the second striker and the left-back.

Martínez’s Portugal kept the ball and the corner count, and scored less as the level rose. They had 62.8% of the ball, took 18.20 shots to 10.70 and won more corners in 14 of 20. Against League A-tier sides that thinned to 53.4% and 14.29 shots to 13.29, with 1.44 goals scored per match and every one of the nine finishing within one goal. Their first halves against this level were quiet: 1.00 goals per match, level at the break four times in nine. Away from home they were booked 2.00 times per match to their hosts’ 1.62, at 5.25 fouls per card. The summer changed the coach, not the players. The squad made 231 of the window’s 242 starts, and all eleven who started Jesus’s first match are here. What changed on Thursday was the shape (a 4-1-3-2 with two forwards) and the tempo it produced against Wales: 23 shots to 5 and 10 corners to 2, against a promoted side at home. Whether that holds away to a side that has won eight of eight at Ullevaal is a question Jesus’s CV raises and one home match cannot answer.
