A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

SC Paderborn 07 are promoted, and the manner of it matters: they came up through the relegation/promotion play-off, beating Wolfsburg 2–1 on aggregate in May, and return to the Bundesliga for the first time since being relegated at the end of 2019–20. The club's two previous top-flight spells, in 2014–15 and 2019–20, both ended in relegation after a single season, which is the division's own answer to how it usually treats this particular promoted side. They came up alongside SV Elversberg and Schalke 04, replacing St. Pauli, Heidenheim and Wolfsburg, and the previews reached here judge Paderborn to face the hardest survival task of the three. Ralf Kettemann is in his first season as a Bundesliga head coach. Freiburg are not promoted and did not change coach: Julian Schuster continues after a season that produced 7th place on 47 points from 34 matches and, more strikingly, the first continental final in the club's history, lost 3–0 to Aston Villa. The opening weekend split them cleanly. Paderborn drew 0–0 at home with Mainz 05 and are now unbeaten in nine across all competitions, having beaten Phönix Lübeck 4–2 in the DFB-Pokal. Freiburg won 4–1 against Werder Bremen on 30 August and arrive on a four-match winning run.

  • Paderborn — promoted, and the squad lost its 15-goal striker while spending a club record to replace him. Kettemann continues from the promotion campaign, so there is no manager change layered on the division change — the same configuration as Elversberg elsewhere on this card. The departure that matters is Filip Bilbija, 15 goals in the 2. Bundesliga last season and central to the promotion, gone to Derby County on a free transfer; Mika Baur and Calvin Brackelmann also left. Against that, the club record was broken to sign Gabriel Vidović from Dinamo Zagreb for a reported €3m — a 22-year-old with a combined 12 goals and assists in the Croatian top flight — and Rayan Philippe arrived on loan from Hamburg with a purchase option reported at €6m. A €3m club record is the smallest headline fee anywhere on this slate, and it is the whole of Paderborn's permanent investment as far as the search reached. Gap: no goalkeeper or centre-back arrival was established, which is conspicuous given the absences below.
  • Freiburg — a €70m sale reinvested across five signings, and the coach who reached a European final stays. Schuster continues. The window is dominated by one number: Johan Manzambi to Aston Villa for a reported €70m including bonuses, a club-record sale and the largest fee on this entire slate — and, notably, to the same club that beat Freiburg 3–0 in the continental final. The proceeds went into breadth rather than a single replacement: Igor Matanović from Eintracht Frankfurt, Yuito Suzuki from Brøndby IF, Derry Scherhant from Hertha BSC, Philipp Treu from St. Pauli and Anthony Jung from Werder Bremen, with Yannick Engelhardt brought back to the club. Out alongside Manzambi: Chukwubuike Adamu to Celtic, Eren Dinkci to Heidenheim, Noah Weisshaupt to Hannover 96, Merlin Röhl to Everton and Robert Wagner to Dynamo Dresden. The early returns are on the board already — Suzuki scored a hat-trick against Bremen, and Matanović has four goals and three assists in his last four appearances. Gap: only the Manzambi fee is published; nothing is reported for any of the five arrivals, and no goalkeeper movement was established.
  • Suspensions / absences. One-sided, and in the position that decides the fixture. Paderborn are without both first-choice centre-backs: Felix Götze with a concussion and Nyamekye Awortwie-Grant with a knee injury. Kettemann is expected to respond with a back three of Jano ter Horst, Mattes Hansen and Tjark Scheller behind wing-backs Laurin Curda and Raphael Obermair — a reshaped defence in a promoted side's first home match of the season. Freiburg are expected to name an unchanged side after the Bremen win, with Niklas Beste shaking off an illness to start on the right. No suspensions are reported on either side.

B · Style & early-season reality

Paderborn arrive with the least resourced squad in the division and a coherent identity to set against it: the promotion campaign is described as built on intense football, a high work rate and a clear plan, and Kettemann has kept that group together rather than rebuilding it. The 0–0 with Mainz is the first evidence of how that transfers, and a goalless draw is the most ambiguous result available — it says the structure held for 90 minutes and it says the attack did not function, and one match cannot tell you which will persist. What is not ambiguous is the personnel problem behind it. A promoted club that has just lost a 15-goal striker on a free, replaced him with a €3m record signing and a loanee, and now goes into its first home match without both first-choice centre-backs is a side improvising in the two positions that decide matches at this level. The predicted back three of ter Horst, Hansen and Scheller is a reshuffle, not a plan. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Paderborn in either division, so nothing here characterises their pressing height or set-piece volume beyond the descriptive language in the previews.

Freiburg are the more settled side and, unusually, their summer made them richer without making them unrecognisable. Schuster continues, the 7th-place finish and the run to a continental final were his, and the €70m Manzambi sale was reinvested across five players rather than concentrated on one replacement — which is a squad-depth strategy rather than a rebuild, and is consistent with a coach whose system is already established. Two of those signings are producing immediately: Suzuki with a hat-trick against Bremen, Matanović with four goals and three assists in four appearances. The four-match winning run and the 4–1 opener are the most emphatic early-season form on the built cards. The caution is the standard one and it is real: a 4–1 against a Bremen side that is itself one match into a new season is a single result, and the club has just lost the player it valued most, with the replacement goals so far coming from players who arrived weeks ago. Gap: Freiburg's own goals-for and goals-against columns for 2025–26 were not obtainable beyond the 47-point total, and no xG or shot series exists on this page for either club.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Paderborn's nine-match unbeaten run spans two divisions and a play-off. Seven of those matches were in the 2. Bundesliga or the promotion play-off and one was a cup tie against a lower-league side; exactly one was in the Bundesliga. It is the longest run on this card and the least transferable.
  • Both of Paderborn's first-choice centre-backs are out — Götze concussed, Awortwie-Grant with a knee injury — and no centre-back arrival was established in the summer window. A promoted club improvising a back three at home against a European finalist is the single most concrete structural fact on this card.
  • Freiburg's summer was defined by a €70m sale rather than a purchase. Manzambi's departure to Aston Villa is the largest fee on the slate, and the goals since have come from Suzuki and Matanović, both signed in the same window. Four matches of evidence that the reinvestment works is four matches.
