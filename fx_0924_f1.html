A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted and neither is new to this division: both have played League D in every edition since 2020–21. The two met twice in the 2024–25 League D campaign and produced one goal between them — Andorra 0–1 Malta in Andorra la Vella on 10 September 2024 and Malta 0–0 Andorra at Ta’ Qali on 19 November. Malta finished second in Group D2 on 7 points, 2 scored and 2 conceded, then lost the C/D promotion play-off 0–5 on aggregate to Luxembourg in March 2026 (0–2 at home, 0–3 away). Andorra finished third and last on 1 point, with no goal scored in four matches and 4 conceded. In World Cup qualifying through 2025, Andorra finished 5th of 5 in Group K on 1 point, 3 scored and 16 conceded; Malta 4th of 5 in Group G on 5 points, 4 scored and 19 conceded, their one win 1–0 in Finland. The structural headline is that the only like-for-like record either side has — the 2024–25 League D campaign the baseline line comes from — was played by a Malta side two coaches ago in a back three, and by an Andorra built around players who are not in today’s matchday squad. B · Style & early-season reality

Andorra are the most deep-lying side on this slate by every counted measure. Across their four 2024–25 League D matches they averaged 42.2% possession, 7.75 shots and 2.50 on target, and committed 20.75 fouls per match against 14.50 by their opponents — the highest foul rate in the division that season, with 28 fouls in the 0–0 at Malta alone. Against World Cup qualifying opposition in 2025–26 the same side had 27.3% of the ball, took 3.50 shots and won 0.50 corners per match while conceding 6.50. Across all 12 competitive matches since September 2024 Andorra averaged 5.08 shots and 1.33 on target per match and scored three goals. All of that was produced in a 5-4-1. Today’s 4-4-2 has one competitive precedent and a front two with seven previous starts between them; the evidence that the shape changes what Andorra produce is one match against Albania and a friendly win over Liechtenstein that ESPN has no box score for.

Malta under De Leo are a different team from the one in the League D sample, and the numbers show it in both directions. In 2024–25 League D, in a back three, they had 54.2% possession, took 8.50 shots and conceded 10.75, and drew 21.00 fouls per match against 11.75 committed. In 2025–26, in De Leo’s 4-2-3-1 and against stronger opposition, they had 40.4% of the ball, took 9.50 shots and conceded 15.00. Their two League D goals in 2024–25 were a first-half goal in Andorra la Vella and an 87th-minute Teuma penalty against Moldova. What the summer changed is almost nothing: the eleven is the March eleven, the coach is the coach confirmed in July, and the one new element since the League D sample — Cardona up front — has been in place since September 2025. What has never been tested is De Leo’s Malta in a competitive match against a League D side: all ten of his competitive matches were World Cup qualifiers or the League C play-off.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Every number on this card predates the window. Neither side has played a competitive match in 2026–27; Andorra’s last was ten months ago and Malta’s six. The eight friendlies since those matches, six Andorra’s and two Malta’s, have no usable box score in ESPN’s record and contribute results only.
  • Malta’s League D record belongs to a different coach and system. The 2024–25 campaign — the only one read against the League D baseline — was played under Marcolini and then Mazzotta in a back three. De Leo has ten competitive matches, the last eight in a 4-2-3-1, and none of them against a League D side.
  • Andorra’s record belongs to a different shape and partly to different players. Eleven of 12 competitive matches since September 2024 were in a 5-4-1; today is a 4-4-2. Joan Cervos (14 starts), Marc Vales, Marc Pujol, João Teixeira and Marc García Renom are outside the 23, for reasons not published.
  • The divisional baseline moved more between editions than either team’s rates. League D went from 2.44 goals and 3.94 cards per match in 2022–23 to 1.58 and 7.42 in 2024–25, on 18 and 12 matches, and 2026–27 has swapped two of its six sides.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and Discipline — the first two need result evidence produced by the coach and shapes on the pitch today, the third needs a referee and a division that agree. Least sensitive: BTTS and Totals — both rest on Andorra’s scoring record, the one series that holds across League D, World Cup qualifying and friendlies.

E · Short market-type guide

  • BTTS is more robust than 1X2 here because the goal evidence survives the coaching change and the result evidence does not. Andorra’s failure to score — 100% in League D, ten of 12 competitive since September 2024 — is a property of their attack across three windows; Malta’s 2–1–1 League D record belongs to Marcolini and Mazzotta.
  • Totals is more robust than Corners because the goal windows agree and the corner windows do not. League D’s 16.7% over 2.5, both sides’ 0% in the campaign and one goal in two meetings all point the same way; Andorra’s corners swing from 3.25 to 0.50 per match between windows.
  • Team goals on the Andorra side is more robust than on the Malta side because only one attack has a record at this level in its current form. Andorra’s 5.08 shots and 1.33 on target per competitive match cover 12 games; Malta’s current front line, Cardona included, has never played a competitive League D match.
  • Discipline is less robust than its data volume suggests. Kooij has 23 Eredivisie matches of record and Andorra a 20.75 fouls-per-match League D rate, but the official books below his league while the division booked 7.42 per match, and the division’s own line was 3.94 an edition earlier.
  • The single fact that would most change which family reads best is whether Andorra’s 4-4-2 produces more than their 5-4-1 did. BTTS, Totals and Team goals all rest on 1.33 shots on target per match produced in the old shape; the new one has one competitive precedent (three shots against Albania). Nothing published before kickoff can settle it — it needs a competitive sample in the new shape, and the next is in Gibraltar on 27 September.
