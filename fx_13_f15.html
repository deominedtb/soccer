A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Hull City are promoted, and the manner of it is the frame for the whole fixture: they came up through the 2026 EFL play-offs on a late Oli McBurnie goal at Wembley, returning to the Premier League for the first time since 2016–17 after a decade away, alongside Coventry City and Ipswich Town — both of whom also appear on this slate, at f10 and in the f7 and f9 research. How this division normally treats a promoted side is the standing question, and Hull have answered the first fortnight of it emphatically: six points from two matches, three goals scored, none conceded, beating Manchester United 2–0 on the opening day and winning 1–0 at Coventry, with Konstantinos Tzolakis keeping both clean sheets. Aston Villa are the reigning Europa League winners — they won the competition in 2026, beating Freiburg 3–0 in the final, and Freiburg appear on this slate at f5 — and have started with two defeats, no goals and five conceded: 4–0 at Brighton on 23 August, recorded from the other end on this slate's f8 card, and 1–0 at home to Arsenal. Neither club changed manager: Unai Emery continues at Villa, and Sergej Jakirović has been at Hull since June 2025, when he replaced Rubén Sellés.

  • Aston Villa — the most extensively rebuilt squad on this slate, in both directions. Emery continues, so the system is not new; the players executing it largely are. Out, at scale: Morgan Rogers to Chelsea for a reported £120m, Ezri Konsa to Arsenal for £51m, Ollie Watkins to Al Hilal for £50m, and the goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez to Chelsea for £7.5m — a centre-back, a centre-forward, the creative midfielder and the goalkeeper, which is all four positions the research spec weights. In: Johan Manzambi from Freiburg in a deal reported at up to £59.5m, the club record, after he starred for Switzerland at the 2026 World Cup; Nicolas Jackson from Chelsea for £48m to replace Watkins; Joao Gomes from Wolves for around £38m; the goalkeeper Zion Suzuki from Parma for £30m to replace Martínez; Matteo Ruggeri permanently; Leon Goretzka on a free; and Alejandro Garnacho and Aaron Wan-Bissaka on loan. Conflict logged: the Manzambi fee is reported here as up to £59.5m and on the Freiburg side, researched for f5, as around €70m including bonuses — the two are not obviously reconcilable at any plausible exchange rate, and neither is corroborated, so both are recorded and neither is adopted.
  • Hull City — promoted, and spending in units the rest of this card would round off. Jakirović continues from the promotion season, so there is no manager change layered on the division change — the same configuration as Elversberg and Paderborn in the Bundesliga fixtures on this board. The signings established are Lucas Gourna-Douath for a reported £3m and Joe Gelhardt returning for £6.5m. Against Villa's roughly £235m of published incoming fees, Hull's established outlay is under £10m. The club is reported to be moving quickly in the market with the explicit aim of avoiding Derby County's record-low Premier League points tally. Gap: no departure ledger and no goalkeeper or centre-back fee was established for Hull, and Tzolakis — the goalkeeper keeping the clean sheets — does not appear in any transfer record reached here.

B · Style & early-season reality

Hull are the most interesting promoted side on this slate because the usual caveats are inverted. The standard warning is that a promoted club's early results flatter it before the division catches up; here the results are not flattering, they are structural in the one dimension that survives promotion — the defence. Two clean sheets in two matches, one of them against Manchester United, with a goalkeeper who is not the club's established first choice because Butland is injured. The squad behind that is thin and getting thinner: six players out and two doubtful, one of the doubts being Gelhardt, half of the recorded transfer business. Jakirović has been in place since June 2025, so the shape is his and the promotion was his, which removes the CV problem that afflicts most promoted-side analysis on this board. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate or card rate was obtainable for Hull in either division, so the clean sheets are a result, not a process that can be described here.

Villa are the opposite: a squad transformed around a manager who stayed. Emery's 2026 was arguably his most successful season at the club, ending in a Europa League title, and the response was to sell the four players the team was built through — Rogers for £120m, Konsa for £51m, Watkins for £50m, Martínez for £7.5m — and reinvest roughly £235m in Manzambi, Jackson, Gomes, Suzuki and Ruggeri, plus a free-transfer Goretzka and two loans. Almost every position of consequence has a new occupant executing a familiar system. Two matches in, that squad has not scored and has conceded five. Both defeats came against strong opposition, so the results are not a diagnosis; the goal drought is the more notable half, given that the man who scored the goals and the man who created them both left in the same window. The one thing this card can say with confidence is that Villa's 2025–26 numbers describe a different team. Gap: no xG, shot volume, foul rate, card rate or final league position for Villa was obtainable.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Hull's perfect start is two matches long and was produced by a squad now missing six players with two more doubtful, including a first-choice goalkeeper. The clean sheets are real; the availability behind them is deteriorating, and no underlying series exists on this card to say how much of the record was control and how much was variance.
  • Aston Villa sold their centre-forward, centre-back, creative midfielder and goalkeeper in one window — Watkins, Konsa, Rogers and Martínez — and reinvested roughly £235m. Their Europa League title and last season's numbers describe a team four key players lighter. Two goalless matches is the only evidence of the new one.
  • The Manzambi fee could not be settled: up to £59.5m from the buying side, around €70m including bonuses from the selling side researched for f5. Both are reported and neither is adopted. It matters only as a measure of the scale of Villa's rebuild, but it is the largest unreconciled number on the board.
  • Hull's promoted status is the structural fact the price is built on, and the division's own history is mixed on it: the club is explicitly reported to be recruiting against the risk of a record-low points total, while simultaneously sitting on six points from six after two matchweeks.
  • Most sensitive: both teams to score, which requires an attack that has not scored and a defence that has not conceded to both break in the same ninety minutes. Least sensitive: the discipline row — a confirmed, heavily-sampled official with the division's highest foul count, a derivable fouls-per-card ratio and the lowest penalty rate on this slate.
