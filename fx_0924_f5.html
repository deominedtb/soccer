A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Portugal are the Nations League holders and have played in League A in every edition since 2020–21. In 2024–25 they won Group A1 on 14 points, 13 scored and 5 conceded (W4 D2 L0). They then beat Denmark 5–3 on aggregate after extra time in the quarter-final and won the Finals in Munich: 2–1 against Germany, then 2–2 against Spain and 5–3 on penalties. They won World Cup qualifying Group F on 13 points, 20–7; the one defeat was 0–2 in Dublin. At the World Cup they were second in Group K on 5 points (1–1 v Congo DR, 5–0 v Uzbekistan, 0–0 v Colombia). They beat Croatia 2–1 in the round of 32 and lost 0–1 to Spain in the round of 16. Wales are promoted and back in League A after one edition away. In 2024–25 they won League B Group B4 unbeaten on 12 points, 9–4 (W3 D3), with Türkiye second on 11. Their only previous League A edition was 2022–23, when they took one point from six matches (D1 L5, 6–11) and were relegated. In qualifying they were second in Group J on 16 points, 21–11 (Belgium 18). They lost the play-off semi-final to Bosnia-Herzegovina on penalties. The structural headline is a contrast. Portugal have a new coach and a new shape but, man for man, a World Cup eleven. Wales’s eleven is missing its goalkeeper, both first-choice centre-backs and its top scorer. The eight sides promoted into League A for 2022–23 and 2024–25 took 0.79 points per match. Away from home they took 0.62 points per match over 24 matches, scoring 0.96 and conceding 2.04 per match (over 2.5 in 62.5%, BTTS in 50.0%, failed to score in 37.5%). Four of them opened away on matchday one and lost all four: Wales 1–2 in Poland in 2022, Austria 0–3 in Croatia, Israel 1–3 in Belgium and Bosnia-Herzegovina 2–5 in the Netherlands. None of the eight arrived without its goalkeeper, both first-choice centre-backs and top scorer, so this history is a thin guide to tonight. The FAW counts four meetings since 1949, with Wales’s only win a 2–1 in 1951. The most recent was the Euro 2016 semi-final, won 2–0 by Portugal, which Ben Davies missed through suspension. Goal.com also lists a 3–0 Portugal win in a June 2000 friendly. The RESEARCH SPEC’s transfer-fee field has no national-team equivalent. B · Style & early-season reality

Under Martínez, Portugal were a possession side with one centre-forward and a low foul count. In the 2024–25 League A group they had 62.3% of the ball, took 17.33 shots against 10.17, and won 7.00 corners against 5.00. In qualifying they had 71.3% of the ball, with 27.5 crosses and 25.00 shots per match. Against the 2026–27 League A sides they met, the ball was shared (51.2%), shots ran 12.83 to 14.67 (six matches with a box score), and corners 5.75 to 5.25. At home in Portugal the corner split widened to 7.29 won and 3.71 conceded. Their own fouls fell from 11.83 per match in the League A group to 7.82 in 25/26, and their cards from 2.33 to 1.45. Opponents fouled them 9.67–10.36 times per match. They won seven penalty kicks in 21 competitive matches, five of them at home. Jesus’s changes are to the structure, not the players. He puts two central forwards (Félix beside Ronaldo) in front of a single holding midfielder (Rúben Neves), with Bruno Fernandes and Neto listed as the right and left midfielders rather than a No. 10 and a winger. The full-backs, the centre-back pair and the goalkeeper are the World Cup’s. The only evidence for how this Portugal play is Jesus’s club CV and his press statements. No competitive match with this squad in this shape exists.

Bellamy’s Wales are a back-four possession side. They mostly played a 4-2-3-1, moving to a 4-1-4-1 in their last three competitive matches. In League B they had 56.4% of the ball but were outshot 11.00 to 14.67 and out-cornered 4.67 to 5.33. Away from home they had 59.0% of the ball, took 11.29 shots against 15.71, and won 4.43 corners against 6.43. In qualifying and the play-off they had 68.0% of the ball and 30.4 crosses per match. Against the 2026–27 League A sides (Türkiye, Belgium) they were outshot 10.00 to 16.00 and out-cornered 4.50 to 6.00, and they conceded four penalty kicks in four matches. Opponents fouled them 16.00 times per match in League B, against League A’s 11.39 per team. That fell to 11.00 in 25/26, and Wales’s own fouls and cards fell from 11.83 and 2.67 to 7.80 and 1.40. The summer change is to the unit, not the system. The goalkeeper has three competitive starts under Bellamy. Only once has the likely centre-back pair started as a pair, in a friendly. The front three has no centre-forward, and between them the three have 6 of the side’s 31 goals. The side’s leading scorer and creator (Wilson) is out. None of this back line has played a competitive match together; the evidence for it is club form.
