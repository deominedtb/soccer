A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side came up this summer, but one of them came up the summer before, and that matters here. Köln were promoted into 2025–26 alongside Hamburger SV, and survived at the first attempt in 14th with 32 points from 34 matches — 7 wins, 11 draws, 16 defeats, 49 scored, 63 conceded. This is therefore their second season back, the one in which a promoted side's first-year allowances stop applying and the squad is judged on its own terms. Stuttgart finished 4th on 62 points with 18 wins, 8 draws, 8 defeats, 71 scored and 49 conceded — the fourth-best attack in the division. The structural headline is that the manager change on this card belongs to the visitors and the squad change belongs to the hosts: René Wagner has been Köln's head coach since March 2026, promoted from within and then confirmed permanently on a contract to 2028, so this is his first full season; Sebastian Hoeneß continues at Stuttgart, where the summer removed the first-choice goalkeeper and spent a reported €56m on three attacking or midfield signings. The head-to-head favours the hosts — Köln have won once in the last seven meetings and lost five, including 3–1 here last season — and so does the venue record: Stuttgart lost only three domestic matches at this ground in 2025–26, winning 13 with eight clean sheets, while Köln have won once in their last 14 away matches in all competitions.

  • Stuttgart — turnover high and concentrated at the top of the pitch, plus a new goalkeeper. Hoeneß continues, so the system is not being rebuilt from a CV; the personnel around it partly is. Goalkeeper: Alexander Nübel returned to Bayern Munich, and two keepers arrived to replace him — Dennis Seimen back from a loan at Paderborn and Marius Funk on a free from Energie Cottbus. A club that finished fourth has changed its number one. Centre-forward: Dženan Pejčinović from Wolfsburg for a reported €25m, the largest fee either club paid this summer. Midfield and wide: Bilal El Khannouss from Leicester City, permanent, reported around €18m; Leo Sauer from Feyenoord, reported around €13m; Grischa Prömel from Hoffenheim on a free. Defence: Leonidas Stergiou back from a loan at Heidenheim; no outfield defensive signing for a fee was established. Out: Nübel to Bayern, Chema Andrés to Brighton, Lazar Jovanović to Udinese, Noah Darvich on loan to Elversberg. Gap: no fee is published for Nübel, Andrés or Jovanović, and the Pejčinović, El Khannouss and Sauer figures are all reported as approximations rather than confirmed by either club.
  • Köln — a coach appointed mid-season now in his first full one, and a window that sold the best asset and rented the replacement. Wagner took over in March 2026 as an internal promotion from assistant, was confirmed as permanent head coach and signed to 2028. The window: 10 arrivals against 8 departures, with the sale of Jakub Kamiński to Benfica for a reported €17m the largest item on either side of the ledger. Also out: Ellyes Skhiri to Eintracht Frankfurt and Gideon Mensah on a free — to Auxerre, the away side in the first fixture on this card. In, and this is the part that has already mattered: Thijs Dallinga on loan from Bologna for the season with a purchase option reported at about €10m, plus three more loans — Jahmai Simpson-Pusey from Manchester City, Julian Pauli from Dynamo Dresden and Elias Bakatukanda from Blau-Weiß Linz. That is a front line and a defensive rotation assembled largely from borrowed players. Conflict logged: two German outlets give incompatible window balances for Köln — one reports €25.65m spent against €18m received, a net −€7.65m; the other reports €9.4m spent against €18m received, a net positive. Both agree on the €17m Kamiński sale. Neither balance is carried as established.

Köln are without Timo Hübers (knee) and Sebastian Sebulonsen (hamstring), with Gian-Luca Waldschmidt expected back at some point this month; Hübers is the more significant of the two for a side whose defensive problem last season was 63 goals conceded.
  • Sample. One match each; this is matchday 2. Stuttgart lost 5–1 away at Bayern Munich — the champions, who scored 122 goals last season, so the venue and opponent carry most of that scoreline. Köln beat Hoffenheim 3–2 at home, with Dallinga scoring twice on his debut. That leaves Stuttgart 18th on goal difference and Köln 6th after one round, a table position that means nothing yet and is quoted only because it is the entire 2026–27 record of both clubs. Two matches of evidence, one of them against the strongest side in the division.

B · Style & early-season reality

Stuttgart's 2025–26 was an attacking season with a soft edge: 71 goals scored, fourth-most in the division, against 49 conceded, which is more than any of the three sides above them. At home that profile was much harder — three defeats in nineteen domestic matches at this ground, thirteen wins, eight clean sheets — and the summer has now removed the goalkeeper who kept them. Nübel's return to Bayern is the kind of change that does not show in a transfer balance sheet: the replacements are a keeper returning from a loan in the second division and a free signing from Cottbus, and neither has an established Bundesliga first-choice record. In front of them the spending went the other way entirely, roughly €56m across Pejčinović, El Khannouss and Sauer, all attacking or creative additions, with the only defensive movement a loan return. The opening 5–1 at Bayern is a single, heavily conditioned data point — away at a side that scored 122 last season — but it is consistent with a squad that was rebuilt in front of the ball rather than behind it. Gap: no xG, shot-volume, home/away goal split, foul rate or card rate for Stuttgart's 2025–26 was obtainable from any source reached, so nothing here rests on a pressing or possession metric.

Köln are the more interesting case structurally, because their change of direction is a year old rather than three months old. Wagner was appointed in March 2026 with the club in the lower half, kept them up, and has been given the job permanently to 2028 — so unlike almost every other manager on this card, he has both a body of work with these players and a full pre-season with them. What he does not have is the squad that produced it: Kamiński was sold to Benfica for a reported €17m, Skhiri left for Frankfurt, Mensah left on a free, and the replacements are overwhelmingly loans, including the centre-forward. Dallinga arrived from Bologna — where he was one of a long list of departures in a summer that also cost that club its first-choice striker — and scored twice on debut in a 3–2 win over Hoffenheim, a side that finished fifth last season. One match is one match, but it is a win over a top-six team achieved by the specific player Köln borrowed to solve the specific problem of scoring goals. Against that sits the away record: one win in the last 14 on the road in all competitions, and 63 goals conceded across last season. The same gap applies as for the hosts — no underlying series for Köln was obtainable, so this card describes results and personnel and does not pretend to a style measurement it cannot source.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Stuttgart's opening 5–1 was away at Bayern Munich, who scored 122 goals last season. Reading it as evidence about this defence rather than about that opponent is the single easiest mistake available on this fixture — but the goalkeeper who kept eight home clean sheets last season has genuinely gone back to Bayern, and the replacements are a loan return from the second division and a free from Cottbus.
  • Köln's 3–2 win came from a striker playing his first match for the club. Dallinga is on loan from Bologna with a reported €10m option, one of four loanees brought in; a front line assembled on borrowed terms in August has no established baseline in this division, and a debut double is the smallest possible sample of one.
  • This is Köln's second season back, not their first. The allowance normally made for a promoted side — unfamiliar opposition, a squad built for a lower division — expired in May, and what is left is a 14th-place finish with 63 conceded, a mid-season coach now in his first full campaign, and a summer that sold the €17m asset and rented the replacement.
  • Every quantitative claim on this page is a goal count, a card count or a transfer fee. No xG, shot volume, corner series, foul rate or home/away split was obtainable for either club from any source reached, which is why the corner line above is marked unevidenced rather than argued either way.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total at 1.33, where a very short price rests on eight goals from two matches, one of them at the champions, and on season-long records belonging to a Stuttgart side with a different goalkeeper. Least sensitive: the corner race and the handicap, which need only the venue and away records — three home defeats in nineteen, one away win in fourteen — to hold rather than any projection about the new personnel.
