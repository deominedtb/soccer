B · Style & early-season reality

Como under Fàbregas are a possession side: build-up from goalkeeper and centre-backs through Perrone as the link, full-backs stepping inside against a high press, immediate counter-pressing after central turnovers, and a 4–4–2 or 5–3–2 block without the ball; Valle is a principal outlet from full-back and Nico Paz a direct free-kick threat. That profile produced the division’s best defensive record last season and it has survived the summer at coach level. What changed is the personnel at the two ends of the spine — Chalobah in for Posch, Sánchez added in goal, Kean in for Morata — and the early evidence is that the attack has not been diluted by it: seven league goals in three and four against Leipzig. The early evidence on defence runs the other way from last season’s record: Como have conceded in every league match and the BTTS rate is three from three, with one conceded each time. Gap: no possession, shot, foul or corner series for Como in either season.

Parma under Cuesta arrived last summer with a 4–3–3, possession-and-high-press identity and moved to a back three before the 2025–26 opener; this season’s probable elevens are a 3–5–2 with wing-backs, and Cuesta’s own framing of the fixture is that Como are beaten “by scoring one more goal”. The problem that framing addresses is documented: 28 goals last season and one this season, and the man who led the line has been sold. His replacements — Touré, Romero, Diallo, with Lontani the only scorer so far — are evidence by CV rather than by match. Behind them the goalkeeper and a first-choice centre-back were also sold, and Diego Carlos, Circati’s replacement, arrives from a season in which he was on the other side of tonight’s fixture.
