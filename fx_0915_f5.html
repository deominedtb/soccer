A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

West Ham were relegated from the Premier League in 2025–26 — 18th, 10 wins, 9 draws, 19 defeats, 46 scored, 65 conceded, 39 points — under Nuno Espírito Santo, appointed on 27 September 2025 when Graham Potter left with the club 19th. They are now top of the Championship after seven matches, a division newly theirs. Fulham finished 11th in the Premier League — 15 wins, 7 draws, 16 defeats, 47 scored, 51 conceded, 52 points. The structural headline is two clubs whose squads and dugouts were remade over the summer in opposite financial directions: West Ham sold three first-team starters for a combined €205m and a fourth for €10m, while Fulham, under a coach appointed on 7 July, spent over €100m and have not won a league match.

B · Style & early-season reality

West Ham under Nuno in the Premier League were a low-possession side — 42.5%, 10.7 shots for and 14.9 against per match on Understat, most minutes in a 4–2–3–1 or 4–4–1–1 — with a specific set-piece weakness: 15 league goals conceded from corner situations. In the Championship the same coach has a different profile: 55.9% possession, 2.30 xG and 5.9 shots on target per match, only 8.9 fouls per match, and 6.00 corners, against a division averaging 5.20 per team. The squad producing it has lost the Premier League side’s most valuable midfielder and winger and its left-back; Engels, Austbø and the loan players are the replacements, and evidence for them against Premier League opposition is a CV.

Fulham under Silva were a 4–2–3–1 side in almost every minute of 2025–26 (3,330 Understat minutes) with 51.7% possession and 4.92 corners per match. Arbeloa has kept the shape and pushed the ball share to 57.3% and corners to 6.25 per match, with 15.0 shots per match on Understat — but the four league matches have produced 1.88 xG against per match and seven goals conceded against Chelsea, Sunderland, Crystal Palace and Liverpool. The summer’s biggest additions, Gonzalo García and Shea Charles, are not in tonight’s eleven.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • West Ham’s current numbers are Championship numbers. 2.30 xG and 20 goals in seven were made against second-tier defences by a squad that sold €215m of players.
  • Fulham’s four matches are all Premier League and three are against top-half sides from last season. Arbeloa has had those four and one cup tie.
  • Selection. Fulham’s published eleven leaves out Gonzalo García and Shea Charles; West Ham’s starts Engels but also Herrick in goal; how far either is rotated is not stated anywhere reached.
  • Set pieces. West Ham conceded 15 league goals from corner situations last season; that weakness has no current measurement in the Championship.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, the handicap and team goals, all of which need the two divisions’ rates to be comparable. Least sensitive: the corners-for rates, equal last season and rising together this season.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners are more robust than 1X2 here because both clubs took 4.92 corners per match last season and both rose this season (6.00, 6.25), whereas 1X2 needs a Championship record and a Premier League record to be put on one scale.
  • BTTS is more robust than team goals because both clubs sat at 53% last season in the same division and near the 54% baseline now, while each team total is built on a division or a set of players not on the pitch tonight.
  • Discipline is more robust than halves because Ward is confirmed with two published records below his division’s booking average, whereas West Ham have no half-time series this season.
  • Totals are more robust than the handicap because every over-2.5 rate on the card is at or above the 51% baseline, while the handicap’s xG inputs come from two divisions.
