A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Paderborn are promoted, and this is the fourth fixture on the slate involving a promoted club. They came up third in the 2025–26 2. Bundesliga on 62 points from 34 matches — 18 wins, 8 draws, 8 defeats, 59 scored, 45 conceded — level on points with Elversberg and behind champions Schalke on 70, all three of whom are now in this division and two of whom meet each other earlier today at f13. Hoffenheim finished 5th in the Bundesliga on 61 points — 18 wins, 7 draws, 9 defeats, 65 scored, 52 conceded — the third-highest goals-for total of any club on this board, with a 70.6% over-2.5 rate. The structural headline is the relationship between the two clubs rather than anything inside either of them. Paderborn signed eighteen players this summer, more than any other Bundesliga club, for a total of about €8.7m — and three of those arrivals are on loan from Hoffenheim, today’s opponent. Hoffenheim, meanwhile, moved twenty players in and twenty-two out for roughly €65m, kept their head coach, and sold Bazoumana Touré to Newcastle for €47m. One club used the other as a loan pipeline, and they meet on matchday four.

Paderborn’s numbers are the most extreme attacking collapse on this board and they are not a matter of pressing or possession. They hold 50.9% of the ball — down only marginally from 53.2% in the 2. Bundesliga — and press at 12.31 PPDA, which is unremarkable. They take 11.33 shots a match, which is low but not exceptional. The collapse is entirely in what those shots are worth: 1.00 shot on target per match, the lowest figure in this entire dataset by a wide margin, and 0.58 xG, also the lowest here. Four shots on target in three matches, and no goals. Their 5.33 deep completions is middling; their corner concession of 7.33 against 3.33 won has inverted from a near-neutral 5.09 and 4.56 in the second tier. The one thing that has held is the defence, in the counted sense: 1.33 goals conceded against a divisional 3.97 goals per match, including a goalless draw at Mainz. The model disagrees sharply at 1.96 xG conceded, and there is no 2025–26 modelled figure to test any of it against.

Hoffenheim are the opposite in every column and have kept the coach who made them that way. PPDA 8.78 against 8.25 last season — among the most aggressive presses on this board, and essentially unchanged — possession 53.8% against 55.2%, shots up from 14.12 to 17.67, deep completions from 8.68 to 11.67, and xG from 1.86 to 2.35. The defensive model has gone the other way: 2.34 xG conceded against 1.59, so their matches have become open at both ends — and the counted record follows it exactly: all three league matches finished 2–3, 2–3 and 2–1, with both teams scoring in every one and every one clearing 2.5. They have neither kept a clean sheet nor failed to score. Their fouls have risen from 13.94 to 14.00, the third-highest on this board, which their 8.78 PPDA explains, and they score 1.00 first-half goal a match while conceding none. Two clubs at the extremes of the same columns: one that cannot put a shot on target and one whose every match has been a 2–3.
