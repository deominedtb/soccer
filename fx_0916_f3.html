A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted. Leverkusen finished 6th in 2025–26 Bundesliga — 59 points, 68 scored, 47 conceded — and went out of the Champions League to Arsenal in the last 16 and of the DFB-Pokal to Bayern in the semi-final. Celje won the 2025–26 Slovenian PrvaLiga — 23 wins, 5 draws, 6 defeats, 85 scored, 32 conceded — then went through three Champions League qualifying ties and lost the last of them to Slovan Bratislava, which placed them in this league phase for the first time. Division note: the Slovenian league publishes results and goals but no xG, shot, card, foul or corner series through any source reached, so every Celje rate beyond goals on this card comes from six UEFA qualifiers, and some from only two of them. The structural headline is that both dugouts have changed since last season, and Celje’s changed on Monday.

B · Style & early-season reality

Leverkusen last season were a possession back-three side: Understat logged 2,866 league minutes in a 3–4–2–1, with 59.2% of the ball, 15.2 shots for and 12.9 against, only 8.6 fouls per match (the Bundesliga mean was 10.4 per team) and 5.59 corners per match. Their defensive record was loose for a top-six side — a 24% clean-sheet rate and 1.36 xG against. Martínez has moved them to a back four: 245 of the 284 logged minutes this season are a 4–2–3–1, possession is up to 64.7%, shots to 24.0 per match and corners to 9.33. What the summer changed is almost the whole back line — Doué, Medina and Gutiérrez in for Hincapié and Grimaldo — and the evidence that it holds is three league matches in which it conceded five, three of them at Elversberg, in their first Bundesliga season.

Celje’s title season was built on scoring: 2.50 goals per match, 71% of their league matches over 2.5 in a league at 65.4%, and a 43–12 home record. The published picture stops there. In the six qualifiers they averaged 58% possession, 11.0 shots for and 10.0 against and 5.17 corners, and conceded ten goals; the two matches with full data show 14.0 fouls and 4.00 cards per match. The domestic results since late August describe a side in decline under Campelos, and what Zeljković will change after one session is unknown — the evidence for his approach is his Koper side, which beat Celje 1–0 on 6 September, not anything he has done at Celje.

What stops it being well-suited is Leverkusen’s own evidence — three league matches under a new coach, a new back four and a defeat at a promoted side.

A new Celje coach with one session and a Leverkusen side missing four unregistered squad players make any margin reading a projection the evidence does not support.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Celje’s coach changed two days before kickoff. Every Celje figure on the card, including last season’s title numbers, was produced under Campelos; Zeljković has had one session.
  • Leverkusen’s back line is new and its sample is three league matches. Doué arrived on 1 September, Medina on 11 August, Gutiérrez on 2 August; last season’s 1.36 xG against belonged to a back three with Hincapié and Grimaldo.
  • The Slovenian league publishes almost nothing beyond goals. Celje’s xG, cards, fouls and corners come from UEFA qualifiers, some from two matches, against Albanian, Armenian and Slovak opponents.
  • Leverkusen’s registered squad is smaller than its league squad. Boniface, Hofmann, Oermann and Eichhorn are not registered; Maza is doubtful and Tella and Culbreath injured.
  • Most sensitive: discipline, halves and the handicap — each depends on Celje series that do not exist or on a margin with no common reference. Least sensitive: Leverkusen’s attacking volume and corners, which have risen under the new coach from an already high base.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals are more robust than the handicap here because every over-2.5 series on both sides sits above the 52.1% baseline, whereas the margin question has no opponent common to both clubs’ samples.
  • A Leverkusen team-goals reading is more robust than a Celje one because Leverkusen’s attack is measured in xG and shots across two seasons, while Celje’s is published only as goals and has lost its suspended striker.
  • Corners are more robust than discipline because Leverkusen’s corners series is full and Celje’s covers six qualifiers in both directions, whereas Celje’s card and foul rates cover two.
  • BTTS is more robust than halves because Celje’s scoring record (13 of 14 matches) is complete, while no Celje half-time series exists outside Europe.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: Celje’s eleven and shape under Zeljković. Whether he keeps Campelos’s back four and who replaces Kotnik decides whether any Celje series on this card still describes the team; the team sheet settles it before kickoff.
