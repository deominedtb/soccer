A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither side is promoted and neither is new to the division: both have played League A in every edition since 2020–21, and they shared Group A3 in 2024–25. Germany won that group on 14 points, 18 scored and 4 conceded; the Netherlands were second on 9 points, 13 scored and 7 conceded. The two meetings were Netherlands 2–2 Germany in Amsterdam on 10 September 2024 and Germany 1–0 Netherlands in Munich on 14 October 2024. Germany then beat Italy in the quarter-final (2–1, 3–3) and lost the semi-final 1–2 to Portugal and the third-place match 0–2 to France; the Netherlands went out to Spain in the quarter-final on penalties after 2–2 and 3–3. In 2025–26 the Netherlands won World Cup qualifying Group G unbeaten (20 points, 27–4) and Germany Group A (15 points, 16–3, one defeat, 0–2 in Slovakia). At the World Cup both won their group — the Netherlands Group F on 7 points, Germany Group E on 6 — and both went out in the round of 32 on the same weekend: Netherlands 1–1 Morocco (2–3 on penalties) and Germany 1–1 Paraguay (3–4). The structural headline is that both coaches left within five days of those exits, and neither successor has taken a match. B · Style & early-season reality

Koeman’s Netherlands were a possession side that fouled little and was rarely booked. In 2024–25 League A they had 62.8% of the ball, took 15.33 shots, won 5.83 corners and committed 9.33 fouls per match against 14.67 by their opponents; in 2025–26 the figures were 65.5% (regulation matches), 15.00 shots, 5.80 corners, 9.44 fouls, and six cards in ten competitive matches. The exception was the tie that ended the regime: a 3-4-2-1 against Morocco with 29.9% of the ball and six shots in 120 minutes. In the one meeting in Amsterdam they had 40.2% possession and 11 shots to Germany’s 21. Xavi’s stated intent — possession, initiative, no back five — describes the Koeman norm rather than the Morocco exception, so the direction of change is small on paper; but the midfield that carried the possession figures is the one most depleted, with De Jong, Schouten and Wieffer injured and Veerman, Koopmeiners and Taylor the alternatives. The evidence for how Xavi’s Netherlands play is a club coaching CV, not a match.

Nagelsmann’s Germany had even more of the ball and allowed less. In 2024–25 League A: 63.7% possession, 17.33 shots for and 8.50 against, 6.50 on target for and 2.67 against; in 2025–26: 69.3%, 17.33 shots for and 6.78 against, 6.30 corners won and 3.20 conceded. They were booked twice a match in the League A group (12 cards in six, four of them in Amsterdam) and less than once a match in 2025–26. Thirteen of 20 competitive starts were a 4-2-3-1, with a back three tried three times. What Klopp changes is unknown in both directions: Bundesliga.com notes a 4-2-3-1 at Dortmund and largely a 4-3-3 at Liverpool, and both previews predict a 4-2-3-1 here. The bigger change is personnel: the creative core of the Nagelsmann attack — Wirtz, Sané, Woltemade, Undav — is not in this group, the goalkeeper and one centre-back of the World Cup side have retired, and seven of the 24 are uncapped. Everything on the strip describes a German team that will not take the field today.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Every rate belongs to a coach who has left. Koeman and Nagelsmann produced all 40 competitive matches behind the strip; Xavi and Klopp have none between them with these teams, and their stated or reported systems rest on club CVs.
  • Germany’s selection is split by design. The side that produced Germany’s figures is divided across two squads and two retirements: of the eleven that started their last match, five are here. Seven of today’s group are uncapped, and the goalkeeper is either ter Stegen, back after 15 months, or Nübel, with two starts.
  • 25/26 is inflated by weak opposition. Six of each side’s ten 25/26 matches were qualifiers, including two each against Malta and Lithuania (Netherlands) and Luxembourg (Germany); the League A group is the only like-for-like sample, six matches each, and it is two years old.
  • The referee’s relevant record is the thin one. His 22-match LaLiga record says strict; his 10-match UEFA and international record, the one that matches this fixture, says lenient, and it rests on ten matches across five competitions.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, Asian handicap and Germany team goals — all need the strength of a German side that has never played together, under a coach with no match. Least sensitive: Totals and match corners — both are match-level counts that land near the League A line in every window for both sides, and the Netherlands’ scoring record that props the first survives the coach change in personnel.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Totals is more robust than 1X2 here because the goal counts agree across windows and the results do not survive the coach changes. The sides’ over-2.5 rates (50.0/70.0% and 66.7/60.0%) bracket League A’s 56.3%; the results were earned by Koeman’s and Nagelsmann’s teams.
  • Team goals on the Netherlands side is more robust than on the Germany side because only one attack is the attack that produced its rate. The Netherlands scored in 19 of 20 with Gakpo and Brobbey available; Germany’s 3.00 and 2.70 per match came with Wirtz, Sané and Woltemade, none of whom plays.
  • Match corners are more robust than the corner split because the totals converge and the territory does not. Both sides’ match totals sit at 8.3–9.8 against a divisional 9.5; which side wins them depends on possession no one has yet seen under Xavi or Klopp.
  • Discipline is less robust than its data volume suggests. 19 LaLiga matches in 2025–26 say 5.74 cards per game; ten UEFA and international matches say 2.20; the teams booked 0.60 and 0.90 per match in 25/26 against a League A 4.13.
  • The single fact that would most change which family reads best is Germany’s starting eleven and shape. A side built around Kimmich, Tah, Schlotterbeck, Musiala and Havertz inherits part of the Nagelsmann record; one built around the uncapped players inherits none of it, and Germany team goals, BTTS and the corner split move with it.
