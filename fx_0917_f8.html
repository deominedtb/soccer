B · Style & early-season reality

Real Sociedad under Matarazzo were an even, open side: over 22 league matches, 50.8% possession, 11.6 shots for and 15.1 against, 1.50 xG for and 1.65 against, both teams scoring in 81.8% and a clean sheet in 9.1%. They foul heavily for LaLiga (14.2 per match last season against a league 12.6) and card at 2.42. This season the chance balance has worsened — 0.97 xG for, 1.67 against — and they failed to score in three of six, though they won 7.67 corners per match. The summer changes are small; the absences tonight (Aramburu, Zubeldia, Odriozola) fall on the right side and centre of defence.

Bournemouth under Iraola were a high-intensity, direct side with 50.2% possession, 13.8 shots and 1.63 xG for, 1.49 against, 5.58 corners won and 5.29 conceded, and 65.8% BTTS. Under Rose, the 4-2-3-1 has produced 1.48 xG for and 1.32 against in four league matches, draws in three of them, a rise in cards to 3.25 per match and a first-half scoring rate of 1.25 per match. Rodríguez, Silva and Juanlu arrived in July and August; Rayan, Kluivert and Tavernier behind Evanilson are the expected front four. The evidence for Rose’s side is five matches, not Iraola’s 38.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Bournemouth’s 6th place belongs to Iraola and, for half a season, Semenyo. Rose has five matches; Rodríguez, Silva and Juanlu arrived in July and August.
  • Real Sociedad’s cup win sits on top of a 10th-place league season with negative chance data. Under Matarazzo they were out-created, and this season more so.
  • Real Sociedad are short at the back. Aramburu suspended, Zubeldia and Odriozola injured; Sarr arrived on 28 August.
  • Bournemouth play their first European match, away. None of their series includes a match outside English competition.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, handicap and Real Sociedad team goals. Least sensitive: discipline, where the official’s strictness and both clubs’ card rates are long published series.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Discipline is more robust than the 1X2 here because Raczkowski’s 5.92 and 5.00 yellows per match and both clubs’ card rates are two-season series, whereas the result depends on two coaches with six and five matches of this season.
  • A Bournemouth team-goals reading is more robust than a Real Sociedad one because Bournemouth’s xG has held near 1.5 across the coaching change and they have scored in every match, while Real Sociedad’s xG has fallen to 0.97 with three blanks in six.
  • BTTS is more robust than the handicap because both clubs’ both-score rates (79% and 66% last season) are long series, while every margin on the card is small and unstable.
  • Totals are more robust than corners because both over-2.5 series agree across seasons and coaches, while the current corner gaps are six- and four-match samples.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: Real Sociedad’s back line. With Aramburu, Zubeldia and Odriozola out, who plays at right-back and beside Sarr decides whether the 8% clean-sheet record or this season’s tighter first halves (0.33 conceded) describes tonight’s defence; the team sheet settles it.
