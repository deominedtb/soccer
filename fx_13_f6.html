A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Reduced-depth card: research was capped at two searches per club plus the referee lookups. The RESEARCH SPEC fields not pursued here are both clubs' 2025–26 goals scored and conceded, xG, shot volume, home/away split and foul and card rates; Leipzig's full departure ledger; and any injury list beyond the two items named below. Those absences are a budget decision, not a finding.

Neither club is promoted. Both changed head coach, but at opposite ends of a season rather than in the same window. Daniel Thioune was appointed at Werder Bremen on 4 February 2026, mid-campaign, replacing Horst Steffen — a rescue appointment that ended in 15th place, three points clear of relegated Wolfsburg, and this is his first full season. Martín Demichelis took over at RB Leipzig this summer, reported as the first permanent Argentine head coach in Bundesliga history. Matchday 1 separated them sharply: Bremen were beaten 4–1 by Freiburg and sit 16th with no points, Leipzig won 3–0 against Borussia Mönchengladbach and sit 5th with three. The windows were as different as the results. Bremen made 14 incoming signings, most of them young or loaned; Leipzig sold one player for a reported €125m and spent heavily on two centre-forwards.

  • Werder Bremen — the highest arrival count on the slate, and almost none of it permanent or senior. Thioune's first full season begins with a squad rebuilt by volume: 14 arrivals, of which the ones reached here are Chuki, a 22-year-old playmaker from Valladolid, three teenagers or near-teenagers in Dariusz Stalmach (20), Kenny Quetent (20) and Darwin Soylu (19), and three loans — Youri Regeer from Ajax with a purchase option, Niclas Füllkrug for the season, and the Brazilian full-back Arthur, confirmed as the 14th and final arrival of the window. Out went the goalkeeper Mio Backhaus, plus Romano Schmid, Karim Coulibaly and Leonardo Bittencourt, while the previous season's loanees — Sugawara, Milošević, Boniface and Wöber — all left. The plan was reportedly revised mid-window after Jens Stage suffered a severe thigh muscle injury, with no return expected before the end of the 2026 calendar year. Gap: no fee is published for any of the 14 arrivals or any of the departures in anything reached, so a window this large cannot be quantified at all. Conflict logged: the predicted XI reached for this match lists Eren Dinkçi for Bremen, while a Freiburg transfer summary from the same window records Dinkci moving to Heidenheim. Both were reached; neither is corroborated, and the card does not choose.
  • RB Leipzig — one enormous sale, two centre-forwards bought, and a new coach to spend it. Demichelis arrives with the biggest transfer number on this slate behind him: Yan Diomande sold to Real Madrid for a reported €125m. The reinvestment went straight into the position the spec asks about first. Marc Guiu was signed permanently from Chelsea for a reported €17m including realistic performance bonuses; Fisnik Asllani arrived for a reported €25m on a contract to 2031; Neil El Aynaoui came from Roma on an initial season-long loan carrying a purchase option reported at around €25m; Brajan Gruda's loan from Brighton was extended with a conditional obligation to buy; and Ørjan Nyland arrived as understudy to the goalkeeper Maarten Vandevoordt. Conrad Harder went to Strasbourg on loan with no purchase option. Gap: the wider departure ledger was not pursued at this depth, and no centre-back movement was established in either direction.
  • Suspensions / absences. Not pursued systematically at this depth. The one established item is Bremen's Jens Stage, out with a severe thigh muscle injury and not expected back before the end of the 2026 calendar year. No suspension is reported for either side, and no Leipzig injury list was sought.
  • Sample. One Bundesliga match each; the season began on 28 August 2026 and this is matchday 2. Bremen: 16th, no points, beaten 4–1 at home terms by Freiburg. Leipzig: 5th, three points, a 3–0 win over Mönchengladbach with a clean sheet. Behind that, Bremen's 15th place last season is a full-season number produced under two coaches — Steffen until February, Thioune after — which limits what it says about either. Leipzig's own 2025–26 finish was not pursued at this depth.
  • Referee. Benjamin Brand, named in the match preview reached, and not directly confirmed by the matchday-2 designation list, which was retrieved for other fixtures on this round but not for this one. The claim is more credible than it first looked: the same preview source named Brand for Leverkusen–Union Berlin at the identical kickoff time, and the federation list gives that match to Dr Robin Braun instead — so one of the two preview claims had to be wrong, and it was the other one. Brand's record is the best-documented on this board: in 2025–26, 19 matches with 72 yellow cards and 3 reds for 3.95 cards a game (3.79 yellow, 0.16 red) and 4 penalties, a rate of 0.21 per match; across a 214-match career, 101 of them in the Bundesliga, 4.04 yellows and 0.10 reds a game with 66 penalties, roughly 0.31 per match. Gap: his fouls-per-card ratio is unpublished, and no Brand record specific to either club was found.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Bremen changed 14 squad places in one window, mostly with players aged 19 to 22 or on loan, and their first XI as predicted contains a loaned centre-forward, a loaned Ajax midfielder and a loaned Brazilian full-back. Last season's 15th place was compiled by a different squad under two different coaches, so it is a weak baseline in both directions.
  • Leipzig's summer is dominated by a single figure — the reported €125m for Diomande, the largest fee on this slate and larger than Freiburg's €70m Manzambi sale elsewhere on the card. The reinvestment went into two centre-forwards who have played one Bundesliga match between them for this club.
  • Both coaches are effectively new. Thioune arrived in February 2026 as a mid-season appointment and this is his first full campaign; Demichelis is one match into his first Bundesliga job. Any stylistic claim about either side is a claim about a curriculum vitae.
  • The referee is named in a preview rather than in the designation list retrieved for this round, though the same preview source was wrong about the official at another match in the same kickoff slot — which is a reason to hold the name loosely even though Brand’s own record is the best-documented on the board.
  • Most sensitive: the goals total at 1.37, which extrapolates two matchday-one scorelines with no season baseline pursued for either club and with one of those matches being a Leipzig clean sheet. Least sensitive: the discipline row, where a 214-match career record at 4.04 yellows a game sits above a 3.5 line the market has leaned under — a disagreement that can at least be stated precisely.
