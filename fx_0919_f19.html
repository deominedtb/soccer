A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Troyes are promoted as Ligue 2 champions — 67 points from 34 matches, 20 wins, 7 draws, 7 defeats, 60 scored, 33 conceded — and are back in the top flight after three years away. Angers finished 13th in Ligue 1 on 36 points — 9 wins, 9 draws, 16 defeats, and just 29 goals scored, 0.85 per match, with 29.4% of their matches going over 2.5 against a divisional 52.6%. That is the lowest scoring rate of any club on this board. The structural headline is that the promoted club kept its promotion-winning coach and sold most of what won it, while the established club changed coach and has transformed at the only end that was broken. Angers’ expected goals have risen from 0.98 to 1.50 a game and their corners from 3.56 to 9.00; Troyes have banked more than €34m in departures and their arrivals column is close to empty.

B · Style & early-season reality

Angers have changed more than any Ligue 1 club on this board and the change is concentrated in the two columns that were worst. Under Gilli their expected goals have risen from 0.98 to 1.50 per match and their shots from 9.29 to 15.75 — both up by more than half — while corners won have gone from 3.56 to 9.00, the highest figure of any club carded on this slate, against a divisional 4.84. The pressing data explains where it comes from: PPDA has fallen from 18.48 to 13.24, so a side that last season stood off almost entirely now engages, and possession is up from 41.92% to 48.67%. The defensive side has improved too but less dramatically — expected goals against from 1.77 to 1.59, deep completions conceded from 7.21 to 7.50, essentially flat. Two things have not followed the chances. The goals: 1.00 per match against 1.50 xG, with only 3.00 shots on target, so they are creating volume rather than quality. And the venue split has inverted: 0.50 scored and 2.00 conceded at home against 1.50 and 0.50 away, where last season they were 1.06/1.18 at home and 0.65/1.65 away.

Troyes are playing a deep, disciplined, very low-contact game and conceding almost nothing in dead-ball terms while being beaten in open play. Possession 38.85% against 51.76% in Ligue 2; PPDA 17.11, the second most passive on this board, against opponents pressing them at 8.32. The two numbers that stand out are both league extremes and both point the same way: 0.75 cards per match — three in four matches, the lowest of any club on this board against a divisional 2.95 — and 1.50 corners conceded per match, also the lowest, against a divisional 4.84. A side that does not foul (11.25, under the divisional 11.74, and down from 13.26 in Ligue 2) and does not concede corners is being hurt another way: expected goals against 2.03 per match and 9.50 deep completions conceded, against 0.97 goals conceded and a 35.3% clean-sheet rate in the division below. Their own attacking figures have held up better than the defensive ones — 13.75 shots against 13.76 in Ligue 2 — but the conversion has collapsed, 2.75 shots on target now against 5.09 then. The evidence that this is the level rather than a bad month is four matches of possession, pressing and shot-on-target data all moving together, against a promotion season whose xG nobody published.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Troyes’ entire 2025–26 record is from Ligue 2 and its xG column does not exist. Sixty-seven points, 60 goals, a 35.3% clean-sheet rate and 2.24 cards per match came in a division running at 2.53 goals and 4.14 cards. No public source publishes Ligue 2 expected goals, and the squad that produced that season has since lost its goalkeeper to Rennes, its leading scorer to a loan return and five players for roughly €34m.
  • Angers’ transformation is four matches old and measured against a very low base. Going from 0.98 to 1.50 expected goals per match is a large relative move, but the starting point was the worst attacking record on this board — 29 goals in 34 matches. The shots-on-target column has not moved at all (3.26 to 3.00), so the improvement is in volume rather than in quality, and the coach responsible arrived this summer.
  • One result distorts most of Troyes’ defensive numbers. The 2–6 at home to Strasbourg on 6 September contributed four of their nine goals conceded and much of the 2.03 expected goals against. It cannot be excluded, and it is also the match in which they took 27 shots and won 11 corners to nil — so it distorts the attacking and corner columns in the opposite direction at the same time.
  • The referee has no meaningful record in this division. One Ligue 1 match, zero cards in it, and no 2025–26 top-flight season. His 16-match Ligue 2 record at 8.09 fouls per card describes a division that books more often and whistles more often than this one. That is why the discipline row is rated dangerous despite both clubs having the cleanest card rates on the board.
  • Ligue 1’s home-win rate is the most extreme divisional figure on this slate. 29.7% against 46.1% last season, with draws at 35.1%, over 37 matches. It is reported as an observed rate rather than a trend, but every row above that touches the result or the margin is being read against a baseline that has moved by sixteen points.
  • Most sensitive: the discipline row, which has no usable referee input, and the Asian handicap, which has a four-goal tail inside a four-match sample. Least sensitive: corners — Angers’ 9.00 won repeats across all four matches and tracks their shots, possession and pressing together, and Troyes’ 1.50 conceded repeats across all four including the match they lost by four.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners are more robust than the match result here because Angers’ 9.00 won and Troyes’ 1.50 conceded each repeat in every one of four matches and each moves in step with three other columns — shots, possession and PPDA on one side, possession and deep completions on the other — while the result rests on a new coach with four matches and a promoted side whose comparison season has no xG.
  • Angers’ shot volume is more robust than their goal rate because 15.75 shots and 9.00 corners per match are corroborated by possession and pressing data, whereas their 3.00 shots on target is essentially unchanged from last season’s 3.26 and their goals have risen only from 0.85 to 1.00.
  • BTTS is more robust than a first-half result here because both clubs’ blank halves have the same documented mechanism — the two lowest on-target conversion rates on this board — while four matches each and a 35.1% divisional draw rate leave no readable margin inside 45 minutes.
  • The clubs’ card rates are more robust than the discipline row as a whole because Troyes’ 0.75 and Angers’ 1.50 per match each continue a below-divisional rate from last season in their own divisions, while the official has one match at this level and zero cards in it.
  • The single fact that would most change which family I choose is a Ligue 1 card record for Azzedine Souifi. Both clubs are the cleanest on this board and a lenient official would compound that, while his Ligue 2 line of 4.00 cards per match sits above Ligue 1’s own 3.91 — the two readings point opposite ways and one match at this level cannot separate them. What would settle it is four or five more Ligue 1 appointments, or a published French federation breakdown of his record by competition; neither exists in any source reached today, and until one does the discipline row is the weakest on this card despite having the strongest club evidence.
