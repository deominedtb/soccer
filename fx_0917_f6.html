A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, but NEC are playing their first group or league-phase match since the introduction of the group stage (they reached the UEFA Cup group stage once before). Juventus finished 6th in the 2025–26 Serie A — 19-12-7, 61 scored, 34 conceded, 69 points — and qualified directly. NEC finished 3rd in the 2025–26 Eredivisie — 16-11-7, 77 scored, 53 conceded, 59 points — lost the KNVB Cup final 1–5 to AZ, then beat Olympiacos and lost to Bodø/Glimt in Champions League qualifying. The structural headline is that the two clubs’ long series sit at opposite ends of the goals scale — Juventus 39% over 2.5 in a 45.8% league, NEC 74% in a 61.5% league — while both squads arrive depleted: Juventus by injuries and a replaced front line, NEC by sales and a reported thirteen injuries.

B · Style & early-season reality

Juventus under Spalletti were a controlling, low-concession side: over 29 league matches, 57.3% possession, 16.9 shots for and 9.7 against, 1.94 xG for and 0.83 against, and first halves that produced 0.69 goals for and 0.31 against. Only 44.8% of those matches went over 2.5. The four league matches this season continue the pattern at the back (0.45 xG against, 2.25 corners conceded, 9.75 won) but show the attack short of its chance volume — six goals, and one first-half goal in four matches — and a jump in fouling to 16.0 per match. Kolo Muani, Alajbegović, Lucumí and Vicario started at Sassuolo; Woltemade (1 September) and Sarr are not in that eleven; and tonight’s eleven is shaped by the absence of Yıldız, Locatelli, Thuram and Cambiaso.

NEC under Schreuder play an open, high-possession 3-4-2-1: last season 56.5% of the ball, 17.0 shots and 1.89 xG for, but 1.51 xG against, with BTTS in 82% of league matches and a failed-to-score rate of 3%. This season’s league matches are more open still (63.5% possession, 1.97 xG against, every match over 2.5). Against Champions League opposition the attack fell away: 1.26 xG per match over four qualifiers and 0.12 at Bodø. The flanks that drove last season (Önal, Ouaissa) have been sold; Kabar and Bischoff are loans; Tadić and Geiger are experienced additions whose fit is a CV and a few matches.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Juventus are short of first-choice players. Yıldız, Locatelli, Thuram, Ekhator, Cabal and Boga injured, Cambiaso out, Kelly suspended; three academy players were called up.
  • Juventus’s attack is new. Vlahović, David and Openda are gone; Kolo Muani has four league matches, Woltemade none.
  • NEC’s numbers are Eredivisie numbers, and the team that made them has been sold on the flanks. Önal and Ouaissa left; against Bodø/Glimt NEC lost 1–6 on aggregate with 0.12 xG in the away leg.
  • The leagues score very differently. Serie A 2.43 goals per match, Eredivisie 3.17; neither club’s totals series can be carried across without that gap.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, handicap and NEC team goals. Least sensitive: halves, where both first-half profiles have held across seasons and coaches.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Halves are more robust than the 1X2 here because Juventus’s first-half series (0.63/0.39, 0.69/0.31 under Spalletti) and NEC’s (0.88/0.88) are complete and stable, whereas the result depends on two depleted squads.
  • A Juventus team-goals reading is more robust than an NEC one because Juventus’s 1.94 xG under Spalletti is a 29-match series, while NEC’s attack fell to 1.26 xG against qualifying opposition and lost its wing-backs.
  • Totals are more robust than BTTS because both clubs’ over-2.5 series are long and internally stable, whereas BTTS sets NEC’s 82% directly against Juventus’s 42% clean sheets.
  • Discipline is more robust than the handicap because the official’s two seasons and both clubs’ low card rates are published, whereas no margin series is on a common scale.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: Juventus’s front three. With Yıldız out, whether Kolo Muani or Woltemade leads the line, and who plays wide, decides whether Juventus’s chance volume this season translates into goals; the team sheet settles it.
