Liechtenstein’s numbers change completely with the level of opposition. In their four 2024–25 League D matches they had 58.3% of the ball, took 7.75 shots and conceded 11.00, and committed 19.25 fouls per match against 13.25 by their opponents — the 2–2 in Gibraltar alone carried 15 cards. In eight World Cup qualifiers they had 24.7% of the ball, took 2.38 shots and 0.88 on target, won 0.75 corners and conceded 9.12, and fouled only 10.75 times a match. Across all 12 competitive matches the shape was a back three or five with two forwards. Today’s listing puts four at the back and three forwards, with a centre-back, Weissenhofer, who has no competitive start in the record; the evidence that this changes what Liechtenstein produce is ESPN’s formation label, not a match, and the label is the same one ESPN gives Lithuania, so it may be a default rather than a tactical statement.

Lithuania concede in almost every competitive match: one clean sheet in 14 since September 2024 (0–0 in Malta), and scored in eight of those 14. In League C they had 53.9% of the ball, took 9.83 shots and conceded 11.00, and lost all six by one or two goals; in qualifying against stronger sides, 43.2%, 8.62 shots, 14.25 conceded. Their foul rate is steady across windows, 16.67 in League C and 16.00 in qualifying, and they drew more cards on opponents than they took in League C (3.33 against 2.00 per match). What the summer changed is a new starting goalkeeper, two midfielders without a competitive start and, on ESPN’s listing, a back four after five straight competitive matches in a 5-4-1. What has never been tested is Lithuania against League D opposition: every competitive match in their record was in League C or World Cup qualifying.
