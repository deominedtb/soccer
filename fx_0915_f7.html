B · Style & early-season reality

Liverpool are a 4–2–3–1 side under both coaches — 3,194 Understat minutes in the shape last season and every minute this season — with around 60% possession, 15–16 shots per match and a high corner count last season (6.11). Under Iraola three things have moved on four matches: corners down to 4.25, fouls up from 10.1 to 13.0 and cards from 1.53 to 2.50 per match. Last season’s set-piece weakness (11 goals conceded from corners) has no current measurement. Tonight’s eleven is a rotated one — Mamardashvili in goal, a changed midfield and front line, Araújo and Gomez at centre-back — so the team rates describe a different group of players.

Tottenham under De Zerbi have turned into a possession side on the numbers: 59.8% of the ball and 6.75 corners per match this season, against 50.4% and 5.50 last season, with 14.3 fouls per match but only four yellows in four matches. What has not followed is output: 0.85 xG and no goals in four league matches, after a season in which they scored 18 set-piece goals and conceded 0.74 first-half goals per match. The summer spent heavily in midfield and at centre-back, and the evidence for how Tonali, Fernandes, Sávio and van Hecke change the side is a handful of matches — none of the four starts tonight.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Both elevens are rotated. Liverpool start Mamardashvili, Nyoni, Ngumoha, McConnell and Koumas; Tottenham leave out Tonali, Fernandes, Sávio and van Hecke.
  • Tottenham’s zero is four matches long. 0.85 xG per match and no goals, set against a 5–1 cup win over Charlton.
  • Liverpool’s discipline and corners moved with the coach. Cards up by a card a match and corners down by nearly two, on four matches.
  • No VAR. Penalty and red-card decisions rest on the referee alone, which matters most for the discipline row.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, the handicap, BTTS and team goals, all dependent on who is on the pitch. Least sensitive: the corners-for rates, at or above the baseline for both clubs in both seasons.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners are more robust than 1X2 here because both clubs’ corners-for rates sit at or above the 4.9 baseline in both seasons, while 1X2 depends on first-choice elevens that neither club has picked.
  • Discipline is more robust than team goals because Madley is confirmed and below the booking average in every record, whereas both team totals describe forward lines not starting tonight.
  • Halves are more robust than BTTS because both clubs have first-half series in both seasons, while Tottenham’s BTTS rate is a four-match zero from league elevens that differ from tonight’s.
  • Totals are more robust than the handicap because the two clubs’ over-2.5 rates moved in the same direction in both seasons, whereas the handicap needs a margin between two rotated sides.
