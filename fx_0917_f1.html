A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, and neither is new to European football, but this is Levski’s first group or league-phase match since the 2010–11 Europa League. Levski won the 2025–26 Bulgarian First League — 25 wins, 6 draws, 5 defeats, 71 scored, 25 conceded, 81 points — ending Ludogorets’ run of titles, then dropped into this competition after losing the Champions League play-off 0–4 on aggregate to AEK Athens. Salzburg finished 3rd in the Austrian championship group — 13-9-10 over 32 matches, 56 scored, 41 conceded — after leading the regular season, and went W2 D0 L6 (10–15) in last season’s Europa League league phase. The structural headline is that Salzburg’s last-season record belongs to a different squad and three different coaches, while Levski’s belongs to a coach and spine that are largely intact but were built in a league scoring 2.30 goals per game that publishes no xG.

B · Style & early-season reality

Levski under Velázquez dominate the ball at home in Bulgaria: 62.5% possession, 11.6 shots for and 5.3 against, and 6.17 corners won against 2.61 conceded per league match last season, conceding 0.22 goals per first half. They lined up 4-2-3-1 at Vratsa. This season’s league numbers are the same shape (65.8% possession, 12.3 shots for, 4.9 against, no first-half goal conceded in nine). Against qualifying opposition the picture changes: corners fell to 4.50 each way over eight matches, first-half concessions rose to 0.63 per match, and in the three qualifiers where xG is published Levski produced 0.81 against 1.73, including the 0–4 at AEK. The summer’s effect is concentrated in midfield: Serginho and El Moubarik started at Vratsa, El Moubarik is out tonight, and Grujić arrived in mid-August.

Salzburg under Röhl have become a very high-volume, low-foul side in six league matches: 65.0% possession, 21.3 shots and 8.2 on target per match, 2.84 xG for and 1.14 against, 8.33 corners won, and only 5.0 fouls and 0.83 cards per match — against 9.7 fouls and 1.75 cards last season. In four qualifiers they won 7.00 corners and conceded 1.50. Last season’s side, under Letsch and Beichler, was more open (68.8% BTTS, 15.6% clean sheets). The evidence for the rebuilt spine is six league matches and four qualifiers: Boma, Mazurek and Barry started at Ried, Tabaković leads the scoring, and neither Salzburg goalkeeper has a settled run; Lindstrøm’s arrival is a CV, not a match.

1X2 / MoneylineDangerous — no common scale, and Salzburg’s record is a different team’s
Levski’s dominance was built in a division scoring 2.30 per game with no published xG, and in the three qualifiers with xG they were second best. Salzburg’s last season (3rd, 2-0-6 in this competition) came under two coaches since dismissed and with a spine since sold; their unbeaten run under Röhl is 12 matches against Austrian, Cypriot and Swedish opposition.
Asian handicap / DNBDangerous — margins made in unlike leagues
Levski’s +46 league goal difference last season and +17 this season, and Salzburg’s +1.70 xG difference per league match, measure the gap to domestic opposition only. No series on the card measures these two sides against a common opponent.
Totals (O/U goals)OK — long series, but Levski’s are made in a low-scoring league
Levski’s league matches went over 2.5 in 53% last season (league 43.3%) and 67% of nine this season; Salzburg’s in 63% and 50%, against a baseline of 52.1%. Levski’s eight qualifiers went over in three. Both series are complete; what limits them is that Levski concede 0.44 per league match and Salzburg’s defence is newly assembled.
BTTSOK — Salzburg always score, Levski rarely concede at home
Salzburg have scored in all 12 competitive matches and failed to score in 19% of league matches last season; Levski kept clean sheets in 56% of league matches this season and 42% last, and in all four home qualifiers (Borac 4–0, Craiova 1–0, Kairat 1–0, AEK 0–0). Both inputs are long; Salzburg’s scoring record is made against weaker defences than Levski’s at home.
Team goalsSplit — Salzburg OK, Levski dangerous

Levski’s rests on goals with no league xG behind them, 0.81 xG per match in the three qualifiers that publish it, no goal in 180 minutes against AEK, the departure of Dugandžić and El Moubarik’s absence.

D · Early-season risk notes

  • Salzburg’s 2025–26 numbers describe a squad that no longer exists. Both first-choice centre-backs, the first-choice goalkeeper, three central midfielders and three wide forwards were sold, and the coach who finished the season was dismissed in May.
  • Levski’s numbers are Bulgarian numbers. A 2.30-goal league with no published xG or fouls; in the three qualifiers that publish xG, Levski were out-created 0.81 to 1.73.
  • Röhl’s sample is short and soft. Six league matches and four qualifiers against Pafos and Mjällby; the foul rate halving is real in the data but ten matches old.
  • Levski’s 15-1-2 home league record was made almost entirely at Georgi Asparuhov (16 of 18 home matches). Tonight is at the national stadium, where their only match this season was the 0–0 with AEK.
  • Most sensitive: 1X2, handicap and Levski team goals — each depends on translating Bulgarian dominance or a rebuilt Salzburg into a common scale. Least sensitive: corners, where both clubs’ domestic and European series are published and already show how each changes against stronger opposition.

E · Short market-type guide

  • Corners are more robust than the 1X2 here because the change in Levski’s corner count from league (6.11/2.89) to Europe (4.50/4.50) is itself measured, whereas no result series measures it.
  • A Salzburg team-goals reading is more robust than a Levski one because Salzburg’s volume is backed by 2.84 league xG and 2.21 in qualifiers, while Levski have no league xG, lost Dugandžić, and failed to score in 180 minutes against AEK.
  • BTTS is more robust than the handicap because both inputs (Salzburg scoring in 12 of 12; Levski’s 56% league clean sheets) are long series, while the margin has no common scale.
  • Halves are more robust than full-time totals for Levski’s side of the ledger because their first-half concessions are counted in every match of both seasons and in all eight qualifiers.
  • The fact that would most change the family choice: an xG series for Levski in the Bulgarian league. With it, Levski’s domestic dominance could be put on the same scale as Salzburg’s; without it, every Levski attacking figure on this card is a goals-only record. It would need FotMob, Opta or the league to publish it.
