A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Neither club is promoted, and the tie crosses a division. Genoa finished 16th in 2025–26 Serie A — 10 wins, 11 draws, 17 defeats, 41 scored, 51 conceded, 41 points — with De Rossi taking over in November. Südtirol finished 16th in 2025–26 Serie B — 8 wins, 17 draws, 13 defeats, 38 scored, 48 conceded, 41 points — and kept their place through the relegation play-out, which ended 0–0 on aggregate against Bari and was decided on league position; their 13-match winless run was the longest in the division. The structural headline is that the two clubs’ league samples this season run the other way from the divisional gap: Genoa are 19th in Serie A on one point, Südtirol are unbeaten in Serie B on eight, and this competition’s first stage has no extra time, so a level score at 90 minutes goes directly to penalties.

B · Style & early-season reality

Genoa under De Rossi are a back-three side: last season Understat logged 1,537 minutes in a 3–5–2 against 783 in a 3–4–2–1 and 740 in a 4–2–3–1, with 48.7% possession, 12.4 fouls per match (league 12.7 per team) and 3.68 corners per match, below the Serie A team average of 4.41. Set pieces cut both ways: 10 goals from set plays and corners, and 12 conceded from corner situations alone. This season the back three has persisted across three shapes (3–4–2–1, 3–5–2, 3–4–1–2) while possession has dropped to 45.3% and the shot count faced has risen by more than five a match. What the summer changed is the personnel of that system at its ends: four of the flank defenders are new (Ehizibue, Mitaj, Puczka, Drameh) after Norton-Cuffy and Martín were sold, and the front two is a pairing one preview calls inedito — evidence for it is a CV, not a match, and Havel, one of the summer’s forwards, is not in the squad.

Südtirol under Castori were a low-possession, high-contact Serie B side — 40.6% possession, 16.6 fouls per match against a division average of 15.0 per team, 2.37 cards per match and 4.34 corners per match. The four matches under Possanzini show different numbers on almost every one of those axes: 48.1% possession, 12.8 fouls, 2.50 corners per match, with a new goalkeeper and a new centre-back pairing behind a defence that has conceded twice. Whether that is a changed style or four matches is not something the sample can separate; Südtirol’s chance data (0.68 xG for, 1.10 against per match) describes a side whose results so far have exceeded what it created and allowed. Gap: no published tactical profile of Possanzini’s Südtirol beyond probable elevens that disagree on a back three or a back four.
