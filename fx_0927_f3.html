A · Squad, promotion & transfer context

Wales are the promoted side; Denmark stayed in League A. Wales won League B Group B4 in 2024–25 on 12 points, 9–4, unbeaten (W3 D3), one point ahead of Türkiye. Denmark were second in League A Group A4 on 8 points, 7–5 (W2 D2 L2, behind Spain on 16). They beat Portugal 1–0 at Parken in the quarter-final first leg, then lost 2–3 at 90 minutes and 2–5 after extra time in Lisbon. In World Cup qualifying Denmark were second in Group C on 11 points, 16–7 (W3 D2 L1, behind Scotland). In the play-offs they beat North Macedonia 4–0, then lost to Czechia on penalties after 1–1 at 90 minutes and 2–2 after extra time. Wales were second in Group J on 16 points, 21–11 (W5 D1 L2, behind Belgium). They then lost their play-off semi-final to Bosnia-Herzegovina on penalties after 1–1 at 90 minutes and 1–1 after extra time. Wales have not won in 2026: after Bosnia they drew 1–1 with Northern Ireland and Ghana, lost 1–2 in Romania and lost on Thursday. Their last win was 7–1 against North Macedonia in November 2025. Format: the top two in each group go to the quarter-finals. The two worst last-placed sides go straight down, and the two best last-placed and two worst third-placed sides go into play-offs (Wikipedia’s League A page, citing UEFA). How the division treats promoted sides: the eight sides promoted into League A for 2022–23 and 2024–25 went W4 D3 L17 away from home. The stayed-in-A hosts in those 24 matches went W17 D3 L4, 49–23, and won more corners in 20 of them (baselines_0927.md). Five of the eight promoted sides finished fourth. One was Wales in 2022–23 (W0 D1 L5, 6–11, relegated): they lost all three away matches, each by one goal. The structural headline: a promoted side missing its leading scorer, first-choice goalkeeper and most-used centre-back plays at a ground where the hosts have lost once in eight competitive matches since September 2024, won more corners in seven of the eight and kept five clean sheets. Denmark’s own spine has also turned over: players outside tonight’s squad made 79 of Denmark’s 187 competitive starts in the window. Head to head: the international-results record has 11 meetings. Denmark won 7 and Wales 4, with no draws (Sports Mole agrees on the count). In Denmark, Denmark won 1–0 in 1964, 1987 and 1990 (all Copenhagen) and 2–0 in Aarhus in 2018. Wales won 2–1 in Copenhagen in 1998 and 1–0 at Brøndby in 2008, and Craig Bellamy scored the winner both times (Pembrokeshire Herald). The last meeting was Denmark’s 4–0 at Euro 2020 in Amsterdam. B · Style & early-season reality

Denmark control the ball and the corner count at Parken, and concede little there. At home since September 2024 they had 57.0% of the ball and 17.88 shots to 8.38, and won 9.00 corners to 4.00 (more in 7 of 8). They conceded 0.62 goals per match and 0.12 before half-time. Visitors to Parken were booked 3.38 times per match and fouled 13.00 times to Denmark’s 9.88, and two of the four red cards shown to Denmark’s opponents in the window came there (Switzerland). Against League A-tier opposition the control thins: 48.4% possession, 11.75 shots to 13.12 and 5.88 corners to 5.00. The shape has moved under Riemer. Knudsen’s four matches were all a back three. Riemer has played a back four in 11 of 13, mostly 4-2-3-1, with a back three only against Scotland at home and Belarus away. The personnel under that shape is newer than the shape. Schmeichel and Vestergaard, the two most-used defensive starters, have retired. Christensen is injured. Thursday’s back line had a 3-cap goalkeeper and a 3-cap left centre-back, and conceded twice in the first 18 minutes and three times in all. Eriksen, the side’s most-capped midfielder, is absent on medical grounds. The evidence for the new spine is one match.

Bellamy’s Wales keep the ball against weaker sides and lose it against stronger ones. Overall they had 61.8% of the ball, and 68.8% in qualifying, where they took 15.12 shots to 9.38 and won 6.88 corners to 4.00. Away against League A-tier sides (Türkiye, Belgium, Portugal) they had 43.3% of the ball, 7.00 shots to 22.00 and 3.00 corners to 8.67, and never led at half-time against that level. Their away matches are tight rather than open: all eight finished within one goal and averaged 2.38 goals. Wales were booked 2.62 times per match away, against their hosts’ 1.62, at 3.86 fouls per card. Their penalty count is high: 8 penalty kicks in 16 matches, four of them to Belgium. The summer has changed the personnel more than the shape. Wilson, the side’s set-piece taker and leading scorer, is out, along with Rodon and Darlow. In Lisbon, Bellamy went to a 4-4-2 with Jordan James and Lewis Koumas up front, and it produced 5 shots and 2 corners. The previews expect Moore, Dan James or Thomas to come in. Moore has 3 goals in 4 starts in the window, but none of those starts came against a League A-tier side. Whether Wales’s front line changes the away pattern against this level is a CV question, not a match question.
