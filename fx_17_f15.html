B · Style & early-season reality

Blackburn are a club that has not settled since the middle of last season, and the summer did not address the thing the goals markets care about. Three managers took charge in 2025–26 on the way to 20th; Mowbray is the fourth man in the job inside roughly a year, appointed 5 June with a new assistant three weeks later. The spending that followed went into central midfield — £2m for Baradji, a free for Morsy — with £500,000 on a winger from Stockport County and a defender from Modena, and no centre-forward signed for a published fee. The output matches the shape: a top league scorer on 2, a 0–0 with Lincoln City inside a five-match winless run, and 5 points from 6. Add five absentees including Wharton and Jørgensen, with Baradji not yet having featured, and this is a side whose attacking evidence is thin in both senses — low in volume and produced by a team still being assembled. Gap: no xG or shot data was obtainable, so this is read from results, personnel and the transfer ledger rather than from underlying numbers.

Millwall are the more interesting side for this card, because their profile is unusual in a specific and quantifiable way: their matches produce goals but not shared ones. Eighteen goals across five league games is a high rate, and yet only the 5–1 at Southampton featured both teams scoring. They won 2–0, 3–0 and 4–0 and lost 0–3 — four matches in which one side was shut out entirely. That is what a heavily rebuilt team looks like before its level settles: £15.2m spent across a striker, a midfielder, a winger and a centre-back, funded by a £12.5m sale, under a manager who has kept his job through it. The complication for today is that the rebuild is currently missing the two most expensive parts — Coburn (£5m) and Servais (£5.1m) are both out, alongside seven more — so the side that produced 4–0 six days ago is not the side available here.
